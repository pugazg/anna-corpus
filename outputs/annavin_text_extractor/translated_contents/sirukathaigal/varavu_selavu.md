## English Translation

“Hello! Who is it? Oh, namaskaram. Yes, this is Nandagopal speaking. Vanakkam, vanakkam. How long have you had this habit? I mean saying vanakkam. Yes, the elders used to say namaskaram. Quite right, quite right. So many things have changed. I merely mentioned it. I too love Tamil. Am I not a Tamilian? Perfectly true.

“It is nothing. Our rice. I mean the rice we supply. Yes, the new arrival is due on the tenth. We hold the usual contract.

“What is all this about our rice being infested, spoiled, unfit for the stomach, and harmful? That is what I am asking.

“The officer responsible for that. I hear they call him the senior doctor. Yes, him. I heard he had found fault with our rice.

“What? A report? Has he sent it in writing? It has come to you, has it not?

“What? It must be a very severe report. Some people have that nature. Oh, I have no grievance against you. Shame, shame. Do I not know your character?

“That doctor’s report is merely book learning. One must understand the world, possess experience, and know the rank and quality of men. Is it enough merely to study and enter government service?

“What? What are you saying? The contract may... He says it must not be approved? Categorically?

“He would say so. Listen to me. This contract amount is nothing remarkable to me. It may be thirty or forty thousand, not ten lakhs. By God’s grace, I am not a destitute beggar. Losing this contract will not drown my household.

“It is not the money that grieves me. My mind cannot bear that someone dares to ruin the good name I have enjoyed for so long.

“What? A salary of one thousand rupees? Let it be. He is only an official. Have I not seen many such fellows? Do you yourself not know?

“I want a just decision. You can give it. In one line, you can write that the report is incorrect. Do I not know your ability? No trouble will arise.

“In any event, he is only responsible for submitting a report. Accepting or rejecting it is your responsibility.

“Will he appeal higher? How high can he go? Is that novice officer going to discover some place above the minister?

“All the ministers are people close to us. Who holds this department? Oh, him? That is excellent.

“You throw that report away. Next Friday at our Amirthalayam, that very minister is going to unveil our portrait.

“Why do you ask like that? His family and ours are related in a certain way. Many people do not know it, not merely you.

“The banquet is at our house. There will be at least a thousand people. Naturally, there will be a dance performance too. Mala will perform.

“Why be shy? You must certainly come. He is a very good man. I mean the minister. Unlike some, he does not consider officials inferior.

“I shall introduce you. You must not always remain withdrawn in the background. Are you not a man who should advance? This is not flattery, but the truth.

“You must come. Shall I send a car? No? Good heavens, you are very timid. Then come by taxi.

“What? You are thinking what reason to give for rejecting the report? Is that even a question? There is an important reason. I can obtain favourable opinions from ten doctors for our rice.

“Let me tell you something in confidence. Do you understand why that officer wrote in this way? That is where you err. You believe everything white is milk.

“Perhaps you have known him for many years, but the man is deep and devious. Listen to this. You remember Duraisamy who contested the election? Yes, Duraisamy, who was defeated.

“That is the subtle point. Tell me, how could you know? Duraisamy is this officer’s younger father-in-law. Yes. Do you now understand why the report is like that?

“What? Should this be included in your note? Why endanger that poor officer?

“What are you saying? You think it is a suitable reason for concluding that the report cannot be regarded as truthful? Very well. If you consider it proper, include the information in your explanatory order. I have no objection.

“If I told the minister about this little matter, he would laugh. It is only a miserable thirty or forty thousand rupees. I care only that my name should not be damaged.

“Many thanks. Shall we begin the supply first? Everything is ready. Must you remind me of every detail? I shall send everything in the prescribed manner. By the Lord’s grace, there is no deficiency.

“My younger brother? He is travelling on some business. Last month he asked after you very closely. He always asks about you.

“I shall tell him, certainly. May I go? Vanakkam, vanakkam.”

* * *

“Nandagopal speaking. Yes, is that the manager? Your voice sounded different. My younger brother insisted that I speak to you.

“I told him that with all the trouble of my work, how would I find time for this? But would he leave me alone? You know his nature. Yes, yes, always in haste, with no habit of considering anything calmly. Well, you must be the one to appreciate him.

“What? Premises? What difficulty is there in finding premises? As you say, my word does carry a little respect in the bazaar.

“It is an excellent place, an upper-storey building. Shame, we have no intention of buying it. Yes, I advanced about twelve thousand. When someone is in difficulty, how can one act without generosity? There is an old saying from the elders: generosity destroys wealth. Yet we have the blessings of great men.

“That is what I say. My brother is hasty. When he spoke with you, he agreed to a commission of a quarter rupee. Of course, you are no simple person. You must have begun from one anna.

“Listen. He says a quarter-rupee commission is not viable. He says so only after calculating. I too examined it and feel the work would be pointless.

“Even if we cover all Tamil Nadu and Malabar as well, a quarter-rupee commission will not suffice.

“Others may manage. They do not advertise and remain satisfied with sales through practitioners. We cannot act that way. We have a name, but must we not protect it? That is why we must spend heavily.

“How great can the business become? Will sixty or seventy people out of every hundred suffer stomach pain? It is medicine, after all. It would not be righteous to pray for disease to spread.

“A tonic? Perhaps, but principally it is good medicine for severe dysentery. That is why it must be advertised properly, and that will cost a great deal.

“What advertising material will you send? How far will that reach? Our method is different. Do we leave out a single market, festival, or conference?

“It will not work. I am not being obstinate; I am explaining the actual condition. I never change my word. My brother accepted it in haste, but an arrangement becomes legally valid only after obtaining my consent.

“Yes, the address may be in his name, but I too own a share. Ours has been a joint family for four generations. That division? It was merely an arrangement for the land law, as though shares had been separated.

“Yes, I speak at such length only because I value association with good people.

“What, you will add one anna? Look here, make it six annas jointly for both of us. There is no injustice in that. You will see later. Six annas. He will come and sign tomorrow itself.

“Yes, your first advertisement arrived. My brother showed it to me. It has a picture of a man clutching his stomach in pain and then another picture of him looking happy.

“You paid the man who designed it five hundred rupees? Look at that. You shower money upon this, yet argue for half an hour over giving six annas to the agent who will shout about your product throughout the country, sell it, and heap money before you.

“The advertisement is good, but in large letters it says, ‘Stomach disease from eating all kinds of rice.’ That is not right. It is not true either.

“I do not say this for my own sake. Everyone knows we are rice merchants. We supply colleges, hospitals, prisons, and every other place. It has gone on for many years.

“Even so, if you say eating rice causes dysentery, the public will not accept it. We must act with knowledge of our people’s temperament.

“Therefore, the advertisement must be changed. The pictures may remain; they are necessary. But replace ‘all kinds of rice’ with ‘adulterated oil causes dysentery.’ That would be excellent. Even the government is strict about adulterated oil.

“Yes, change it and then send the advertising sheet. Would we now lack concern for it? You will say the very opening was spectacular. You will not have anticipated it.

“Do you know who is coming to conduct the opening ceremony? It is next Friday. You tell me: shall I bring the Collector, or do you want a judge? State your wish.

“At least now understand what sort of people you have appointed as agents. Sir, a minister is coming to open our new warehouse. A minister!

“Yes. Are you wondering whether he will agree? He is going to unveil our portrait. He will open our warehouse too, next Friday.

“Your name will come first on the invitation, and my brother’s second. Our name? No, no, that is unnecessary. My brother’s name is enough.

“There will be a banquet and dance and everything else. Now do you see what the initial expenses are like?

“The company has motorcars, does it not? Very well, send two in the morning.

“You must come without fail, with your family. As I said, advertise ‘dysentery caused by adulterated oil,’ in three colours. Excellent. May I go? Vanakkam.”

* * *

“Good heavens, Balu Mudali! Balu! Do you not recognise my voice? Yes, it arrived. I saw it. That is why I called.

“It came from your own printing press, so you must be the one to praise it.

“Why, man? I pleaded that it was a charitable undertaking and that you must help, danced attendance upon you, and gave you a green hundred-rupee note.

“Listen. I am not accusing you of taking it. I ask only after seeing the publication. You have put it there, in the first row: ‘Nandagopal, donation one hundred rupees.’ What is remarkable about that?

“The others gave ten and five. Who else gave a green note? Is it enough merely to print the name?

“Did it not occur to you? Your mind is active when asking for and receiving one hundred rupees. Had you possessed even a little gratitude and loyalty, you would have printed our business address beneath our name.

“Was it forgetfulness? The name of your printing press is present. Did you forget that?

“Go away. Shame! You ask what you should do? What if you put my photograph on the cover? Whether it costs twenty or seventy, ask me and I shall throw it into the street.

“I am the one telling you to do it. Balu Mudali, do not imagine that putting my photograph there gives me great publicity. By God’s grace, if one says ‘Nandagopal,’ a few people already know who that is.

“Enough of this courtesy. If you publish the photograph, ten people will see it and think: ‘We too should do good like Nandagopal and assist the poor.’ The town will benefit. I say it only for that reason.

“I am not some open-mouthed fool who will pay another twenty rupees separately for this. Put it under general expenses.

“Yes, I do not wish to pay money to publish my own photograph. I have told you. Do it if you can. If you intend to approach me again for another undertaking, do it. Remember the one hundred rupees.

“Balu Mudali, why not print it so people understand it is a charitable hospital?

“‘Free hospital’? What wretched phrase is that? What? Good Tamil? Is that so? Very well.

“How many thousands have you printed? What, only that many? Good work must become known to people.

“Is it enough to drawl ‘yes’? Next Friday. Yes, Friday, at our Amirthalayam. Yes, our new handloom textile store in the main bazaar.

“It is the opening of Amirthalayam. Yes, the minister will conduct it. The minister himself. He is coming.

“Do you think he will not come? Do you not know whose party I belong to? The minister’s party.

“Very well. Bring ten copies during that opening ceremony.

“Rupees? Are you mad? Ten copies of the booklet about the charitable hospital. We can give them to the minister.

“A garland? Are you going to bring a garland? You may bring one that has shed all its flowers and ruin my name. Without considering the price, bring a good rose garland.

“Put it on him yourself. Balu Mudali, remember a bouquet too. Garland and bouquet.

“The opening invitation? My younger brother gave it somewhere else for printing. What can I do? I agree that the name of your press should become known. What else can be done now?

“If you wish, print and supply an address of welcome.

“Why do you ask how many thousands as though I were placing an order? Do whatever your means permit. Yes, yes, it is for advertising your printing press.

“Balu Mudali, the address placed in the minister’s hand must look fine. Take care. Your name may otherwise be damaged.

“Do not print merely ‘Nandagopal’ without anything else. Print the complete address. I say this because you may forget.

“For this alone, you ought to give me half your income. Your greed is such that no amount of income is enough. I know, I know.

“Balu, the shop? Beside it. Yes, note this in your charitable-hospital booklet itself. Are you writing it down? It is an advertisement, a full page.

“‘Beware! Beware of dysentery caused by adulterated oil!’ Have you written ‘Beware’?

“‘Karunthanga Chooranam, the proven cure for dysentery. Certain relief in only three doses. Sole agent: Navaneethakrishnan and Company.’

“Yes, my younger brother, our Navaneethan.

“Advertising charge? Very well. Would I fail to obtain it for you? Make sure the advertisement looks fine.

“All right, I shall come. Vanakkam.”

* * *

The above were words spoken one day over the telephone in his mansion by the wealthy and prominent Nandagopal, who possessed many sources of income: a textile shop, a wholesale provisions market, contracts, commission agencies, and more.

Reflect upon the income and expenditure revealed by this conversation, and one part of Selavapuri will become clear. Do not forget: the charge for making three telephone calls must be added to the expense account. If you fail to add it, Nandagopal will not let the matter rest.

20 September 1964
