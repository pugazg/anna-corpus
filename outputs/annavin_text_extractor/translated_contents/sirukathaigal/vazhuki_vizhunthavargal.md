## English Translation

"Get lost, you idler! Trying to fool even me? I ask who broke your leg. Tell me!"

"Shame! When I tell the truth, you refuse to believe it and cross-examine me. This happened because I slipped and fell."

"Whom are you asking to believe that? Come on, just tell me, brother."

"I cannot wrestle with you. If I tell you, will you believe me? You cross-examine like a great lawyer. I had not eaten properly for two days and was exhausted. I had marked a place. The wall was only four feet high. I took a little run and jumped up. I squatted there and looked around.

"I was weak from having no food. I felt strange, almost dizzy. My foot slipped by itself. Look below: a black stone lay there like Saturn himself. My knee and face are all bleeding wounds."

"You stumbled over a blade of grass! This condition after only four feet? Yet your mouth stretches to your ears."

"What is the use of telling you? I no longer possess my former strength, energy, or courage."

"Have you grown old? What is your age? About forty?"

"About that. Even if I have not yet reached it, I no longer possess my old energy. Last month she gave birth to our third child, a boy like a little prince. My woman even said, 'At least when this child is born, may good sense be born in you.'

"My mind has become confused. Earlier, I was alone and had the courage to think that whatever happened did not matter. Now, if something happens to me, her eyes will fill with tears, the family will suffer, and how will she show her face before others? When I think of all this, my heart shudders. So I have reached a decision to abandon this trade entirely."

"Shame! Coward! Go away. Apply something to the wound. Here are eight annas. Buy something and eat. Hey, why are you tying the money into your waistcloth?"

"I must buy something for the children and take it home. Their mother will be consoling them, saying, 'Wait until Father comes. He will bring this and that.' May I go?"

"Go, go. Watch where you walk. Don't slip and fall again on the way."

"Bad luck, bad luck. Have I not climbed and descended all these years? Nothing like this ever happened.

"I was climbing the ladder, telling the women labourers carrying stone and mortar upward to work properly. 'Why are you walking like this, at a tortoise's pace?' I said. I must have gone halfway when my foot slipped. I fell like a log. Every person working there panicked."

"How frightened the others must have been! This is why I ask you to observe omens before leaving in the morning. You never listen. How do you expect me to endure this dreadful sight? Did the proprietor come when you fell? What did he say?"

"What a fine question. Fortunately, he did not see me. If he had, that man would have said, 'Hey, Chinnan! I have repeatedly told you that you have grown old, but you would not listen. You are no longer fit to be a foreman. I will find another. If you fall and die, trouble will come upon my head. Here is the money due to you. Go! From tomorrow, let Ponnan do your job.' He would have thrown earth into our stomach."

"You labour like an ox, without a trace of deceit."

"Yes. When an ox becomes skin and bone, they drive it off to the slaughter dealer. Our fate is the same."

"Do not confuse your mind by imagining all kinds of things. Listen, don't become angry because I ask. Tell me the truth: hasn't your eyesight diminished a little?"

"Have you taken up that old Ramayana again? Pattabhi spread that story that I had night blindness because he did not want me to marry you. Even your father believed it at first. Only after I laughed and explained the matter did his fear disappear. There is nothing wrong with my eyes."

"You have poured milk into my stomach. In the morning, show it to a good doctor and have it bandaged. It will heal in four days."

"Nothing else would be needed to invite trouble upon my head. Foolish woman! Apply something tonight and tie a bandage. In the morning, I must grit my teeth and go to work. If I cannot get up, it is finished. The man will tear up my ticket."

"My heart trembles at the thought of your enduring this pain and going to work."

"Your heart may tremble. Why would the proprietor's heart tremble?"

"That is how our fate is. Even if you go to work, do not strain your body."

"Very well. Go and bring the new physician who is said to have moved into the house at the end of our street. Let us show him."

"I will run and bring him this instant."

"You fool! Is this how oil is stored in leaky tins? Look how much has been wasted. There must be a loss of ten or twenty rupees."

"The tins were not leaking, sir, and the oil did not run out by itself. You arrived in such haste that I could not explain. Before I could, you slipped and fell. Does it hurt badly? In the blink of an eye, you went from this corner to the other."

"Why, the moment I put down my foot, I slipped! Oil lies everywhere. How can anyone manage? Fortunately, the barrels were kept on that side, so it ended here. Is this your idea of proper work? Will asking whether it hurts cool the crown of my head? Answer my question. Leave your sympathy and courtesies aside. Why has so much oil flowed across the floor?"

"It did not leak through holes by itself. I punctured five or six tins and let the oil flow."

"Sinner! Have you decided to destroy my property? You made holes in the tins yourself?"

"Only because I did that did the goods beneath our stock barrels escape the inspector's eyes. We avoided the danger that was coming."

"What are you babbling about?"

"Do not shout. The Ceylon goods are below, beneath the place where the barrels stand."

"Yes, they are. Don't I know? So what?"

"Some treacherous fellow informed the inspector. He arrived with four or five policemen and said he had to search the warehouse. I saw him and said, 'Please sit. I will telephone the proprietor and return.'

"Suddenly, an idea occurred to me. I had to prevent the police from coming to this side. I quickly took an iron spike and punctured five or six tins. Oil began gushing out. It flowed like a canal, covering the whole place.

"After finishing the search outside, he came toward this side. The entire floor was oily. Saying, 'Shame, shame!' he asked what was stored here.

"I said all this oil was for anointing at the Hanuman temple. He became very angry. 'Sir, is this how oil intended for temple service is wasted?' he asked. Then he looked this way and that and went outside.

"We must say this oil saved us."

"Is that so? Tell me first that such an idea occurred to you at a dangerous moment. The inspector feared he would slip and fall if he walked through the oil. That is why he did not search near the barrels."

"He became afraid when I said it was oil for temple anointing, sir. One must not step upon oil meant for a temple ritual. Would that not be sinful?"

"Yes, yes. Our inspector is highly devout. I know. Your trick was not bad. Fine, seal the holes in the tins. Gather every drop of oil lying below and put it separately into another tin. They are holding a festival of one hundred thousand lamps at the Saneeswaran temple, aren't they? Send it there in our firm's name."

"Very well, sir. Shall I have another dhoti and shirt brought from your house?"

"Why? What is wrong with these? They will do."

"How is the pain, sir? The injury is not severe, is it?"

"Blood is seeping from my leg, and there is swelling on my forehead."

"There may be an internal injury. It must be attended to immediately."

"Telephone the doctor. He may become frightened, so say it is only a slight injury. Ask him to come at once anyway. The Ceylon goods will leave tonight, won't they?"

"Yes, sir. I will attend to those arrangements. You look after your body. There is nothing serious with the leg, is there?"

"It will be all right. I am thinking I might stay in a nursing home for a week."

"A golden idea, sir. I was about to suggest it myself. You are no longer young. Why only a week? Stay until your body is fully healed. Is anything more important than our body?"

"One week is enough. This is the very week the college fund committee is setting out to collect money. They would take my life and make me accompany them. Now I shall escape that trouble. What a relief."

"Doctor! I hear the motor car."

Three events followed the incidents in which three men slipped and fell.

## First Event

Police Inspector Ponnuthurai summoned three policemen and issued an order:

> Information supplied by habitual offender Kannan shows that burglar Kuppan climbed the wall of Kaliyaperumal's house last night, slipped, and fell. He has sustained bodily injuries. Go immediately, seize him, drag him here, and put him in the lock-up.
>
> Kuppan's handprints will be present upon the garden wall of Kaliyaperumal's house. Photograph them and bring the pictures.

## Second Event

Building contractor Thinnayiram sent an urgent letter to his manager, Kondarayya:

> From tomorrow, Foreman Chinnan need not report for work. His work is not clean, he lacks bodily strength, and he lacks intelligence. It appears that yesterday his legs trembled even as he climbed the ladder, and he fell.
>
> If we employ such people, the building will not be completed according to our plan. Pay him whatever is due and appoint Ponnan as foreman. Treat this as urgent.
>
> Thinnayiram

## Third Event

A reporter for the newspaper *Thirukkoil* sent the following report to his own paper and, after receiving a special fee, supplied it as an exclusive item to the newspaper *Dharmadhatha*:

> Eminent devotee, supreme philanthropist, and member of a distinguished family, oil merchant Ekambara Bharathiar, while attending to arrangements connected with the anointing ceremony at the Hanuman temple, slipped and fell.
>
> Bharathiar calls his caste "Bharathiar caste," having abandoned his caste name out of patriotism and the belief that all are Indians.
>
> Although there is no danger, Dr Sundaram firmly ordered that Bharathiar, also known as Chettiar, remain in bed for a week. He has therefore been admitted to Sundara Nursing Home.
>
> "My only sorrow is that I cannot participate in the noble work of collecting the college fund," Chettiar, also known as Bharathiar, told our reporter in words that melted the heart.
>
> An X-ray has been taken. Details will appear in tomorrow's issue.
