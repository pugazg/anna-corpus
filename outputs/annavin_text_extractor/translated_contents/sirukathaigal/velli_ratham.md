## English Translation

**Goddess Uma:** Lord of my life! What is this? You look afflicted. At a time when you should appear radiant, proud and crowned with victory, why assume the form of sorrow?

**Ekambareswarar:** Parvati, go lie down without talking. Do not take my life. I have enough anguish already.

**Uma:** What a wonder! On the very day we ought to feel supreme satisfaction and pride, you choose anger and sorrow. My own heart leaps with delight. Should I praise the distinction of the garlands of verse, the fragrance of the flower garlands, the hymns of millions of devotees, or the decorative lights that turned night into day? What can one omit or forget?

“Having received such rare joy, we ought to shower grace, yet you look ready to weep. What causes this agitation? Are you perhaps exhausted, my lord?”

**Ekambareswarar:** You are bewildered because you do not know the cause of my agitation, Goddess. I am bewildered because I do not know the cause of the joy you say makes your heart dance.

“Foolish woman! An event capable of confusing any thoughtful mind has occurred, yet you dance, sing and rejoice like a little child. Daughter of Himavan, you are gradually losing your very capacity for thought.

“We have been humiliated, made objects of ridicule and turned into playthings for useless men. If you thought even a little about this perversion, you would sink into sorrow.”

**Uma:** Lord, what words are these? I cannot understand. You say that devotees calling you “madman” is praise through apparent abuse. No, no; it appears to be literally true. Your mind wanders and you rave.

“Have they humiliated us? Who are those outcast wretches? Did they not see the royal honours paid to us yesterday?

“Hundreds of thousands surrounded us. Fireworks seemed to rain nine gems from the sky. Explosions were so great that Nandi’s face might redden with shame. Millions of devotees acclaimed us as we sat upon a beautifully decorated silver chariot and passed through the streets. The sight still appears in the mind’s eye of the faithful.

“At sacred Kanchi, foremost among the seven cities granting liberation, devotees spent several lakhs of rupees to make us a silver chariot and celebrate the festival. Night turned to day; the town resembled Indra’s heaven. Everywhere people marvelled at the silver-chariot festival.

“When devotees have glorified us in this fashion, why should you grieve? We had long feared an atheist storm was blowing and wondered whether the spirit of worship had burned away. The inhabitants of Kanchi removed our doubt and offered us a beautiful silver chariot.”

**Ekambareswarar:** Parvati, what causes your pride causes my sorrow. The very silver-chariot celebration that makes your heart leap has brought me pain.

**Uma:** I do not understand, Lord.

**Ekambareswarar:** You will not unless I explain. Parvati, did you imagine the silver-chariot festival honoured us?

**Uma:** Did it not, Lord? Spending all those lakhs surely means they glorified us.

**Ekambareswarar:** That is where I think and lose heart. You do not think, and therefore remain happy. Sivakami, thought is necessary. If even we fail to think, what becomes of the universe?

“More than a hundred thousand people came, did they not?”

**Uma:** Yes, from many towns, by motor car and train.

**Ekambareswarar:** The whole town gathered?

**Uma:** Yes. Men and women, children and old people, unlettered and educated, villagers and townspeople: everyone came.

**Ekambareswarar:** They came?

**Uma:** They came and came. Why ask repeatedly?

**Ekambareswarar:** Not without reason. More than a hundred thousand came. For what?

**Uma:** Are you playing with me? Is that even a question? They came to see the silver chariot.

**Ekambareswarar:** Say it once more, gentle one, without anger. Why did all those people come?

**Uma:** To see the silver chariot.

**Ekambareswarar:** Correct, Parvati. You spoke the truth. Lakhs came to see the silver chariot, not you and me. They did not come for our darshan, to worship us or bring us honour.

“Parvati, without shame or sorrow you say they came to see the silver chariot. They did not come to see us!

“We possess neither the glory nor power to gather the millions of devotees and many kinds of people you described. A mere silver chariot brought together that vast multitude.

“I have many exalted names: Lord of Kailasa, Destroyer of Tripura, Kapali, Wearer of the Crescent. Poetry gives me many titles: He whose ear bears an ornament; He whose locks bear the pure white moon; He who smears himself with ash from the forest cremation ground.

“Many titles exalt both you and me. Yet our greatness and glory were insufficient to gather those devotees. The hymns sung of us by the Four and the stories composed by puranic narrators were not enough. A silver chariot was required. Is that not painful, mother of Velavan? You yourself said why the people gathered: to see the silver chariot, not to behold us.”

**Uma:** Lord, I understand to some extent only now.

**Ekambareswarar:** Uma, you too passed the time like those people. You lost yourself in the drums, stared open-mouthed at the fireworks and did not think. You did not listen to the devotees’ conversation. Had you heard, you could no more have suppressed anger and tears than I could.

**Uma:** What did they say, my lord?

**Ekambareswarar:** Everything, except one word about us.

“Whenever they met, people spoke endlessly of which street held the silver chariot, how long it would take to reach another street, when it started and when it would return to the temple. They never thought of us. Every honour and every gaze belonged to the chariot.

“Did they think, ‘The Lord sits there with the Goddess’? No. They forgot us entirely.

“They fired chains of questions: How much did the chariot cost? How much was collected? Who collected it? Where did they go to gather money? A sceptic said, ‘They claim four lakhs, but who knows?’ An unwearying Saivite true believer argued that the account was correct and truthful. A lover of compromise said, ‘Leave the expenditure aside; leave whether the account is right or wrong; the workmanship is excellent.’

“Every conversation concerned the silver chariot, and none our glory. Uma, the chariot hid us. People became absorbed in its festival and forgot even to worship us. ‘See the chariot’s beauty, brilliance and decoration,’ they said. They praised the chariot, not us.

“Do you not know how many entering temples do so for reasons other than pure devotion? Even our darshan normally draws only a small crowd. Show a silver chariot, golden bull or gold palanquin, and the entire town gathers.

“What does that mean? I tell you truly, Uma, the vast crowd made me angry. I wanted to ask:

“‘People who do not understand the Lord’s greatness! You never come with such concern to the temple where I sit every day. Why have you gathered today alone? You evidently possess more love for the silver chariot than for me. If you bore me true affection and were genuine devotees, would not at least the local people come in large numbers every day as they did today? You never come, but today you appeared!’”

**Uma:** You could have asked.

**Ekambareswarar:** Even had I asked, it would not have entered their ears. They were immersed in joyful commotion.

“‘Look at Arumugam; call Arunachalam; she resembles Bhuvana; Ponni’s younger sister is passing; Pontiac motor car; Burma-Shell Company; white silk with gold lace; people from Sir Desikachari’s house; S.O.O. Company; Income Tax Officer.’

“They shouted about countless things, but I heard no word about you or me.

“Silver chariot indeed! It caused pain and diminished our greatness. Place Vinayaka, Vishnu, Narada, Nandi, a bhutagana, Chitraputra, Urvasi, any ascetic, Jesus or Buddha upon the same chariot, and the same crowd would gather. The crowd belonged to the chariot, not the deity.

“That realization angered me. Moreover, Uma, why do they arrange spectacles like silver chariots? Because our greatness and glory somehow fail to attract people. Does that honour us, or ridicule us?

“It seemed the organizers mocked us: ‘Look, Supreme Lord! You complained that devotees had become scarce and temple attendance declined. See our skill in gathering crowds. We made one silver chariot and brought a lakh of people before you.’

“Ten true devotees coming to behold us would be enough. That would bring us honour and pride. What benefit is there in the whole town gathering like this? The glory belongs to the silver chariot, not us.

“Melt all that silver into a scorpion and carry it through the streets, and another huge crowd would gather. Replace us with another deity, and people would note no difference. That is why I became angry.”

**Uma:** Only after so full an explanation do shame and sorrow arise in me. Those acting devotees did not honour us at all.

**Ekambareswarar:** Do you know the ridiculous position in which the organizers placed us? They sent advertising notices throughout the town to announce the spectacle and gather a crowd. In them, the words “SILVER CHARIOT FESTIVAL” were as large as a pumpkin, while our name was not even the size of a rudraksha bead.

“What does that mean? They decided a crowd would not come if told, ‘Come see Ekambareswarar, seek the Lord’s grace and offer devotion.’ Only the call ‘Come see the silver chariot’ would gather people. Was that sacred assembly’s purpose to honour us?”

**Uma:** Certainly not. They mocked us, Lord.

**Ekambareswarar:** Who asked them for a silver chariot?

**Uma:** They possessed excess money.

**Ekambareswarar:** They did not. In the same town, these wealthy gentlemen failed for three years to build a college. They sang laments and held meeting after meeting, but never opened their money boxes. For this alone they poured out wealth.

“Does the town possess a reading room? A playground? A care home for poor children? A municipal public hall? Has the drain’s stench disappeared? Nothing exists. No money can be found for any of it. But lakhs appeared for this.

“This chariot hid us, diminished our glory and damaged true devotion. It swallowed several lakhs merely to provide diversion, idle festivity, spectacle and gossip. What benefit does it give? One day each year. Should so much money be wasted?

“With that sum they could have established an Uma Weaving Mill or Ekambara College, producing benefit every day. The poor could have lived, and rays of knowledge spread. Did they do so?”

**Uma:** They did not.

**Ekambareswarar:** Think, Uma, of those suffering without work, those distressed because they cannot study and those without shelter in the same town. What must they feel when a silver chariot is built at such expense for us to ride one day a year? What will they say?

“Money-seekers who lack even the character to understand devotion have left room for all such criticism and placed us in distress.”

**Uma:** They possess strange minds. Why did leading rich men unwilling to gather one lakh for a college agree to turn several lakhs into a silver chariot?

**Ekambareswarar:** Because they thought they could deceive me. If they built a college, many people would become educated and improve their condition. How could the rich bear to see that?

“They do not mind money being wasted upon explosions and chariots. They will not come forward to enable others to live, improve their position or spread knowledge. Fear, jealousy and distrust afflict them. They worry that if the people’s disease of poverty diminished and ignorance vanished, those people would become their equals.”

**Uma:** They possess no love for the people?

**Ekambareswarar:** If they did, would they claim to lack money for works benefiting the people and then give it to a chariot?

**Uma:** Lord, I cannot bear the pain.

**Ekambareswarar:** Goddess, our mental anguish and that of the country’s poor will not go to waste.

**Uma:** I shall appear this moment in the dream of the man who led this useless undertaking and teach him wisdom.

**Ekambareswarar:** Useless work, Parvati. That man is already lost in dreams. In them, merit accumulates and turns into gold sovereigns; new businesses appear; schemers praise him as a holy man.

**Uma:** Merit indeed! I must dream. Do they not know we shall reject as unworthy of our grace those who refuse to relieve the poor person’s suffering and fail to worship the living deity?

**Ekambareswarar:** They understand only at rare moments. It is not enough for us to ask. The people must ask:

“‘While we remain in suffering, can there be a silver-chariot festival? Is it proper or just? Does God accept it?’

“The poor must ask.”

**Uma:** Will they, Lord?

**Ekambareswarar:** They certainly will. To give the people the courage they require, we have sent our sacred assembly.

**Uma:** Our sacred assembly? Who are they?

**Ekambareswarar:** They do not smear sacred ash, but they are honest. They wear no rudraksha beads, but their hearts are pure. They do not sing hymns, but they are not hypocrites.

**Uma:** Who are they, Lord?

**Ekambareswarar:** The sacred assembly that spreads knowledge.

**Uma:** Does it have a name?

**Ekambareswarar:** Yes, Uma. It does. The members of that sacred assembly are called the Self-Respect Movement.

17 April 1949
