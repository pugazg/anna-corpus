## English Translation

As Lord Parameshwaran performed his dance of bliss, his ears heard Narada's tambura and Nandi's mridangam, the cries of legions of devotees worshipping him with "Hara Hara! Marvellous!", and the songs of divine maidens singing that he danced in ecstasy.

Amid all these sounds, he heard someone sobbing uncontrollably. The Lord stopped his dance of bliss and wondered who was weeping and why that sobbing voice arose.

Mahadevan, who had been dancing while the deer danced, the battle-axe danced and the maiden Sivakami danced, suddenly halted his tandava. The gods, bhutaganas, Nandi and Narada saw him stop and grew frightened. Afraid even to ask why, they slipped away from Kailasa.

The Lord of Kailasa listened closely to discover whether the sobbing sound would again reach his ears. Yes, the sobbing continued. It was a woman's voice. The Great Lord was startled, for it sounded familiar. He looked all around, above and below. No form appeared, but the crying alone could be heard.

Saying, "What wonder is this?" he sat upon his tiger-skin seat. In sorrow, he raised a hand toward his head.

"Enough of your attentions!" said Goddess Ganga, pushing his hand away.

"Oh! Great lady, I had quite forgotten. Ganga Devi, was it you who cried? What is the reason? What villain has harmed you, and how? What cause have you found for such distress? Uma obtained only half my body, but you gained a dwelling upon the crown of my head. When your position is so exalted, why does your heart ache? Holy one, when you hear the people of the world praise your glory, you ought to thrill with delight. Why then do you lament?" Parameshwaran asked compassionately.

Ganga Devi descended from the Lord of Kailasa's mass of matted hair, sat beside him and wiped her eyes. She began speaking in a voice mixed with anger and sorrow.

"So you are dancing the tandava? Is this not the nature of men? Even while lovely women sit with tear-filled eyes, are men not stone-hearted creatures who pass their time in dance, song, feasts and celebrations?"

"Ganga Devi! Did you imagine me no different from other men? I am not a fiend who maligns womanhood. Lovely woman, why confuse your mind needlessly? What is the cause of your anguish? Which evil man inflicted what wrong? Goddess, tell me now! What does such a villain amount to before one who burned Tripura?"

"Enough, enough of your heroic glory. What does it matter to you if anyone insults me? Emboldened by the thought that no one exists to defend me, all kinds of people speak of me with contempt and blame. This is my fate, while you perform your dance of bliss."

"Ah! Did they speak contemptuously of you? Where is that atheist mob? Summon the bull! Bring my trident! I shall destroy them and return in an instant."

"What? Were they then ornaments among believers who spoke degradingly of you? Jewel among women, what did they say? Whether believer or atheist, anyone who reviles you becomes my enemy. Who are they?"

"You have always said, and the gods say too, that the glory I gained by the merit of dwelling in your matted crown is praised throughout all fourteen worlds."

"What doubt can there be? Is it not a truth accepted by mortals too? Who does not know your greatness? Who does not praise your holiness? Many hymns celebrate your glory."

"There are old stories too, about Bhagiratha's effort to bring me down and the marvels that occurred."

"Yes. People everywhere praise you by saying that a single immersion in the Ganges washes away every sin, brings merit, cures every disease and grants good fortune. Wherever they live across Bharata, people come to behold you. Do they not say that Ganges water possesses supreme greatness? What doubt is there?"

"They say all this greatness is false. They say Ganges water is not holy and that it is wrong to claim that immersion in the Ganges makes sin disappear. What use are all the texts about my glory? They have even declared that Ganges water lacks the power to cure every disease."

"Ah! Did they speak with such audacity?"

"You mean that they mocked me!"

"Yes. Those who deny your greatness are supreme sinners. What did they say?"

"They say they conducted research. Contrary to what ordinary people believe, Ganges water apparently possesses no special greatness. Scientific investigation has shown that the claim that bathing in the Ganges cures disease and restores health is untrue."

"The great sinners do not merely deny your glory and holiness. Forgetting even that your source is the lofty Himalaya, they say that you lack the power to do something as lowly as curing disease."

"Yes. Ever since I heard it, your servant has been unable to bear her grief. They spoke so plainly before many people. I do not know how many laughed. In one instant they destroyed a greatness that had endured age after age."

"This is the disorder of the times, Ganga! Even if they dismiss as a 'purana' the truth that Ganga dwells in my matted hair, could they not at least praise Ganges water as exceptional because it flows from the mountains, carries the essences of medicinal herbs and possesses a quality that strengthens the body? What audacity! Did they deny even that greatness?"

"Yes. Look at this!"

Such was the conversation between Parameshwaran and Ganga Devi. Ganga Devi read him a report from the March 17 issue of *The Hindu*:

> Science does not accept the common people's belief that Ganges water has the power to cure all diseases. The river water was tested scientifically. The Bihar provincial Health Department conducted this research between 1917 and 1923. Following that research, it became clear that the water of the Ganges does not possess disease-curing power, Health Minister Rajkumari Amrit Kaur stated in the Delhi Parliament.

On hearing this, the Lord of Kailasa grew angry. After calming somewhat and deciding to speak cleverly, he said, "Ganga! We should not grieve needlessly over what the princess said. She said only that you lack the power to cure disease; she did not say you lack the power to remove sin. Why should you cry?"

Ganga Devi grew angrier than before.

"Splendid! You speak as though offering a banana to a crying child. Once they have said that I lack the power to cure even ordinary diseases, who will continue believing that I possess the glory of removing sin? Ruinous science is destroying what ordinary people believed for so long.

"Those who have come to rule have dared say that mine is neither water that removes disease nor sacred water that removes sin, while calling themselves believers!"

"Yes. They chant 'Hara Hara! Jai Shankar!' and offer worship."

"They worship, yet at the same time they revile my glory as a fabricated tale, a delusion of the common mind unable to stand before science."

"Yes. The matter is certainly complicated."

"And for a woman to say such a thing in the Delhi Parliament!"

"See the injustice! A beautiful woman saying it?"

"An elderly woman, and a lady of rank too."

"Such people have become ministers."

"If a minister speaks this way, how will the people speak?"

"Contemptuously, of course. They will mock you: 'Do you now understand the worth of your Ganges water? You boasted that it was pure. A lady serving as minister herself has declared that it possesses no such worth. All this time we have merely raised a pointless clamour, crying Ganges water, Ganges water.'"

"Would that not burn my stomach? Would my heart not tremble?"

## Translator's Notes

The printed title clearly reads “கனவில் கீரதர்.” Because the source does not explain “கீரதர்” and its intended sense is uncertain, it is conservatively transliterated as “Keerathar” rather than silently emended. The scientific investigation and parliamentary statement are translated as the story's attributed source report and have not been independently verified.
