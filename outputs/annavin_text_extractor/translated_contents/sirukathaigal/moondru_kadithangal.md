## English Translation

I had an irresistible desire to see that wedding. I borrowed money and set out for Killi. I also wanted to see my aunt's daughter Muthamma. There is nothing wrong in telling you the truth, so I have told it. The wedding was between a neem tree and a peepal tree.

In Killi there were many people I knew: Muthu, Manickam, Meenakshi, Aburupam, Akilandam, Innasimuthu, Ibrahim, Sokkan, Marimuthu, and many others. Many weddings had taken place there, but I had attended none. Where was the leisure? Here, I had punishing work at the Universal Relief Fund. Would its manager, Markkabandhu Sastri, grant leave?

But I felt compelled somehow to see this particular Neem–Peepal wedding. You ask why? It was a wedding between a neem sapling and a peepal sapling. Does such a thing happen often? The parrots are disappearing, are they not?

I learned of the wedding only by chance. At the rationalist reading room in which I was deeply involved, the newspaper *Palakani* had printed the announcement in bold type. Former minister Bajirao was its editor.

So many people mature enough to marry suppress the hunger of their hearts because they lack money. Why speak of others? Had I possessed a thousand rupees, Muthamma would be here with me now. Such was the situation. Yet in Killi, neem and peepal were to be married. Thanga Nanayam Thanikasalam would give a concert; there would be a dummy-horse dance; Poyyamozhiyar of the Bogar lineage would present a musical discourse titled "Matted Locks and Flowing Locks"; and there would be a feast with payasam. Devotees would sponsor everything. Minatha Mudaliar had accepted responsibility for collecting both the devotion and the money that demonstrated it.

All this had appeared in *Palakani*.

Such a Neem–Peepal wedding might not occur again for a long time. I thought: let us see it while we still have eyes. If possible, let us explain how mad the undertaking is. Whether anyone else listens or not, Muthamma will be there to listen.

Obtaining a loan was not difficult. Reaching Killi was the difficult part. I arrived.

You ask whether I saw and spoke to Muthamma? Do not probe too much, sirs. What would you have done if you had seen your Muthamma?

The Neem–Peepal wedding was on Friday. I had arrived four days early. I gently raised the subject with a few people. They mocked me as a mad boy.

I was sitting wearily on the veranda of my aunt's house when Surul-Kathi Sundara Vathiyar approached, twisting his moustache and carrying letters.

No one in that region equalled him in the sport of the coiled sword. He ran a *silambam* school, hence the title Vathiyar. In Killi and for ten miles around, his word was law.

People would say among themselves, "He is a thug. If we travel alone at night, he may arrange for someone to stab us. Why make an enemy of him?" But when they saw him, they asked, "Vathiyar! Are you well?"

"By the Lord's grace, I lack nothing in health," he would reply.

"Brother! Is that the young man from the city?" he asked as he came closer. His eyesight was deteriorating with age.

"Yes, Vathiyar. It is I, Natarasan," I said.

"What good fortune! Look, three letters have arrived all at once. I was going to give them to Muthu and ask her to read them. Why seek the queen when the king himself is here? Read!"

He handed me the three letters and sat beside me.

I opened one. It bore a picture of Hanuman, beneath which was printed: "Hanuman Worshipper, Jai Hindu Astrological Society."

"Read! Read!" he urged eagerly.

## Letter One

> To Sriman Sundaram, with the grace of the Lord:
>
> Your horoscope has now been calculated by combining the vibration of your name, the time at which this letter is written, and the year, month, and date of birth you supplied.
>
> Yours is a fortunate horoscope. That is, you will enjoy good standing and fame. You will not lack prosperity, nor will you suffer even the complaint that it is absent. In matters of health, however, the horoscope is not equally fortunate.
>
> A favourable planet has now come to assist you. Very soon, you will receive wealth and grain, together with friendship in high places, happiness of mind, and honour. The ruler's gaze may fall upon you; that is, you may receive special rewards from the government.
>
> If you send your horoscope or that of any person you desire, a complete lifelong prediction will be prepared and returned. Send six rupees as an offering to Hanuman.
>
> Blessings,  
> Hanuman Worshipper Jai Hindu

Even as I read, Vathiyar became jubilant.

"He says 'reward,' brother, and even says a government reward. Why would the government reward me? Am I standing in an election? Am I a Rao Bahadur or a Daylight Bahadur? I don't understand," he said.

What answer could I give? I ought to have said, "Boasting, Vathiyar. Empty boasting." But would that not anger him? Vathiyar believed every word the astrologer had written and sat there swelling with pride.

I opened the second letter. It was written on paper printed with:

> Vande Mataram Store  
> Varadappa Chettiar

"Vande Mataram Store? What is that, brother? Who is Varadappa Chettiar?" Vathiyar asked.

I checked the sender's name. It read, "Chinnasamy, Sendamangalam." I told him.

"Ah! Our Chinnan? My son-in-law's younger brother. Read, read," Vathiyar said.

## Letter Two

> Respects to the most illustrious Vathiyar from Chinnasamy.
>
> A thunderbolt has fallen upon our heads. The police have arrested elder brother on a murder charge. The whole village looks as if death has descended upon it. Sister-in-law and I have fallen at the feet of all sorts of people, but they say they will not release him. She has fainted.
>
> This danger came because last Thai month you sent us that sinful fellow. Elder brother is now in prison because of Pal-Kavadi Vel Swami. They have imprisoned that man too.
>
> The wicked fellow deceived elder brother by claiming that a large treasure lay buried in our garden. Brother foolishly believed him and, following his instructions, reportedly sacrificed a five-year-old boy, a child from the next street. The matter reached the police, and they arrested brother.
>
> Borrow one hundred rupees immediately and come. Do not forget: bring the money. Because the whole village is hostile to brother, we cannot borrow even a single coin here. Our luck has failed and the household is sinking. Come immediately.
>
> Chinnasamy

I felt pity when I saw the suffering of the Vathiyar who terrorized the whole village.

"Alas! A thunderbolt has struck my head! A murder charge is no small matter. What shall I do?" he said through tears.

I opened the third letter. Vathiyar sat sorrowfully with his hand upon his head and did not even ask me to read. At first glance, I too had no desire to read it. I folded it, replaced it in the envelope, and said:

"Make arrangements to go to Sendamangalam, Vathiyar."

"What are you saying, boy?" he asked, weeping.

"I am setting out, boy, I am. My son-in-law has met such a fate..." he said. Then he added, "What more can this wretched day contain? Read it. What further trouble may fall upon my head? Read."

My heart would not permit it.

"It is nothing, Vathiyar. Someone has written all sorts of things about the wedding between the neem and peepal trees," I said.

"Whatever wretched thing it is, read it out!" he said. I could no longer resist.

## Letter Three

> To Surul-Kathi Sundara Vathiyar, from Arulvakku Ambalavana Guru.
>
> You know that I rely upon you for every affair conducted in our Killi. That is why the lease of the grove belonging to our Arulsami temple has been given to you this year. I do not think I need remind you of it.
>
> I was greatly pleased to hear that Buminathar is performing, as a meritorious deed in Killi, the "Auspicious Marriage Ceremony of Neem and Peepal." You must see that the tender coconuts, plantains, and other things he requests are supplied from our grove, and attend to everything required. You are not a frog in a well, so you know the affairs of the village. I mention it nevertheless.
>
> A band of wastrels has now arisen, shouting that there is no god or spirit, astrology is false, divination meaningless, and that neither temple nor temple tank is needed. They are trying to ruin every good undertaking.
>
> A rumour has reached me that these fellows are arranging to speak against, abuse, and create a disturbance at the forthcoming Neem–Peepal wedding in Killi, calling it madness. These witless men do not understand the distinction and greatness of our religion. People like you do.
>
> Therefore, see that no disorder occurs in our village. Use threats of the tongue or threats of the hand, whatever is required, and ensure that Buminathar's undertaking suffers no obstruction.
>
> One member of this association has apparently already arrived in Killi from here. His name is Natarasan. Attend to that fellow first; then the others will not show their heads. Do not fear a police case. I am here.
>
> Yours,  
> Gurunathan

I read it only with great difficulty. Even amid such sorrow, Vathiyar laughed.

"Brother! All three letters arrived together, and my eyes have opened. Only now do I understand that trusting these astrologers, holy-men in disguise, and gurus is madness. I sent three rupees as readily as slices of radish, and that Hanuman fellow sent me a cosmic lie saying I would receive a reward. There, my son-in-law became a murderer because of a swami's words. Now this fellow who grabs whatever he can obtain flatters me while ordering me to beat you. Do you see the joke? I am a fool. I went on believing such things. Only now has sense come to me," he said.

In the happiness I felt, had I possessed one hundred rupees, I would have given it to him immediately and sent him to Sendamangalam. But I was a man who broke his back working for forty rupees a month.

Vathiyar tore the letters into tiny pieces.

"If a man trusts these astrologers, fellows who pretend divine possession, and those who point to tanks and ponds while robbing him blind, a thunderbolt will fall upon the believer's head. Has it not fallen upon mine?" he said as he walked away.

I hear Muthamma's voice. She is calling the child... She is calling me. I alone know her "plan." I am going!

14 January 1954
