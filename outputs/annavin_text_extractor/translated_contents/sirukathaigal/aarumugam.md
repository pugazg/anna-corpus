## English Translation

"Joy and sorrow are the two wheels of life's chariot. Abandon worry and trust in God. Everything happens according to his command. Worship him, and hardship will depart and happiness will arise."

This is the counsel a Vedantist gives a poor man who stands distressed with folded hands, seeking comfort for his groaning heart.

Joy appeared upon God's face.

"We feared that this poor man might forget us because of his hardship, or become heartsick and begin to revile us. Fortunately, the Vedantist became our advocate. He taught that joy and sorrow must both exist. The poor man's mind will find peace," God thought happily.

"Does not God give even a goat a tail suited to its size? However high a village sparrow flies, can it become a hawk? The fellow has only now begun to suffer. He deserves it."

The man responsible for the poor man's suffering rejoiced, declaring that God had punished the poor man and that the punishment was proper.

God's face reddened with anger.

"Cruel man! It was not enough for him to ruin the poor man. He drags me into the public square as well. He boldly declares that the poor man's suffering is my arrangement and even a just arrangement. What audacity! Not content with ruining him, he incites the poor man against me by saying that I arranged everything. What a hard heart he possesses!" God thought, his anger rising.

"Why, brother, do you babble 'God, God' every other moment? You suffer because of the cruelty committed by that wrongdoer. Why would God do such a thing?"

A friend who knew the wicked nature of the man who oppressed the poor man told him that God was not responsible for his misfortune.

God's anger cooled upon hearing this.

"The Vedantist advised the poor man not to forget me even in hardship. In direct contrast, the cruel man who ruined him tried to turn him against me. Fortunately, this friend arrived. He directed the poor man's eyes and thoughts towards the person who caused the suffering, telling him, 'Do not worry about God. Think about the man who brought this calamity upon you.' That is excellent," God thought with relief. The anger left his face, and clarity appeared.

"God! We do not know why you make us suffer like this. Do you stand as an ally of that sinner? Is that justice? What wrong have we done? O God, is this proper?"

The poor man's wife appealed to God and asked him to deliver justice.

Without his realizing it, tears began flowing from God's eyes.

"This helpless woman asks me to dispense justice. She says the wrongdoer caused her suffering. Up to that point, I am pleased; she does not accuse me of causing the cruelty. But she now charges me with complicity. What am I to do?

"That woman reminds me that I must perform my duty. She makes an appeal. Could she not simply abuse me? Then I could escape by saying, 'You reviled God, so I cannot grant you grace. You insulted the deity instead of seeking peace.'

"But this woman lays her hardship before me and asks me to give justice. Alas! I must offer some answer for those tears."

As God sat thinking, tears poured down in streams.

"As God knows, I have done nothing wrong. Do not suspect me without reason. He is lying. I did not seize his property. Ask whether he has any witness. Do not humiliate me. With God as my witness, I did not take that poor man's property!" cried the man who had ruined him.

Some townspeople had joined the poor man's side. They beat the wrongdoer and compelled him to return the property he had seized. Hearing this cry, God wiped his eyes. Resolve appeared upon his face.

"The townspeople have begun demanding justice! There are people willing to speak in defence of the poor man. I will have no more trouble," he thought, and stood with a firm expression.

After his property was restored, the poor man appeared joyful.

"Everything is his doing!" he declared.

Those who had helped him said, "Quite so, father. But did we not rescue you from that cruel man? Good heavens, what a difficult task it was!"

"Can injustice endure? Would God spare the wrongdoer?" the poor man replied happily.

"Give us what is due," said his helpers.

"I should give it? God will grant you long life, health, and unfailing wealth," the poor man answered.

The helpers lost patience and asked with some anger, "All right, let God grant those things. You take out what you owe us."

"I have no objection to giving. I am only worried that doing so may amount to denying God's marvellous deed. Not even an atom moves without him," said the poor man.

Unable to contain their anger, the helpers delivered a powerful slap.

"Without him, you would not receive even this slap!" they crowed.

God grimaced and hurried away to the goddess's chamber.

On that day alone, marvelling that God had assumed six sacred expressions, I recorded the day's events in my notebook under the title "Six Faces."

7 November 1946
