## English Translation

"Swear before God that everything you say will be the truth."

"Why would I tell lies, sir? If I do, won't God put out my eyes?"

"Very well! Your name?"

"Samikannu, sir!"

Samikannu has come to court to testify. When he enters the witness box, the clerk first asks him to take an oath. Swearing upon God, he gives his statement. This is what he says:

"I work as a village watchman, sir! It would have been midnight. As I was walking along Raja Street, I heard someone cry, 'Oh! He has stabbed me! He has stabbed me!' I ran there and saw a woman lying in a pool of blood. This man standing here was holding a knife. I saw him. I ran straight to the police station, reported it, and we caught him, sir."

She was a young woman. That she had been murdered was true. The panchayat members who examined her corpse had also ruled that death had been caused by murder. There was an eyewitness. The police investigation, too, had gathered evidence to show that the young man "caught red-handed with the knife" must have committed the murder. Sub-Inspector Sambu, who conducted the investigation, submitted the following account of that evidence:

Kamala and Karuppan were lovers. They belonged to different castes, but they loved each other enough to wish to marry. Kamala's father, however, did not want to give her in marriage outside their caste. He had arranged for her to marry Bhagyanathan, a wealthy relative. Hearing this, Karuppan had gone to Kamala and asked whether it was true. Kamala had told him that she could not oppose her father's wishes and had consented.

Here was documentary evidence seized from Karuppan's house: a letter Kamala had written to him.

"My beloved, Father has become Yama to us. They say I must marry Bhagyanathan because he has money! Mother threatens to throw herself into a well if I refuse. We must reach some decision about this. I am bewildered and do not know what to do. Forget this unfortunate woman. It seems that in this world there are distances beyond the reach of both the poor and love. Kamalam."

In the letter, she had announced her decision. On seeing it, Karuppan had become savage. Overcome by anger and imagining that his beloved had rejected him, he had committed this dreadful murder.

Before presenting this account, the court clerk had not neglected to question the sub-inspector. Nor had the sub-inspector neglected to say, "I swear upon God that everything I say will be the truth."

Both the witness and the officer had sworn upon God that Karuppan was the man who murdered Kamalam. And here was Karuppan's statement. He too swore upon God as the court clerk required and spoke:

"I'm not a murderer, sir. I didn't kill her! Would I kill my Kamalam, the woman I loved and cherished? Master, believe my words. I swear upon God that I didn't kill her. On the night of the murder, Kamalam sent word through a man asking me to come to her house at twelve. That is why I went. The back door was always kept shut, but that night it was open. I went inside. Nobody else was home. Kamalam alone was asleep. I woke her. She was startled to see me and said that she had sent no word. She told me her father and mother had gone to the cinema. After that, the two of us put out the lamp... Then I heard somebody approaching. Kamalam, who was lying beside me, cried, 'Oh! They have stabbed me! They have stabbed me!' Suddenly I heard someone running away. When I lit the lamp, my Kamalam lay there dead. Beside her lay the knife that had taken her life, like Yama himself. Master, I don't care if I die. But do not put the blame for Kamalam's murder upon me. Master! Master! I swear upon God!"

With tears brimming in his eyes, Karuppan told this tragic story while crying, "God! God!" But who could believe him? The sub-inspector's investigation notes and the testimony all declared that Karuppan was the murderer. The government prosecutor cited them and argued his case. It was only a murder trial, yet he spoke so elegantly that his fellow lawyers had to marvel. Listen:

"Your Honour! The accused Karuppan says, 'I loved her. I called her my darling. Would I have killed her?' You have seen the evidence proving that he did kill her. Even so, he speaks this way in an attempt to escape the grasp of merciful law. In every corner of the country, many people hack one another down at street corners and in gutters because of illicit love! That same illicit love caused Karuppan to murder Kamalam. He loved her, but she was about to take Bhagyanathan as her husband. Karuppan could not bear it. He brooded that his beloved had become his betrayer. This tragedy was the result."

It was a clear case. Therefore, the honourable court faced no difficulty in dispensing justice.

The death sentence was passed and carried out. Each person had testified "upon God." That testimony took Karuppan's life.

After Karuppan had been hanged, here stood Samikannu, the witness, before Bhagyanathan. Bhagyanathan spoke:

"Samikannu! Everything you said in your testimony was fine. But what use was it? I sent you, Uthandi, and Muthan to finish him. I told you to kill that fellow Karuppan and return. Instead, you sent Kamalam, the woman I desired, to Yama's world and now come asking me for money! Hmph! Do I have to pay you as well? To keep all of you from being caught in this case, I have spent money on this man and that man. You should be paying me. Understand?"

"Sinner!" "Scoundrel!" "How could he have done this?" Samikannu, Uthandi, and Muthan spoke about Bhagyanathan along the roadside and in the tea shop. Meanwhile, he rode in a bullock cart to the temple with Kokilam, whom he had obtained in Kamalam's place.

Copies of *Kavadi Chindu* were being sold. The witnesses who had spoken about Karuppan "upon God" remained just as they were. Before every person who entered the witness box was examined, the court clerk continued to make each one repeat the very same oath. Yet unfortunate people continued to be punished; others continued to escape punishment; false witnesses and perpetrators of injustice continued to exist.

What could poor God do? He remained stone.

10 April 1955
