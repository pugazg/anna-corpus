## English Translation

Valli's call of "Alolam" was a song for the whole forest. Birds came to the millet field simply to hear it, pretending to peck at the grain. When Valli swung her sling and sang, "Shoo! Shoo!" the birds forgot themselves, sat upon the branches, and listened in delight. When the song stopped, they returned to eat the millet as though demanding an encore. Valli's musical voice would begin again. Even warriors setting out to hunt would first enjoy Valli's feast of song for a while.

The gifted poet Somasundarar was supremely skilled at delivering a *Valli Kalyanam* musical discourse. He would tell of thoughts Valli never had and utter words Velan never spoke. His songs too were delightful, and people greatly enjoyed his performances.

That day, at a wedding in Arumugam Pillai's house, he was presenting *Valli Kalyanam* with great flourish. A young woman named Valli, not a girl of the Kuravar clan but a relative of the Pillai household, listened in delight.

The performer vividly narrated the episodes in which the old hunter asked, "Dear parrot, you have quenched my thirst for water. Will you not also quench my thirst for love?" then bound her by an oath, and finally married her. Valli listened happily.

Until three in the morning, she lay turning upon a bed in the third section of the house, unable to sleep. The performer's snoring supplied the melody, and the sound of his hands slapping away mosquitoes provided the rhythm. Everyone else slept. Valli's eyes would not close, and her mind too refused sleep.

She lay there marvelling at the performer's skill: how beautifully he had sung the refrain,

> Even if a passer-by utters  
> the name of Valli's husband...

and how he had staggered about to imitate the old man's walk.

Hearing a soft rustling sound, she started and looked. The bridegroom married that morning stood there. She tightly shut her eyes and pretended to sleep.

During the *Valli Kalyanam* performance, the bridegroom had kept looking at Valli. For a long time, he had held her in his heart. Arumugam Pillai had originally thought of arranging the match. But Valli was from a poor household and lived in the third section. When a wealthier alliance became available, the bridegroom's father refused Valli's marriage.

Savithri's wedding took place. Possessing every kind of wealth, Savithri married Singaravelan that morning. That very night, Velan came searching for Valli and stood in the third section of the house.

He glanced sideways at the people sleeping there and recognized Valli. Slowly he went to the foot of her bed. Looking all around, he stepped upon her toe.

Valli's whole body tingled. In fear, she pulled back her foot. The sudden movement made Velan stumble. Someone among the sleepers awoke at the sound and asked, "What was that?"

The bridegroom silently slipped into the kitchen. Valli could not suppress her laughter.

"Some cat came in. I chased it away," she said. The awakened guest resumed snoring.

Valli grew angry. "This man has so much desire for me, yet when a woman arrived with money, he tied the wedding pendant around her neck before ten people in the pavilion. At dawn, he steps on my foot and calls me to come. What arrogance! What injustice!"

But could she open her mouth? Poor man, he had been married that very morning. If the wedding guests found him in this condition, would they not clap, laugh, and spread the story throughout the town? Her innocent heart thought, "Why should I cause him trouble?"

Inside the kitchen, the bridegroom's heart throbbed. He repeatedly came out, then hid again whenever a sleeper moved a hand or foot. He grieved over the foolishness of what he had done. Once he knew the awakened person was Valli, however, his fear vanished and desire prevailed.

He went near her and softly called, "Valli."

She sat up and asked rather loudly, "Why? Who is it?"

"Speak softly, Valli. It is nothing. I have something to tell you. Come here alone."

"Hmph! What outrage is this? I will not come."

"Shame! Are you afraid I will do something to you? Nothing of that kind. Just come here."

The poor girl feared that if the conversation continued, the sleepers would wake and scold her. She rose quietly and went toward the garden, with Velan following.

When they reached the garden, she said:

"Look here! If anyone wakes, what will become of me? They will abuse me: 'What business has an unmarried girl speaking to a man at this hour, especially a man married this morning?' Go and sleep without another word. Are you really the man who claims to desire me so much, after marrying that faded little creature? She climbs the wedding platform, but I am meant for your bed? We may be poor, Maharaja, but we fear dishonour. We have played together since childhood. That is enough. Go. I am not a woman who will yield."

"Valli, do you think I came with an evil intention?"

"Do not touch me while speaking. Stand at a distance. I will scream and wake the whole house."

"The storyteller said tonight that a man will swear any number of false oaths for a woman. I heard him."

Again they heard footsteps.

Valli wrung her hands. "My honour will be lost! Go away somewhere. Someone is coming!"

Velan sprang behind a large tree and hid.

The person who arrived was Valli's aunt, a very crafty woman. She had noticed Valli and Velan enter the garden and followed them. She had a son, Palani, whom Valli had stubbornly refused to marry. Because of that, the aunt bore a Vedic fury toward Valli. Thinking she had found an opportunity for revenge, she called out from a distance:

"Why, Valli! Chaste woman! Is this your true worth?"

"What is this, Aunt? Why are you saying all sorts of things? I came into the garden because my stomach hurt, and you give it a dreadful meaning."

"Did Velan come as your companion?"

"Where did he come?"

"Why are you lying?"

"Aunt, I swear I did not ask him to come. He came himself, stepped on my foot, and brought me into this condition."

"That is not my question. What will people think if they see the two of you alone here at this hour? Valli, you are a commodity awaiting sale. If this becomes known, who will marry you? I will go wake everyone in the house, bring them here, and strip both of you of your honour."

"Aunt, I worship your feet. If Grandmother comes, she will turn me into a corpse. Everyone will spit on me. I know no sin. You wicked man, you have brought this harm upon me!"

"Why curse him? Where did your sense go?"

"Save me, Aunt! I fall at your feet."

"Very well. Swear that you will obey me. Give me your hand."

"I swear I will listen to you and act accordingly. Mari is my witness."

"Good. I will tell no one what I saw here. You must marry my son."

"Palani? I will not."

"If you forget your oath, you will not find even earth to eat. Do not forget that you swore upon Mari."

"Oh! What a terrible time this is. What shall I do?"

"Will you obey your oath, or shall I call the people in the house?"

"Do not call them. I will marry Palani. I truly swear it."

"Then it is settled. Vela! How much longer are you going to remain behind that tree?"

Having frightened Valli into submission, the aunt went behind the tree and was startled to find that Velan was not there.

"Valli! What magic is this? The man who went behind the tree has vanished!" she said. Valli too searched and stood bewildered.

While the two had been speaking, Velan had climbed onto a branch, jumped down toward the neighbouring street, crossed it, and lain beside the storyteller like the most innocent of men.

"Wherever he has gone, let him go. Go inside and lie down. According to your oath, you and Palani will be married next month. Understand? Even if Grandmother objects, you must say, 'I will marry only Palani.' Do you understand? Go inside and sleep."

"Aunt, it is enough if he gives up acting in plays. And he performs women's roles too!"

"Very well, I will tell him. But when he takes a woman's role, his speech and walk are exactly like a real woman's."

Valli laughed, returned inside, and lay down as before. By invoking the oath, he had secured his marriage. The aunt thinks she saw through our foolishness and made me her daughter-in-law, Valli thought. She felt like laughing, and a smile played upon her lips even as she fell asleep.

In the garden, the "aunt" removed the wig, sari, and other parts of the disguise and became Palani.

"Had I not been skilled in women's roles, could I have acted as my own mother and obtained Valli's oath?" he thought proudly.

If Valli sleeping inside had taken a lamp and looked at the people lying there, she would have seen the real aunt asleep. But she had sworn her oath. Believing that Palani was now destined to be her husband, she slept.

At dawn, Valli saw the face of Palani, her future bridegroom, and grew shy. At that very wedding pavilion, ceremonial betel was exchanged to confirm Valli's engagement. Palani told his mother that his drama had succeeded. The next month, Valli's marriage took place.

*Dravida Nadu*, 1943
