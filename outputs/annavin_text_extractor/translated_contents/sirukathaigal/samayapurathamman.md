## English Translation

Tamil title: சமயபுரத்தம்மன் - மானநஷ்ட வழக்கு

To the compassionate Government,

This is the opening petition in a defamation suit sent by Samayapurathamman, who cures every disease, grants all wealth and good fortune, bestows peace and happiness, and showers grace upon everyone.

Though you call yourselves a secular government, you grant leave for the festivals, auspicious days, and days of worship belonging to every religion, thereby encouraging people to visit temples and tanks and perform the rites and customs through which devotion is displayed. When hungry people protest in anger, you tell them to practise devotion and that all their troubles will disappear, and you proclaim the excellence of bhajans. By adopting the temple tower as the emblem of the place from which you rule, you make perfectly clear that you have not forgotten religion. It is therefore evident that this Government possesses the courage to protect religion, Hinduism in particular, and the Sanatana faith most especially. With complete confidence that I shall receive proper justice, I send this petition.

I was thrilled to hear ministers fortunate enough to belong to this compassionate Government repeatedly explain to the people the necessity of religion and the benefits of temple worship. Above all, when I heard the rare religious discourse delivered by the Acharya, I danced in bliss. When the great leaders who govern the country preach in this fashion, how can the few who babble about rationalism, science, progress, and discovering root causes ever succeed in destroying the influence of religion and the authority of puranas, epics, and caste? They call the epics fabrications, say those fabrications are useless, and keep asking: "What caused this? How is that consistent? How could this have happened? Is this the act of an intelligent person?" The very thought of their defeat gives me more pleasure than an ablution with a thousand tender coconuts. Because you are such devout people, I have sent this petition with confidence.

What is wrong with speaking openly? At first I had not the slightest wish to do so. I was even ashamed. I feared it might invite mockery and contempt. After careful thought, however, I gained courage: there was nothing wrong or degrading in making a complaint to my own devotees. Only then did I write this.

I nearly forgot. The packet enclosed with this letter contains six dried neem leaves and some vermilion. They possess great power. Use them and obtain happiness in this world and the next.

I must explain who I am, for the time has come to file a suit. Please do not mistake this for self-praise.

I am the "goddess" near Tiruchirappalli. My name is Samayapurathal. The world knows that my own name has become the name of the place, Samayapuram.

People call me Mahadevi, Mother, the gracious mother, our Samayapurathamman who grants every boon, and the goddess who cures every disease. My temple and its influence grew slowly but surely, until I became the Samayapurathamman worshipped by all.

There was nobody who did not seek me and no holy tongue that did not sing of me. What moving songs I have heard! What wonderful rites! Even now my body thrills when I remember them.

Ranganatha, the reclining Lord, has conducted his kingdom at Srirangam for a very long time. Yet the fame of my shrine grew until one might have thought it could make even him rise, come to see me, and depart.

"Mother! A film has fallen over the eye of my child, whose body is like a golden statue. There is no medicine we have not tried and no doctor we have not sought. Nothing has helped even a little. Mother, I have brought the child and laid her in your presence. Only your grace can save this golden creeper. I obtained her only after going from temple to temple. Samayapurathal, I came here for refuge because I know your glory. I have placed the child at your feet. You alone are our support and destiny; this child is your alms to us. Only if you open your eyes and look will she live. Mother, I shall offer you a golden eye! I shall install a silver cradle! I shall arrange an ablution with a thousand tender coconuts, offer five thousand oranges, prepare divine rice with milk pudding and pulses, establish your sacred lamp, hold a festival, and bathe you in honey. Samayapurathamman, mother of all, grant us your grace. You can do it!"

They would worship me so. The memory makes me want to sigh deeply.

"It has been eight years since my marriage. Emalatha across the street was married on the same day and has borne her fourth child. It is nothing but skin and sinew, but still it is her fourth child! In my barren womb not even a worm has stirred. There is no tonic I have not swallowed and no lady doctor I have not consulted. I have eaten dozens of preparations from Lodha and box after box of Siddha medicine and red oxide. I have completed the pilgrimage to Rameswaram. Nothing has helped. Samayapurathamman, you alone must grant me a child and allow my lineage to flourish. I possess wealth in lakhs and comforts that make others envious. All I lack is a child to gather into my arms. Mother, give me the nectar-fruit of a child. Once it is born, I shall bring it to your shrine and offer its weight in silver. If it is a girl I shall name her Samayapurathal; if a boy, Samayapuratharasan. Do not test me any longer, Mother. Grant me your grace!"

Their hearts would melt as they pleaded.

"At first I thought it was merely a scratch caused by itching. But the wound grew, and foul-smelling fluid began to seep from it. A country physician examined me, and I have taken English medicine too. Goddess, it will not disappear. It has become horrible to look at and stinks when one approaches. My own wife, not knowing I can hear, tells the woman next door. What karma is this? What sin did I commit to acquire this disease? They call it leprosy, Goddess! Why did leprosy come to me, when I harmed nobody? Those who know say that no disease remains incurable if one circles your temple. A single glance from you is enough to make severe leprosy vanish in an instant. Goddess, can you not restore a golden body if you wish? When all power is yours, can curing this sinner's leprosy be impossible? I shall serve you according to my means. I shall sell my house and property to serve you. Wipe away this evil deed, Mother! O you whose great power ends incurable karma, queenly Mother who rules glorious Samayapuram, look upon my fate!"

They would stand worshipping with folded hands and streaming eyes.

I know that common people trouble you by repeatedly writing that they need bridges, schools, roads, work, lower prices, and lighter taxes. Do not think I am adding to that nuisance. I shall shorten this letter, though the distress in my mind is as great as a mountain and has forced me to say so much.

To state it briefly, devotees came to me in endless streams: asking for children; asking me to remove disease, insanity, ghosts, and spirits; asking for wealth and a livelihood; asking for a suitable marriage; and asking me to flatten an enemy. They came on foot and family by family, bathed and wearing vermilion, carrying children at their hips. Vatsala in diamond earrings came; Velathal with stomach pain came; the gentleman with tuberculosis came; the minor with venereal disease came. There was no distinction of community or caste. Everyone came.

They conducted worship with pomp: the sixteen forms of service, ablutions in gold, hymns, songs of praise, ritual bathing and adoration, ceaselessly. Without any deficiency, rival, or opposition, I spread my fame to all eight directions, became the chosen goddess of lakhs upon lakhs of people, and sat enthroned in majesty.

But now my heart leaps in terror. I feel like weeping, wondering who can know what tomorrow will bring. Disaster has struck, a great disaster. My realm is endangered, my standing shaken. A conspiracy is underway to destroy my influence. Cruel people have arisen to put an end to my splendid life. When I wonder how I can escape and survive, my whole body trembles.

Small-minded people boldly commit a petty act that destroys the influence I established and cultivated in the Tamil land. Instead of condemning the act and opposing this ruinous trend, people support it.

I offer an example; please do not imagine that I am cursing in anger. You gained popular influence, lawfully became ministers, and now administer the Government. Suppose a group openly treated you as insignificant, ignored your orders, established a "rival government" before your very eyes, advertised it ostentatiously, gathered the support of ordinary people, and issued commands that you must submit to its rule. Would you not become furious and distressed?

I have long ruled with every kind of authority, as a visible deity and a goddess whose glance bestows grace. And now they have established a rival government against me! Is this not the work of base people? Could I possibly smile at it? There is no wrong in telling you the truth: my tears are mingled with the neem leaves I sent you. Such is my anguish.

What sort of influence do they imagine I possess? How many fasted for me! How many fell at my feet! How many motorcars sought my shrine! How many lords worshipped me! How could I fail to swell with pride?

The king of sevenfold music, Thyagaraja Bhagavathar, had been sentenced to life and spent two years in prison. Through the skill displayed by a barrister from the white man's country and by the Madras barrister Ethiraj, whose intelligence even that foreign barrister admired, Bhagavathar was released. Immediately he came to my shrine, worshipped with reverence and faith, performed rituals, and bathed me in music. That is one small example of my influence.

When I think that a time has now come for such influence to decay, my mind becomes a volcano. People circled my temple tirelessly and worshipped my name without fatigue. They loudly declared that nothing lay beyond my power. My millions of devotees never thought to ask or investigate whether I had granted all the boons they requested, ended their incurable karma, or fulfilled their demands. They kept worshipping. Now I keep weeping.

I saw mountains of fruit, pools of milk, heaps upon heaps of silk garments, mounds of delicacies, fragrant sandal paste, and flowers whose scent carried for miles. After seeing all that, would anxiety arise in me? I rejoiced, thinking that whether or not I cured their troubles, these devotees would never leave me wanting. I lay there in pride.

Were only common people my devotees? I saw barristers, doctors, engineers, collectors, police officers, and soldiers circle my temple. In the fields where each possessed authority, they became ambassadors bringing others to me. Those who submitted to my dominion were no ordinary people. And now this has happened to me!

I delighted to hear the townspeople say, "I am going to have a vision of Samayapurathamman," "I performed worship for Samayapurathal," and "I have made a vow to Samayapurathamman." Now another kind of speech falls upon my ears like a red-hot iron.

The Self-Respecters, monsters of the new age, roam about asking, "What religion? What sacred town? What worship? What ending of karma? What grace? What bestowal of prosperity?" They call religion stupidity, the puranas fraud, the epics repositories of degradation, rituals superstition, and sastras conspiracies. Their opposition seems to have brought me low.

Those fellows conduct a whirlwind campaign: "Do not become a husk by trusting Samayapurathamman. Do not squander the money earned through hard labour. Worship the walking temple, the human being. Do not follow the path shown by deceivers." It may appear that they have exhausted the minds of my devotees and ruined my influence.

Do not hasten to console me: "Mother, is that all? Today itself we shall issue a prohibitory order and attack and demolish those upstarts. We shall build a barrier so that none of their speech, writing, songs, or performances spread through the country. Put away your grief."

Sirs, the blow and humiliation that have fallen upon me did not come from the Self-Respecters. Everything is the work of our own people. Those destroying me are not people deficient in devotion. They are not those who ask where the Supreme Being is. They are not those who say that the puranas and epics are lies fabricated by men who want to live without labour and deceive the ignorant. This consequence came entirely from devotees, sirs. Entirely from devotees!

After all my former splendour, an act that causes me loss of honour is taking place openly. My greatness is being diminished and my fame stained. It is happening very near me and with great urgency.

A few miles from Samayapuram lies a backwater village whose name nobody would once have heard and which possessed no importance. Today its name has become famous. It has become another Samayapuram, one might say. I say it with a burning stomach.

The hamlet is called Purathakudi. One could scarcely find a hundred people there altogether. Yet today it is in an uproar. Crowds travel there from villages and towns within twenty or thirty miles: men and women, the withered and the brightly adorned, those walking and those riding on the road, those with money in hand and those hungry in the belly, tonsured heads and ostentatious gaits, every variety. All go crying, "Purathakudi! Purathakudi!"

They exclaim, "Ah, what glory! What glory! What power! What power!" My ears break and my eyes stream.

"He regained his eyesight! The court case was won! A child was born to one who had been childless for twenty years, even though her husband is dying of a terrible wasting disease! Treasure was found! Venereal disease fled! Leprosy vanished! The dragging leg, the convulsing hand, everything was cured!"

They tell story after story. It is all falsehood, but today it has become sacred nectar.

Before my very eyes, close to the place where I rule, the same people who praised, blessed, and worshipped my glory and grace now go to Purathakudi with precisely the devotion with which they once sought me. What stupidity, confusion, and ingratitude! My name once resounded everywhere. Today everybody sings and dances in praise of Purathakudi. Imagine my suffering at the sight.

It concerns some ascetic. Thousands of ascetics lived on the alms I gave them. A cat supposedly bit this man and he died. A dog had bitten the cat that killed him, and that dog was rabid. The story itself is contemptible. It begins with the rabid dog: the dog bit the cat, the cat bit the ascetic, and the ascetic died. Yet people, without a trace of clarity, believe that smearing themselves with ashes taken from the place where he died grants every supernatural attainment.

This contains information that an important Government must consider urgently. The ascetic responsible for this new sacred place that has sprouted to destroy my influence was a Christian, not a Hindu. I ask you to note that immediately. Those streaming to Purathakudi like swarming insects are Hindus. The man who created the situation that torments me today was a Christian.

Hinduism is our own ancient religion, bestowed by the thirty-three crores of gods, possessing incarnations, and producing Alvars and Nayanmars. Despite such a religion, people born and raised within it treat the burial place of a Christian ascetic as sacred and travel to Purathakudi. Can this be tolerated?

Fortunately, they have forgotten the dog and cat. They stop with the ashes.

Why do those who sought me, sang my fame, proclaimed my greatness, and made me believe completely that my influence would never perish now run to a new place?

If a patient tells the doctor treating him, "I am going to bring a good doctor to examine me," what does it mean? Is it not an insult?

If a man's friend asks him, "Could you not find a beautiful woman?" what does it mean? Does it not mean the woman he has is ugly?

When I, Samayapurathal, am here as mother of all, ready to grant every desired boon and established for so long, is it not an injustice to travel in herds to a place that sprouted only yesterday?

These people must be thinking: "We worshipped Samayapurathal and gained nothing. We believed she possessed every power, yet no trouble was solved. Worship there is useless; going there is empty work. Sore feet and lost money are the only benefits. We hear that a marvel is spreading at a place called Purathakudi, and that the ashes of an ascetic who died there cure every disease and make everything succeed. Let us go there and have our trouble solved."

Does that not mean they no longer trust me? Without saying it aloud, are they not declaring that my greatness is a useless fabrication? You tell me what my heart must feel.

Do not dismiss this by saying, "It is some matter concerning gods; what do we know?" The hardship that came to me may visit you tomorrow, so you must think.

Suppose you decorated a stage on the Marina at Triplicane, lit a thousand lamps, and began, "Beloved friends! Dear friends!" Tens of thousands have gathered. Then someone in a distant corner begins, "Comrades rich in affection!" If the crowd before you rises and goes there, how will your heart feel? Would you not even wish for the sea to rise and drown those thoughtless people, or for a fierce wind to blow sand into their eyes and destroy them?

Now consider how the affair at Purathakudi must torment my mind.

My honour is vanishing. The life I have led is being ruined. My fame declines and my influence crumbles. Is a newly sprouted place to be called sacred and destroy the greatness of my Samayapuram? Am I to endure that and still remain a goddess?

You who know the superiority of devotion! You virtuous people who honour antiquity! You meritorious souls who call the puranic age a golden age! This is not merely my problem. Think carefully. It is a public problem, a complicated question for which all of us must confer and find a remedy.

"If they seek a new place, it means faith in the old place has died. It means they were deceived all these years," say the Self-Respecters.

"How long will this last? Until another one sprouts!" they mock.

"So much fraud is being exposed one piece at a time," they thunder.

"Which is the place of great power, Samayapuram or Purathakudi?" they ask in ridicule. "Would people with true devotion and belief in God change their place of worship as though changing a shirt? If they possessed clarity, would it take them so long to understand that Samayapuram amounts to no more than this?"

There is no use becoming angry with them. If another miracle-worker can arise while I am here, and if there is such competition, jealousy, rivalry, and quarrelling among us, will they not say it is all fraud?

Therefore, sirs, since Purathakudi is destroying my influence, undermining my standing, insulting my greatness, and causing people to doubt religion itself, you must protect religion and my glory by immediately issuing a prohibitory order and closing Purathakudi. Otherwise there will be humiliation for me and disgrace for you.

"Increase production" is the policy of the present Government. Some of our people, without understanding the matter, apparently argue: "Even if production cannot be increased in anything else, surely it is proper to increase the production of sacred places."

In this field, however, the more production increases, the more the value of the existing products declines. Ultimately the value of the entire field will be ruined.

Moreover, do not forget that a Christian is the cause of Purathakudi. *Satyameva Jayate!* *Raghupati Raghava Raja Ram!* These are our mantras. Take care.

If you fail to recognize the urgency of the situation and the danger of the problem and do not act immediately, I have resolved to file a defamation suit accusing both the man of Purathakudi and you, his accomplices, of destroying my honour. The priest assures me that victory will certainly be mine. Even I know how many judges have visited my shrine.

Do not ignore this as something or other that I have written. Find and provide a remedy immediately.

Thus,

Samayapurathamman,
who has lived in glory
for a very long time.

**Postscript:** Do not make the excuse that you have too much political business. I remind you that our very politics is built upon religion. Moreover, now that you have tamed even parties that opposed the Congress, such as the Labour Party, by giving them office, the Government has no trouble, the priest tells me. The new minister is supposedly a devotee. I do not know whether he is coming here or going to Purathakudi. Attend to my affair immediately and do what is necessary.

**Tiruchi, May 2:** Hundreds of patients, believing that their incurable diseases will be cured, have been coming to the village of Purathakudi, twelve miles from here.

Purathakudi lies on the main Tiruchi-Madras road. Most of its inhabitants are Roman Catholics. A church of the Mother, built like a Hindu temple about a hundred years ago, stands there. In the cemetery adjoining it, Reverend Mudiyappar, who lived in the nearby village of Irungalur, was buried many years ago.

During the past few days, numerous patients, mostly Hindus and Muslims and including women, have been arriving at the church. Many are said to be possessed by evil spirits.

The patients are seated in an enclosed place. After a short time they emerge and say that the evil spirits possessing them have flown away. Members of the church hold a vessel of fire in their hands and ask the patients to swear three times that the spirit has declared it will leave.

The patient then runs to a nearby palmyra grove and strikes his or her head against a tree. A member of the church cuts off a little of the patient's hair and ties it to the tree. With this the treatment ends, and the evil spirit is considered to have left the patient.

People suffering from leprosy, defective eyesight, and other diseases also come here.

No one, however, is prepared to guarantee that the treatment is genuine. Yet people continue to arrive in great numbers, creating additional transport and public-health problems.

16 May 1954

## Translator's Notes

The story is a satirical petition spoken in the voice of the goddess Samayapurathamman and followed by a period news report. Three heavily corrupted OCR pages were transcribed directly from the scans before translation. Administrative, legal, commercial, and political language is intentionally retained because the goddess treats religious influence as a monopoly threatened by a rival enterprise. Historical names and claims are translated as they appear in the source and are not independently asserted here as verified fact.
