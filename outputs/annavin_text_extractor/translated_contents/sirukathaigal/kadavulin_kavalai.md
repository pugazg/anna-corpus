## English Translation

“Parvati! Have you seen these sinners? As though the trouble they have caused us year after year were not enough, they now bring us further humiliation and blame. Vedaranyam! Saivites call it Thirumaraikadu, a shrine celebrated in sacred song. And Thiruthuraipoondi, is that an ordinary place? The entire town belongs to the temple. Tiruvarur is the abode of Thyagaraja. Bhuvaneswari lives in Pudukkottai. Madurai is the dwelling place of you and me. In which town do we lack a temple, large or small? Ah! Is Thanjavur district ordinary? Wherever one looks, there are temples...”

“What are you speaking about with such sorrow, my lord?”

“What else would I speak of, Parvati? I mean the recent atrocities committed by Vayu and Varuna.”

“You began to mention that, but instead you speak painfully of sinners.”

“Yes, Parvati. In town after town, every temple has its presiding deity and sacred legend, and for so long people have proudly proclaimed those gods’ powers. Now a cyclone has blown and rain has poured down. Thousands have died and lakhs suffer. Reports of terrible scenes arrive, bringing tears. Do you know what these sinners are doing at such a time?”

“Which sinners, my lord? The Self-Respecters whom people abuse as atheists?”

“I have often told you how good they are. Not they, goddess. Their hearts have been shaken by the cyclone and rain, and they run everywhere with humanity, helping people. I mean those sinners on earth who wander about calling themselves devotees. They are the ones bringing us humiliation and blame, Parvati.”

“You call devotees sinners, my lord?”

“What would you call someone who betrays you when trouble comes?”

“An evil man.”

“Someone who knows the truth yet deceives the town?”

“A cheat.”

“Someone who asks for a light for his beedi while the whole town burns?”

“A fool, we might say.”

“I combined all those qualities when I called them sinners. Parvati, look here at the starving families. Do you see the swollen, decaying corpses over there? How many thousands of goats and cattle lie dead? Houses float in water, and though floodwater is everywhere, crowds suffer for drinking water. Ah, what cruelty! Does it not bring tears to your eyes? At a time like this, should they offer whatever help they can, or drag me into it and make the marketplace laugh at me?”

“What have they done, my lord?”

“The devotees have chosen this very moment to speak of my prowess, goddess. Look, there is apparently a village called Mangudi near Kumbakonam, and a temple there. A boy took shelter in it from the rain, and a scorpion stung him. He ran out screaming. A little later, thunder and cyclone came and the temple collapsed with a crash. Had the scorpion not stung him, the boy would not have run and would have been trapped inside the falling temple. So they say I went in the form of a scorpion and saved him. They have even performed ablutions and worship in gratitude.”

“How is that sinful, lord of my life? It is a certificate they give you!”

“Fine, then. Do you know what happened at Manur near Aruppukkottai at the same time?”

“Did another story occur there in which a palmyra fruit happened to fall just as a crow flew away?”

“No. Floodwater came. The frightened villagers entered a Subramania temple there. A little later, the temple collapsed and everyone inside was destroyed.”

“Alas! Is that so?”

“Yes, goddess. At the same time, another report appeared in the newspapers. The sea itself entered Vedaranyam. They conducted a Rama bhajan so that the cyclone would cease, and only afterward, they say, did it stop.”

“Even I feel like laughing when I hear it, my lord.”

“You laugh. But suppose they looked at you and asked, ‘Parvati, you went to the bank of the tank at Sirkazhi to feed milk to the child Gnanasambandar when he cried. Come and give a little to our children now.’ Leave aside how you and I would feel. How angry would our legions of devotees become?”

“Thirupananda Variyar and the Kundrakudi pontiff would turn into fierce Rudra forms!”

“If the Self-Respecters asked them now, what answer would they give? Why, Parvati, even I feel ashamed. Rameswaram is a place visited and worshipped by President Rajendra Prasad himself. Kumbakonam will soon hold Mahamaham, and the government is taking special care. Thayumanavar is in Tiruchirappalli; you know his story. The puranic writers say he bestowed grace upon a mother suffering in a flood. In Karaikudi, Paramakudi, and Pattukkottai, Nadiyamman is renowned. Where is there a town without a temple? There is even a proverb saying one should not live in such a town. Despite all this, if someone asks, ‘Why then the cyclone and rain?’ what answer will devotees give?”

“At this time, the Self-Respecters will not ask about all that. They keep saying that people are more important to them than Mahesan.”

“Then, Parvati, should we not call them the true devotees?”

“Yes, my lord. That seems right to me too.”
