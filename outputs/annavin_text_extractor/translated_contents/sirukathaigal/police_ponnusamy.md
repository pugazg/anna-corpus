## English Translation

There he comes: tall, long-moustached, and imposing. Who does not know Police Ponnusami?

Thugs, ruffians, murderers, and robbers are all mere specks of straw to him. He never lets them go. He has even received medals in appreciation of his courage.

To him, law is law. He is such a dutiful and honourable man. He bows to no one. Bribery and corruption are bitter as neem to him. More than fearing punishment by the government for taking a bribe, he dreads the inquiry that Yama, lord of justice, will conduct after his death. The shining religious mark upon his forehead declares how devout he is.

“Whoever it is, what concern is that of ours, sir? Duty is duty. If he is rich, go to his house; if he is a dangerous rogue, two punches should make him howl,” he says boldly. But when he sees temple priests, Ponnusami never fails to remove his police cap and bow. A priest receives more respect from him than the Deputy Superintendent of Police. Such is his faith in Sanatana tradition.

He now faces a difficulty. He must travel to a neighbouring village to catch a “case.” Not a murder case, but a marriage case. Yes, the police department has received information that a man intends to marry a second wife while his first is still living.

To Ponnusami, law is law, so he is preparing to leave for the village. Sub-Inspector Sampath is an unusual fellow. He knows a great deal about Ponnusami and has a slightly mischievous nature. As Ponnusami took leave of the station and started home, Sampath called him.

“Ponnusami!”

“Sir!”

“Do you remember your duty?”

“I must go to the village, stop the second marriage, arrest the man, and bring him here, sir.”

“Hmm. In your ten years of service, have you ever failed to catch anyone who broke the law?”

“Never, sir. To this man, law means law!”

“You will not spare anyone who violates it?”

“I would not spare even my own brother. First thing, I would put handcuffs on him.”

“Very good. Marrying another woman while one wife is living is wrong, is it not?”

“Under the law, it is wrong, sir. Wrong.”

“Good. Then what is this? Look.”

Ponnusami looked. Why did he stare like that? Why did his face assume such a strange expression? Why was it covered in sweat? Every moment he appeared more sorrowful.

Here is the reason. Tomorrow, a wedding for Rukmini was to be held at Bhimarayar’s house on Big Street. Bhimarayar had requested special police protection for the celebration. Rukmini was not Bhimarayar’s daughter. She was none other than the wife of Lord Krishna himself, the very same Rukmini. And now another wedding was to be held for her.

The Sub-Inspector held up the notice and asked:

“What is this, Ponnusami? While Bhama is still living, may this Krishna marry another woman?”

“Krishna... is a god, sir.”

“A god may do wrong, may he, Ponnusami? Law is law, is it not?”

The Sub-Inspector asked, and Ponnusami stared. Poor man, what answer could he give? In our puranas, which god lives with only one wife? If he must make arrests according to the law, which god can Police Ponnusami possibly spare?

It is indeed a difficult matter. What answer can bewildered Ponnusami give? He remains bewildered.

24 April 1955
