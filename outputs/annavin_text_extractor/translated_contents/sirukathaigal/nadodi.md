## English Translation

"A wandering fellow, a vagabond! You donkey looking for a livelihood, could you not earn it here? Did you run off to Chidambaram on some great business? Go away!" Emperuman asked when the gardener came to complain about his son.

Master Emperuman owned Pillaiman and Company. The gardener had been a childhood friend of Emperuman Pillai's father. A change in the times had forced him to become a gardener. That is another story.

Even though he was a gardener, Emperuman Pillai customarily spoke to him with a little affection and concern because he had been his father's childhood friend.

That day, when the gardener came to complain of his son's "bad conduct," Emperuman Pillai displayed his concern by speaking of the son as a vagabond, a dog, and a donkey.

"What can I do, sir? I told him so many times."

"Yes. Even when he first went to Nagapattinam, I told you he should not."

"Yes, sir. I asked him, 'Why must you go to Nagapattinam?' He said he had to go because he would find work there, and then he left.

"On the tenth day after reaching there, he wrote that he was going to Pondicherry. Even I felt a little happy. I thought, let him go. My brother-in-law lives in Pondy, after all. Let him stay there.

"He had not remained there even two full months when he said, 'Father, I am going to Ceylon.'"

"Look at the course taken by a man determined to go. If he wished to work and live quietly within a town, could he not have remained in Nagapattinam? Why go to Ceylon? That is what I say: these fellows have wheels upon their feet."

"I beat my head and pleaded. 'Marudachalam, do not go, son. We once lived with some dignity. A change in the times has brought us down a little. Let us remain with the town and attend to some work that gives us a livelihood. Stay here.'

"As you say, perhaps he really has wheels upon his feet. Nagapattinam, Pondicherry, and finally Ceylon. He went, the foolish fellow. What would people there have thought of him? They would have said he looked like a vagabond, would they not?"

"What else would they say?"

"Apparently he returned from Ceylon to Tuticorin. Now he writes from Chidambaram that he intends to go to Singapore. What can I do, sir?"

"What can be done? His nature has become like that. He has become a wanderer. Let the donkey go, suffer, and roam. He will return by himself."

The master had spoken for a long time. Moreover, he had a committee meeting that day and was in a hurry to attend it.

The gardener was satisfied that the master had spoken to him with so much concern.

"What was wrong in what the master said? The fellow truly is a wanderer," thought Marthandam Pillai, Marudachalam's father, as he returned to the garden.

Emperuman owned Maan and Company, but he also had interests in several other commercial companies. A committee meeting of one such company was taking place. That was why Emperuman Pillai had hurriedly ended his conversation with the gardener and left.

The committee held a somewhat heated debate. Complaints had been made against the manager appointed by the committee: he did not attend properly to his work.

Emperuman Pillai presented the charge sheet and conducted the cross-examination that day.

"Yes, sir. You went to Arcot on the sixth, did you not?"

"Yes. I was told the goods would be available in Arcot, so I went. There was not even a proper bus service. I had to travel half the distance by horse carriage."

"That is all very well. What happens if you do not attend diligently to the work? You treat travelling by horse carriage as some tremendous ordeal. Those who believe their bodies must never be jolted should remain at home. Why claim to work and cause the company needless loss?

"Your diary itself reveals the distinction with which you performed your duties. You went to Arcot on the sixth and returned to town the very next day."

"Yes. What else could I do? The goods were not available in Arcot."

"So you immediately returned home like a pampered bridegroom. If they were not in Arcot, why did you not go and look in Vellore?"

"Was the strain of reaching Arcot itself small? To add a journey to Vellore on top of that..."

"If you are incapable, you should say so. Why should a man who sits in his own town like a stone Pillaiyar take a manager's post? Our company's business can function only if one travels around. We pour money upon you, yet going from town to town is a great hardship."

"I do travel, sir. The week after returning from Arcot, I went to Sholinghur."

"Why speak of visiting Sholinghur as though you had travelled to Ceylon and Singapore? What a tremendous journey you completed!

"The man who returned from Arcot on the sixth did not move from town until the fourteenth. After one day in Sholinghur, you remained here for another seven days. Then for about four days you visited several towns."

"The roaming I did during those four days, sir! I suffered more than a dog. I went all the way to Nellore, left the next day for Chittoor, stopped at Vellore on the way, returned to Arcot, spent an hour in Kanchipuram attending to that business, and then came back to town. I travelled like a vagabond."

"Oh! Visiting four towns feels like a vagabond's life to you? Business operates only when one travels like a wanderer.

"Every month you receive ninety rupees as regularly as a bundle of radishes. How will the business yield profit if you do not travel? From whose father's house should your salary come?

"A vagabond, he says! Yes, man, yes. None of us ever travels on business, do we?

"If you wish, remain within town like a tethered cow, an idol fixed in place, or a lump of compressed tamarind. Who tells you not to? But to serve as manager of our company, you must roam through many towns.

"If you complain that Tuticorin is too far, Bangalore too cold, and Nellore flooded, what will become of the company? You must wander and roam. Today one town, tomorrow another. You must keep going and returning.

"Look here, manager. I shall tell you plainly and firmly. Listen, and do not blame me. Our company needs a man who roams about. If you think you must remain within town and cannot travel frequently, settle your account and leave.

"When asked why you did not inspect a few more places after visiting four towns, your insolence made you ask, 'Am I to wander like a vagabond?'

"Listen. I say that travelling out of town is the mark of a businessman. A man who remains in his own town merely rides a horse inside a clay pot. What can you say to that?

"A vagabond, indeed! The company functions only if you keep going to several towns. If you do not want a wanderer's life, remain like a maharaja with yourself, your house, and this town. I have no objection.

"Everyone here is a wanderer. They attend to their businesses by going to one town today and another tomorrow. You do not want that wandering livelihood. You are a crowned king. Never leave your palace. Go!"

The committee unanimously adopted Emperuman Pillai's resolution. The manager lost his job.

The following week, at Jolarpettai Junction, the Malabar Mail arrived and stopped. The Bangalore Mail came in from the opposite direction and stopped as well.

Emperuman Pillai alighted from the Malabar Mail and walked upon the platform. From the Bangalore train came Emperuman's friend Sambandham Pillai, one of the company's directors.

"Good heavens, Pillai! Are you coming from Mysore?"

"I left Mysore yesterday. I had some work in Bangalore, attended to it, and then departed. Are you going to Coimbatore?"

"I must attend to some work in Coimbatore this morning. I intend to go to Palakkad in the afternoon."

"I am going directly to Madras. After reaching there and examining market conditions, I must go to Mangalore."

"Did you not say you had to go to Masulipatnam?"

"Yes. I must visit there too. There is plenty of work. Did you get a comfortable berth on the train?"

"Second class was unavailable, so I bought first class."

"Fine. After finishing at Palakkad, are you returning directly home?"

"Yes. After coming home and attending to the salt-marsh contract, I must immediately go to Ooty. Our Bombay Seth, Mulji, is there. Apparently he says he wants an agent for an American radio. I am thinking of taking it up."

"Do it. Very well, the trains are about to leave. Shall I go?"

The trains departed.

Lying down in second class, Emperuman thought of Sambandham: "Good heavens, that fellow roams like a demon for money."

Meanwhile, Sambandham inwardly abused Emperuman: "He never tires of travelling. He roams about sniffing for wherever money may be found."

Carrying the businessmen, the two trains proceeded majestically.

At the tea shop inside Chandra Talkies in Chidambaram, Marudachalam sat half asleep. Inside, a Tamil film titled *Illusory World* was being screened.

17 November 1946
