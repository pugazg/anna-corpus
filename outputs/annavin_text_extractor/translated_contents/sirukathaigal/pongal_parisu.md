## English Translation

To the Esteemed Proprietor,

I beg your pardon for writing and sending this letter amid your many pressing duties and business affairs. I appeal to you only because I have no other way, support, or place to which I can turn to resolve the difficulty that has befallen me.

Last month, under the plan to economize on office expenditure, you dismissed the employee who had attended to outstation correspondence and accounts and instructed me to handle that work as well. At the time, you delighted me by saying that if my work gave you satisfaction, you would grant me a substantial Pongal bonus. I wrote to my wife at length and with pride about your generosity and your noble quality of fulfilling any promise you make. The reason I had to write to her was a letter I received from her. Here it is:

> To my dear husband,
>
> Kamali asks me to convey her respects. She asked, "Mother, last year we expected Father to come for Pongal somehow and were disappointed. That will not happen this year too, will it?" I reassured her that Father would certainly come this year.
>
> A rat has bitten and ruined the sandalwood-coloured silk sari bought four years ago. Kamali asked, "Mother, do you remember Father saying how beautiful you looked when you wore that sari?" I replied, "What use is it if I remember? It is Father who must remember." She said that if I told you what the rat had done, you would surely come with a sandalwood-coloured silk sari. She asked me to enclose an advertisement she saw for such a sari. Here is the advertisement:
>
> **Low Price! Captivating Beauty!**  
> A sandalwood-coloured silk sari  
> suited to a golden complexion.  
> Strong! Lustrous! Exquisite workmanship!  
> Ajanta–Ellora Store, Madras.
>
> Kamali said, "Father himself knows what I like. Let him buy whatever he chooses. But you must also write and tell him what the headmistress has ordered me to wear for the dance at the school's Pongal celebration, and ask him to bring it."
>
> The school headmistress has written that Kamali requires:
>
> 1. A green silk blouse with zari and embroidered dots.
> 2. A red silk skirt.
> 3. A zari silk sash for the waist.
> 4. White ladies' shoes.
>
> In front of ten children, that lady even said, "Is this anything extraordinary for the daughter of a manager in a large company?" The reason she is so strict is an order sent to the school by a higher official.
>
> The order says:
>
> **School discipline is the headmistress's responsibility.**
>
> A school is the place where Goddess Saraswati sits enthroned. No repulsive sight must appear there. During the Pongal celebration in particular, the children must shine like golden idols. This must be attended to. We provide free education and a free midday meal. Should parents not at least provide good clothes for their children, especially during the Pongal festival?
>
> Arrangements have been made to film this year's Pongal celebration. Therefore, attractive children must be taught dance and song and made to participate in an ornamental appearance.
>
> This is important, essential, and urgent.
>
> — Higher Official
>
> The higher official also sent a postscript on the matter, conveying an important notice received from the Education Minister's department. The notice concerns a question in the Legislative Assembly:
>
> During the debate on the education grant, legislator Sowriyappa asked whether there was a proposal to film the festivals of our country's children and send the film abroad.
>
> Minister Arimanathar replied, "Yes."
>
> Educational institutions are requested to cooperate with the government in its responsibility to make good what was stated in the Assembly.
>
> This may be regarded as an order issued in the form of a request.
>
> The reason Sowriyappan raised a question that placed the government under an obligation was the great praise American journals had given an article he published after last year's American tour, titled "Cinema and Boys and Girls."
>
> The American journals praised it because the Soviet government was reportedly accusing America of caring only about business. Writing about America's commercial purpose, the Soviet government said:
>
> "America enters every field and establishes new industrial companies in its hunt for profit. America has not the slightest concern for India's art, culture, education, or the condition of its children. Its desire to sell American goods in India has become paramount. Only last month, the John and Son and John Company of America offered to join Madras's Centenary Company and invest seven crore rupees in a factory manufacturing electric ceremonial lamps. It is well known that this Centenary Company belongs to Emperuman, who has taken the agency for American oil."
>
> It is said that this news reached the Soviet Union, and caused such commotion, because of a speech by Muzhumathi, editor of *Bedha Ozhippu*, at a protest meeting against staff reductions in Emperuman's Centenary Company. Muzhumathi declared that if Emperuman failed to pay a Pongal bonus this year too, the workers would hold a sit-in. The daily *Pori* has therefore written an editorial saying that Emperuman will not fail to pay the bonus.
>
> Since it appears that the company bonus will be paid, Kamali insisted, "Mother, write and ask Father to bring these things too": 
>
> 1. A yellow imitation-silk sari for Mother.
> 2. Slacks of the kind your brother-in-law, who is staying here, likes to wear.
> 3. Whooping-cough medicine for the child.
> 4. Tonic for me, the kind Dr Murali prescribed earlier.
>
> I have written it all. The choice is yours. I doubt whether you even remember that we are here. Kamali firmly says, "Do not think so poorly of Father." We shall see whose prediction proves true.
>
> Yours,  
> Your wife

The proprietor's confidential secretary, after receiving this letter, sent the following communication:

**Petition: Bonus**

Having considered the length of the letter and the many matters contained in it:

1. It is evident that the petitioner, instead of attending properly to office work, devotes much of his attention to his personal affairs.

2. The petitioner has used office paper for his lengthy personal letter. This amounts to misuse of company property.

The petitioner is ordered to submit within three hours an explanation of why action should not be taken against him under Company Disciplinary Rule 76 for these offences.

For Proprietor Emperuman,  
Mothi  
Confidential Secretary, Temporary

>
> Assistant Doctor
