## English Translation

Herd after herd of goats ran about in terror. No one was chasing them, yet the sheep scattered in every direction.

There, with red fire blazing in her eyes, a woman came running. Yes, it was Mariamman of Mandaveli.

Mariamman was said to spread epidemics through the town. Even one who inflicted cruelty had a temple. She too possessed the title of deity, offerings, worship, and festivals. After all, devotion is produced in the brains of cowards frightened out of their wits.

Before a crude temple stood a crowd with savage minds. Among them, two goats had been bathed, smeared with turmeric, garlanded, and made to stand.

The Goddess of Mandaveli ran toward them and cried aloud:

"Do not slaughter the goats! If you slaughter them, I will unleash smallpox and destroy the whole town. Are you my devotees? You are great sinners! My very name is the Goddess of Mandaveli, and it is my duty to protect these herds. Yet you have brought two goats to slaughter for me. Wretches! Who are you to make an offering to me? Do I not possess the power to satisfy my own hunger? If I want a goat, do I not know how to take one for myself?"

"Mother, can you speak like this? A short while ago, that worthless fellow said the same thing. We threatened to slaughter him too, and the witless man became quiet."

"He has not lost his reason. It is your minds that are unsound. If you truly believe that I am a deity, and if you truly accept that I possess every power, how dare you sacrifice to me a life that I myself created?"

"Mother, that is the custom!"

"By calling it custom, you have betrayed the entire family of gods today. You speak of countless gods, give each one a family, build many temples, and hold festivals, offerings, and fireworks. In doing so, you have shown that it is all merely a game with dolls."

The Goddess of Mandaveli drove away the goats brought for sacrifice. Before those goats could run, the "human herd" gathered there had already scattered.

What justice is there in sacrificing one of God's creations to obtain the grace of a goddess? Leave aside the lack of compassion: there is no reason in it. If people slaughter a goat or chicken for food, there is at least an intelligible purpose. But to slaughter one as an offering is utter madness.

When human knowledge had not developed, when a human had not yet become a "complete human," in the age of savagery, people cut and killed, sprinkled drops of blood, and satisfied their brutal devotion.

Can that continue in this age? "Is a god who asks for a goat really a god? Let it go to the butcher's shop," a man had said as he tried to stop them. Very few supported him. Yet does a diamond lose its value because diamonds are scarce? Sand is plentiful, but who pays attention to it?

How that human herd had frightened him!

"Would it not be better for all of us to leave this earthly world together and return to our own realm? Only in India do 'our people' still survive. Elsewhere in the world, so many divine families have fled their towns. Some were banished. All of them have become former gods.

"Who knows where deities such as Thor and Odin vanished? Only here do Mari, Kali, Trisuli, Muthalu Ravuthan, Muniyan, and Sangili Karuppan continue to live, without a single one missing.

"As for me, I have reached the firm decision to leave the human world.

"The work assigned to me is to torment the town with disease, yet these people have spared even me! But this cannot continue much longer.

"The moment smallpox spreads, people now arrive to administer injections. What value remains for my power? My devotees lack not only intelligence but courage. If they truly trust me, do they oppose those vaccinators? No. They run and hide. What am I to do? I shall leave for our realm.

"And they say they slaughter goats for me. How horrible! I shall ask the founder of this temple, Konangi Pillai, about it right now."

The priest, Pogalingam, who had been lying down, awoke with a start. He heard a muttering from inside the temple and was shocked to see the Goddess of Mandaveli walking out while talking to herself.

"Mother!"

"Stand aside, useless man. It is dangerous to stop me."

"Where are you going, Mother?"

"To take leave of the town."

"To Konangi Pillai?"

The Goddess of Mandaveli walked away. The priest's legs trembled violently. He tried to step in the direction she had gone, but could not. He sat down where he stood.

"You supreme sinner! You wicked man! Are you my devotee?"

"I said it was wrong to slaughter goats," said the goddess.

"Even after you told those fellows so, I slaughtered four goats."

"Murderer!"

"I offered a sacrifice. I did not commit murder."

"Is that all? You have lied. Worse still, you told the townspeople that I have no power."

"You had my statue carried away from my temple by your farmhand."

"Oh, that? It was to trap those worthless fellows with the police."

"Thinking me a mere doll, you told a lie!"

"If you believed I had power, would you not have reported the matter to me? You would not have gone to the police and filed a false case."

"It is not like that, Mother. Shouldn't those wrongdoers be punished?"

"Who are you to punish them? When I, the goddess, am here, how could you, a human, attempt it? Besides, he committed no wrong. He said something good."

"He said that no sacrifice should be offered to you..."

"He said it and stopped the sacrifice out of the good intention that no blame should attach to me."

Konangi Pillai's head began to spin. He understood nothing.

"Konangi, devotees like you make our honour fly away in the wind. Because of the actions of people who roam about under the name of devotees, the situation has reached a point where we can no longer remain here. It is not because of those others. So I am leaving. Mandaveli will remain, and your human herd may gather there and revel. The goddess will not be there."

With those words, she departed.

Konangi Pillai opened his eyes with a start. Only then did he understand that all these scenes had occurred in a dream.

Before him stood the goddess's statue, which he had secretly brought away without anyone's knowledge. It seemed to look at him and laugh mockingly.

This was the conspiracy he had devised to stop the rationalist campaign spreading through the town: he would file a case accusing members of the rationalist movement of stealing the temple idol. He had instructed his farmhand to tell the townspeople that Paramasivam and Parthiban of the rationalist movement had stolen it. Priest Pogalingam had received the same order.

But before dawn, the farmhand, overwhelmed by the deceit, had told the entire town the truth.

Before the memory of the dream had faded, Konangi Pillai saw a crowd of townspeople assembled before his house and became distressed. Now he had reason to be afraid: he knew that those gathered were not a human herd, but a people's assembly.
