## English Translation

**Place:** Madras Memorial Hall. **Time:** 6 January 1946, morning and evening. **Performers:** Sir P. T. Rajan of Madurai, vocal; the Mirzapur zamindar, mridangam; Madanagopalar of Madras, kanjira.

At today's music festival, the audience lost itself in the singing of Sir P. T. Rajan, who had come forward to raise a fallen party after himself previously being cast aside. His elaboration of the raga Mukhari was especially accomplished.

**Principal song - Raga: Mukhari; Tala: Rupakam**

What shall we do, my friend? What can we do now, comrade? The election was lost and the body grew thin. Even the treasure we sought rejected us. He ran away after issuing a report that was nothing but a wail; having come seeking us, he abandoned us midstream.

“I am the leader of the party, wealthy gentlemen! Seeing your faces bearing sorrow, my mind yearns and my heart sleeps. Tell me properly: is any benefit still possible?”

The country does not know us, and we possess not a single newspaper. There is no person of character willing to labour. Why this trouble for me? Remove this difficulty. Is the torment of defeat honey, and am I the wick that must burn in it?

If we do not stand properly in the coming election, the sky will not collapse and life will not dry up. I understood the truth before it arrived; recognize this good. If there is a way, tell me - open your mouths and speak!

Go throughout the towns; defeat the enemy within. Seek a newspaper as our steadfast ally. Noble penance will assist us; one will stand before the Lord. We trusted Velan; let us go forth and win!

## Translator's Notes

This is political satire framed as a Carnatic concert review. `Mukhari`, associated with lament, is the central joke. The source explicitly says `நாதன்` means God, not a former editor of the Justice newspaper.
