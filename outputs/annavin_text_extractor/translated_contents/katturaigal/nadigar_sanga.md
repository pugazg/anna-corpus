## English Translation

To the attention of those who mockingly call them "mere actors," we bring the scenes that have unfolded in the Madras Legislative Assembly over the past couple of days. The government has yielded and sent the drama-control bill it was eager to introduce to a Select Committee for examination. Will any benefit result? That is something to be seen later.

But the "Honourables" who roared in haste, "We are going to throw it down at once—shall we permit reformist plays?" are now obliged to pause and think. Had our ministers possessed this habit of reflection and the character needed to correct mistakes, the country would not have witnessed many calamities. We are glad that at least now this good sense has become visible.

It is said that the Sahajanandas girded themselves in the Assembly. That is natural; it is the conduct of a person who dances upon the heights of bliss, and therefore no problem. What is strange is that in the city of "non-violence" a soul should wander about saying, "I shall kill anyone who speaks about the *Ramayana*." Such herd mentality is not good for the growing new age.

Recognising this, several Congress members themselves have said that there should be no drama-control law. In the Assembly, the Communist Party, the Praja Socialist Party, and many others joined together and opposed the bill with all their strength. Consequently, instead of becoming law, the bill has gone to the "picking committee"—the Select Committee. That committee is said to be considering the amendments and proposals that should be made. We do not know what the result of its deliberation will be.

Nevertheless, we are glad that the government has yielded this far. We congratulate the Actors' Association, whose members rose united and worked for this result. Statistics reportedly show that more than 13,000 people in Tamil Nadu are engaged in fields connected with drama. The government's law threatens the very livelihood of these people.

It is said to be a bill prepared on expert advice. What do experts care? They can offer a thousand proposals to strengthen those who govern. A government should never have dared to introduce so bad a bill merely by trusting them.

When representatives of the Actors' Association met comrade Kamaraj, he reportedly said, "What can be done? The bill cannot be abandoned. Tell us what changes must be made, and we shall make them." Like the comrades of the Actors' Association, we too are watching closely to see whether those words are fulfilled.

The government must not smile and speak pleasantly to those who come to meet it, then later place a knife at their throats. Let it accept the Actors' Association's proposals and, if this law must proceed at all, let it proceed in accordance with them. That is the proper path. We warn that leaving it and following a crooked course would be to stir a rumbling volcano.

We commend the friends and administrators of the Actors' Association who, realising that their rights were being taken away, came forward to beat the drum of opposition. We salute the purity of heart with which they united in a spirit of rights instead of thinking, "The drama ban concerns someone else."

If everyone acquired the courage to challenge authority in this way, no government could follow a path of injustice. Yet there are many souls here intoxicated by the thought that when the government attacks progressive principles, it is an arrow aimed at somebody else. That attitude must disappear from every sphere.

Freedom is common property. Progressive principles likewise belong to all. If everyone understands this and acts accordingly, the habit of angrily attacking us at every turn will subside and a path for truth to bloom will appear.

Understanding that the government's course is not merely hostile to one movement but imposes a ban upon knowledge itself, people from various parties and patrons of the acting profession have come forward to raise their voices. We offer them our heartfelt greetings. Such public solidarity is a heartening sight.

The bill has gone to the Select Committee. Everyone must watch what form it takes when it returns. If it remains the same old creature, no one should hesitate to lift the already raised flag and sound the war drum. Our support will always be available for the Actors' Association's efforts to make sound changes in the bill.

The D.M.K. is a movement that does not shrink from activities serving the country's welfare. It therefore rejoices at the unity formed among actors and at the generosity with which members of many different parties came forward to condemn the danger to rights.

There are people who speak with a stench in their words: "This drama-control law is meant only for those atheists!" The actors' front has stepped forward to demonstrate, "No, this is a law concerning the country," to fight for common rights, and to preserve dignity.

Those hitherto regarded as voiceless insects have clearly demonstrated the connection between themselves and the life of the country. Had this occasion not arisen, there would have been no fine evidence of an all-party struggle. We congratulate the Actors' Association for growing in this manner.

After the "operation," let the drama-control law return and move about only if it has a body free of disease. Otherwise...!
