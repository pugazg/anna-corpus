## English Translation

...we would say that it was confidence founded upon affection. When a father is separated from his son, a son from his father, a husband from his wife, or a wife from her husband, what one expected from the other was help: that is our view.

But today, when twenty million Tamils cry out and writhe over the disappearance of our Selvam, we shall proclaim from any mountain peak that the reason is nothing but love. Anyone who witnessed the agitation in their eyes and the movement of their lips on the evening of 26 February, when the train carrying our Selvam was about to leave Central Station, knows how genuine his love for Periyar was. That scene alone, we believe, is enough to demonstrate Selvam's devotion to the welfare and life of the Tamil people.

Like a pot breaking just as the butter is gathering, at the very moment when the Justice Party, long mocked by political opponents, was rolling together and taking shape, news arrived that Selvam had vanished. There can be no doubt that twenty million Tamils felt as though lightning had struck their heads.

The fact that the Justice Party was his very life is sufficiently shown by the statement he issued in May 1939, even while lying ill in Madras General Hospital, urging Tamils to carry out constructive work for the party.

Our Selvam lived believing that the life of the Tamils was his life and their advancement his advancement. As Periyar says, it appears that "accursed office" itself became a furnace for his life. How can we console the millions of grieving Tamil people by explaining what an immense and irreplaceable loss his disappearance is to the Justice Party and the non-Brahmin community? We do not know.

Appearance and disappearance are the way of the world. But Selvam's disappearance cannot be placed in that ordinary category. We would call such a disappearance extraordinarily rare. We have neither seen nor heard of an aircraft vanishing in the sky and becoming impossible to find. Such incidents may perhaps have occurred occasionally at sea, but never, so far as we know, in the air. Why, then, should such a danger have befallen the aircraft carrying our Selvam?

The disaster that overtook the *Hannibal* remains a great mystery. Seventeen days have now passed, yet we have received no information about the accident.

In reply to questions in the Council of State, whose full details appear in another column, the Secretary of the Government of India's Department of Communications stated that correspondence was taking place between the Indian and British governments concerning the method of inquiry into the accident involving the aircraft *Hannibal*, on which Sir A. T. Pannirselvam was travelling, and the persons to be appointed to the inquiry committee.

We remind the government that it must not consider its responsibility discharged merely by holding an inquiry. It has an enduring duty to find adequate relief for his family, who suffer after losing the treasure of the Tamil people.

Some may become angry if we say that this great non-Brahmin community remains in its present condition because it lacks genuine devotion to its leaders. But we wish to ask them one question. When the great Dr T. M. Nair died in London, when the great Theagaraya Chetty gave up his life, and when the Raja of Panagal passed away, what did the people who cried and writhed then do to follow them?

Those who received the rewards and benefits of their sacrifices now wander about asking, "Where are our relatives? Where are our houses and property? Where are our lands?" How many, following those leaders' sacrifices, have renounced self-interest and made public welfare their own welfare?

Our Selvam followed them. Today he too has vanished.

Periyar cries: "The disappearance of Pannirselvam torments my mind. Whenever I see Tamils and think of their condition, my heart shudders. The reason... Pannirselvam's disappearance concerns public welfare; it concerns the condition of the Tamils... My mind reels at the question: who can take his place?"

How many will come forward to reassure Periyar's cry by saying, "Here we are"?

Therefore, if Tamils truly grieve over Selvam's disappearance, every one of them must come forward to labour for the same principle for which Selvam worked day and night and for the advancement of the same community for which he laboured tirelessly.

Every word he uttered must one day be inscribed in letters of gold under Tamil rule. Every Tamil must feel that a town without his statue has lost its beauty. Every Tamil home must contain Selvam's honoured portrait.

If twenty million Tamils thus become Selvam themselves, what greater consolation or joy could there be for the family that grieves after losing him? If it is true that twenty million Tamils are distraught and beside themselves over our Selvam's disappearance, will they come forward to do this?

Let Selvam arise everywhere!

Let Selvam live forever!

Let Selvam arise throughout the land!

*Kudi Arasu, 17 March 1940*
