## English Translation

Power is an intoxicant. The more one drinks, the sweeter it tastes. Very few who become accustomed to it avoid falling into its spell. Political power is more intoxicating still. Power obtained through illusion and preserved through intrigue produces the greatest intoxication of all.

Those who drink it experience supreme bliss and dance so wildly that their feet scarcely touch the earth. Scholars call that a political clown show. Its consequences are autocracy and dictatorship. If no one challenges him, will not even a younger brother become a swaggering bully? And if he adopts the belief that he has no opponent at all, need one ask about his divine sport? Declaring, "Whoever it may be," he girds himself for a fight over everything. This is called the frenzy of power.

That is the condition in our Presidency today. This is the ill omen produced by the yellow ballot box, flourishing under Rajaji's rule.

His associates murmur. Some "patriots" stand bewildered. Old friends now offer advice. Political scholars issue counsel. Even the heroic speaker from the meek printing press prays to "God" that righteous rule may prevail among the ministers. Opponents warn him. Yet Rajaji has drunk without limit, like a starving cow grazing in a field of millet.

The intoxication of power has seized him so completely that he says, "No one but us can ride in this palanquin." Otherwise, would the man who begged and coaxed until he attained power now bully and intimidate? Would one who recited the verse "We are all kings of this country" rein in and drag along the members of his own Congress legislative party? Would he develop the Vande Mataram controversy, which harms the Hindu-Muslim unity essential to the self-rule struggle, until it created an ugly scene in the legislature? Would he send to prison the socialists who worked day and night for him and his group? Would he impose the unnecessary "Lambadi" language Hindi at spear-point while Tamil hearts seethed? Would he revile its opponents as "ignorant people"?

Members of the upper house, political experts, and those who had previously administered government asked that debate on the Debt Relief Bill be postponed for a week because copies had not reached them in time. Would he treat them like blades of grass and stubbornly declare, "Absolutely impossible"?

However beautifully a national curtain is hung to conceal the condition, it cannot hide that democratic government does not prevail in this Presidency. Those condemning it have also increased.

Because he possesses a legislative majority, because no organised opposition exists, and because the Governor is a "friend, guide, and spiritual teacher," Rajaji proudly assumes that his power cannot be harmed and reclines pleasantly upon a swing. But what does history say? The last Tsar's story, the extreme point of the frenzy of power, is now a political lesson to the world. Mussolini and Hitler, roaring from the summit of authority today, are derided by political scholars throughout the world as brutes, madmen, and men possessed. Yet the public may forget even history's lesson.

Rajaji's class says, "The public supports us. The votes they gave placed us in this office." His accompanying drums repeat the same refrain. But what is the truth? For what purpose did the public support him and his group? Was it for this dictatorial rule? Were there no members of the public who did not support him then?

In the last Assembly election, 4,269,834 people participated and voted. Of them, 1,514,632 voted against him and his party. All these people are Indians too. They too live under today's government, a government they did not want then and do not want now.

Does Rajaji know that all these people now tell the others, those who trusted the yellow ballot box, that the ostentatious rule they had predicted has arrived? We ask Rajaji to recognise that the number of people realising that he is using against them the power received from them increases every day.

The lament "We lost our senses by trusting the yellow box" must already have reached Rajaji's ears. His failure to understand it is not his own fault; it is the fault of that frenzy of power.

Tamils will not endure Rajaji's dictatorship for long. The palanquin ride will not continue much longer; the shoulders of those carrying it have begun to ache severely. The nature of Rajaji's rule has become clear. One group thinks, "One who sows millet will reap millet; one who sows evil will reap evil." Another thinks, "We dug a well and a demon emerged." We wish to say that the time when the two groups unite has drawn near.

When that time arrives, Rajaji's intoxication will clear by itself. We ask Tamils to prepare themselves and awaken others too.

When the body's strength declines, the blood is corrupted, fatigue and exhaustion arise, and the body and eyes turn yellow, a person suffers from jaundice. The medicine is pure blood. Tamil Nadu is in a similar condition, but the medicine is ready. It is for Tamils to understand their present condition, rise courageously, and seek their own protection.

It is the duty of Tamils to demonstrate that this is the land of Nakkirar, who declared, "Even if you display the eye upon your forehead, a fault in what was said remains a fault." If they set out to do so, every kind of madness will certainly be cured.

(*Kudi Arasu*, 13-2-1933)
