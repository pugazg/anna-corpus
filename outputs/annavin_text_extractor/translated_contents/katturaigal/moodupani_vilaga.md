## English Translation

Soviet Russia has acquired the power to command the world’s attention. The great powers have now reached a condition in which they must await even a smile from Molotov. That country has attained such an exalted position.

Only thirty years ago, the same country lay caught in the net of the great powers, sunk in the deceptive mire of religious zealots, held in the Tsar’s grip and Rasputin’s fraud, and degraded amid the revelry of aristocrats. It witnessed famine and massacre and languished under tyranny and poverty. Today that same country shines with a splendour seen nowhere else in the world.

No one denies this new condition of Soviet Russia. Little explanation is needed to prove that it has acquired new power. We see many great powers now afraid of Soviet strength.

A country once diseased now flourishes in strength. Its primitive condition changed in an instant. Yes, in the life of a nation, are thirty years not merely an instant? Within that time, tyrants were driven away, fraudulent holy men abolished, capitalism defeated, and a new system of government established. A new world itself appeared.

No one can deny this. But many have worked to spread a veil of fog called false propaganda so that the full truth and complete picture do not reach the people. Through this journal, we seek to destroy that fog to the extent our strength permits.

The story of a tortured people who rose and fought and attained distinction and prosperity is an important part of human history. Capitalism spread—and continues to spread—false propaganda so that the world would not know their suffering. Until recently, many great powers used their financial strength for the destruction of the Soviet Union, yet victory was not theirs.

Every country today guards its borders in needless panic, asking, “From which side will Russia enter? How many troops will it bring?” No troops are needed, nor will they come. When sufficient armies already exist in the tears of the poor, the sighs of workers, and the anguish of farmers, why would the Soviet Union send its army here?

Talk of a Soviet invasion and Russian domination is the incoherent cry of capitalism in its final stage. By inflaming this fear, capitalism hopes to make people recoil from the Soviet Union and thereby create hatred for the Soviet system of social equality, so that capitalism may survive a little longer. That hope will not be fulfilled. As we said before, tears flow from the eyes of the poor. That is a sign that society is seeking a new structure.

It is in this condition that we have gladly come forward to discharge the duty of stating some truths about Russia, which has been made the target of false propaganda. We have both reason and right to regard this as our duty. As members of Periyar’s movement, we labour with social equality as our goal.

“As long as one man eats from another’s labour without suffering; as long as one man lies starving without a way to obtain even one meal of gruel while another eats five times a day and sits in a reclining chair rubbing his stomach; as long as one man struggles without a dhoti for his waist while another strolls luxuriously wearing three; and as long as the rich believe all their wealth exists solely for their private lives, the Self-Respect Movement must continue to exist.”

Everyone knows that Periyar strongly condemns not only corruption arising from caste and religion but also economic inequalities among the people. To speak the truth, just as mischief-makers described this labourer for social reform to the common people as an “atheist,” they also denounced him to the government as a “communist.” At one time, the late Sathyamurthi and others described his programme as a communist programme.

Periyar did not grow weary by treating these as accusations. He continued tirelessly to propagate social equality in Tamil Nadu. He did so not merely from book learning or solely with the support of experience. He travelled directly to the Soviet Union, saw the beauty of the new world flowering there, learned its true condition, and began that propaganda.

Nor is that the whole truth. Even his campaign to abolish caste is not merely religious reform or reform of the people’s minds. He believes that the caste system—varnashrama itself—is a method of economic exploitation devised in former times by cunning people, and he has demonstrated this with adequate reasons. Thus, when he opposes caste, it means he attacks and destroys the cruel arrangement that created economic inequality, keeps it in people’s minds, and can make it grow further. In other words, he creates the conditions for social equality.

He condemns ancient doctrines lodged in the minds of Tamil people by showing that they are the crown ropes of capitalism developed through the caste system. The work of his great movement serves social equality, even if others refuse to accept it or shrink from valuing it.

Such work was carried out in the Soviet Union more than adequately before the revolution, and that is why the revolution became possible. Propaganda toward this end was conducted through song, dance, story, epic, painting, and many other fields. Long-established ideas implanted in the minds of poor Russians were utterly removed. Therefore, when the great Lenin showed the path of revolution, a pride of lions rose roaring and tore down the enemy host.

Because it understands this fundamental principle, the movement Periyar leads today continues without interruption to spread sound knowledge among the country’s people. It proceeds along the path of its ideal, determined ultimately to behold Soviet splendour.

That is why, at the time of the November Revolution anniversary, we published articles explaining the splendour of the Soviet country and the conditions required if we are to see a similar new world. Various portraits—such as the condition of the wanderer and the path by which prostitution arises—can be seen in the country today. At street corners, beside roads, in cities, and in hamlets, you can meet these people everywhere.

These articles are intended to provoke thought about why such people exist in society. We trust that Tamils will read this issue and benefit.

17 November 1946

## Translator's Notes

This article is distinct from `மூடுபனி / Fog` dated 28 January 1945. Here the title metaphor refers specifically to anti-Soviet propaganda. `சமதர்மம்` is consistently rendered as social equality in the movement’s broad ethical and economic sense, while historical accusations of communism are translated separately as such.
