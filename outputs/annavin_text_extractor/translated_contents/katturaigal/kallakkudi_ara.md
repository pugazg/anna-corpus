## English Translation

Those who know the methods Congress leaders once used to demonstrate the growing voice of rights can understand the character and strength of the moral struggles now initiated and conducted by the DMK.

Even a ruler of the jungle would not condemn a method by which people, without carrying guns or cannon, display only the power of rights and communicate their dissatisfaction to those fortunate enough to govern their towns.

Throwing explosives upon opponents, taking revenge and fulfilling one's desire is one method.

Displaying the strength of one's demand for rights and the feeling of the people's assembly, and thereby attempting to open the opponent's eyes, is another.

The first method wastes human blood and life.

The second peacefully unites and demonstrates human affection and the vigour of principle.

The DMK has adopted this second method as its form of struggle.

The reason is not: “Congress rules now, so we shall demonstrate the methods its members once used.” The DMK follows this path because the second method contains humanity and the philosophy of peace.

Creating lawlessness and atrocities in a country is not difficult. Under this Congress government in particular, the people's assembly lies like dry leaves. One word and a small matchbox, and the deed is done. But we have never liked paths such as setting fires and demolishing government offices.

That is why we condemned Congress members when they participated in the disturbances of August 1942.

Political parties must raise the voice of rights through the strength of principle and the people's power it produces. Forcing others to submit through clubs and knives is neither honourable nor suited to a democratic age.

That is why the DMK repeatedly uses the words “Peace! Dignity!” in writing and speech and also demonstrates them in action.

Our desire is that society should progress upon a foundation of affection and without staining human character.

That is why, to demonstrate our opposition to northern domination and raise Tamil Nadu's voice of rights, we devised a peaceful method.

Congress members once burned mill cloth, not because they were angry with the fabric, but to condemn and explain to the people the unjust exploitation of the Englishman who carried wealth away.

They roared angrily, “Manchester! Lancashire!”

The Dalmiapuram struggle is similar. For the past twenty-five days, warriors of moral resistance have gone to the station named Dalmiapuram and pasted over it a sheet bearing the name “Kallakudi.” They do this not from anger towards the board, but to condemn and explain the evil of northern domination, which grows fat by exploiting the South and plundering its language and culture.

Northern domination has entered not merely the country's economy but its language and way of life too.

At the same time, we learn that nationalist friends attempted to erase with kerosene the tar applied by Dravidar Kazhagam comrades. It may have been easy for them to use kerosene to remove that tar.

But northern rulers smear the faces and hearts of southern people with the tar of “domination.” Even if the nationalist friends poured the kerosene they carried on August 1 gallon after gallon, they could not remove that.

The purpose of the August 1 agitation to erase Hindi was to make that evil understood. “They erased the writing. Ah! Did you see? We wiped it away and wrote it again in Hindi,” government members may boast. National newspapers may publish pictures and sing every day about it.

The erasing and the rewriting after clearing it away are not what deserve attention. No government can be so lacking in resources or strength that it cannot remove an erasure and write afresh. If it were, it would not be a government but a mendicant's monastery. Boasting about that is therefore ridiculous.

What must be noticed is that a section of the people disliked and erased something done by a government.

Some heated Congress members may flare up like embers and ask, “Why should we pay attention?” Whether they attend to it or refuse, the world will continue watching. Neither Congress members nor anyone else can cover its eyes.

Unlike last year, we did not participate this year in Periyar's agitation mentioned above. He knows, and the country knows, that we are engaged in different work.

At the time Hindi signboards were being erased, we were, and remain, engaged in removing a symbol of northern domination.

The name Dalmiapuram exemplifies how far a northern seat of power has grown and gained victory. Dalmia turns Kallakudi's soil into money. Though thorns pierce their feet and the mill wheel claims human victims, our people labour daily inside Dalmia's factory until their bodies are broken. The soil is theirs. They use pick and iron crowbar to dig and supply it. But Dalmia enjoys the reward.

This condition, in which one man grows fat while many suffer, persists because this land has no government belonging to its working people. The government in Delhi rules the whole South. “Greater India” gives its associates such as Dalmia, Tata and Birla the opportunity to stretch out their enormous hands and carry away wealth. The enchanting curtain conceals that enterprise.

The ideal called “Dravida Nadu” is to tear down this curtain and make their own wealth the property of the southern people.

We hold aloft that ideal and explain it. Delhi refuses to accept it and looks on arrogantly. It considers even saying that such a voice for rights has arisen to be dangerous. It attempts to cover the issue with a blanket of propaganda.

Before we can finish grieving over the trickster's conduct, we see his excesses spreading into every sphere of our lives.

Not only is the wealth of Kallakudi exploited; the town's name itself has been changed.

Like one who tortures the body and then attempts to destroy an eye as well, those who plunder our property also pillage our honour.

How long can this be endured?

When we explain, no one listens. When we send a resolution, it is treated with indifference. That is why we began the moral struggle.

On the very day it began, the government gave us corpses, showed us blood flowing, and locked us in prison.

It did these things imagining that our struggle would collapse on seeing them.

Yet the warriors did not give up. From that day onwards, they have continued pasting sheets bearing the name “Kallakudi.”

The struggle will continue until “Dalmiapuram” is changed to “Kallakudi.”

Every day, the volunteers go and see whether the name Kallakudi remains. If it is absent, they paste it again. If it is torn, they repair it and return.

Each day, we require only a little paste and one sheet of paper.

The rulers require armed police and a guard of repression.

Our daily expense is a few minutes.

What the government spends is its honour. Each poster pasted by our comrades every day is a poster affixed to the face of Delhi's autocracy. The “paste” applied daily by our warriors is not upon the railway board but upon the face of Delhi's seat of power.

The whole world mocks the spectacle of the people's assembly responding through action to a government's conduct while the government of the “Light of Asia” pretends not to see.

The American newspaper *The New York Times* has written about this in detail.

Newspapers throughout India have understood the strength of our demand.

Today our ideal's cry has begun to be heard even in places thought unreachable. For us, continually pushed into darkness, this too is a victory.

The reason for this victory is the movement's warriors who have participated in the Dalmiapuram moral struggle for twenty-five days. Every day they march in formation like an army and peacefully “teach Delhi's domination a lesson.” We praise the feeling of those comrades.

Comrades, your work deserves to be engraved upon the pages of the future Dravidian land. You paste the name, and the Nehru government, said to enjoy fame throughout the world, has been unable to tear it down! The people's voice has opened the eyes of the arrogant.

We shall continue the moral struggle. We shall continue it without any decline in peace or dignity until it opens their minds and they change the name to “Kallakudi.”

The people's voice will triumph!

Arrogant rule will submit!

(*Dravida Nadu*, 9 August 1953)

## Translator's Notes

`அறப்போர்` is rendered “moral struggle,” referring here to disciplined nonviolent civil resistance. The article describes the continuing action after the 15 July 1953 Kallakudi railway protest, in which activists replaced the name Dalmiapuram with Kallakudi to oppose both Dalmia's industrial dominance and the displacement of the Tamil place-name. The OCR source contains a damaged line at the Image 3 transition and several spelling intrusions (`திராவிட தாடு`, `நியுயார்க்க டைம்ஸ்`); their meanings are clear from context, and the Tamil block remains verbatim.
