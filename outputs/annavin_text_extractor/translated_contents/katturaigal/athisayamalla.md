## English Translation

A buffalo calf with six legs, a cow with three eyes, a chicken that is neither male nor female, a snake with a head at each end, a palm tree with branches, and many similar things are called curiosities of nature because they differ from nature's usual form.

But stories of a Sivalingam giving testimony, a bone taking the form of a woman, a strip of palm leaf travelling against a flood, a black stone floating, a fox becoming a horse, a child who was cut up and cooked as meat rising alive again, a boar and a swan seeking the top and bottom of the divine form, a blow struck upon one person's back appearing upon everyone's back, God arriving when a pot cries out, a spider erecting a canopy, and an elephant attaining Siva's realm after worshipping him are fabricated tales that could never have occurred anywhere or at any time. They are colossal lies.

They are stratagems created by religious teachers to recruit people into their respective religions and then deceive those recruits while passing their own lives in comfort.

All these are portraits of thought from a barbarous age. This blind course existed among people at one time not only in this country but in every country. In other lands, many years have passed since this degraded condition and darkened intellect changed and a new course suited to reason arose.

Educated people there did not undertake the disorderly work of polishing antiquity until it glittered, supplying philosophical explanations for ideas that had lost their appeal with time, and making the people's minds crawl forever within that old age.

Though they faced punishment from foolish crowned rulers and the anger of priests, though they risked death and suffered cruelty while alive, they did not retreat from seeking a path that would bring reason to the people. They worked to advance public knowledge. Without mercy or hesitation, they exposed the gods of the churches and the stories told about them as storehouses of absurdity that damaged reason.

Thought grew, a new age was born, and the people obtained a new and prosperous life.

Leave aside educated intellectuals working in other fields. What work have newspaper editors undertaken, those who have studied, earned degrees, travelled through many countries, gained experience, and occupy a position capable of directing public knowledge?

Do they not allocate column after column to the greatness of Kokilattami, the greatness of Sivaratri, and similar matters, giving them more space than the Berlin problem, American domination, Soviet industrial development, and other subjects? Do they have the tongue to deny that they do this?

> An eight-year-old village girl named Suraj says that she has been in the company of gods. For the past ten days this has created a sensation in the city. Everyone comes to see the girl. She lives in a village on the bank of the Sabarmati.
>
> The girl reportedly told her mother that she had personally seen Lord Sri Krishna and other Hindu gods. From this, she believes that she has returned after being in the company of gods. At times she dances and plays the flute.

This report appeared in *Dinamani* on 6 October 1948. Now tell us what our educated people are doing.

Teleprinters, machinery that prints many colours simultaneously across many pages and folds thousands of copies an hour, and experience gained through travel in many countries: do you see what all this is being used for? To what can we compare the condition of educated people who conduct such propaganda?

As stated at the beginning of the article, this is not a wonder of nature. It is an absurdity of the puranic age of the latter kind.

Because the puranic knowledge taught to the girl by her parents has ripened fully, madness may have risen in her to the point where she imagines herself one of the *gopis*, if the incident was published with even some basis in truth. But when educated newspaper editors themselves have still not attained clarity, it is no wonder that the girl's mind is disturbed.

The moment newspapers receive reports that such "wonders" are occurring, they somehow develop the desire to stop however many essential reports may be waiting and give these stories first place.

If news arrives that a person four or five hundred years old is still alive, most newspaper editors do not possess the good sense to learn the complete truth before publishing it.

Such reports of "wonders" disappear a short time after publication. What is the girl who spoke with God doing now? Where is the 465-year-old man now? We wrote excessively in praise of them in newspapers and captured the people's minds, yet now we know nothing about them. Why? Newspaper editors do not even reflect upon these questions.

Many editors behave as though it is enough for something to offer the ordinary public a fresh attraction. They do not examine the reports they receive or consider that readers, not only writers, possess intelligence. They do not believe news should be published in a manner consistent with truth and experience.

Until this pitiable condition changes, many more wonders will continue to be manufactured in this country.
