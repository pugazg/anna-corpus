வேறு தேசங்களோடு போர் தொடுக்கவோ ஜனங்களை ஒன்றுதிரட்ட
முடியாது. ஜாதியைச் அஸ்திவாரமாக வைத்துக்கொண்டு, எந்தக்
கட்டடத்தையுமே கட்ட முடியாது “நேஷனை” உருப்படுத்தவும்
முடியாது; ஒழுக்கத்தை வளர்க்கவும் முடியாது. ஜாதியை ஆதாரமாக
வைத்துக்கொண்டு செய்யப்படும் எதுவும் உருப்படவே செய்யாது;
அழிவது நிச்சயம். ஆகவே, ஹிந்து சமூகத்தை எப்படிச் சீர்திருத்துவது?
ஜாதியை எப்படி ஒழிப்பது என்பவையை இப்பொழுது முக்கியப்
பிரச்னைகளாக இருந்து வருகின்றன. ஜாதிகளுக்குள் இருப்பதைவிட
உப ஜாதிகளுக்குள் ஒழுக்கத்திலும் அந்தஸ்திலும் மிக்க ஒற்றுமை

பிராமணர்களும் மத்திய இந்தியப் பிராமணர்களும் சமையற்காரராயும்

பிராமணர்களும் தென்னிந்தியப் பிராமணர்களம் சமூக வாழ்வில் மிக
அந்தஸ்தான நிலைமையிலிருக்கிறார்கள். மற்றும் வட இந்தியாவில்
வைசியர்களும், காயஸ்தர்களும் தெக்காணப் பிராமணர்களுக்கும்
தென்னிந்தியப் பிராமணர்களுக்கும் சமமான அந்தஸ்தில் இருந்து
வருகிறார்கள். மற்றும் தெக்காண பிராமணர்களும் தென்னிந்தியப்
பிராமணர்களும் சைவ உணவுக்காரர். காஷ்மீர் பிராமணர்களும்
வங்காளப் பிராமணர்களும் மாமிச உணவுக்காரர். எனவே, உணவு
விஷயத்திலும் அவர்களுக்குள் ஒற்றுமை இல்லை. ஆனால்,
தெக்காணப் பிராமணர்களுக்கும் உணவு விஷயத்தில் குஜராத்திகள்,

பிராமணரல்லாதார், சைவப் பிராமணர்கள் ஜாதியில் சுளுவாகச் சேர்ந்து

## English Translation

Tamil title: அபேதவாதமும் சமூக சீர்திருத்தமும்

Brahmins are the greatest enemies of reform efforts. Brahmins stand in the forefront of political reform and certain fields of economic reform, but they take no significant part in movements that have arisen to abolish caste. Is there at least room to hope that they will play an important part in the future? There is none.

You might even say that Brahmins will not obstruct such efforts. You may say that Brahmins know caste is a stain upon Hindu society and that intelligent Brahmins will not tolerate the evils it produces. You may point out that Brahmins include both secular and orthodox Brahmins, and that even if the orthodox do not support the abolition of caste, the secular Brahmins will.

On the surface, everything you say appears correct. But many fail to understand that the destruction of caste would create a grave danger for Brahmin society. Can Brahmins therefore be expected to support a caste-abolition movement capable of destroying their community's power and influence?

It is foolish to divide Brahmins into secular and orthodox groups and trust that the secular Brahmins will be progressive. Both sections belong to the same lineage. They are the two hands of one body, and one hand will inevitably fight to protect the other.

## Social Organization

Without changing your social organization, you cannot advance even an inch. You cannot unite the people either to defend the country or wage war against another country. No structure can be built with caste as its foundation. A nation cannot be formed, nor can morality be cultivated. Nothing based upon caste can prosper; its destruction is certain.

How, then, is Hindu society to be reformed? How is caste to be abolished? These have now become the principal questions.

The belief that subcastes possess greater uniformity in conduct and status than castes is the basis of one proposed view. I consider this conclusion mistaken.

North Indian Brahmins occupy a much lower social status than Brahmins of the Deccan and South India. Brahmins in North and Central India commonly work as cooks and water carriers, while Deccan and South Indian Brahmins hold positions of high status in social life. In North India, Vaishyas and Kayasthas possess status equal to that of Deccan and South Indian Brahmins.

Deccan and South Indian Brahmins are vegetarians, whereas Kashmiri and Bengali Brahmins eat meat. Thus, even in diet, there is no uniformity among them. On the other hand, Deccan Brahmins share dietary practices with non-Brahmins such as Gujaratis, Marwaris, Banias and Jains. Vegetarian non-Brahmins could therefore more easily join vegetarian Brahmin castes. It is also practicable to unite North Indian Kayasthas with other non-Brahmins in South India.

## Chaturvarna

It may appear very simple and excellent to say that only Brahmins should be educated and increase knowledge; only Kshatriyas should bear arms and wage war; only Vaishyas should engage in trade and agriculture; and only Shudras should perform servile labour.

But what happens in practice? The majority lose the right to bear arms for the sake of a few. So that a few may gain success and knowledge, the majority are condemned to failure and ignorance.

Why do ordinary people tolerate the corruptions of their own society? Social revolutions have occurred in many countries of the world. Why has a social revolution not occurred in India alone? I considered this question for a long time and found only one answer: the cruelty of chaturvarna has weakened the common people so thoroughly that they lack the power to revolt.

They cannot bear arms, and a revolution cannot succeed without arms. They are all cultivators, compelled to yoke the plough and till the soil. They were not permitted to transform the ploughshare into a sword; they had no such right.

The cruelty of chaturvarna also prevented them from receiving education. They could neither seek nor understand a path to salvation. They were submerged and made to remain slaves forever. Not knowing a path to liberation, they became satisfied that a life of slavery was sufficient.

Nothing is as dishonourable as chaturvarna. It made the people incapable of effort.

There is only one period in Indian history filled with freedom, dignity and fame: the age of the Mauryan Empire. In all other periods, defeat and ignorance made their home in India.

## The Death Knell of Chaturvarna

During the Mauryan kings' rule, however, chaturvarna's death knell was sounded. Ordinary Shudras became rulers of the country. It was precisely when chaturvarna possessed strength that defeat and destruction befell India.

When we try to civilize a group, our effort can succeed only if we regard ourselves as belonging among them, mix and associate with them, share their joys and sorrows, and develop fellow feeling and sympathy. But can any Hindu do so?

Protecting their respective caste customs has become the purpose of Hindus' lives. Caste is their priceless property, and they feel bound to preserve it at any cost. Hindus cannot associate with India's indigenous inhabitants, the non-Aryans of the Vedic period, and thereby demolish their caste pride.

Hindus are not unaware that helping the fallen is an important duty. Yet, for them, protecting caste customs is a still higher duty. Caste is the chief reason India has failed to attain a lofty civilization and Hindus have failed even to grieve over that failure.

"I do not believe a free society can be established in India while one class continues to enslave and oppress another. Yet, because I possess complete faith in the socialist ideal, I believe equality will arise among the many different classes. I believe socialism is the proper solution to every problem," says a Brahmin who is a Congress leader.

## Caste Must Be Abolished

But I ask a question. Is it enough merely to believe that equality among all communities will arise when socialism is implemented? To say that this belief alone is sufficient is the claim of those who do not properly understand socialist principles.

If socialism is not merely an ideal to be implemented in the distant future but a programme that can be put into practice, the socialist's principal concern is not whether he personally believes in equality. The relevant questions are these: does he support one class oppressing another through the social order? Will he continue to tolerate one class oppressing another?

I shall explain my position further so that everyone may grasp the meaning of these questions.

It is certain that the economic freedom desired by socialists cannot arise without a social revolution. If power is to be seized through revolution, the poor and humble must come forward. Will India's common people join others in carrying out such a revolution? What can inspire that degree of united solidarity among the people?

Common people will join others only when they trust that those others treat them as equals, brothers and with justice. If they do not believe that after the revolution they will be treated as exact equals and that distinctions of caste and religion will not be observed, will they come forward to join others in revolution?

It is not enough for the socialist leading the revolution merely to say that he does not believe in caste. A feeling of equality and fraternity must arise and remain firmly established in the heart of every participant.

Can anyone claim that India's common people truly believe there are no divisions other than rich and poor? Can anyone claim that caste and religious differences do not exist among the poor and humble? If caste and religious differences remain, is it possible for all Indians to unite in revolution?

Let us suppose a revolution arises in India, socialists triumph, and they seize political power. Can they solve social questions only after that exceptional situation has arisen? It is certain that a socialist government cannot operate for even one second without resolving the problem of high and low caste.

If socialists sincerely desire genuine economic freedom rather than erecting a pavilion of words and evading the issue with empty speech, they must abolish caste hierarchy. Social reform must therefore be the socialist's first task.

A revolution cannot even be conducted without social reform. Even if, by chance, a revolution succeeds, social reform remains essential if its true ideal is to be realized. Before revolution, the socialist will certainly be compelled to abolish caste.

Whatever reform one attempts, the spectre of caste stands as an obstacle. Unless that spectre is destroyed, neither political nor economic reform will arise.

## Why Did They Fail?

Why, then, did the party of social reformers fail? To understand the reason correctly, we must examine the kinds of reform for which the reformers of that time agitated.

For this purpose, social reform must be divided into two categories: reform of the Hindu family, and reform or reconstruction of Hindu social organization. Widow remarriage, child marriage and related matters belong to family reform. Abolishing caste distinctions belongs to the reconstruction of Hindu social organization.

The Social Reform Party concentrated chiefly upon reforming the Hindu family. It was largely composed of educated, high-caste Hindus. They did not recognize the need to agitate for caste abolition, or lacked the resolve to conduct such an agitation.

They therefore tried only to abolish evils such as enforced widowhood and child marriage. Those evils existed within their own communities, and many among them had experienced their effects. They made no attempt to reconstruct Hindu society.

Their reform struggle affected only family reform. It did not affect reform aimed at abolishing caste distinctions, and the reformers did not include that question in their programme. That alone is why the Social Reform Party failed.

No one should therefore conclude that political reform was shown to be necessary before social reform. What was revealed was only the reason the Social Reform Party failed.

It also became clear that the political reformers' victory was very slight, and that the opinion that social reform need not precede political reform has value only when social reform is treated merely as family reform.

If Indian social organization is to be reconstructed, no one can deny my principle that social reform must precede political reform. Even Ferdinand Lassalle, friend and fellow student of Karl Marx, said that leaders shaping a political constitution must give principal attention to social questions.

In my opinion, the underlying principle is that political organization must depend upon social organization. The Communal Award compelled Indian politicians, who had claimed that political and social questions bore no relationship to one another, to admit that social questions must be considered while designing the political structure.

The Communal Award arose as penance for the sin of Indian politicians neglecting social reform. It represents a great victory for the Social Reform Party. Although social reformers lost their struggle against political reformers, the Communal Award has emphatically demonstrated that their position was correct.

As far as India is concerned, the path to social reform is as rough as the path to heaven. It is arduous and full of danger. Social reform has few friends in India and many enemies. Those enemies are of two kinds: one group consists of political reformers; the other consists of socialists.

*Pagutharivu*, 1 October 1938.
