## English Translation

...because it has nourished us, and because that fresh memory has not faded, we feel poetry welling up and wish to sing of Tamil, the lamp of our life.

But when the event ends and we enter our home, imagine the anguish of the heart if the only lamp in the house, dim for lack of oil, goes out and darkness fills the home!

Throughout the ten days of the World Tamil Conference, as we listened to the exposition offered by eminent Tamil scholars, we experienced the proud feeling that arises beneath moonlight in the sky.

When we think of the present, our hearts become like a house whose lamp has gone out.

Should we rejoice when we recall the worth and greatness of the Tamil language, which created the Five Great Epics and other classics? Or should we grieve to see that some people still claim another language is superior to Tamil? We do not know.

Tamil society functions upon the foundation of the Tamil language. Though we may understand this, we may be unable to express it, or may not know how to express it. Yet, deep within ourselves, we realise that Tamils can live only if Tamil lives, and Tamil society can live only if Tamils live.

But our ancient stories conveyed this message long ago.

Once we lived in a manner admired by the world!

Today we look longingly to the world, wondering who will give us what.

Once we shone like the sun! Today we struggle, trapped in a dark cave!

That does not mean the sun has vanished! Our sight has become a little defective; I would not even say that it has become dim. Wipe those dim eyes a little and the sun will be visible!

We too can look up, hold our heads high, and sing like Ilango Adigal: "Let us praise the sun, let us praise the sun!"

I will not say that Tamil Nadu has forgotten its natural distinction and fallen asleep today; nor will I say that it lies unconscious! I will say only that it lies hesitant.

We must free ourselves from hesitation!

For there is work to be done! There is an abundance of work for everyone! There is work every day!

We must clear and improve the forests; we must increase prosperity; we must give the unemployed work and transform them into people who bring skill and excellence to the work they perform. We must dig into the earth and extract its minerals! Not merely ordinary materials:

We must also excavate the materials of history!

We must restore the silted-up harbour. From it, Tamil sailors must launch Tamil boats and ships, singing Tamil poetry as they sail.

It is this day that we envision. Valluvar says that what is intended will come to pass if those who intend it possess firmness.

What we intend in this world will happen, if we possess firmness!

"Do not hesitate, Tamilian! Do not lose your way, Tamilian!" Along with these lessons, Tamils must also become resolute!

If we become resolute in this way, every undertaking we begin in the world will be completed.

*Samaneethi Pongal Annual, 14 January 1968*
