## English Translation

It is said that Sambandar cured the Pandyan king's fever with his Tevaram hymn beginning "The sacred ash is the mantra," and that the Jains were defeated. After this divine exploit had been completed, two more tests were reportedly conducted to determine victory and defeat in the contest over the superiority or inferiority of Saivism and Jainism. One test was fire and the other water.

To establish the superiority of Saivism, Sambandar is said to have opened the manuscript containing his Tevaram hymns, selected a verse from it and placed the leaf in the fire. The Jains likewise wrote the truth of their religion upon a palm leaf and placed it in the fire.

The leaf deposited by Sambandar did not burn or perish in the flames, it is said, but shone greener and more brilliantly than before. The leaf deposited by the Jains burned to ashes.

Friends, is this evidence alone not sufficient to know the superiority and truth of Saivism? What a miracle it is! Even after such an immeasurable miracle demonstrated and established the truth of Saivism, some people, indeed many, refuse to follow that excellent religion and travel along the paths of various other faiths to their "ruin." What "ignorance" is theirs!

Can there be a greater marvel in the world than a palm leaf placed in flame yet remaining undestroyed? People who do not know what a miracle is or how one occurs talk vaguely of an eighth wonder after the seven wonders of the world. Apparently they do not know the truth that communities of devotees once performed this and many other astonishing miracles.

The dead returned to life; a bone became a woman; a severed nose and a worn-away hand grew again; a child cooked as meat later returned as a young man; an eye was plucked out and applied; lamps burned with water; poison became nectar; a stone was sailed upon the sea as though it were a boat; a child swallowed by a crocodile was called back; a male palmyra became female; a locked door opened; a mute person spoke; a Pariah became a Brahmin; elephants and horses flew through the sky; a brick became a block of gold; something cast into a river was retrieved from a pond; an old woman became a maiden; and a man sent his wedded wife away behind another who had seen her. How many more miracles were performed through the grace-filled power of devotees!

These few miracles alone were performed by the devotees of the *Periyapuranam*. Countless others, too many to reckon, too many for manuscripts to contain and too many for a lifetime to recite, abound in other Saivite and Vaishnavite Puranas and epics.

Despite so many boundless miracles standing as examples establishing the truth and superiority of Saivism, what are we to call the confusion that led Jains and the many adherents of other religions throughout the world, ignorant of them, to behave however they pleased?

When we consider why all those people failed to stand upon this "excellent religious path" and instead adopted other faiths, doubts arise: did all the miracles described in Saivite and Vaishnavite Puranas and epics truly happen? Could they happen? We feel the need to investigate them.

Ask even a small child what happens when a dry palm leaf, aged and decayed from being tied in a bundle, is put into fire. The child will say that it immediately burns to ash. But Sekkizhar, reputed to possess enough intelligence to serve as minister of a kingdom, says that the leaf Sambandar placed in the fire did not burn to ash.

His abundance of intelligence would not permit him to stop there. He says further that the dry palm leaf placed in the fire gleamed with a greenness and freshness it had not possessed before. Immediately afterward, in his next verse, he says that the leaf placed in the fire by the Jains burned to ash.

Did he stop even there? No. He adds that the Jains felt and sifted through the ashes repeatedly, kneading and scattering them in search of their leaf. Did he at least leave it there? No. Sekkizhar adds another statement attributed to the Pandyan: seeing the Jains feel, knead and sift the ash in search of their manuscript, the king laughed and told them, "Sift it still more thoroughly!"

Look at Sekkizhar's account! Had he stopped with saying that Sambandar's leaf did not burn while the Jain leaf burned to ash, Sambandar and his Saivism might perhaps have retained at least a little esteem and dignity.

But Sekkizhar left no room even for that. With his keen intelligence he poured out his story wholesale, like someone attempting to hide an entire pumpkin in a serving of rice.

His sole aim was somehow to prove that Sambandar's leaf did not burn. He never considered that, if not then, at least someday a person possessing even a little reason, and no great rational power is needed to expose fabrications like this, would drag his ignorance into the open. Without the habit of considering a matter, discerning its merits and faults, and expressing an opinion accordingly, Sekkizhar tried to bring distinction to Saivism.

Seeing his damaged intelligence, and because he too was a Tamil, we feel pity mixed with anger towards him.

Generally, even liars and braggarts measure and arrange their falsehoods according to their invention so that others may believe them at least to some extent. But our Sekkizhar heaped up his lies and boasts like river sand. Could his hasty intelligence count and measure all that sand?

Now let us concede that the leaf Sambandar placed in the fire did not burn. It may have been because the fire did not burn properly, or because the leaf was covered with so much encrusted dirt that it could not catch fire. Why offer such a reason? The divine images in Saivite and Vaishnavite temples are likewise covered with inches of grime, until one cannot identify their form or even whether they are male or female.

Perhaps the Tevaram manuscript, revered and preserved in the position of a god, had similarly been coated with protective oil and dirt, keeping the fire at a distance for some time.

But Sekkizhar was not content merely to say that the Jain manuscript burned to ash. He said that the Jains felt and sifted the ash again and again. As though that fabrication were insufficient, he supplied the distilled falsehood that the Pandyan told them, "Sift it repeatedly." What are we to say of the deficient intelligence of the man of Kundrathur, who disfigured the Pandyan's intelligence to support his own ignorance?

As the saying goes, nine lies are needed to conceal one. Reflecting on how much trouble Sekkizhar underwent to prove that Sambandar's leaf did not burn and that the Jain leaf burned away fills us with mingled sorrow and shame.

Seeking to invent superiority for Sambandar's Saivism, Sekkizhar forgot even the ordinary truth that fire burns whether touched knowingly or unknowingly; it does not cool and make radiant either the person touching it or the object placed within it. Behold the beauty with which he invented a miracle for Saivism, imagining that people would not know even this!

The third test conducted to establish Saivism's superiority, the test by water, is more amusing still. Sambandar wrote the Tevaram hymn beginning "May the Brahmins prosper" upon a leaf and released it into the Vaigai. The leaf is said to have travelled against the current, cleaving the water, while the Jain leaf was swept away and reached the sea.

Behold the novelty of Sambandar's palm leaf travelling upstream. Can anyone detect a difference between that claim and a tale that a man twisted sand into rope, fashioned a ladder from it, and that a legless cripple climbed the ladder into the sky and returned with a lotus plucked from a pond there? Think about it.

25 December 1946

## Translator's Notes

The source date reads “35-12-1946,” an impossible day of the month; the probable reading 25 December 1946 is used in English while the Tamil remains verbatim. Several miracle descriptions are densely allusive to Periyapuranam stories; the translation preserves the catalogue without attempting to harmonise the legends. “குன்றத்தூரார்” denotes Sekkizhar by his native place, rendered descriptively as the man of Kundrathur.
