## English Translation

## The Puppy on the Rubbish Heap

A puppy on a rubbish heap!

Exhausted from barking, the puppy lets its tongue hang out, looks at the rubbish heap, lies down there and supposedly reflects: “Why do I need this trouble? Why should I eat disgusting matter? Why should I bark at passers-by, be struck by stones and break my leg? From now on I shall live intelligently. No more needless trouble!”

But while it is thinking in this fashion, let a sound arise somewhere. At once it abandons its rubbish-heap Vedanta, curls its tail, runs away, puts its mouth into filth, barks at a passer-by, is again struck by a stone and breaks its leg, then returns to the rubbish heap as before.

This puppy's character is perfectly present in one rag that earns its food by singing Congress songs. Recklessness, hasty judgment and the language of the bazaar are its own property. It suddenly abuses our movement, Justice Party leaders and Muslim League leaders. Then it suddenly rests, lies upon its rubbish heap, reflects and sheds tears.

It laments: “Yes, what have these Congress ministers achieved? They have laid hands upon the Tamils. They have reduced the Corporation to a ruined wall.” But in the next instant its philosophy flies away. It resumes its old occupation, curls its tail and runs barking toward the filth.

Sometimes a dog becomes rabid and begins leaping upon and biting everyone it sees. Will people simply leave a rabid dog alone? In our town during April, municipal orders call for such dogs to be caught with noose ropes and killed. Who bears responsibility for teaching an appropriate lesson to rags in the press that possess this character? You, the public.

## The Courtesan's Parrot

Let the rubbish-heap dog's nature stand aside. What do you think of a parrot? You have heard the proverb that the little parrot repeats whatever it is taught. A parrot in Kandan's house says, “The Kambhoji raga is splendid,” because Kandan loves music.

What, then, will the parrot in a courtesan's house say? It will repeat what she says, because repeating speech is a parrot's nature, and it will repeat it only partially. Sitting inside its cage and waiting until the cat is absent, a newspaper with this parrot's nature recites exactly whatever anyone teaches it.

Must the obscenity of that parrot talk be explained? Listen. The parrot, whose instructor sits beside its cage eating a kovai fruit rotten at the tip, says: “Self-Respecters create disturbances at meetings. The Congress government must not leave them alone.” Did not the drum called *Dinamani* say precisely this? The parrot merely repeats it. It has no inquiry into cause and effect. How could a parrot that repeats speech possess any? If it grows in a courtesan's house, what will its language and subject be?

## “These Self-Respecters...”

One day I went toward the market street. On seeing my head, a nationalist there began saying something like, “These Self-Respecters...” I could not understand his words clearly, so I stopped to hear what he meant. His tone showed that he was abusing us without reason.

He is not alone. Many people, because they have not associated with us or patiently listened to the matter, hold mistaken views and consider abusing Self-Respecters a kind of national sacred service.

We should not be angry with these people. Many in this group, if one day they patiently listen merely to what we actually say, become genuinely satisfied and turn into a source of strength for our movement. We must somehow make such people hear us. Once they listen, both they and we can see the result immediately. The history of our movement offers many examples.

## One Example

Half the history of our movement consists of such incidents. A few days ago, Comrade E. V. R. spoke at a village called Ayakkavundanpalayam. A notable person who had abused the Self-Respect Movement without cause attended the meeting, reportedly prepared to ask questions and create confusion.

Some friends told me in advance and asked me to inform E. V. R. Why should I? Answering questions is the life force of our movement. We ourselves ask questions about social organization; that is our service. Only if I imagined E. V. R. would fail to do it would I need warn him about “questions.” I therefore said nothing.

The speech began and E. V. R. poured forth his address. The questioner sat near the platform. At first he kept twisting his moustache, sharpened like a needle. As the “injection” entered him, he let the moustache go. If anyone nearby sneezed or spoke, he began glaring at them, worried that a sneeze, cough or conversation might spoil the splendid speech.

Without his awareness, his head began nodding, “Yes! Yes!” He smiled and laughed. When the speech ended, he returned home satisfied. If anyone now questions us, he himself will answer. From that day he became our friend.

## Surviving Tour Account

The archived source breaks at this point and resumes during an account of public meetings. The surviving passage says that despite a nationalist comrade's disturbance, the speakers neither shortened nor ended their meeting. Everyone spoke in the usual sequence, and it concluded only after the chair's final address and vote of thanks.

The writer and Eelathu Adigal then went to Aruppukkottai, while Comrade Ponnambalanar went to Erode for an election. Their subsequent victory showed that he went to witness the Congress defeat with his own eyes.

They travelled to Mayavaram, grieving for the departed Lion of Mayuram. The public meeting there was excellent. A Tamil association and a South Indian Welfare Rights Association were active, comrades were plentiful and feeling flowed like a spring. Mayavaram was no insignificant town: after giving Congress the Honourable Ramanathan, the region helped the movement by giving his brother, Comrade Sambandam.

The writer and Eelathu Adigal went to Kanchipuram. Meanwhile Ponnambalanar travelled to Bellary to see Periyar. At Kanchi they held public meetings with the heroic ascetics Shanmugananda and Arunagirinathar, who had come to inaugurate a Non-Brahmin Youth Association, Corporation councillor Comrade Sankaran, and Parvathi Ammal, who performed Tamil service.

At Chinna Kanchipuram, Comrade Ponnappan had arisen as an echo of the labour of the movement's hardworking Comrade Thangavelu Mudaliar. He was young, from a wealthy family, and overflowing with non-Brahmin feeling. The writer trusted that he and his associates would establish the movement firmly there and hoped their enthusiasm would grow.

Though the speakers had failed their comrades at Dharapuram, Ponnambalanar went and conducted an excellent meeting. Enthusiasm was abundant there too. The events in Salem were already known to readers.

There was indeed so much attachment. Though satisfied by it, the writer grieved that the movement still resembled a merchant who possessed great capital but failed to advertise properly. If the people lacked attachment to the movement's principles, its workers could remain idle. But the people's interest rested upon the Justice Party, while Congress was losing public influence. Should movement comrades not travel from village to village?

Only by gathering comrades, performing organized work and establishing South Indian Welfare Rights Associations everywhere could they lay the foundation for victory. Public hatred of Congress was beyond description.

The writer therefore called lovingly and confidently: “Tamils, come and join us!” If Tamils united, none could defeat them. If everyone joined the association, liberation would become certain that very day. The speakers travelled from place to place issuing that call.

They did not invite Tamils without giving reasons. They explained the past, present and future, stating clearly that the ruling Congress had done no good and instead caused harm. That was the essence of their campaign.

## Dyarchy

Tamils were asked to reflect. The constitutional system under which the Justice Party governed did not possess the full powers available in the later period; it was the incomplete arrangement called dyarchy. The most important powers, police and finance, were not held by its ministers.

Many boasted that dyarchy was so complicated nobody could administer it. Yet the Justice movement governed successfully and worked in major fields such as village amenities, indigenous medicine and local self-government. In public employment, politics, religion, society and education, it fought and defeated monopolies that had long reigned, advancing equal rights.

The Honourable Sastri once referred to the Justice movement's achievements in a speech at Ranade Hall, Mylapore, and the Honourable Yakub Hassan spoke of them at a political conference.

Nevertheless, Justice Party leaders weakened the party by failing to arrange propaganda. Internal disorder ruins a country; internal hostility among leaders harmed the party.

Sir P. Theagaraya Chetty, jewel of the Devanga community and gem of Dravida Nadu, wore white and possessed a pure heart. He rejected a ministerial office offered to him and laboured to elevate non-Brahmin society. Dr T. M. Nair worked for non-Brahmins and died abroad in that cause. The statecraft of the Raja of Panagal was known to the world.

Tamils allowed the party nurtured by such great leaders to slip away in the previous election. Why?

## Political Fraud

The previous general election was a great political fraud. The true issue was never stated during the campaign. When an old motor car is offered in a market and two people compete to buy it, a clever bidder may spread the claim that its engine is defective. Once the rival believes the rumour and leaves disappointed, the clever man buys it cheaply. The election proceeded in that fashion.

Congress declared the new constitutional reform utterly rotten, something that made the hand stink if touched and the mouth foul if discussed. It claimed to contest only to prevent other parties from accepting and administering it. Gandhi's disciples said the yellow ballot box had appeared to abolish British imperialism.

They further promised to reduce taxes, relieve cultivators' distress, improve workers' conditions, expand trade and end poverty. They said honey and milk would flow under their rule. The people believed them and filled the yellow box.

Congress leaders had said, “We are not hunters of office. Why should we want legislatures or ministerial work?” Yet after a drama of promises they accepted office, like the drunken husband who orders his wife to scrape imaginary greens from a wall.

## What Have We Seen?

What do we now see through the Congress Party that was welcomed with praise? Has even one hair fallen from the British lion's mane? Where has their former platform heroism gone?

Dr T. S. S. Rajan, minister among those who intended to drive the white man away with his baggage, worshipfully called the Governor “friend, guide and teacher,” adding that had they expected to hold office in this manner, they would not earlier have abused him improperly. Which nationalist felt ashamed on hearing this? Who asked the minister why he spoke in a way that stripped national honour? Nobody.

Dr Rajan, once accused of betraying Congress and expelled by several committees, had driven Rajagopalachari toward the Pudupalayam ashram. Yet he was given ministerial office. Who condemned it? Without even contesting an election, he went directly to Fort St George like a king and became minister.

Congress members had denounced any Justice Party nomination to a municipality or district board as entry through the backyard. They themselves brought Dr Rajan through that despised backyard and made him minister. Was this justice? Nationalists who did not question it shower questions only upon us.

## The Cruelty of Taxes

Sir K. V. Reddy reduced land revenue by 7.5 million rupees and was abused for reducing only a small sum. How much did these sons of Bharat reduce? Did they reduce even one paisa beyond that amount? Where is the honour of their pledge?

They had said taxation would not be cruel under their rule and that repeated burdens had bent the cultivator's back. Have taxes now fallen? No. Have they at least refrained from imposing new ones? Registration tax, tax on indigenous medical practitioners, customs duty, petrol tax, tobacco tax, entertainment tax, electricity tax and sales tax have been added in succession. Only January and February seem to remain untaxed.

When asked whether the people can bear all this, they point to prohibition and accuse every critic of the new taxes of opposing it.

## Prohibition or Prohibition of Thought?

The question is whether Congress policy is prohibition of liquor or prohibition of reason. Let it implement prohibition freely. Periyar E. V. R. included prohibition in the Justice Party programme in 1934. Its ministers then lacked full powers and could not implement it.

Even now prohibition does not cover the entire Presidency. Province-wide prohibition would create a deficit of only four crore rupees. Numerous new taxes have already been imposed to cover it. Why another sales tax estimated to yield as much as twenty crore? Why trouble traders and make the poor suffer through rising prices merely to cover a four-crore shortfall?

## Workers' Suffering

Congress grew strong chiefly through workers' support. Although Periyar E. V. R. once went to prison for workers, and although Justice Party leaders protected labour leaders threatened with deportation, workers developed affection for Congress. What benefit did they receive?

Were working hours reduced? No. Section 144 is freely used on behalf of employers against workers. Labour leaders are imprisoned. Workers conducted pickets and as many as 150 went to prison in Madras. There were baton charges in Madurai and firing at Chirala and Chittivalasa in Andhra. These were the workers' rewards.

## Tamils in Prison

For picketing under the cry “Long live Tamil! Down with Hindi!”, 1,001 Tamils were imprisoned. More than fifty women and over twenty children were jailed. Two young heroes died in prison. Yet Rajagopalachari's heart did not soften. Like pronouncing a dreadful curse, he continued saying “compulsory Hindi.”

How are all these troubles to end? Comrades, Tamils, there is only one way for our suffering to cease and well-being to arise: the South Indian Welfare Rights Association.
