## English Translation

Three figures are burning! The eminent elder hurries towards the crowd with the delighted expression little children wear during temple festivals. The police push back those standing around. The people shift their attention from the burning effigies to the great man hurrying forward.

A small boy sits upon the elder's shoulder. He is the world of the future.

“Grandfather, what is that?” The child does not ask. He merely watches in wonder. Grandfather lifts him and points: “Look, little brother! That is Ravana. That is Kumbhakarna. The other is Meghanada!”

The child does not ask why they are being burned. He already knows the story Mother Indira told him about it. And so he claps and laughs. The people cheer. The eminent elder applauds too!

A few days ago, the orthodox are said to have celebrated Ramlila in the city of Delhi. It is supposedly the sacred day of Rama, who destroyed Ravana and his lineage. India's Prime Minister, Pandit Nehru, reportedly came with his grandson to see the festival. Rajendra Prasad, President of the Indian Republic, attended. Delhi's ministers, diplomatic representatives and all the rest were there. Our newspapers photographed and published the scene.

The Indian government's information department filmed this spectacle and is showing it in cinemas this week.

Nehru is not a man ignorant of who Ravana and Rama were. He himself explained beautifully to his beloved daughter Indira the struggle that arose between the two. That writer of history, who explained to Indira that “the Ramayana describes the struggle between the Aryans and the Dravidian people,” has gone with his grandson to witness Ravana, Kumbhakarna and Indrajit made into effigies and burned. No, no: he has gone to witness the festival! Pointing to them as they burn, he laughs in delight together with his grandson.

Ravana was a king of the South, a great son of the Dravidian people. Nehru knows this. Yet Nehru took part! He even brought along one member of his future generation and rejoiced!

Pandit Nehru is not a temple priest. He is the holder of the sceptre.

Yet this festival of effigies has given delight to that ruler. The figures consumed by fire may have been made for one or two rupees. Once lit, they caught fire and became ash within minutes.

But when we saw that scene on the screen, our whole heart trembled. When the Congress conference pavilion built at a cost of two lakh rupees under Indira Gandhi's supervision burned down, who knows how Nehru, who was to preside there, suffered? A thousand times greater fury and anger surged within us.

The scene was on the screen. My friend Karunanidhi, seated beside me, asked:

“What if we made an effigy of Rama and burned it?”

I gave no answer. If we did as my friend asked, what could anyone do? Ravana was a sovereign of the South. Research texts say so. Nehru's own writings emphasise it. Countless reports travel daily to Delhi describing us, the Dravidian consciousness spreading in the South and our desire to honour our respective ancestors. Despite this, they conduct Ramlila. This extravagant festival that arouses our spirit takes place, and world-renowned leaders lend it their support.

“Ramlila is a traditional festival.”

“Can it be stopped for someone's sake?”

“Does that sacred day not proclaim the holy virtues of our Lord?”

Many may come forward with such replies when they see our condemnation.

Some will argue: “Do not all these activities demonstrate the greatness of Hinduism?”

Researchers say that when the Aryan lineage entered this land, it described in its Vedas the great kings who opposed it as demons.

History says that Aryan religious preachers called the original Dravidian inhabitants by degrading terms such as demons, rakshasas and low-born people.

Pandit Nehru, who came to explain that history, has acknowledged this truth. Yet the day on which the demon was destroyed is celebrated. They stage the slaying of Ravana!

If questioned, the orthodox will say that according to Hinduism these events must be celebrated.

They will summon scripture as evidence, bring Queen Victoria's proclamation into the argument, and attempt to condemn us as perverse disputants and enemies of the law.

We wish to bring the following information to the attention of those orthodox people who beat the drum crying, “Hinduism! Hinduism!”

“In the name of religion, Brahmins oppose the democratic principle that everyone should have equal opportunity and that needless burdens should be removed from human beings.”

We are not the ones saying this. An American writer named Bullitt, formerly a representative of the United States government in India, made this observation in an American magazine. In his article titled “Hinduism,” he explains in detail what kind of cloak Hinduism provides for the orthodox.

He aptly describes Hinduism as the sword used by orthodoxy to subdue the people and prevent them from living in freedom.

In that article in *Life*, he goes on to discuss the obscenities of Hindu mythology:

“I looked for the god worshipped by all Hindus. They worship something called the lingam. Do you know what it is? The lingam is the male sexual organ.”

The person saying this is Bullitt, an American and, moreover, a man with political knowledge. He has written with such pointed mockery about the degrading condition here.

On seeing these words, the Sanatanists will raise their staffs. They will roar: “Can this be tolerated? What an injustice!”

For no foreign scholar who came to India and described the degrading practices rampant here has escaped the writing of these vultures.

Miss Mayo was condemned by India's Sanatanists. When the British writer Beverley Nichols drew a written portrait of India, every Indian nationalist journal raged. Now that Bullitt writes about Hinduism, will they remain quiet, especially when independence has just been obtained?

On seeing Bullitt's article, India's “Minister of Hunger,” Munshi, has set forth as the guardian of Sanatana Dharma.

“American, what arrogance you possess!” he cries. He has taken up not a gun but a pen and departed for a great war.

Articles! Condemnations! They have issued from him. Every Indian nationalist journal has reproduced them. Northern journals too have written notes condemning the American's article.

The blade of truth flung by Bullitt has thrown Munshi, who advises people to eat grass and cotton, into a fit of rage!

Perhaps he thought he had found a place to display the fury people pour upon him. Whatever the reason, he has written a lengthy article condemning Bullitt's piece.

Bullitt makes two points in his article:

Brahmins practise coercion beneath the cloak called religion.

The gods of Hinduism are products of uncivilised imagination.

Munshi does not refute either statement. How could he? The *Linga Purana* itself exists to explain elegantly that the lingam is the male sexual organ.

Unable to refute the American writer's statement, this wooden minister has nevertheless leapt forward in heroic fury. “Can one speak like this? Can it be tolerated? Alas!” he cries in distress.

Though the daily cries of crores of suffering people reach his ears without troubling him, a few words written by Bullitt have produced both a storm and a volcano in his mind.

The Munshi lineage is described in the North as a support of orthodoxy. He is one such orthodox support, a thoroughly ripened Brahmin. That is why, on seeing Bullitt's words, he has set forth holding a pen rather than a blade of grass.

Munshi's agitation fills us with pity. Having no capacity to enter into argument, he howls like a fox whose tail has been cut off. His conduct would make even a mute person laugh aloud!

This Munshi is the present heir of the lineage that exhausted itself abusing Mayo and Nichols.

When we think of these lions who spring up roaring whenever the degradations here are pointed out, we recall the proverb: “What an elegant coiffure, adorned with screw-pine flowers! Yet lice and nits are said to live within it!”

They try to conceal an internal wound by applying civet perfume. When others point it out, they become delirious and cry “O mad one who wears the crescent,” wailing, roaring and making an uproar.

But they do not perceive the truth in the words published by foreigners. Even when it is made clear, they merely become enraged; the thought of identifying and correcting those defects never arises in them.

Hinduism rolls about upon the mounds of mythology.

The mythological texts lie filled with obscene imaginings.

Those obscene imaginings are useful for protecting Aryan dharma.

The Munshi lineage does not come forward to surrender Aryan dharma.

For it is their gateway to heaven, their beautiful grove.

Thinking only of this selfish benefit, even the educated and degree-holding, and those who boast that they drove out the Europeans, praise the ancient myths and celebrate festivals for the stories they provide.

Nehru knows that Rama is not a divine incarnation and that no benefit is gained by continuing to celebrate the day on which the lord of Lanka was slain.

Nehru is educated. He is the man who stated the truth that Ravana was a Dravidian king. He occupies a position honoured throughout the world. Nor is he ignorant of the shock that celebrating the sacred day on which a Dravidian king was destroyed would cause southerners who have studied history.

Yet he permits the festival. He participates in it. He displays the story even to his tiny grandson!

The growth of this mentality will only help Hinduism fall into the burial pit.

The cries and fury of the Munshi lineage will not save this wretched religion, laden with obscenity.

So long as Hinduism and the Munshi lineage, its imperial heirs, continue to exist, the mockery and ridicule of foreigners will inevitably arise.

The rage of people like Munshi cannot stop it. The internal wound must disappear; perfume is not its medicine!

*(Dravida Nadu, 28 October 1951)*

## Translator's Notes

`புனுகு` is the traditional aromatic substance civet. The title contrasts treatment of the underlying wound with merely covering its smell; “perfume” is used in English to preserve that governing metaphor naturally.

The American writer identified only as `புல்லிட்` (Bullitt) and the exact *Life* magazine article require bibliographic verification. The translation does not expand the name beyond what the source safely supports.

The proverb about the ornate coiffure and the devotional phrase `பித்தா பிறை சூடி` are translated for their rhetorical function. Several isolated OCR distortions remain in the verbatim Tamil source and are not silently rewritten.
