## English Translation

Delhi has become an advertising booth!

Foreign ambassadors and those who arrive after sniffing out commercial opportunities are gathering here in ever-growing numbers.

The Government of India frequently holds receptions for them, and they in turn welcome and entertain representatives of the Indian government. This has become a daily occurrence.

Sweet music! Graceful dancing! Merriment! Mingling! Hospitality! Everything proceeds without stint.

The Russian ambassador is invited to the Deepavali celebration; likewise, Governor-General Acharya is invited to the anniversary of the Soviet Revolution. Foreign ambassadors have viewed and enjoyed exhibitions of Indian art. They show Indian government officials lists of goods ready for sale to foreigners.

It is repeatedly asserted that India has had some form of cultural relationship with every country since ancient times, and speeches proclaim that India will continue to maintain friendly and affectionate relations with those countries. These costly banquets and festivities, garden parties held in the name of fostering relations, and ceremonial speeches all serve one principal purpose: publicity! Publicity! That is the aim!

One ambassador leads a life of splendour in Britain with nine motor cars; another lives in Soviet Russia amid luxurious golden clothing and possessions; a goodwill delegation is sent to Abyssinia; a propaganda council is established in America; and ambassadors travel even to Brazil and Argentina. All of it has the same purpose: publicity. India and the ability of the party that governs it must be spoken of proudly in many lands. That is the objective.

We are not some village Kuppanna from the edge of a pond who says that the world must not praise India, that India needs no praise, or that no efforts should be made to obtain it. What we say is that the money poured into this publicity enterprise is far too great, and that this expense amounts merely to applying perfume over a festering wound rather than genuinely helping the world learn of India's greatness. India's attempt to spread its fame by giving ostentatious banquets abroad is not merely foolish; it is also a betrayal that wastes the tax money of the poor.

Did the Soviet nation, which challenges the whole world, spread its fame through such wasteful expenditure? It is only now that even Delhi has a nominal Soviet embassy! Yet many years before this arrangement came into being, we learned of the Soviet Union's fame through the Five-Year Plans that country successfully carried out and demonstrated.

Likewise, our charge is that it is wrong to avoid accomplishing something tangible through which the world's esteem can and ought to be won, and instead spend vast sums arranging every variety of banquet for publicity and many kinds of people and channels for propaganda. No matter how many thousands of banquets are held, India's condition cannot be hidden from the eyes of foreigners. They cannot fail to learn that, under the government entertaining them, tens of millions of people still live lives of suffering. Those capable of discovering their condition from statistics cannot be deceived with smiles and bouquets...
