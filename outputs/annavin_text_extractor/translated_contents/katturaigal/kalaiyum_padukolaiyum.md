## English Translation

This is the sinner who killed sixteen people!

He poisoned and killed sixteen people!

He turned sixteen people into corpses and carried away the money!

So said the court. The murderer did not deny the charge.

“True! It is true that I poisoned and killed sixteen people.

“It is also true that I took the money and fled.

“But do not describe me merely as a sinner who committed mass murder. I am an artist, not only a killer. I carried out the massacre, but when those sixteen people fell as corpses, I became not merely a murderer but also a devotee: I prayed for the peace of the dead people's souls.

“I became a killer so that I might live as an artist and obtain love. Even after the massacre, I did not fail to pray.”

The accused reportedly said this and submitted a fifty-eight-page confession.

Hirasawa was the murderer's name. Disguised as a health inspector, he entered a bank in Tokyo. He said a diarrhoeal disease was spreading in the city and that he had come to administer preventive medicine. He gave poison as medicine to the sixteen clerks working there.

Calmly, without the slightest agitation, he administered the poison. After sixteen bodies collapsed, he appealed to God for the peace of their souls, gathered all the money he could find and fled. When caught and placed before the court, he admitted the crime and also declared, “I am an artist!”

He was reportedly the head of an art association in Tokyo. The association, devoted to developing art, needed money, and he had taken responsibility for finding it out of artistic enthusiasm. He needed money personally too, and not for an ordinary purpose but for love. Thus the sinner said he committed murder for art and love.

“The act was criminal, but look at my purpose. Remember that this was murder performed for art,” said Hirasawa. This event occurred in Japan during October. Murder for art!

No one would dare perform so sinful an act. He did it because he was a thoroughly hardened sinner. Because he possessed such audacity, he even dared say that he killed for art.

The issue we raise here is not merely whether one may kill for art. We must ask whether anything may be done for art; whether every act committed in art's name can be considered just; and whether it is right to answer every charge by saying, “I did it with an artistic purpose.”

No one here has committed massacre and robbery for art. But in art's name, many have murdered reason and plundered self-respect.

Imagine an artist who truly wished to display his skill by painting the agony upon an infant's face as it died without its mother's milk or any other nourishment. Suppose he stole a baby from its mother, placed it alone without milk or drink, stood watching, dipped his brush into his colours and painted.

Then suppose he summoned the town, showed the painting to connoisseurs, heard everyone exclaim, “Ah, wonderful! Wonderful!”, rejoiced, accepted the title “king of artists,” and began thanking them by saying, “Listen while I tell you why this painting possesses such artistic subtlety.” If he then revealed the infanticide he had committed, what would happen? What fate would await this pitiless man?

“Anything may be done for art!” The Japanese murderer offered that argument after a cruel deed. Although people in this country have not committed identical massacres, some have performed acts that poison public life in the name of art.

Some have massacred a people's cultural qualities for art. Saying, “See how I have enhanced artistic beauty,” they have destroyed people's intelligence and reviled reason. There are even connoisseurs who honour those who killed reason for art.

Someone asks, “What is this picture, sir? I cannot bear to see it. Women are shown naked, while a man plays the flute as he looks at them. Why was such a painting made? What state of mind will it produce in the men and women who see it?”

The reply attacks immediately: “Ignorant of meaning! Insensible to art! You do not understand its artistic subtlety. You think it arouses lust, but that was not its purpose. It was created for art, to display artistic beauty!”

Erotic sports and obscene acts have been made into stone figures and installed in temple towers, corridors and halls. If someone asks whether such obscenities and sexual performances should stand in a place that calls people toward purity, the answer comes angrily:

“Small-minded fellow! You speak without understanding the greatness of sculpture. It is stone! The sculptor shaped it with only a small chisel in his hand. But look at the expression. See the face of the mother in labour. It is for art, brother, for art!”

If someone asks, “The song and story do not seem suitable for the home. They do not foster devotion or good character. Is this a proper epic?”, connoisseurs become furious.

“Do you dislike the story? Throw it away! Do you dislike the religious teaching? Throw it away! Look at the art within it, the art! Even if the story is unbelievable, read it for art. Even if its religious instruction provides no path for reason, read it for art!” they say.

They have killed reason for art, and some still defend that killing by invoking art's name.

In Tokyo an artist killed for art, and the court declared him guilty. Here, those who killed reason in art's name, and those who spread that “art,” should be brought before the tribunal of reason and questioned, if reason is not to be destroyed.

Instead, in a painful reversal, those who identify and expose the murder of reason committed in art's name are dragged forward, placed not before reason's tribunal but upon the artistic stage, and charged as offenders.

Yet the day when those who believe anything may be done for art and anything depicted in art's name are examined by the tribunal of reason is approaching rapidly.

Orders have already been issued under the present rule of a disciple of Ramana to remove obscene stone figures from temples and destroy paintings of the same kind. In many temples one can see obscene scenes carved in stone that create disgust at first sight, work performed in the name of art. Now that very “art” is being destroyed.

The arrogant assumption that anything may be done in art's name and accepted however it appears is collapsing. If, even under rulers so obsessed with antiquity that they grant a holiday for Avani Avittam, an order has been issued declaring that obscenity can no longer be permitted merely because art's name is invoked, then...!

*Dravida Nadu*, 28 November 1949
