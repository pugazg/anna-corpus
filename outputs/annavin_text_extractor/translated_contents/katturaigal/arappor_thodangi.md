## English Translation

At nine in the morning on 10 August 1948, anti-Hindi picketing began in front of the Thondaimandalam Tuluva Vellalar High School in Madras. Comrade Annadurai, the first commander of the anti-Hindi campaign, led in procession the ten volunteers who came for the first day's picketing under field commander Comrade C. T. T. Arasu. The procession set out from outside the Kasi Viswanathar Temple and reached the school amid the peaceful cries of many thousands of people: "Down with Hindi! Long live Tamil!"

The volunteers and their commander, C. T. T. Arasu, peacefully and affectionately asked the students going to school not to study Hindi.

Seven of the students entering the school walked out again as a sign that they would not study Hindi.

After the school opened, at half past ten, the volunteers set out in procession under Comrade C. T. T. Arasu and reached Sivagnanam Park. When it was announced there that picketing would resume at 2:15 in the afternoon, the gathering dispersed peacefully.

The picketing began again at 2:15 p.m. As in the morning, the volunteers stood peacefully in formation and asked the students not to study Hindi.

The volunteers and many thousands of people then set out in procession. They travelled through Thiruppalli Street, Mulla Sahib Street, Ayalur Muthiah Mudali Street, Bashyakaralu Naidu Street, Basin Bridge Road, and Pethu Naicken Street before reaching Sivagnanam Park. There Comrades Annadurai and Arasu thanked everyone who had supported the successful conduct of the anti-Hindi struggle. After they announced that anti-Hindi picketing would take place again the next day, the gathering dispersed peacefully.

Several police officers, including the Commissioner of Police, observed the peaceful procession and picketing.

Both morning and evening, many thousands of members of the public assembled about a hundred feet away on either side of the school, thereby making the government recognize the extensive popular support for the anti-Hindi campaign.

The picketers were:

1. Comrade C. T. T. Arasu
2. Comrade Dhanalakshmi
3. Dravidamani, Dhanalakshmi's son
4. N. Subramanian
5. M. Ananthippan
6. D. D. Pandian
7. N. Krishnan
8. S. Natarajan
9. Kalaichelvan
10. N. Mariappan

Many members of the public garlanded the volunteers who had picketed and expressed their support.

Dr Dharmambal, who had formerly served as commander of the women in the anti-Hindi campaign and had been imprisoned, went to the procession's starting place that morning. She garlanded Comrade Dhanalakshmi, sang a classical Tamil song, and inaugurated the procession.

On the 11th, 12th, and 13th too, the picketing and processions continued in an entirely peaceful manner.

The anti-Hindi committee is making arrangements to conduct picketing in towns outside Madras as well.

*Dravida Nadu*, 15 August 1948.
