## English Translation

The white mansion standing at the centre of civilised Madras is called Ripon Building. It houses the Corporation of Madras. The “great men” enthroned in that ornamental hall are responsible for governing the city. The education and health of its people, its transport and water supply are all under their supervision. They collect the tribute paid by the city’s inhabitants and sit as their guardians; the civilisation of the city itself can testify to their administrative ability. They have been adorned with the title “fathers of the city.”

## The Measuring Rod

The distinction and prosperity of Madras, the capital of our province, depend upon the efficiency of that Corporation. If self-governing bodies are to flourish in a country and acquire administrative skill in accordance with the poet’s thought that “everyone is a king of this land,” then the smaller departments called corporations, district boards and local bodies are the country’s political laboratory. The manner of administration here provides a sound measure of the country’s course.

Today it is Congress that governs this sacred Corporation, laden with so many responsibilities and foremost among the municipal institutions of our province. Most of our provincial municipalities have been entrusted to the fortunate Congress Party that rules the country.

## The Invasion

Congress began capturing local bodies three years ago. With the platform, the press and the “Mahatma” as its military strength, it launched an invasion, defeated the Justice Party and seized local institutions one after another. Its intense false propaganda demolished many fortresses. Victory everywhere! Celebration in every place! Congress rejoiced without restraint.

## Those Days

Until then, the Justice Party had governed from Ripon Building. It possessed neither the glitter of propaganda nor the light of the press. Justice Party leaders worked there politically and increased the city’s distinction. When Sir P. Thyagaraya, the victorious champion, the great leader of our party and the sovereign clad in white, governed from the white mansion, need anyone explain that the city’s prosperity and distinction grew?

Good roads were laid throughout the city. Signs of civilisation were created in such measure that visitors from every province could admire them and even feel a little envy. There was no deficiency in drinking-water facilities. Public health was tended with a mother’s affection. Beautiful gardens, playgrounds for children, reading rooms that served as centres of public knowledge, fountains supplying city water, schools for children and hospitals: the Justice Party administration of that period gave the city’s people all these and many similar graceful benefits.

## The People’s Delusion

Yes, the people refused justice to the Justice Party, which had achieved so much with only the petty authority available under the complicated system of dyarchy. They defeated its leaders in the election, took away the power it had held and handed it to Congress.

Congressmen had campaigned in a manner that made people believe that the city could survive only if power were entrusted to Congress, and that destruction would otherwise follow. Their speeches of those days still echo in our ears.

## Malicious Propaganda

“Justice Party administration is dreadful. The people’s money is being wasted; corruption flows like a spring in flood; selfishness performs its dance; bribery dances beside it; education declines and disease grows. They do not appear to govern for the people. What patriotism do they possess?

“Send us, the honourable men of Congress without such attachments, inside Ripon Building. Administration will improve; corruption will flee the town; education will grow; public health will flourish; selfishness will run away without even taking leave.”

They campaigned in this fashion, pouring honey into listeners’ ears. Subsequent events exposed in broad daylight whether it was propaganda or merely prostitution. What did the people find? What did those who helped Congress defeat the Justice Party receive? Honey and milk, or grief and bewilderment? Who can conceal what happened, and what did those events reveal?

Within Congress’s first year in office, the people’s dissatisfaction, hatred and bitterness had gathered into a great mass. City residents winked and clicked their tongues. “The nationalist younger brother talks but does not act! He shouts but does nothing after understanding the whole matter. He abuses others but does not know how to clear away his own dust. He is a hero only in words, an ignorant troublemaker,” they said.

The distinguished people of Madras did not stop with talk. At the next election they sent him away, saying, “Go on, younger brother; your conduct is excellent indeed!” Congress became furious and boasted, “Just you wait.” It faced the following year’s election and bit the dust in many constituencies again. Only then was its delusion complete. Its heart broke; it suffered on discovering that its disguise had been exposed. It became afraid to face another election.

In the meantime, Honourable Achariar established Congress rule and suspended that year’s election too. They hoped to stop the flood with a dam. How long can it hold? Even now the floodwater is slowly entering, splitting the barrier little by little at many points.

Why did Congress come to this condition? Why do all who once welcomed it with praise now tell it not to cross their threshold?

## A Garland for a Monkey

When one party defeats another and captures authority, it must administer more efficiently than its predecessor. It must govern through selfless service in a manner that even those previously in office can praise. Its discipline and order must delight all who see them. It must reduce taxation, increase the conveniences of life and earn the people’s praise. Only then will people rejoice that transferring power from the former party to another has proved beneficial.

If, instead, administration is full of defects and deformities, new and heavy taxes are imposed and public facilities diminish, what will people say? Will those who took a garland from a maiden and placed it upon a monkey rejoice?

Whoever examines Congress’s conduct in Ripon Building by this measure, if honest, will not say that Congress rule improved the city’s distinction.

## *Mithran*’s Sorrow

With honesty as its companion, *Swadesamitran*, a friend of Congress, lamented both last year and this year while pointing out the defects of Congress administration. Like a mother advising a daughter after hearing that the girl quarrelled with her uncle and aunt in her husband’s home and behaved in a manner that displeased her husband, *Mithran* offers counsel to Congress itself.

“Education has declined in the city! Public health is deteriorating! You have failed to provide proper facilities even for pregnant women! Education has not grown; the city has become a hell,” says *Mithran*.

“The city is a hell!” Splendid! What finer review could Congress require? Nor did a Justice Party member deliver this rebuke. It is the verdict of the mad devotee who once burned camphor praying that Congress should come to office.

Last year, *The Hindu* condemned Congress rule by pointing out the ruined condition of roads and other facilities.

## Zero

This year, in addition to those papers, the *Indian Express* has criticised Corporation administration. The *Express* gives Congress a score of zero for administrative ability, and it has awarded that mark only after investigation.

Education and public health reveal the character of an administration. The *Express* shows that Congress has painted an ugly picture in both.

## At a Snail’s Pace

Do you know how rapidly education grew in Madras under Congress? Previously there were 134 elementary schools. As the result of Congressmen labouring without rest for a whole year, there are now 136. Two brand-new schools were established in one year.

Is this not educational growth at a speed with which even a snail cannot compete?

## But There Was a Mayor!

Is it not Congress that gives the “untouchables” a new name and imagines that the new name alone gives them a new condition? Under its rule, did the children of untouchable communities in Madras schools find a greater wealth of education?

The Congress Party is filled with devotees who complete their “Harijan service” by giving a handful of oil and a fistful of grinding powder. During its year in office, did the number of ancient-Tamil children attending school increase? Let the figures answer.

In 1935–36, 5,447 ancient-Tamil children received schooling. As a result of Congress labouring and administering for a full year, the number attending school in 1936–37 was 5,091. In a single year, 356 children of the excluded communities lost access to education. Is there any reason? Has any explanation been offered? No, laments the *Express*.

And during this splendid performance, Comrade Sivasunmugam, himself belonging to a depressed class, was Mayor of Madras. Honourable Munusami Pillai of the same class is Minister of Agriculture.

Let the Agriculture Minister observe the beauty of this harvest!

## Fear of Death

There is no joy and no education. Are the city’s people at least alive, wavering about like walking corpses? Even there, matters are worse. The mortality rate has risen above its former level. The previous average was 35.6 deaths; according to the 1936–37 figures under Congress rule, it rose to 39.7.

Glory indeed to the yellow ballot box! Auspicious indeed are the blessings it brought!

We have cited above only the defects mentioned by the *Express*, *The Hindu* and *Mithran*. For some reason, they have left many other ugly features unspoken.

“There are many other corrupt practices, but we do not wish to describe them,” says *Swadesamitran*, drawing a delicate curtain over them. We too do not wish to investigate those corrupt practices, because the people of Madras know them well.

## Where Are the Votes Now?

The friendship formed between the yellow ballot box and auspicious slogans! The bitterness between Congress and the education officer! Party divisions so public that the street laughed! Chapters of corruption published daily in *Dinamani*! The people know well the many other obscenities discussed throughout the town. They know them thoroughly and are ready to deliver their verdict.

But in Madras, the “yellow box” itself has disappeared. It is afraid to emerge! If it does appear, we can say with certainty that a lament will arise in the words we have all heard upon the stage:

“The yellow has turned black;
the fragrant marjoram has become bitter neem!”

*Viduthalai*, 10 June 1939

## Translator's Notes

The article uses the “yellow box,” Congress’s ballot-box colour in the period, as a recurring satirical image. Historical newspaper names and administrative terms are retained. The source’s term *pazhanthamizhar* is translated literally as “ancient-Tamil” people rather than silently replacing the period terminology with a modern category. The mortality figures are reproduced as printed, without independently correcting or reinterpreting their unit.
