## English Translation

A storm among those of the “mud huts”; a breeze among the “sacred-thread tradition”!

The tiger cried that it was being treated unjustly and obtained “justice” against the goat. The Brahmin community, which had constantly wailed that it was being wronged in education, has won against non-Brahmin society. With the assistance of law and in the name of justice, it has obtained a ruling from the Madras High Court that communal representation is legally invalid.

The fate that has befallen communal justice leaves the great people of the South standing in sorrow, writhing in distress.

Imagine a man climbing towards the summit with the sea below and a sheer rock face before him. He has found the hanging root of a banyan and is climbing joyfully when a knife suddenly flies in and severs it. What will happen? The Dravidian people lie stunned, fearing that their future may now be placed in that very condition.

Dravidian society, already floundering amid grief, sorrow, poverty and affliction, has had its one support irretrievably taken away by the Indian Constitution framed in Delhi.

Pain! Pain! Unbearable pain!

This spectacle was not produced by a sudden thought. Before and after the Indian Constitution was framed, when the case was filed, and now when victory has been obtained, Brahmins deliberately gathered, planned and performed many acts to create precisely such a result.

On one side, they kept their propaganda cannon roaring through the newspapers they controlled. Whenever they travelled to North India, they spoke about the matter everywhere. They cried that hatred of Brahmins had arisen in the South, even within the government and Congress. Brahmins occupying editorial chairs in North Indian newspapers poured out editorials condemning “Madras.” South Indian Brahmins who had assumed ministerial responsibility in the central government did not fail to perform their own “holy service.”

Petitions to Nehru, motions and supplementary questions in the legislatures, letters in newspapers, and cries from public platforms were all systematically raised.

At every place and every moment, they spoke in whatever manner suited the occasion: “Civil rights are being denied. Hatred is shown towards Brahmins. They are not admitted to colleges.”

Concerning the arrangements made in this manner by the South Indian Brahmin community, *Dinasari* writes:

> It is repeatedly claimed that the communal representation order has left one particular community, namely the Brahmins, without facilities for higher education. Wherever they go, they present this as a major grievance. They have conducted tremendous propaganda about it in the legislatures and in other parts of India. Even in the Indian Parliament, they speak heatedly in condemnation of the Madras government's order.

Lamenting that our South Indian friends in many newspapers constantly campaign as though some injustice is occurring in South India, and that their refrain is that “the Madras government denies Brahmins the very foundation of civil rights,” the paper asks: “We have gathered and supplied the government's statistics on this matter. Look at them and decide which community has received justice.”

If one examines the statistical table given by *Dinasari* on the final page of its July 31 outstation edition, the heart of the Dravidian community, already burning towards death, cannot fail to seethe. For our reader comrades, we have condensed those figures below. Examine them sharply, again and again.

These figures concern college admissions in 1949-50. We have also provided the total population of the communities living in the Madras Province. Using them, observe the places allotted in colleges to Brahmins and to everyone else.

Brahmins are not even three persons in every hundred in the Madras Province. To be precise, they constitute only 2.7 percent. Out of the province's population of roughly four and a half crores, “their” number is only thirteen lakhs!

A Brahmin community containing only thirteen lakh people, men and women together, makes more than four crore Dravidian people tremble and writhe in bewilderment!

Look at the advantages “they,” a population of thirteen lakhs, received in colleges:

| Course | Places for Brahmins |
|---|---:|
| Intermediate | 44.22 |
| B.A. and B.Com. | 7,190 |
| B.A. Honours | 318 |
| B.Sc. Honours | 318 |
| Medical | 442 |
| Engineering | 198 |

Now compare those figures with the places granted in colleges to approximately three and a half crore non-Brahmins:

| Course | Places for non-Brahmins |
|---|---:|
| Intermediate | 6,542 |
| B.A. and B.Com. | 7,273 |
| B.A. Honours | 206 |
| B.Sc. Honours | 206 |
| Medical | 827 |
| Engineering | 359 |

Have you seen it? Does your heart tremble? Bear it a little longer and continue examining the table. Look at the places received by the Adi-Dravidian people, who number eighty lakhs. Look at the total places received by Muslims, with a population of thirty-nine lakhs, and those granted to Christians, numbering nineteen lakhs. Look at them all!

Tears will fill the eyes. If the table is compared with even a little concern, the heart will pound and thoughts will become enraged. There are only thirteen lakh Brahmins, but what position do they occupy in the educational field that gives life to society? Alas, the answer can emerge only as tears.

This, they say, is “unjust” and “contrary to law.” The heirs of Chanakya have beaten their drums and won!

Two and a half persons in every hundred, and how much comfort and privilege they enjoy!

Ninety-seven in every hundred are Dravidian people, and how painful is their life!

The more one thinks, the more the heart laments; tears flow when one considers our ruined condition.

Yet in the name of “civil rights,” they have sent the system of communal representation to its grave, because convenience and opportunity stand on their side.

B.Sc. Honours is a course that makes students proficient in science. In it, 318 places go to a population of thirteen lakhs, while 206 places go to three and a half crore people!

A throne for the little fox; an empty hut for the lion!

What a grotesque spectacle! Yet these people have “sought justice” and won. They cried that they had not received “fairness” and obtained a judgment!

The judgment was delivered upon a provision of law. Those who brought the case relied upon a provision of the Indian Constitution, and those who gave the judgment used that same provision in arriving at their decision.

If we calculate how extensively “these people,” who went so far as to file suit and challenge the rulers, hold the country's institutions in their grip, still more painful details will emerge.

Even so, they cried out as though they were people who possessed nothing. Through the weight of their voice, they created trembling in the lives of those to whom this country belongs.

Their complaint is that they have not been given enough places in colleges.

These statistics show how far that claim is true. Nevertheless, “they” desire the expansion of their monopoly and discover the means to achieve it.

A new imperialism sets out in the name of “justice,” intending to make the Dravidian community, which stands bereft of life, disoriented and bewildered, into a servant for its purposes.

When one thinks of it, waves of pain strike one after another. All our thoughts stagger. Signs appear that the future of a disordered Tamil society may become a dark cave!

It is a painful spectacle, one that cannot be seen in any other country, an intolerable sight. A conspiracy succeeds in perpetuating superstition and ignorance in the lives of the workers who are the jewels of the country!

Honesty and justice, meanwhile, are made to turn their backs by “law.” On seeing this wretched condition, only the words of the revolutionary poet come to mind:

> Who made this country possess
> an unfailing revenue, offering up
> the green blood shed from sacrificed
> legs, hands and bodies? At this hour,
> all those people live as rats and rabbits!
> The man who rose in an age of deception
> now wears the guise of a tiger! Does he grant
> even a blade of grass's worth of respect to the public?

(*Dravida Nadu*, 8 August 1950)

## Translator's Notes

The article responds to the Madras High Court decision invalidating the Communal Government Order governing educational admissions. `மார்புநூல் மரபு` (“sacred-thread tradition”) is a polemical reference to Brahmins; `சாணக்கிய மரபினர்` invokes Chanakya as a figure of political cunning. The Intermediate-course figure appears in the corrected OCR as `44.22`; it may represent `4,422`, but the translation reproduces the visible source figure because the scan must be checked before correction. The Tamil source title contains OCR punctuation and spacing damage (`கு.டிசை7`); the display title uses the clearly recoverable reading `வெறுங்குடிசை!`, while the source block remains verbatim. The closing poem is by Bharathidasan, referred to here as the “revolutionary poet.”
