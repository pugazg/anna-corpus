## English Translation

Pitch darkness, darkness everywhere, has surrounded us. The whole world today lies trapped in the darkness of grief.

Knowledge laments. Literature sheds a flood of tears. Drama continues to weep. The very thought of discerning people has become motionless.

The scholar Bernard Shaw has died. The world of knowledge has grown dark.

He was a world genius, an incomparable dramatist, an unequalled literary creator, and a political sage. Yes, he was the representative of twentieth-century intellect. He was a mine of ideas, a treasure of knowledge, and a storehouse of truths.

Shaw was the sun of rationalism, the moon of reform, the breeze of innovation, and the messenger of a new world.

Hearing the news that Shaw had died, we lost our balance and our senses. Grief seized and shook both our hearts and bodies.

He was born in Dublin on 26 July 1856 to George Carr Shaw, a Protestant, and Mrs. Elizabeth Gurly. He had two siblings.

Shaw struggled in poverty during his youth and grew up subjected to many humiliations. Through his enthusiasm and unceasing labour, he became a world genius.

He worked for several publications as a critic of literature, drama, music, and politics. For some years, he also spoke on political questions from public platforms.

He married Mrs. Charlotte Payne-Townshend and led a happy married life. They had no children.

He wrote more than fifty plays. Though they employed structures in an ancient style and Greek and Roman methods, his plays always contained innovative ideas and the fragrance of renaissance.

The world awarded him the Nobel Prize in 1925. He arranged for it to be used for the development of Swedish literature and English art.

He spoke of the greatness of women and expressed golden socialist ideas. He always opposed capitalism and was a direct enemy of feudal privilege. He continually offered witty commentaries upon daily political developments, blending laughter with ideas that stimulated thought.

The great scholar Bernard Shaw lived in our time and walked the earth while we too walked it. Thus he brought us pride and gave a new honour to the age in which we live.

Shaw was a bell-tower that gave knowledge to the world, a lamp lit by rationalism, and a great treasure impossible to find by searching.

News reached us that he had died on 2 November 1950 at the age of ninety-four, and we were shocked. The anguish of losing this storehouse of knowledge, whose labour never weakened even in advanced age, has never left us. Though Shaw lived longer than a normal span and reached the summit of fame during his own lifetime before he died, we cannot bear his departure.

Shaw has died, but we have not forgotten him, and in truth he has not vanished from us. As long as this world exists, as long as people move through it, as long as clarity enters their thought, and as long as knowledge spreads its fragrance through that clarity, Shaw will remain. He is here with us.

May his fame rise ever higher!

(*Dravida Nadu*, 12-11-50)
