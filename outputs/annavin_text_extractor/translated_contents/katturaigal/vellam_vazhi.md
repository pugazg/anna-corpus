## English Translation

## A Sea of People at the DMK Conference

They paid no heed to the pouring rain. Barren ground became their “bed.” The people's enthusiasm captured the heart.

### Scenes from Lalgudi

It must have been one in the morning. A splendid moon was playing charmingly in the sky. Savouring the lovely sight as it scattered sandal-coloured light, I came outside.

At the place where, by day, I had heard thunderous applause and roaring voices, peace now reigned. Night is indeed a secret cave. Otherwise, where could the cries I heard and the scenes I saw during the day have gone into hiding? The spotless sky, the stars lying there like blue lotuses, and the moon swimming playfully captivated the eye.

I passed beyond the pavilion and came outside. “Good heavens, what an enormous pavilion!” I thought as I looked at its entrance. Enchanting lamps shone as though challenging the light in the sky. Amid them sat the departed hero Pandianar. Startled, I looked closely. It was a painting by the Balu Brothers of Madras, rendered with such beauty.

At the top of the facade, a beacon revolved and cast light on all four sides, telling distant travellers, “This is the Lalgudi conference pavilion!” A lighthouse stands by the sea to guide vessels tossed off course by waves and sharks in the deep. Likewise, the beacon above the conference pavilion seemed to call: “O people buffeted by the Aryan shark and the Vedic storm, forgetting life's happiness as you struggle, here is the path of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam!”

After that arresting sight, another scene startled me. I was bewildered: the same sight extended as far as the eye could reach. Wonder grazed through my heart as I asked what marvel this was.

No polished platform surrounded the pavilion. It was uneven open ground full of hollows and rises, without even grass to please the eye. Only here and there lay tiny mounds of sand, like beautiful Tamil epics surviving beyond Aryan outrages. Even at midnight they still released the heat absorbed from the noonday sun.

In that place, using their hands as pillows and the barren earth as a bed, not one or two but tens of thousands of people lay asleep. We have seen crowds moving and compared them to a flood. We have piled up exclamation marks while speaking of waves upon waves of human heads. But only that night did I see what it looks like when thousands sleep together in one place under night's rule.

Every one of them had come for the conference. Were the organizers wealthy estate-holders who could welcome them by saying, “Come! Here is your bedroom, and there is the bathing place”? No. They could not provide for everyone. And were these visitors people who would say they would attend only if all such comforts existed? No, no! It was their people and their movement. So, carefree beneath the sky as roof and the moon as guard, they spent the night on that barren ground.

Looking at them, I wondered: are these sleeping forms the people who made the conference pavilion tremble?

Literature's garden contains many descriptions of warriors in ancient camps. When they halted while returning home with cries of victory after defeating an enemy, they supposedly lay immersed in dreams of when they would see their beloveds. We have enjoyed those descriptions but never witnessed them. These warriors of the camp lie near an upstairs house; what dream fills them now? There can be only one. Is it not to turn that dream into reality that they have made the wilderness their bed? How splendid is the intensity of their commitment, and what kind of ideal can inspire such intensity!

The devotion and idealistic fervour of those who gathered for the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam conferences at Lalgudi in Tiruchirappalli district on 25 and 26 April 1953 cooled and delighted our entire mind.

Anyone who saw the conference pavilion would call it enormous. Yet it filled completely, leaving people standing without space even to sit. The flood overflowed: it filled and overflowed for lack of room. Those who had asked on the previous day, “Why such a huge pavilion?”, frowned during the conference and said, “The pavilion is not large enough!” Such was the crowd.

And the human flood that came on the first night to see the play *Kadhal Jothi*: goodness, words cannot contain it.

There are prosperous gentlemen in our country who satisfy themselves by writing: “A play indeed! What pointless work. Can principles be spread through play-acting?” and “We began condemning plays, and now their popularity has declined.” They should have witnessed this sight.

So many people came to see our play that the organizers, unable to manage the crowd, admitted everyone free of charge. The result became still more unmanageable, and the performance had to be stopped.

It was one in the morning when I came outside the pavilion and saw these bulls sleeping in the thorny waste.

The second day gave another opportunity to understand the enthusiastic hearts of Kazhagam comrades whose devotion to the cause made them think: “We shall eat if food is available, bathe when we perspire, use hard earth as our bed and our hands as pillows.”

After noon on the second day, heavy rain began unexpectedly. The pavilion could not withstand it. The rain bathed the people. Paying no heed to the downpour, many thousands remained and listened to the speeches. The rain did not cease, and the ground became all mud. The people did not hesitate or move from their places. Only after the General Secretary finished speaking did they disperse. Where can such enthusiasm be found? Where but among us!

Many thousands filled with enthusiasm and passion had assembled at the Lalgudi conference.

Before proceedings began on the morning of 25 April, conference leaders were brought in a great procession from Siruthaiyur. Mudikondan Munusamy sang the anthem of Dravida Nadu. The elderly reformer Muvalur Ramamirtham Ammaiyar then inaugurated the Social Reform Conference.

“I have spent fifty years in public service,” she advised. “Never in my life have I received an opportunity like this. I have not come here merely to speak. What will you young people do now? Enough speech. Set out to act!”

Ambil Dharmalingam welcomed those attending the reform conference. Kalaivanar N. S. Krishnan and his troupe then narrated the life of the late hero Pandianar through a meaningful *villu pattu* performance.

### Actor-Scholar K. R. Ramasamy

Actor-Scholar K. R. Ramasamy opened the conference art exhibition. He said:

“I opened an art exhibition here today. Last week Chief Minister Achariar opened an art exhibition in the great city of Tanjore. Achariar has always desired to occupy our place. Otherwise, did he not seize in the legislature the place that ought to belong to artists and become a minister?

“He opened an exhibition of temple statues. I am opening an exhibition of rationalism. Aryanism enslaved us through art, and through that same art we too must win.”

Ambil Dharmalingam proposed Comrade Ponnambalanar of Poovalur as president, and Comrades G. Parankusam, T. K. Thamaraiselvan and Sampath seconded the motion. Ponnambalanar presided and delivered a rare address. Tamil professor K. Anbazhagan of Pachaiyappa's College and Comrade N. V. Natarajan followed with excellent speeches.

At three in the afternoon, after movement comrades presented music, the conference reconvened. Comrade Thillai Villalan, B.A. (Honours), spoke on the development of the human mind; T. K. Thamaraiselvan on “Drama and Cinema”; Comrade Arunmozhi on “Social Reform”; and Comrade S. K. Sami of Kumbakonam and Comrade Sathyavani Muthu also addressed the gathering. Comrade R. Nedunchezhiyan then spoke on “Science and God.” After the president's closing remarks, the conference ended.

## DMK Conference

As Comrade Hanifa's song rang out, “Behold, the flag of liberation flies and calls!”, the second Tiruchirappalli District political conference of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam began on the second day.

Comrade K. A. Mathiazhagan, an officer of the headquarters organization, inaugurated the conference with a distinguished speech. Ambil Dharmalingam delivered the welcome address and proposed Comrade E. V. K. Sampath as president. Comrades Kannadasan, Ilamvazhuthi, M.A., B.L., R. Nedunchezhiyan and A. Ponnambalanar seconded the proposal. Sampath assumed the chair and gave a remarkable presidential address that seemed to rain ideas upon every mind.

### After the Interval

After the midday meal, Comrade K. R. Chellamuthu and several others sang movement songs. When the conference resumed at three, Comrade Govindasamy, M.L.A., spoke about the injustices of Congress rule. Comrades P. P. K. Raja Chidambaram, M.L.A., and Palaniyandi, M.L.A., followed.

### M. G. Ramachandran

Actor Comrade M. G. Ramachandran participated in the conference and declared that artists must have a place in political life.

“I have seen no movement other than the DMK attempt to improve artists' lives,” he said. “Many people ridicule and abuse us. But Anna alone respected us as human beings, recognized that we too possess a concern for public life, and came forward to give us a place.

“Recently, when film actors from northern India came to Madras to play cricket and raise money, Kalaivanar N. S. K. and I, with several others, demonstrated against it. Someone apparently told Anna that we had been arrested. He immediately wandered from place to place to discover whether it was true. Who else possesses such a disposition and affection? What is wrong in our regarding him as our leader?

“I have known Anna for about ten years, and he shows the same affection today as he did then. Though I belong to Kerala, I shall do everything I can for the intellectual work of the DMK under his leadership.”

### Sivaji Ganesan

When Comrade Sivaji Ganesan began speaking, he said that Anna had introduced him to the world.

“Through his path of affection, people like me have attained distinction today. The intellectual movement is our heart. For it, we are prepared to give our body, wealth and life. People like me are always ready to secure our rights and attain Dravida Nadu!” he declared passionately.

Comrade A. V. P. Asaithambi then spoke with enthusiasm, saying that the youth must be ready to attain Dravida Nadu. Comrade N. S. Ilango addressed agricultural problems with suitable statistics, followed by Comrade A. K. Velan.

At that point rain began to fall. It was a heavy downpour. As though competing with the human flood inside the shed, water itself swelled into a flood. There was mud everywhere and no possibility of sitting, yet the people did not hesitate.

The conference president announced, intending to resume after eight at night: “Let the rain stop first.”

“No, do not stop. Continue. We do not care about the rain!” the people shouted as they remained seated, unconcerned about the mud beneath them.

One special feature of the Lalgudi conference was the participation of a great many mothers. Thirty women volunteers under Comrade Ilamurugu Porkodi worked exclusively to assist them. Not one of those women feared the rain; they remained seated.

Seeing the people's enthusiasm, the president continued the proceedings, and resolutions were adopted. Comrade M. Karunanidhi then delivered an emotionally charged speech, followed by Comrade R. Nedunchezhiyan.

General Secretary Annadurai spoke about the two days' proceedings and the DMK's growth. After President Sampath's closing address, Comrade M. S. Mani, one of the conference secretaries, offered thanks, and the gathering ended pleasantly. Because of the rain, the play scheduled for that night did not take place.

At the Lalgudi conferences we saw the full extent of the people's enthusiasm for us. We witnessed how widely both discipline and enthusiasm had spread among the comrades.

People filled the pavilion beyond its capacity and overflowed. On one side stood the art exhibition, itself crowded. Beside it was a small market street containing bookshops, small stalls and more floods of people.

I witnessed all this on both days, and my heart marvelled. I heap congratulations upon them. May the service of the DMK's heroes grow! Long live the valiant people of Tiruchirappalli!

*(“I Saw and Returned”)*

*Seythi, 3 May 1953; Dravida Nadu*

## Translator's Notes

The main title appears with catalogue number `23.` in the scan; the number is not part of the title. `கறம்பு நிலம்` is hard, barren or uncultivated ground. `பாசறை வீரர்கள்` evokes warriors resting in a military camp and is applied to conference volunteers and attendees. `வில்லுப்பாட்டு` is a Tamil narrative performance using a bow-shaped musical instrument.

The OCR inconsistently gives the conference date once as `25-4-52`, while the opening says 25 and 26 April 1953 and the closing publication date is 3 May 1953.
