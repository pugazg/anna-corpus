35. 'தெனாலி' போய் வந்தோம்!

கவிராஜ் இராமசாமி செளத்ரீ என்பார், தெலுங்கு
நாட்டிலே, சிந்தையைக் கிளறிவிட்ட பெரியார். இங்கே
எப்படி, சுயமரியாதைக்காரர்கள், வைதீகத்தையும்
பார்ப்பனீயத்தையும் கண்டிக்கத் துவங்கினரோ; அதோ
போல, அங்கும் கிண்டலும் கேலியும் செய்யத் துவங்கிய
கவியரசர் அவர். அவர் உயிரோடிருந்தபோது, எவரெவர்
தூற்றினரோ அவர்கள் எல்லாரும் இன்றைய தினம் அவரது
பெருமையை உணரத் தலைப்பட்டிருக்கிறார்கள். மேல் சாதி,
கீழ்சாதி, உயர்ந்தவன் தாழ்ந்தவன், என்றெல்லாம்
பேசப்படுவதை எதிர்த்துப் போர் முழக்கம் செய்தவர் அவர்.
இப்போது, அவைகளைப் பற்றிப் பேசுவது எளிது! கக்கன்
கூடக் கர்ச்சிக்க முடியும்!! ஆனால், பதினைந்து, இருபது
ஆண்டுகளுக்கு முன்பென்றால், சனாதனிகள் சபிப்பர்;
வைதீகர்கள் வம்புக்கிழுப்பர்; வாலிபர்களில் பலரும் வசை
பொழிவர்; குறும்பர்கள் கோலெடுத்து வருவர்; வசதி
படைத்தவர்கள் ஆட்களை ஏவுவர். ஒரு கூட்டத்தை நடத்தி
விட்டு வருவதென்றால், இவ்வளவு எதிர்ப்புகளையும்
சமாளித்தபின்தான் முடியும்! அதிலும், ஆயிரமாயிரம்
காலமாக, மக்கள் மனதிலே ஊறிப்போன சாஸ்திர
சம்பிரதாயங்களையும், புராணக் கருத்துக்களையும்
பகுத்தறிவுக்கொவ்வாத சில சேதிகளையும் எடுத்துச் சொல்ல
முற்படும்போது தக்க ஆதாரங்களையும் தகுதியான
சான்றுகளையும் சொல்ல வேண்டுமன்றோ? இப்போது நாம்
எளிதில் 'வழிகாட்டிகள்' தீட்டிய நூல்களையும் விளக்கங்
களையும் வைத்துக் கொண்டு வாதாடக் கிளம்பலாம்!

புராணங்களைப் பயின்றார்; &தையை மனனம் செய்தார்.
அவைகளிலுள்ள கருத்துக்களையே தேடியெடுத்து,
அவைகள் மூலமே, முரண்பாடுகளையும் தேவை

கேட்டவர் கவிராஜ்! 'சூத்திர புராணம்' என்கிற நூலே

கண்டித்தனர்! ஏசினர்! தூற்றினர்! எங்கும் எதிர்ப்புக்
கிளம்பிற்று! அந்த எதிர்ப்புகளைக் கண்டு கவிராஜ்
மருளவில்லை. அதுதானே அறிவியக்கத்தின் சரிதத்தில்
ஏற்படுவதில்லை! எதிர்ப்புக் கிளம்பக் கிளம்ப, உற்சாகம்
அதிகமாயிற்று அவருக்கு. எதிர்த்தவர்களைக் கண்டிக்க,
வரிந்து கட்டி க்கொண்டு கிளம்பவில்லை; வசைபாடவில்லை;
சமுத்திரத்தில் உருட்டிவிடுவேன் எனச் சங்கநாதம்
செய்யவில்லை. மாறாக, கிண்டல் செய்தார்! பழைய
புராணங்களிலுள்ள வினோதங்களை எடுத்துச்சொல்லிக்
கிண்டல் செய்யலானார், சொல்லச் சொல்லச் சிரித்தனர்!
பிறகு சிந்திக்கவும் தொடங்கினர். வைதீகர்களைச்
சமாளிப்பதில், நகைச்சுவைக் கலந்த கேலியினைவிடச்
சரியான ஆயுதம் ஏது? அதைப் பயன்படுத்தினார்
கவிராஜ்!

அத்த மேதையினுடைய ஆண்டு விழாவை முன்னிட்டு,
சென்ற கிழமை, தெனாலிக்கு சி.என்.ஏ. சென்றிருந்தார்.

## English Translation

Kaviraj Ramaswamy Chowdary was a great man who stirred thought in the Telugu country. Just as the Self-Respecters here began to condemn orthodoxy and Brahminism, that poet-sovereign began to mock and ridicule them there. All those who abused him while he lived have today begun to recognise his greatness.

He sounded the battle cry against talk of upper caste and lower caste, superior and inferior people. It is easy to speak about these matters now. Even Kakkan can roar today! But fifteen or twenty years ago, Sanatanists would curse; the orthodox would provoke quarrels; many young men would pour abuse; troublemakers would arrive carrying sticks; and wealthy people would send hired men. One could return from conducting a meeting only after overcoming all these forms of opposition.

Moreover, when one attempted to expose scriptural customs and mythological ideas that had soaked into people's minds over thousands of years, along with certain claims incompatible with reason, one had to offer sound sources and worthy evidence. Today we can easily set out to argue with books and explanations prepared by our “guides.”

But then! Those who set out to condemn these things had to search for and discover the evidence themselves. Kaviraj studied the shastras, examined the Puranas and committed the Gita to memory. He drew out the ideas contained in those very works and used them to expose their contradictions and superfluities.

Just as Periyar asked in Tamil Nadu, “Who is a Shudra, sir? Why is he a Shudra?” Kaviraj asked the same in Andhra. He even wrote a work titled *Shudra Purana*.

Would the orthodox leave him alone? They hissed “Shoo, shoo!” They condemned him, reviled him and abused him. Opposition arose everywhere. Kaviraj did not lose heart on seeing it. Is that not what happens in the history of every intellectual movement? As opposition increased, so did his enthusiasm.

He did not gird himself and set out to denounce his opponents. He did not sing songs of abuse or blow a conch proclaiming that he would roll them into the ocean. Instead, he mocked them. He drew out the oddities in the old Puranas and ridiculed them. As he spoke, people laughed; then they began to think. What better weapon is there for dealing with the orthodox than ridicule mixed with humour? Kaviraj used it.

Last week, on the occasion of that thinker's anniversary celebration, C. N. A. went to Tenali.

Kanchi C. V. Rajagopal, R. Dharmalingam, MP, K. Rajaram, Ethiraj and other comrades of the organisation came with me. Members of the local Brahmin Sabha, who had seen advertisements announcing that “C. N. A. is coming to Tenali,” had spoken in fierce attack at a conference they held a week earlier.

Need one guess what they said? “An atheist is coming! A rakshasa is coming! The man who seeks to divide the country is coming! Members of the public, do not go!” Just as lovers of the past frighten people here, they raised the same voice there.

Speaking about this at the Kaviraj celebration, C. N. A. said:

“Some people apparently remarked, in connection with my visit, that a rakshasa was coming here. Here I am. Look at me and decide for yourselves whether I am a rakshasa. If those called rakshasas in the Puranas looked like me, no one would believe that the rakshasas persecuted the devas.

“The Sanatanists need not fear an ordinary man like me. Like them, I have the right to express the ideas in my mind, whether in Tamil Nadu or Andhra. They suspect and fear that their position may be lost and that everything they say may be understood as false. Otherwise, there would be no need for them to fear me and shout in this fashion.

“I shall tell them only one thing; please do not forget it. I come from Kanchipuram, filled with 108 Shiva temples and eighteen Vaishnava temples, a sacred centre praised and worshipped by devotees. If even I, living in that town of such glory and greatness, have changed in this way, devotees ought to think a little about the reason.”

Applause split the celebration hall. Is it not true? In what town is there no temple, no sound of drums? Yet if rationalists increase day by day from town to town despite all that, what does it mean?

Just as reactionaries here tell people all manner of false and malicious things about us and meet defeat, the crowd that assembled there showed that they had suffered the same defeat in Andhra. What respect! How many flower garlands! What affectionate addresses!

The journalists' meeting also revealed how many misconceptions about us had been spread there regarding Brahmins. Even if good news about the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam does not travel in every direction, lovers of the past remain keenly vigilant in spreading false ideas.

The Tenali Journalists' Association had arranged a tea reception. Mr Narasimhan of *The Hindu* had also been invited as a special guest. Narasimhan holds a responsible position at *The Hindu*. Hearing his name might make one imagine an elderly man, but he explained that he too had studied during the years when C. N. A. was in college.

At this meeting, it would be more accurate to say that C. N. A. placed before their eyes the newspapers that impose a blackout than merely to say that he spoke. He did not spare *The Hindu*. He explained how these papers sing the praises of those who have come to rule, suppress growing principles, and thereby inflict great harm upon democracy.

When the speech ended, the association president requested: “They will ask a few questions. Please answer them.” Those present asked C. N. A. a series of interesting questions. It truly became a fine forum for clarifying ideas. A young man opened the questioning.

**Question:** What is the DMK? What is its policy?

**C. N. A.:** The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is a democratic organisation that arose from the Dravidar Kazhagam. Its objective is to obtain Dravida Nadu.

**Question:** What is the difference between the Dravidar Kazhagam and the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam?

**C. N. A.:** I used the word “democratic.” Had you noticed it, you would not have asked this question. When I say that the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is a democratic organisation, it is evident without being stated that this quality is absent in the Dravidar Kazhagam. It is enough to understand this difference.

**Question:** The Dravidar Kazhagam hates Brahmins. Do you accept that position?

**C. N. A.:** Ask our friend Narasimhan here! *(Laughter.)* We are not people who hate anything. Because we favour certain systems, we oppose those contrary to them. We want the excellence of Dravidian civilisation to be made known. Because Aryanism and Brahminism obstruct that, we speak about them.

When I appealed in the High Court against the ban imposed upon my book *Arya Mayai*, the judge hearing the case himself said that Brahminism and a Brahmin were two different things. There are many non-Brahmins who follow Brahminical practices. Therefore, in labouring to establish a society without caste distinctions, we are compelled to confront Brahminism. That does not mean we are enemies of every Brahmin. To say so is false.

**Question:** It is believed here that your demand for Dravida Nadu is a plan to trap the Andhras under your control. Will you explain?

**C. N. A.:** Please change that idea immediately. The very purpose of demanding Dravida Nadu is to cut away the fetters of domination. We do not demand Dravida Nadu in order to join Tamil Nadu and Andhra and make them one.

By Dravida Nadu we mean a collective responsibility established by the four language regions of the South. In Dravida Nadu, Tamils will not be masters over the Andhras. Andhra will remain with the Andhras. In the same way, Tamils will rule Tamil Nadu, Malayalis Kerala and Kannadigas Karnataka. Dravida Nadu is the federation created by these four regions.

Though the four speak different languages, we believe they can exist in unity because all belong to the Dravidian land and the Dravidian people. History and ancient records support us in this. Moreover, under the present arrangement, all our governments have been made more degraded than ordinary municipalities, and a form of dictatorship is emerging.

**Question:** A movement demanding a Tamil state is said to be emerging. What is your opinion of it?

**C. N. A.:** Yes, that is a voice that has arisen recently. Looking at the political history of the person raising it, I think it is best to express no opinion at all.

Their faces themselves showed that everyone was pleased by the explanations. All insisted that he remain with them for another day or two and continue the conversation. C. N. A. said he would return at the end of April.

When we saw the organisers of the celebration, those who participated in it, those who asked questions and took leave at the Journalists' Association meeting, and the enthusiastic students, it seemed no surprise if a DMK organisation should soon arise there. Most of those who came were graduates, lawyers, outstanding young people, people with progressive ideas and people deeply dissatisfied with the conduct of the present government.

Welcome! Banquet! Affectionate addresses! Delight! As we witnessed all these, our confidence steadily grew that an opportunity for the organisation to take its first step there would soon arise. Even now, merely thinking of it makes the journey sweet, and confidence grows still more.

*(Report written by “Kettu Vandhon,” Dravida Nadu, 8 February 1959)*
