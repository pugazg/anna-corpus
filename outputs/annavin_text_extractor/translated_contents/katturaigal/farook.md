## English Translation

He was once a king!

Farouk ruled Egypt without equal or opposition.

A life of splendour!

Farouk's authority was limitless. Legislatures obeyed his pointing finger. Ministers were dolls he made dance.

Beauties! Dance and song! Finery! These were for Farouk.

Poverty, hunger, and starvation were for the people.

The country's "big men" played accompaniment for Farouk, and Farouk protected them.

The people stood helpless and bewildered.

From that bewilderment arose a force.

Within the soldiers' camp, a plan blossomed to abolish the king.

If the people were to live, the king who swam and played in their blood had to be driven out. The military leaders did not proclaim this aloud; they met and discussed it secretly.

Naguib, Nasser, Salem, and others rose. The army gave them its full support.

There was no terrible revolution, yet the task that would otherwise have required a terrible revolution was accomplished.

Farouk was driven out!

"The people will not lift even a little finger for you; you know that yourself. The army has given us the authority to remove you. If you wish to save your life, leave the country at once," Naguib's group told him.

Farouk understood that this was an order and that Naguib's group possessed the strength to enforce it.

Thinking it enough to surrender the crown and save his head, he left the country.

A newspaper has published a photograph of Farouk, bereft of crown, country, and splendid life, living as a "common man" in Italy. The man who was once a lord is going for a swim! The scene seems to demonstrate that all the authority he once possessed has been stripped away and that he has been reduced to a mere human being.

(*Dravida Nadu*, Pongal Special Issue, 55)
