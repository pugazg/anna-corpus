## English Translation

## 1. The Poona Battle Episode

“Gandhi is the God whom we see before our eyes!

“He alone is our guide!

“He alone is the sun of wisdom for benighted India!

“We shall never cross the line Gandhi draws!

“The wisdom of the loincloth-clad sage is wisdom indeed; what are all other things worth?

“Gandhi is our commander!

“We are his troops!

“The British lion cowers upon seeing the aged Gandhi!

“The world worships this noble man!

“He is an incarnation of God!”

From Achariyar down to the most recently enlisted volunteer, Congressmen moved through politics until now by worshipping Gandhi in this fashion, praising his special qualities and creating a Purana that made him God's incarnation. Whenever opposition arose in the country to Congress policy or programmes, they silenced and harassed the critics by asking: “When Gandhi himself says so, when the Mahatma speaks thus, can anyone deny it? Can these people oppose him? Does Gandhi not know? Have they learned what he does not know?”

“Gandhi has spoken; Gandhi has ordered it; it is Gandhi's command.” When socialists questioned them, Congressmen threatened: “Listen, impatient socialist! Behave as Gandhi says, or receive the curse!”

However devoted we may be to a leader, may we treat his speech as scripture and disregard democracy? Those who wanted their proper rights under democratic practice asked whether it was right to crush Nariman, overthrow Khare and destroy Subhas. They were told: “Can mercy be shown to Gandhi's opponents? Is there another scripture above Gandhi's word? He is our leader; his word is our law. We must not cross the line he draws.”

Rationalists observed that Gandhi admired and fostered the ancient *varnashrama* order and that Brahminism thereby pushed the country's great communities into an abyss. When they asked whether this was proper, Congressmen answered: “Fie, scoundrel! If Gandhi favours *varnashrama*, it must be right. He has studied the Gita, tasted the sweetness of Rama's name, lived in an ashram and become a heroic renouncer. We must obey his spoken word.”

When critics said that nations practising nonviolence still fought one another, and that nonviolence might belong to spiritual life but was unsuitable for politics, Congressmen replied: “Ignoramus! You do not understand its wonder, but he does. Nonviolence looks like a tender shoot yet possesses the strength of steel. It is a new weapon for the world. Even a thousand roaring cannon cannot show their faces before our beloved Gandhi's nonviolence. The British empire, built and ruled with swords, shields, bombs, equipment, forts, ships and aircraft, trembles and loses its balance before it. No power in the world exceeds nonviolence. Gandhi's word of truth is grace not only for India but for the whole world.”

Because propaganda first made ordinary people trust Gandhi, they were bound. Congressmen then accomplished whatever they wished by repeatedly invoking Gandhi's command. The “Mahatma” served them until they became ministers. And afterward?

They themselves refused to travel along the guide's path. The sun of wisdom gave them no illumination. They erased his line with their feet, and his wisdom became mere speech.

Periyar's speeches and newspaper editorials alone are enough to show it. Gandhi said Rajaji considered him mad about nonviolence. Achariyar spoke of the pain involved in abandoning the man who had guided them for twenty years. Ghaffar Khan said he was resigning from the Working Committee because he could not associate with those who rejected nonviolence. Together these statements explain that Achariyar's Delhi resolution produced a conflict on a fundamental matter between Gandhism and Congress leaders.

If doubt remains, listen to every Congress leader loudly insist that one must not imagine a breach has arisen between Gandhi and them, that they have abandoned him, or that Gandhi has left Congress. Their very denials make the split within Congress and Gandhism's defeat unmistakable. There is no need to sing a hymn of wifely chastity. What actually happened?

Gandhi's doctrine of nonviolence opened the British government's eyes wide. He preached it to the point of saying that country and home might even be yielded to Hitler provided no blood was shed.

Achariyar immediately began declaring: “Gandhi alone owns the copyright on nonviolence; we are abandoning it.” He discarded it in Delhi and buried it in Poona.

Arguing for his party at Poona, Sri Rajagopalachari shattered the doctrine: “What is this grand nonviolence? Must it be adopted intact merely because the Mahatma said so? Nonviolence is an ideal, certainly a noble one. But is Congress an association for propagating an ideal? Congress exists to govern the country.”

There lies the key. The aim is to rule. If nonviolence assists that aim, retain it, call its preacher the Mahatma and promote it among the people. But if it becomes an obstacle when an opportunity to govern arrives, smash it and tell whoever stands beside it, even the Mahatma, to move aside.

That is Achariyar's position. The Machiavelli of Mambalam will surrender anything to fulfil his heart's desire. Manu said the same, and Chanakya taught it: do not ask whether Chandragupta is right or wrong; hold only the aim of obtaining a great kingdom and disregard everything else. What if the person in the way is an uncle, brother-in-law or provincial ruler who once cuddled you on his lap and recruited an army for you? Look at the emerald crown glittering there.

Achariyar attacks Gandhi by asking why the Mahatma who preached prohibition supplied liquor to a worker, and why the Karachi Congress, even while speaking of nonviolence, passed a resolution seeking Indians' right to bear arms. It sounds as though Achariyar has read bound volumes of *Kudi Arasu* and now repeats their contents. Great men who once threw dirt at Periyar and noble men who roared at him must answer: if criticism of nonviolence deserved mud and stones, will you now place that mud upon Achariyar's head for his Poona speech? Think; ask your hearts.

At Poona, Aryanism tripped Gandhi. Whatever Aryanism desires, it will destroy and torture anyone who obstructs it, whether mother, father, son or daughter.

At Poona, Aryanism defeated Wardha and Achariyar overthrew Gandhi. But the forts Achariyar must capture are neither Wardha nor Simla. One is Malabar Hill in Bombay and the other Erode. Both are commanded by leaders who have made resistance to Aryanism their life's objective: Mr Jinnah and Periyar Ramasamy. The numberless heroes guarding these two forts grow day by day. Moreover, some are present even among the troops Achariyar has assembled.

## The Congress Manner

When people asked why the world advanced scientifically while India alone returned to antiquity, Congressmen angrily replied: “Are you some great genius? Do you know what the Mahatma does not? Do you understand the truth discovered by that sixty-year-old sage, that half-naked beauty who drinks goat's milk and communes with his inner soul? You criticize Gandhi and contradict the Mahatma. What shall we call the madness in your head?” The result was palm leaves, palm jaggery, coarse *khadi*, the spinning wheel retrieved from the loft, the spindle and the bullock cart.

People eagerly asked why socialism, a lofty principle gaining respect, influence and popular support throughout the world, should not be adopted as policy here as well. The surviving source breaks off at this point and resumes repeated earlier material about Gandhi's authority, *varnashrama* and nonviolence.

## Can Nonviolence Work?

Critics asked whether endlessly repeating “nonviolence” could accomplish anything. How could domestic conflict or foreign invasion be stopped through it? Before violence, force and armies, spirituality and nonviolence could no more endure than cotton before fire, butter before heat, a deer before a tiger or a maiden before a drunken brute. One could not say the world had developed through nonviolence. Although Jesus proclaimed it a marvellous doctrine, armed warriors protected his gospels. Japan and China, both associated with Buddhist teaching, did not adopt nonviolence.

Yet, after invoking Gandhi's command to accomplish their purposes and using the Mahatma until they obtained ministries, Congressmen refused his path. The sun of wisdom did not enlighten them. They dismissed his wisdom as a mere ideal unsuitable for practice.

The incarnation of God says one thing; his millions of disciples say another. The commander's order was rejected by his troops. A majority of the Congress Working Committee refused to follow the noble man worshipped by the world. Before the Gandhi who had seemed brave enough to make the British lion cower, his own relative staged a revolt.

Gandhi called nonviolence as precious as his life. Congressmen had prospered by repeatedly proclaiming it a new philosophy sent to redeem the world and the command of a Gandhian incarnation greater than those of Rama and Krishna. Yet Achariyar, heralded as Gandhi's right hand, replica and relative, dug a deep pit and buried that very doctrine.

The service Achariyar began in Delhi was completed in Poona. Gandhi's own article and the statements of others show that this was no ordinary matter.

While preaching surrender even to Hitler rather than war, Gandhi and the Congress negotiated with Britain for self-rule, power to govern and a role in assisting the war. The government began to ask: if an agreement is reached, what help could Congress provide Britain? Under Gandhi's nonviolence, what could you do in war? Gandhi says even surrender to Hitler is acceptable so long as there is no battle. It would therefore be improper to place people led by such a man in office during wartime.

Congress expected the world and the British to exclaim: “What a magnificent doctrine! Even as bombs spill entrails there, Gandhi speaks of nonviolence here. Surely he is an incarnate being.” Instead, the government said it would not entrust the country to such fanatics.

## No Further Evidence Is Needed

No evidence beyond the Poona episode is needed to prove that Achariyar represents Aryanism. No witness beyond Gandhi is required to illustrate the condition of Dravidians trapped in Aryanism, squeezed dry and left as refuse.

Gandhi for votes; Gandhi to deceive the town; Gandhi to suppress other parties; Gandhi to subdue noble awakenings and secure comforts for the worthless; Gandhi to obtain ministerial office. But the instant Gandhi became an obstacle to that office, Gandhi and nonviolence were finished. What became of twenty years of preaching?

Nationalist comrades who raged when people like us said nonviolence was unsuitable for politics, friends who bristled that we insulted Gandhi, did you hear what Achariyar said at Poona?

Without distortion or damage, Achariyar repeated everything we had said. Gandhi, who preached nonviolence, overthrew Pattabhi's opponent during the previous contest; now Achariyar, pursuing his own objective, dares overthrow Gandhi. His required forts remain Malabar Hill and Erode, guarded by Jinnah and Periyar Ramasamy, and their forces keep growing.

What would happen if people within Achariyar's own camp began saying: “Yes, what Achariyar says today is exactly what Periyar Ramasamy said then. Achariyar incited us to revile and attack Periyar, but now, to test his own enterprise, he dares overthrow Gandhi himself”?

Defeat through victory is no contradiction. History contains many such events. The same fate awaits Achariyar.

*Viduthalai*, 30 July 1940.
