## English Translation

### Image 1

30. A New Explanation of “Heir”!

They say a camel has a certain nature. If more and more loads are placed upon it until it can bear no more, the camel stubbornly lies down. The clever camel-driver then removes the last item loaded upon it. As soon as it is removed, the camel thinks, “Good, good! They have taken down the burden placed on us; we have won,” rises, and prepares for the journey. At times this camel's nature can be seen even among good people.

### Image 2

When good people are subjected to troubles that cause mental pain, they eventually reach a point at which they say they can bear no more. Those who know the camel's subtle nature then remove one of the troubles they imposed. With that removal, agitation diminishes in the good person's mind, happiness blossoms, and he may return to obediently doing as told.

Periyar has decided that we possess this camel-like nature and is trying the same trick.

He startled us by announcing that he was going to marry: one bundle of distress upon the mind.

He explained that he was going to marry Maniammai. We were still more startled: another burden upon the mind.

He said this marriage arrangement would prepare a plan for the party's future. Our bewilderment grew: another load upon the mind.

He said, “I am making Maniammai my heir and the movement's heir.” We were alarmed: another great burden upon the mind.

### Image 3

When asked why he was making her heir, he said he trusted no one else in the party. Our hearts reached the point of bursting; an unbearable burden had been loaded upon the mind.

The camel lay down because the load was too great.

With tears, we began to condemn him. We said we could not work with him and that he had made us targets for the world's ridicule and blame.

Thinking that the camel had lain down but the journey must continue, the cunning driver removes one bundle from its back.

In the same manner, Periyar removes one of the burdens he cast upon our hearts, to see whether the camel will rise, and tries the experiment for amusement.

Under cover of condemning the reporters' mischievous propensity for matchmaking, he asks: “When did I say I would establish Maniammai as heir to the Kazhagam?”

I forgot: he does not ask it himself. A *Viduthalai* editorial says it.

On 5 July 1949, *Viduthalai* writes: “E.V.R. nowhere mentioned appointing an heir to the Dravidar Kazhagam.”

### Image 4

He thinks the camel will rise if this is said.

By beginning this marriage talk, he has tormented our minds and placed us in a condition where even opponents must offer us consolation.

An unsuitable marriage!

An explanation for the marriage!

A calamity within the explanation!

A disastrous method of succession!

A strange argument!

A whip-lash across the whole party!

After making us stagger under these many burdens, he now tries saying through *Viduthalai* that he never mentioned making Maniammai heir to the party.

From the day talk of an heir first arose, Periyar has hesitated and has been unable to speak about this appointment all at once, courageously, intelligibly, and with adequate reason.

For he knows that no rational person and no one attached to self-respect can accept his arrangements as right.

### Image 5

That is why he has spoken about the matter little by little, like giving bitter medicine hidden inside a banana.

He thought anything would be digested if administered in the proper manner.

But this time we refused to swallow his arrangement.

He is angry, and we are sorrowful. Still, concealing his anger, he attempts a small feat through *Viduthalai*: he says he never proposed making Maniammai heir to the party.

Is this true? Periyar's Coimbatore Conference speech appeared in *Viduthalai* on 1 June 1949. Look at it once, and it will become clear whether the view published in *Viduthalai* on 5 July 1949 is right or wrong.

On 5 July, *Viduthalai* writes, “E.V.R. nowhere mentioned appointing an heir to the Dravidar Kazhagam.”

Now look at the 1 June issue of the same *Viduthalai*:

“I must arrange a successor after me both for the party and for my personal property.”

### Image 6

“I am going to do this soon,” Periyar said at the Coimbatore Conference. He clearly said there would be an heir not only for him but for the party.

Periyar publicly and plainly said at the conference that he intended to appoint an heir to the party, and *Viduthalai* itself published it. Yet the same *Viduthalai*, frightened by the opposition of Kazhagam comrades and thinking it useful to attempt some verbal feat, printed the blatant lie on 5 July that he had never mentioned establishing an heir to the Kazhagam.

Periyar has trained Dravidar Kazhagam comrades to possess the intelligence to closely identify every inconsistency in Puranas written long ago. Why he forgot that the same intelligence would be applied to matters printed in *Viduthalai*, we do not know.

Periyar reportedly told reporters: “I seek an heir for myself, not for the movement. Nor have I appointed Maniammai. All that is a conspiracy by enemies.”

We remind Periyar of his Coimbatore Conference speech.

### Image 7

It was Periyar who said: “I must arrange a successor after me both for the party and for my personal property. I am going to do it soon.”

To support that speech, Periyar wrote an explanation in *Viduthalai* on 19 June 1949.

Now he performs the feat of claiming he sought an heir only for his personal property and not for the movement. Yet in an article signed by him on 19 June, he wrote:

“In movement affairs, my health no longer permits me to wander as I have until now. I have found no one whom I trust to assume responsibility as I have. Therefore I am deeply concerned to establish someone as my heir and make arrangements through that person before I depart. I spoke about this with C.R.”

What clearer statement is needed that the arrangement of an heir concerned the movement's work?

This passage comes from an article written by Periyar. Its title is “Explanation.”

What is the attempt to conceal this explanation except the feat of one who understands the camel's nature?

### Image 8

In the same explanation, he makes still clearer what purpose the heir he seeks is to serve:

“If I say I have found no one trustworthy, no one should be angry. Who among the full-time comrades is prepared to accept responsibility for service to our movement and surrender himself entirely? Who is there? Who has there been?”

Not merely to serve the movement, but to assume responsibility.

What can assuming responsibility mean other than providing leadership?

He found that there was not and had never been even one person fit to assume this responsibility. He discovered that Maniammai possessed that distinction, rejoiced, and, even if by garlanding her and making her his wife, asks her to assume responsibility for the movement.

Is this all? Seeking support for this explanation, in the statement issued on 28 June 1949 Periyar wrote:

“I must think and act with great concern and attachment regarding myself, the movement, the conduct of the movement, and the need for the movement to continue at least to some extent after me.”

### Image 9

He did not write merely of an heir to personal property. His concern was the movement, its conduct, and its future.

Today Periyar tries through *Viduthalai* to evade the matter by saying it was not an arrangement connected with the movement. Yet in his own statement he wrote:

“If I begin considering everything enemies will say, the work of safeguarding the movement will be completely ruined.”

If it concerned only an heir to personal property, why would the phrase “safeguarding the movement” occur?

The truth is that through the Tiruvannamalai meeting, the Coimbatore Conference, the explanation, and the statement, Periyar gradually told the movement that he intended to crown Maniammai and prepared it accordingly. The movement now blocks this improper arrangement, and therefore hesitation arises.

By their rain of condemnation, Kazhagam comrades have placed Periyar in the position of having to deny so soon what he had stated so openly.

Now, frightened by the opposition, Periyar says he is not making Maniammai heir to the party and never held such an intention.

### Image 10

Let us accept that claim as true and suppose he acts accordingly. Can we then say that the anguish of Kazhagam comrades and the disgrace and blame brought upon the movement will disappear?

Never!

If the leader of a reform movement enters an unsuitable marriage, one directly contrary to the principles he preached, a marriage only a man who regards women as worms would dare undertake, how can we be party to it? How can our hearts permit it? How can the world accept it? After allowing this marriage, if we travel from town to town loudly preaching reform, who will respect or accept us?

People will taunt us: “Preachers to the town! Your leader married at the age of seventy-two. You lacked the capacity, means, courage, and honesty to condemn it, and now you come chattering about reform!”

How can we face the people? Their eyes will become question marks. Their ridicule will send our movement to the grave. Merely saying Maniammai was not made heir to the movement is insufficient.

### Image 11

The disgrace and blame will not thereby be wiped away.

He has said: “I trust none of you. None of you is capable of leading the movement.” Will the wound caused by those words heal? How could it?

What can we achieve in the world while bearing the label, “The very leader of the movement does not trust us”?

Anyone will say, “Periyar himself does not trust these people; how can we trust their words?” How can that humiliation be erased?

After considering these things, if Periyar truly values the movement's growth and future and the feelings of its comrades, he must abandon his marriage arrangement completely. He must not presume to make any arrangement for the movement's future; he must abandon that entire intention.

He must withdraw his statement that he trusts no one in the movement, spoken as though movement comrades were logs of wood or dancing puppets.

### Image 12

Only then can we accept his leadership.

He himself has created circumstances of that severity.

Yours affectionately,

Annadurai

*Dravida Nadu*, 10 July 1949

## Translator's Notes

வார்சு is the source's Tamilized form of the English-derived “waris,” meaning heir or successor. “Heir” is used where property is central and “successor” where movement leadership is central.

The camel analogy structures the entire essay: retracting only the claim of succession to the party is compared to removing the final bundle while leaving all other burdens on the camel.

The quotations and dates from *Viduthalai* are preserved as printed: 1 June, 19 June, 28 June, and 5 July 1949. `C.R.` remains abbreviated because the source does not expand it.

The source's harsh language about Periyar's proposed marriage and Maniammai is translated as Anna's historical argument without modernization or endorsement.

Image 12 was present among the scans but entirely absent from the canonical OCR Markdown. Its conclusion, signature, and publication date were manually transcribed from the scan before bilingual assembly.
