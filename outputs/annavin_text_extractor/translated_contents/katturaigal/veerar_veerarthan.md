## English Translation

General Cariappa is now serving as India’s representative in Australia. He did not rise through the political world. Trained in the military camp, he became an army officer, saw many battlefields, and won distinction. He is therefore neither accustomed to nor likely to trust styles of speech that circle around a subject or seek to soften hearts. He says openly what is in his mind. That too is necessary.

Vast sums are spent on ambassadors enthroned in foreign countries. After paying out crores, people imagine that India will obtain appropriate respect, rights, concessions, and opportunities in the world. Most ambassadors sink into the customs of the countries where they sit in splendour. They smile, conduct dinners and entertainments, live ceremonially, and waste the poor person’s tax money. They sing Pandit Nehru’s praise, but we see no other useful work from them.

They cannot even discover or prevent people from cheating us, swallowing our money, and giving us worn-out motorcars, broken machinery, and worm-eaten wheat. The painful reports that emerge from time to time also bring shame. Anyone with sufficient audacity scoops up our money in this way.

Cariappa does not belong to that crowd. The work he now performs is new to him. Yet the fact that he is not a trained diplomat has produced something beneficial.

Australia is large and rich but sparsely populated. Cattle graze across many thousands of acres. One landed magnate owns such an expanse, and only a small group serving him lives there. The large cities contain many people, but most of Australia is thinly populated.

In many countries of the world, populations have grown so large that crowding and lack of the means of life have become severe. Australia’s population, by contrast, is not proportionate to its size and wealth. Australia belongs to the group that preaches that the world is one and one country must help another. Yet it fiercely prevents outsiders from settling there.

By “outsiders,” it means non-white people—those it calls “coloured.” Australia has imposed severe restrictions on their immigration, claiming that if they settle there the country will be ruined, living standards will fall, and civilization will perish.

Australia belongs to the British Commonwealth. India too has a place in it and possesses equal rights. Australia is never stingy in praising Pandit Nehru and never hesitates to come to Delhi to discuss world problems. This month Australian External Affairs Minister Casey met and conversed with Pandit Nehru, expressed wonder at his extraordinary influence, and praised him. Yet Pandit Nehru’s “coloured people” must not settle in Australia, lest its standards decline. Casey denies racism reaches such a degree, even while readily accepting hospitality and exchanging courtesies in Delhi.

In Casey’s Australia, there is no place for us, the “coloured.” Yet this same Casey formerly governed Bengal as Governor. We permitted it!

Delicate speech will not end racial arrogance. Courage is required to state the truth plainly and declare that there can be no fellowship with those who practise it.

Diplomats will not acquire that courage. They speak the language of the ruling gentlemen, dance, entertain elegant ladies, and imagine that proceeding in this fashion will diminish racism.

General Cariappa is not of that disposition. Unable to tolerate Australia’s racial arrogance, he has said, “Come what may,” and delivered a fitting rebuke.

Touring Queensland, Cariappa saw a prosperous region with abundant natural wealth lying unused because there were too few people. He considered how much greater its prosperity would be if enough people lived there to use that wealth. He also reflected that in India and certain other places the population had grown excessive and become a heavy burden upon nature.

Why should those people not settle here? Is not the British Commonwealth said to be one family? Here there is too little population, while the Indian subcontinent has reached a crisis of overcrowding. Why should settlement not be allowed? Australia’s racially arrogant declaration that it would give no place to “coloured people” came to mind. Cariappa is a soldier, and he delivered the proper rebuke.

He reportedly asked journalists: “You are deeply concerned only with whether skin is black or white. You do not care to learn what lies in the mind. There are only nine million of you here. Do you know how helpful it would be to this country’s prosperity if people from abroad settled here?”

We praise Cariappa for courageously condemning racial arrogance, exposing how irrational it is, and startling the Australian government. Unless someone denounces it this way, the madness will not lessen and the arrogance will not disappear.

It is cruel and foolish for one country to subjugate another, dominate it, and live by exploitation. But to display racial arrogance and thereby allow natural wealth itself to go to waste is sheer folly. Cariappa’s condemnation of the practice of pretending friendship with India while treating Indians with contempt cannot fail to cause agitation within the Australian government.

Australian newspapers have criticized Cariappa’s speech. *The Age* has evaded the issue by saying he spoke without understanding Australian policy. Those possessed by racial arrogance will condemn him fiercely. Diplomatic circles will say Cariappa should not have spoken so bluntly. Even the Indian government may think his speech ought not to have been so explicit.

But Cariappa’s courageous words will make many countries think. Though one cannot say racism will disappear because of them, his speech will certainly help many people recognize how uncivilized racial arrogance is.

Cariappa has also warned that the racism displayed by Australia within the British Commonwealth might even help drive India and Pakistan into the embrace of communism.

Mr Casey speaks ceaselessly: “Communism destroys democracy. Democratic countries, come, let us establish a camp!” Yet anyone may naturally conclude that Soviet cooperation is better than the friendship of an Australia that speaks of democracy while degrading people as “coloured.”

Cariappa spoke as though striking racial arrogance on the head. It may not have been diplomatic speech, but it was filled with courage. A hero is a hero!

*Dravida Nadu*, news item, 27 June 1954.
