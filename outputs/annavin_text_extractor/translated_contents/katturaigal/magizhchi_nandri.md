## English Translation

You have won and given us victory. Great joy, and heartfelt thanks!

The righteous struggle was conducted beautifully. No one failed to rejoice inwardly at the disciplined ranks of courageous people. With an enthusiasm that even an enemy would praise, the heroes entered the field, completed their task and sent us the joyful news of victory. Thanks, thanks!

When I called, “Come, heroes!” poets in the camps sang battle lays, the ranks set forth, songs arose in the small towns, great towns resounded, and drumbeats were heard everywhere. These were my younger brothers, endowed with golden character and hearts of steel.

If this army is defeated, what army can win? Victory was obtained; courage blossomed. The sacred land takes pride in seeing the power of its children, precious as rare medicine. Dravidam, worn down though it is, will not now disappear. Dravidians have risen together with matchless courage and the resolve to labour without rest. Hearing the heroic drum of the liberation army, they have become certain that chains will now be severed, self-government flourish, degradation disappear and a new life be born.

“In fallen Tamil Nadu there must be an awakening; in the body whose force is broken there must be strength,” asked the poet. On the first of August Tamil Nadu displayed the form of that awakening, a sight that astonished enemies and delighted friends.

Before the sun had blinked, the bulls set forth. They hailed the organization; elders praised them; the drum sounded and the first contingent departed. They completed with distinction the task they had undertaken.

DMK comrades received the announcement of the August righteous struggle only on 29 July. There were just two days. I was somewhat afraid. There was no time for comrades to assemble, confer and devise a course. Their hearts were filled with resolve to enter the field, but I wondered whether there was enough time to gird themselves, fix the hour of entry and gather the ranks. I even became anxious.

But the ranks I saw in the August struggle seemed to look at me and ask:

“Did you think we were sleeping, Anna? Are we not people waiting for the drum to sound? Are we not keeping the sword out of its sheath so that, the moment the day is fixed for attacking Dravidam’s enemies, we may enter the field without delay? Did you grow afraid because you did not know that Dravidam’s liberation army remains in formation at every moment?

“Behold the forces! Hear the victorious drum! Here is victory. Give us another task now. Shall we attack the flank or leap upon the centre? In which direction must we go to strike Dravidam’s enemies? Where shall we attack? Why hesitate? Danger? To us it is play. Death? It is the great medicine by which the motherland will raise its head. Fear nothing, O my Anna! A single call is enough and many forces will assemble. Have no doubt. Here is victory!”

With this cry the heroes of the righteous struggle cast me into an ocean of joy.

A pot of tar and a short stick were the weapons. Are these the weapons known to the Tamil? No, no. The form of the enemy was fit for no weapon more dignified than those contemptible implements: a wooden board painted yellow, bearing Hindi letters. Should it be attacked with a spear? Was bow and arrow required? Was a torpedo needed, or a cannon? Of course not. That is why they set out saying tar would suffice, carrying a pot of tar and a short stick.

The Hindi letters were erased. The Tamil began to walk with head held high. The heroes declared to the world their resolve that they would not hesitate to protect the honour of the motherland. Everywhere rose the cry, “Long live Tamil! Long live Tamil!” and the great proclamation, “The Dravidian land belongs to the Dravidians!” Everywhere was celebration, joy in every direction, every face blossoming. They drove away the one with eyes bereft of light, crying, “Go! Go! Go!” They called to the one with radiant eyes, “Come! Come! Come!” Dravidam’s sacred lamp gave the light of victory, and the motherland swelled with joy.

We expected bodies might fall at the railway stations. Yes, is this not the rule of warped minds that can cause bodies to fall even within prison? The central and provincial governments jointly protect and administer the railway stations. To enter them openly before many witnesses, cry “Down with Hindi,” and erase Hindi lettering with tar plainly means: “We condemn the government’s course and blacken its face.” When this was done, the arrogant devotees of domination who imagined that none could oppose them would naturally display their guns; that is their nature.

We therefore thought that on the first of August the blood of our comrades would mix with the black tar, and only then would the two-coloured mud be applied over the Hindi letters. We announced this. The reply we received was not a frightened gasp of “Is that so?” It was the manly answer, “What of it?” The contingents set forth, the righteous struggle occurred, victory was won, and the truth became clear that authority lacked even the courage to touch the heroes, except at Cuddalore.

No gunfire, no baton charge, no prison. We saw no face contorted in anger. The law refused to awaken; our plan was fulfilled. The Hindi letters were erased and the face of northern imperialism blackened. The railway stations lay without anyone even able to ask why. The police appeared to be saying, “Why should we stop the liberation army of the motherland when it performs heroic service?”

In strict discipline, tar was applied at railway stations throughout Tamil Nadu. The government stood open-mouthed, afraid to unleash repression. That fear was produced by the courage your eyes emitted. Otherwise, is this a government that does not know how to be cruel? Even the dead Hitler would have had to seek lessons from these men. Those who came to rule are people practised in many cruelties. Yet even they stood bewildered that day. Such was the power with which the comrades stood in the field; such was the heroic feeling displayed. It is a good sign for the liberation of a people.

Not only men were prepared to accept any hardship. As though saying, “Mothers too are here,” many women participated in this righteous struggle. I regard it as a matter of pride. Although they knew a government of demonic character was in power, many mothers entered the field, moved by the conviction that the courage of womanhood must serve the liberation of the motherland. Their contribution is precious. Everyone blesses them: “May their ardour grow!”

This struggle demonstrated the enthusiasm and power gathered within Dravidian society. It has become clear that, however urgently the call for national liberation is issued, contingents are ready to depart.

The August struggle also made clear that even an autocrat is frightened when he hears the voice of awakening and the heroic cry. He fears to release repression; those in power are struck by anxiety over the form the consequences may take.

Beyond all these things, another matter worthy of pride has become evident, and it gives me immeasurable joy: dignity and discipline have blossomed and spread their fragrance in Dravidam.

The great majority who took part in the August struggle were youths, at the age when they would stamp upon and kill a snake that came to bite them. They were not people disposed to deliberate deeply upon method and consequence. Yet the dignity they all displayed and the discipline they observed that day carry me to a city of delight.

Columns of the army came in procession. Police watched. There were no batons and no signs of official pomp. Yet the comrades did not lose even a millet seed’s measure of dignity. Not a trace appeared of wild shouting, brutality or rowdy mischief. When a procession is angry, its ranks may scatter, righteousness may vanish, sudden desire for destruction may arise, and enthusiasm for improper acts may appear. We have seen this in many movements.

Our comrades, however, kept their thought solely upon the task they had undertaken and displayed the dignity and discipline required of a heroic army, a liberation army. I greatly praise that rare quality. A war conducted in a disgusting fashion is the work of barbarians. We are soldiers of a righteous struggle who maintain dignity while fighting for our birthright and the liberation of the motherland. Dignity and discipline must abound among us.

Everyone who participated in the August struggle possessed this quality. It gives me the inner courage to design newer, broader plans of struggle. For this distinctive excellence I offer my gratitude separately.

Another important feature of the August struggle delighted all of us as greatly as it bewildered our opponents. I refer to the Dravidar Kazhagam and the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam taking part together. In many towns members of the two organizations worked jointly. A condition arose that could split the enemy’s heart.

Dravidam has risen in valour. More than that, Dravidam’s enemies understood that, although differences and disagreements exist, Dravidians stand united when the time comes to attack the common enemy. This can produce a new fear in the enemy’s heart and a new swelling of pride in Dravidian hearts. New strength enters the liberation army. This comradeship is a quality everyone must cherish and develop. The August struggle made that clear to us as well.

The commanders understand the great truth that no split should be created in the ranks of the great army that stands always ready to wage war for the motherland’s liberation.

Those who came to rule had hearts inhabited by the fear that, if they unleashed repression, the result might become disastrous.

This is the Dravidam of today, the picture given by the August struggle, the new epic of Dravidam. Yes, let us understand it and rejoice inwardly. With new confidence and new enthusiasm, let us devise the means for Dravidam’s liberation. Let us show our strength to our opponents and make Dravidam a separate country.

Heroic comrades, you conducted the righteous struggle and won in a manner capable of giving this resolve even to a person of ordinary intelligence and patriotism. I convey my gratitude to every one of you.

No one imagines that future rulers will stand with hands bound and mouths covered as they did this time. Again and again, until the country is liberated, we must conduct many struggles more severe than this one. Then prison doors will open and swallow Dravidian lions, batons will strike, and guns will speak. The August struggle has given us an opportunity to gain the resolve that we shall not be shaken even by these things.

Many years ago we made a decision: the Dravidian land belongs to the Dravidians. Under Periyar’s leadership we proclaimed, “Dravidian land if we attain it; the cremation ground if we do not.” There he is in the field. He stood with manliness in the August struggle. We shall not forget the path he showed or the lesson he gave. We shall surely attain the Dravidian land.

The Dravidian land belongs to the Dravidians.

Annadurai

*Dravida Nadu*, 3 August 1952
