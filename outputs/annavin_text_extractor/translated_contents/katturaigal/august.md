## English Translation

Periyar has announced to the country his proclamations for the August agitation.

He has also stated that the "August" observed every year is going to be intense this year.

The conduct of the champions of Hindi domination can only make every heart seethe.

It appears that the North has decided that Dravidam is merely a camp of cowards.

The leaders of the North have concluded that those who "carry" Congress rule and claim to find pleasure in it are the ones who command the people's minds.

Delhi has acquired an unshakable confidence that, because of Kamaraj's rule, Dravidam has attained complete satisfaction and joy and peace prevail here; and that, in such circumstances, even if not merely Hindi but Sanskrit itself were made a compulsory subject, there would be no one to ask why or to conduct an opposition.

Kamaraj gently says, "Do not be hasty over the Hindi question." Acharya asks what qualification Hindi has at all. T.T.K. advises that a period of twenty-five years be granted. *Mithran* thunderously condemns the conduct of the Hindi fanatics. *Kalki* and *Vikatan*, the Communists and the Praja party, have poured out bitterness and anger over the introduction of Hindi, each in a different manner. Nevertheless, Delhi has declared categorically that matters will proceed according to the plan already made and that Hindi must ascend the throne within fifteen years; it has set earnestly to work toward that end.

Although blows against Hindi have arisen from many quarters, one feels like asking, "What is this arrogant conduct?"

At a superficial glance, one can see Acharya, Kamaraj, T.T.K., Subramaniam, *Mithran*, and others condemning the imposition of Hindi, even to an exhilarating extent. But all these objections concern how Hindi is to be introduced, not why Hindi should be introduced! They accept the three principles of one India, one national language for it, and Hindi as that language. Their dispute resembles thieves discussing among themselves whether to breach a wall or remove the door bolt, whether to enter at midnight or at daybreak, whether to bind a cloth over the eyes or blacken the body, and then falling into a minor quarrel over method. Their contention is only about the methods: twenty years or fifteen, an optional subject or a compulsory one, by inducement or by intimidation. It is not about the fundamental objective; most certainly not.

The Dravidian movement alone opposes the fundamental objective. A separate government for Dravidam is our policy. Therefore, it fights by explaining that talk of one India and one national language for it is merely a means of establishing northern domination.

The Dravidian movement alone can possess the honesty and courage to say that the imposition of Hindi is a conspiracy to bury the idea of a separate Dravidian state, establish northern domination, and enthrone Aryanism.

If told, "It is not compulsory; we are waiting," the Congressmen will be satisfied.

If someone speaks honeyed words saying, "We shall place the Tamil language upon our heads and celebrate it," the Tamil scholars will be supremely satisfied.

None of them will ask, "Why Hindi?" They will not oppose Hindi.

Only the Dravidian movement, which has understood that the imposition of Hindi is a conspiracy for northern rule, can conduct a true and substantial agitation, and it has done so.

When we read Periyar's announcement about the August agitation, the days on which all of us took part and worked in the anti-Hindi agitation, went to prison, and joyfully awakened the Tamil land in an incomparable way stand upon the screen of memory and give us delight.

The plan for the August agitation may perhaps have been published by the time this issue appears.

There are thousands upon thousands eager to learn that plan and do what is required. Questions such as whether everyone should be in a single camp or remain within their respective organizations may stand to one side; yet there is no heart that does not rejoice to see the August agitation blossom, and no Dravidian who does not wish it to spread the fragrance of victory.

(*Seithi*, 17-7-9௦, *Dravida Nadu*)
