## English Translation

If it can be said that the Government has done anything of note for the people of the country, that one thing is prohibition.

The liquor that destroys the intellect has now been abolished by law. Everyone with a sense of humanity must cooperate if this measure is to succeed fully. Yet the happiness that sensible people feel at the disappearance of liquor is dimmed by another cause. Those who had been engaged in tapping toddy until prohibition came into force now have no work, and whole families have sunk into suffering. For generations upon generations, they had all pursued that same occupation; they know no other trade. Until now, they had never had any need to learn another occupation. Consequently, when prohibition arrived, they lost their work and found themselves untrained for any other employment. The responsibility for finding them work belongs to the Government. A large share of the Rs. 18½ crores that the Government had hitherto received as excise revenue came from their labour. Before prohibition, the Government maintained an excise department at great expense in order to supervise the sale of liquor properly. Although a department is necessary to administer prohibition, there will be no need to spend as much upon it as was formerly spent upon the excise department. The Government should therefore spend the money previously used for that purpose on the welfare of these toddy tappers.

Nor would teaching the weaving trade to toddy-tapping workers now be a measure capable of yielding quick results. It would take at least a year. Where is there prosperity in weaving today? Yarn is not available in the required quantity. Even the yarn that is available is in a condition that cannot be woven on their looms. Nor can buyers be found for the cloth already woven.

In Kanchipuram, Salem, Arani, Tiruvathipuram, Kumbakonam, Madurai, and every other important centre of handloom weaving, are not the cries of the weavers being heard without cease? In such circumstances, what benefit would result from toddy-tapping workers learning to weave?

The Government follows a policy of saying that the inam lands of those who live in ease without the least bodily labour, oblivious to the condition of the world and disposed to neglect those who toil and struggle, must not be touched lest sin be incurred. Yet no obstacle can stand in the way of granting, as inam, at least enough land for a livelihood to the toddy-tapping workers who have been eking out their daily existence. Moreover, the Government is inclined to pay many crores of rupees to protect zamindars who are affected by the abolition of zamindari but have never known what hunger means. It can therefore also discover and put into effect means by which the workers reduced to a pitiable state by prohibition may conduct their daily lives with at least some regularity. Alternatively, just as compensation is paid to zamindars, compensation can be paid to the workers who tapped toddy. Their former wages could be calculated, and the wages they would have received over ten years could be paid as compensation so that their condition might be improved. Unless the Government accepts these proposals and provides some such remedy, their pitiable condition cannot be changed. A little attention from the Government in this sphere would therefore be an appropriate means of relieving the hardship of these workers.

They are good labourers with strong physiques. If they are engaged in agriculture and given the necessary training, a way for them to benefit will open immediately. It will provide them with a livelihood and increase production. By introducing cooperative farming, using machinery, and adopting modern methods, the yield can be made to multiply. Cooperative farms may be established in every taluk of the province, and these unemployed workers may be given work. By increasing food production in this way, the food crisis now confronting the country can be reduced. Such employment would become available not only to toddy-tapping workers but also to many others who are without work.

Nor is this an occupation that will yield results only after a long time. It can produce good results as expected within four or five months.

It cannot be said that suitable land is unavailable here. There is an abundance of uncultivated land in our country, suitable for cultivation but lying unattended. Today, only cactus and thorn grow there. Reclaiming it and turning it into productive farms is the foremost duty of those who have entered the legislature in the people's name. Ministers need not worry about where the money required for this is to come from. They can reduce their salaries in accordance with the command once laid down by Gandhi and use the resulting savings as capital for this undertaking. If they possess courage and an understanding of the purposes for which public money ought to be spent, taking a little from the property of religious establishments and a small portion of the wealth lying idle in temples would itself be sufficient. Let us see what they do!

Land was given to Congress sacrificers. Most of them were already engaged in one occupation or another; they were not like the toddy tappers who, once their work disappeared, stood bewildered without knowing any other kind of employment. When land grants were made to such people, it cannot be wrong to grant land as inam to these workers, who are under the compulsion of having no alternative but to receive such a grant. Nor is it an act contrary to the nation. Feeding the hungry is the first task that must be performed.

If the rulers' claim that they govern solely to do good for the people is true, let them demonstrate it by putting this worthy measure into practice.

7 November 1948

## Translator's Notes

The OCR image set contains two works. “வேலை கொடுங்கள்” ends on Image 4 with the printed date 7.11.1948; “நம்பிக்கை இல்லை!” begins immediately below it and continues through Images 5–18. This canonical document therefore retains only the complete first work.

The phrase printed near the beginning of Image 3 appears as “வாழ் வளிக்கும்; அதிகரிக்கும்.” Its wording is defective or incomplete in the scan. The Tamil source preserves that visible wording; the English rendering follows the surrounding proposal and reads it conservatively as providing a livelihood and increasing production.

“கலால் இலாக்கா” is rendered as “excise department”; “இனாம் நிலம்” as “inam land” or a revenue-free land grant; and “தம்பிரான்கள்” refers here to religious establishments or their custodians, not literally to “lords.”
