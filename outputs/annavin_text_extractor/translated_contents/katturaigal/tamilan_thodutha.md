## English Translation

Tamil Nadu lay in darkness. Tamil leaders quarrelled among themselves. The minds of the great Tamil people had nearly grown numb, and thought about Tamil culture lay scorched. Choosing that very moment, one group devised a plan to eliminate Tamil too. They acted not merely with courage but with confidence that their design must surely succeed in a fallen Tamil Nadu.

They thought: “The Tamil has lost many rights, yet we do not even see him tremble at the loss. What will he do if his language too is pushed into degradation? He lies inactive and without thought. Even if a cyclone is unleashed to destroy classical Tamil, he will do nothing. Should longing arise, he will remain idle saying, ‘What can be done? Until now there were good days for Tamil; perhaps there will be none hereafter.’”

Thus they prepared to enthrone another language.

They said they would make India a country with one language.

They declared that language to be Hindi.

They ordered that Tamils must study it as a compulsory subject.

They dared elevate another language in this way while Tamil existed because Tamil Nadu was in disorder and Tamil hearts had broken. At that time they also possessed the authority to issue the order. They sat as rulers; Achariar was Premier.

Election brought victory upon victory. Various political parties fell without being able to stand against them. Wherever one looked, Congress alone had won. They beat the victory drum in eight provinces. Achariar became Premier of Madras, and whatever he laid down became law. Since the country had supported them to this extent, they imagined they could now do anything and succeed. They introduced compulsory Hindi.

The scheme struck not the back but the pure heart of sleeping Tamil Nadu like a whip. Tamil Nadu sprang up like a lion emerging with its mane bristling.

Those who had said, “Whatever else is lost, it is enough if our language remains. Tamil gives us sweetness, rights and encouragement, and will secure us a high place in the world forum,” were stunned by the order making not Tamil but an unworthy Hindi compulsory in Tamil Nadu.

Many had said, “We are duty-bound to support Congress in the election, for it is a fortress of fearless heroes conducting a righteous struggle to end foreign domination. For us Tamils, who value life as straw when rights are at stake, this Congress is the proper companion.” They now seethed:

“What is this? Congress dares take away the greatest of all rights, the right to cherish one's mother tongue. Is this why we protected, honoured and nourished it? It is like the bull we raised goring us in the chest. If Tamils do not stop this plan to ruin classical Tamil, how can Tamil endure? Without Tamil, where are the Tamils?”

Many had sincerely believed that selfish people, caste fanatics and defenders of *varna-ashrama* within Tamil Nadu would use Congress to expand their domination. In every country, selfish people try to exploit a movement working for liberation and sometimes succeed at the beginning, though not until the end. They thought the same would happen here: such people might seize a few offices from Tamils, but could do nothing more; no selfish person's deceit could destroy Tamil culture.

Even these believers groaned when they saw a grave being dug for the language. Shame and sorrow combined to drill into their minds.

Ascetics, people who delighted in Tamil's antiquity and excellence through *venpa* and *akaval*, and many who had imagined that a breeze of rights was blowing across Tamil Nadu were awakened in a new way by the compulsory-Hindi scheme.

There was agitation and fury, but no clarity about what to do or how to stop it. There was bewilderment before an unexpected attack.

Tearing apart this curtain of confusion came a laugh, not a joyful laugh but a mocking one. Everyone turned for a moment toward the direction from which it arose. The voice sounded familiar from long hearing. They sank again into thought.

“Are you bewildered?”

“No... I am merely thinking.”

“Only after the horse has been stolen?”

“Who are you, speaking mockingly?”

“You perceive mockery only in my speech. What do you see in the actions of others?”

“Action? Is that an action? It is a plot to destroy classical Tamil.”

“Yes. Why are you surprised? Why imagine that something impossible has occurred? Will mud smell like sandalwood? Does unripe fruit possess flavour? Can classical Tamil flourish under Aryan rule? Were you not the one who welcomed that rule? Did it not ascend the throne with your support?”

“Yes, but did I support it for this?”

“No. Your pure heart held some other good intention, brother. But did I not warn you beforehand what those rulers would attempt once they obtained your support? I told you Aryan rule would arise, Tamil life would fade, rights would be lost and only the northern language would prosper.”

“Yes...”

“Let what has passed remain past. Wipe your eyes. There is a way to protect Tamil. Do not fear. Look, this way.”

They looked. The leader of the Tamils stood with his white beard moving in the wind and a walking stick in hand. They saw him, and the storm broke. The Tamil began war, a war unlike any fought elsewhere in the world: a language war.

A new formation emerged. Saffron-clad ascetics stood beside sturdy young men. Women came with small children at their waists. Scholars stood beside school students. Former Congress members joined those who had remained outside parties. Many people of many kinds came.

“Bharathiyar” stroked his curled moustache and swore an oath. Maraimalai Adigal spoke in a sweet voice of song, “I shall not fail to protect my Tamil.” Eelathu Adigal, wearing coarse khadi and a piercing gaze, leapt into the righteous struggle. Dr Dharmambal declared, “Medicine before; now the protection of Tamil,” and came forth after assembling a women's force. The “Revolutionary Poet” worked with poetry as his weapon, declaring that he would uproot the villains' scheme.

Many, very many, from diverse fields gathered from every direction, more and more. The battle line grew strong, stood beside the Tamil leader, and Tamil triumphed.

My friend Ilanchezhiyan has portrayed this history in this book in a manner that inspires feeling and provides both sweetness and benefit. This war was the principal cause of today's Tamil awakening. The book is therefore the “Awakening Chapter” in the great epic of the Tamils.

I believe the enthusiasm this chapter creates in its readers will give Tamils the means to win the other portions of that great epic as well. Through this book, our friend Ilanchezhiyan has rendered good service to Tamils and the Tamil language. My commendation.

May this book be present in every Tamil home, infuse feeling into every heart, and give people the power to wage the righteous struggle that will sever the chains fastened upon this land.

It will, certainly! The author has not merely given an explanatory account of the war waged by the Tamil. He comes bearing a sword of war. There is therefore no need to say that his good service will bear good fruit. This work will contribute greatly to the flourishing of Tamil Nadu.

Long live Tamil!
