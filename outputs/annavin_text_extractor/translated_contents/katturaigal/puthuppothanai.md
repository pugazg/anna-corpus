## English Translation

"Hindi is the language of the majority of the people. Therefore, it is the national language, and you must learn it."

This is the answer northerners give to those who ask, "Why Hindi?"

"We, the majority of the people, speak it. Therefore, it is India's common language. You must consequently learn it," they say rapidly, desire mingling with fanaticism in their wish to spread Hindi throughout India.

The Congress "honourables" and national dignitaries here, who regard every northern word as treasure and every northern decree as a command, insist daily:

> Hindi is going to become the language of government. Therefore, knowledge of Hindi will be essential for obtaining government employment in the future. We must not miss the opportunity. Everyone must therefore learn Hindi.

Wherever they go, they offer this as the reason they compel schoolchildren to study it.

When we see these two reasons, the pain in our hearts increases.

The northerners deliberately plan to spread Hindi. People here who do not understand that plan frighten others by saying they will otherwise lose employment. We have continued to explain this.

Kamarajs and Bhaktavatsalams never fail to condemn us when we describe the northerner's lust for domination and the efforts of people here who unknowingly labour to spread Hindi.

Now someone speaks about the reasons we have presented:

> Hatred of Hindi is widespread in South India. In North India, supporters of Hindi crudely insist, "It is the language of the majority and is therefore the national language; you must learn it." That is why opposition in South India increases more and more.

> We repeatedly emphasize that Hindi is going to become the government's language and that those who do not learn it will be unable to obtain government posts. This should not be done.

The northerners insist crudely and stubbornly. We overemphasize the matter here. This should not happen.

The man who offered this teaching is no ordinary person. He is the Governor of this province.

In a recent speech to the Nilgiris District Hindi Prachar Sabha, Governor Bhavnagar uttered these words. Hatred is spreading, he said. If its growth is to be prevented, the present reasons offered for spreading Hindi must no longer be repeated, for they will only arouse greater anger. In teaching that a "new method" must therefore be adopted, he spoke as follows:

> We must learn Hindi for national unity. If India's national unity is to be strengthened, we must study Hindi. Hindi must be learned voluntarily. It is necessary to learn Hindi in order to understand North Indians and maintain close contact with them.

Thus he offered a conciliatory teaching: Hindi is necessary for national unity and for understanding and maintaining contact with North Indians. The Governor of this province now says this to us with affection.

Until now, northerners and our own people offered two reasons:

It is the language of the majority, so you too must learn it.

It will become the government's language, so you may otherwise lose employment. Everyone must therefore learn it.

When we heard these arguments, we asked: "How curious! Merely because everyone there speaks it, why must the little parrots and lovely children here be tormented now? Why must they be compelled to learn it?"

The national dignitaries answered us with ridicule.

Now Bhavnagar speaks. The Governor of this province says that the reasons thus far advanced by the Hindi advocates are wrong, crude, and mistaken.

He offers a new teaching: "Hindi must be learned in order to maintain contact with North Indians."

This new teaching reveals how far their position on the matter has retreated.

He describes the arguments advanced until now as crudity and a mistaken course. He says so not from hatred of Hindi but while offering a new teaching out of a desire that it should spread.

"It is necessary to learn Hindi in order to associate with North Indians," says the Governor.

Very well, Hindi may be necessary for such association. But who is to associate with them? Little children? The general public? Every person in the country?

If one travels from here to the North, it is useful to understand Hindi. But why must Hindi therefore be imposed upon young pupils? Those who need to travel north and associate with people there are neither little children nor all students.

Nor must those who wish to associate with and understand them necessarily study Hindi.

Even so, because his desire has not diminished, Bhavnagar has offered this teaching. Along with it, however, he expressed an idea that the ministry under him must understand:

> Hindi must be learned voluntarily.

He says Hindi must be learned by choice, without directly saying that it must not be made compulsory.

One must not say that because the majority speaks it, people here too must learn Hindi.

One must not insist that Hindi should be learned because it will be necessary for obtaining employment.

The northerner's crude course and the claims of people here are both wrong.

Hindi must be learned voluntarily.

With such words, he has offered his teaching about Hindi. Though it includes the conciliatory phrase that Hindi is necessary for association with North India, Bhavnagar's statement demonstrates how far Hindi fanaticism has now retreated.

We have no objection if adults who wish to learn Hindi do so.

But we cannot tolerate or endure its deliberate imposition upon students here under the cloak of "nationalism."

The Governor has spoken with this understanding. He is the head of this province. He himself says and explains that Hindi fanaticism exists in the North; that what the ministers here say about Hindi is wrong; and that it must be learned voluntarily, without compulsion.

These are the words of the country's Governor. Will the government accept them, or will it, as always, continue its futile game?

*Dravida Nadu*, 9 July 1950.
