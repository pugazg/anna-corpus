## English Translation

Dear American friends! Intellectuals who go about calling me a man seized by Russian madness!

Know this: Russia is laughing at us.

Russia has made fools of us all.

It has advanced very far, while we have fallen behind.

Our condition has become such that we must feel ashamed and bow our heads.

Russia has exposed our disgrace to the world.

We climbed to the summit of a mountain and preached to Russia that no people in the world possessed better morality than we did.

That time has passed beyond the mountain. Today we must hide in shame at the sight of Russia.

Bernard Shaw said something like this to Americans many years ago. It is a statement that ought to be addressed not merely to America but to the whole world. Yes, Russia does laugh at all of us. How could it refrain from laughing?

Would we not clap and laugh at a man with eyes who races towards a pit? Would we not mock one who wanders in search of unripe fruit while ripe fruit is available? Could one avoid laughing at a man who permits another to carry away his butter and then stands elsewhere begging for ghee?

It is in this manner that Russia laughs.

Russia laughs upon seeing one human exploit another in the name of civilisation while the exploited person fails to understand the trick, because exploitation as an occupation has been abolished there.

"We abolished it, regained our lives, and demonstrated it to the world." Yet exploitation continues everywhere else. Seeing that people can still live in such a manner, Russia laughs.

As Bernard Shaw says elsewhere, we all act like a prisoner who, unaware that the prison doors stand open, tries to escape by sawing through the window bars with pieces of tin. What else can Russia do upon seeing our efforts but laugh?

The tyranny of the Tsar, the excesses of landlords, the lustful arrogance of the privileged, the domination of priests, Rasputin's deceit, poverty, ignorance, disease, fear, and countless other shackles bound, tormented, and tortured those people. Their lives hung in the balance.

One effort, one powerful effort. With no fear for life, firm attachment to principle, and a desire to behold the land of their ideal, those heroes, who were not even heroes then but the fallen people of Russia, rose with courage. They split the ranks of the forces that came to suppress and destroy them. They reduced the fortresses of cruelty to dust. The shackles shattered into fragments, and freedom was obtained.

In a single effort, they abolished a cruelty that had tortured them for generations and created a new world. That is why the Russian people laugh at those who remain without thought or action, at those who conduct a lifeless existence, and at those who circle along a worn path for someone else's benefit like cattle harnessed to an oil press.

They are heroes. They overthrew one of the world's cruellest governments. They were not satisfied merely to overthrow it, nor did they perform worship at the feet of a new master. They renewed society.

The people who conducted the November Revolution destroyed in a single stroke religious domination, domination that created social division, military domination, Rasputin's domination, the Tsar's domination, and the domination of the nobility, and attained a new life.

The day cowards became heroes. The day slaves gained freedom. The day Russia became the Soviet land. The day white became red. The day of revolution: November 7.

A magnificent victory for those people.

Prosperity entered their parched lives. They transformed a desert into a flower garden. They did more than seize power from the Tsar, kill the Tsar, and change the system of royal rule. They placed government with the people, the country with the people, and the country's wealth with the people. They created labour according to ability, provision according to need, a condition without the degradation of want or insufficiency, and a social order in which the class distinction between the idle rich and the poor had been abolished. It was an incomparable revolution.

Its distinction was not merely that slavery disappeared. Strength, knowledge, and courage that had until then remained hidden and dim emerged from among the people. Within fifteen years they carried Russia to a progressive condition that could not have been attained even by a century of labour.

They piled into mountains the coal that had lain without miners. They used the steel that had lain without smelters, the fields without cultivators, the minerals without seekers, and natural power without anyone to direct it, accumulating the benefits.

They prevented those benefits from becoming wine cups in palaces, jewels in a king's crown, offerings to a guru, or articles of pleasure for an exploiter. They established a system of government in which the people laboured and the people enjoyed the fruit.

In some countries revolutions have occurred against the cruelty of religion, against a king, or against a feudal lord. In Russia they demonstrated a successful and incomparable revolution fought simultaneously against all three. Why would the heroes who leapt through the fire of sacrifice not laugh at others who suffer through lives spent drowned in worry, with hands folded in helplessness, worshipping with joined palms, or colliding blindly and dying without knowing a way forward?

Laugh, Russia! Laugh, laugh!

When human deceit dims human life, and people hear temple bells ring for Maheswara to brighten that darkened life, clap your hands and laugh.

When people cannot identify conspirators, seek out astrologers, listen to their tales of "fate," fall down, and worship, laugh until your sides ache.

When elders in the town declare that the Lord of the world ordained property for a few and labour for everyone, laugh. Laugh in a loud voice.

Your laughter possesses the power to awaken those sleeping in the world. It has the strength to turn a destitute person into a soldier. Your laughter is the sound of a great drum that rouses people to fight for their rights.

Laugh, Russia! Clap and laugh at us as we wander in search of "supreme wisdom," unable to prevent decay.

17-11-1946
