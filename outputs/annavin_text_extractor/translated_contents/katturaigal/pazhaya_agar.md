## English Translation

Titles! Offices! Should foreigners bestow them upon the descendants of those who ruled the land? Can this be accepted? They will grant the titles, and the heroic sons of Mother India will accept them! What a disgrace! "Sir," "Pur," "Dewan," "Bahadur": those foreigners will bestow them, and descendants of ruling lineages will receive them with every tooth displayed! Better to hang from the gallows!

Some lumps of flesh wander about after receiving titles from the white fanatics who remain in this country only to damage it. Thinking of them produces both anger and pity! They try to subdue us by displaying titles. They hold out offices and belittle us. Throw away the titles! Drive out the foreigners!

This is an old record, so today it reaches our ears only faintly.

But in those days, when the great-mouthed Congress propagandists delivered this sermon, their listeners' ears must have gone deaf! The heroic fury they displayed must have lit up the eyes of those who saw them!

That was how they spoke. They stormed around against the system of granting titles, poured down like a waterfall, and showered fire like a volcano.

The title "Sir" was a serpent that would bite us.

"Bahadur" was poison that would destroy us.

"Rao Bahadur" was a noose that would swing us from a tree.

These were the meanings they assigned to titles. Anyone who turns the pages of the old dictionary today knows this well.

Under British rule, titles of this kind were customarily granted each year. That was what they condemned.

Those who cast away such titles were praised as national martyrs.

Those who mocked the titles were accepted as patriots.

Those who attacked the recipients were themselves decorated with new titles such as "summer thunder," "stabbing blade," and "spark of fire."

They considered national service to consist precisely in abusing and denouncing the recipients of such titles.

The Congressmen's old dictionary is now closed and sleeping in a corner. That is why their tongues today turn in every direction! But the people's attention has not yet shifted.

We do not say this to argue that anyone should cling to titles and offices.

While we remained within the parent organisation and worked without weariness, Salem happened. It was through the service rendered by that "Salem" that we could grow into a mighty force in the people's forum.

Congress raised its howl even before Salem acted. But do you know what those virtuous men who raised it have done? That is the painful joke.

Now, it seems, they intend to restore the system of granting honorific titles and offices! Delhi has supplied this news.

The old accusation was that the British used titles and offices to draw the wealthy of our country into their spider's web.

But when we examine the reason these people now offer, we feel that they have not the slightest regard for their old dictionary.

We have not forgotten what it contained, nor has the country. What we cannot understand is whether they themselves are pretending to forget it.

It seems wealthy people now refuse requests to donate to public causes. To unlock the door of compassion in their hearts, they apparently require the key called a title!

These people call it a key to open the iron hearts of the rich. The wealthy regard that same title as a device for filling their safes and as a curtain that conceals the improper methods used for that purpose.

These rulers will give them titles! They in turn will give money to public causes!

What a marvellous method of raising funds for public work!

They introduce and praise the very scheme they once denounced. But the people turn the pages of the old dictionary and laugh.

They regard this as one more disorder of a regime in its final days. The Congressmen who came to power should at least keep their old dictionary in mind when they act.

The denunciations they hurled at titles and offices still echo. If they ignore them and speak and act however they please, only one title will remain for them: "double-tongued men"! Their rule will pass and chaos will embrace them, but that handsome title alone will never disappear.

Without the slightest shame, he says it openly!

Yet he praises Section 144 for having rendered good service in maintaining peace among the people.

He does not trust the people!

He does not even consider the Mahatma's service great! Nor does their sacrifice appear significant to him!

We place our trust in the prohibition of Section 144.

That, it seems, is what established peace!

A prohibitory law is praised under the rule of those who won independence by defying many prohibitions.

The lathi charge is the friend of today's rulers.

Under today's government, prison has become an academy that grows every day.

When we remember that he has said this too, we do not know whether to weep or laugh!

Their final hour has arrived. As abdication draws nearer, they enact laws of this kind.

They are preparing now to prevent their corruption from being exposed in the next election.

Accusing newspapers of being poison merchants, the real poison merchants seated in this government seek to hide!

The poison merchants in this government speak poisonous brokerage and conduct disastrous bargains! The country will not endure this; good people will not accept it!

*Dravida Nadu, 16 September 1951*

## Translator's Notes

The article deliberately quotes and then reverses Congress's pre-independence rhetoric about colonial titles. The transition on image 4 into Section 144 and attacks on the press lacks its antecedent in the surviving source; it is translated in place without inventing the missing subject.
