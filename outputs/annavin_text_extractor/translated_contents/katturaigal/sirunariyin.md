## English Translation

“This is our country, the golden land of our birth. Can our wish to regain its government, establish good rule and bring it many benefits not come to pass? Must it not?

“You seized our country when we were unprepared and now claim ownership over property to which you have no manner of right. Are we expected to watch this evil again and again and experience supreme bliss? Educated and unlettered alike, you have entrusted the responsibility of ruling your land to others and lain for many years caressing their feet. Have you no sense of honour? Has that quality faded away?”

The liberated nations speak with mingled mockery and ridicule, making us lower our eyes to the ground in shame. Rulers, has none of this entered your ears?

“What does our country lack that you have assumed responsibility for protecting us for so long? We would have matured long ago had your partnership and cruelty not existed. However you ruled in those former days, at least now abandon that thought. Return our country to us, grant us freedom and leave, white men! Unwanted people! Go back by the road on which you came!

“Go away. Do not test our patience further. We have endured for a long time, yet see no path by which you will disappear. We therefore utter these words that cause you pain. If you were in our condition, if another ruled in your country, imagine how your heart would suffer. You would then understand well the condition of ours.

“Here in our country there are fine forests. Rice grows; sugarcane and many kinds of fruit are cultivated. Mica and many other materials lie heaped beneath the earth. Despite all this, we continue to live wearing only loincloths.

“Our people are tormented by poverty while you prosper. We labour; you suck us dry. We toil; you enjoy the fruit. We become skeletons; you grow fat like mountains of flesh. How long are we to remain fools, and you to live by deceiving us? Is this acceptable? You who came to rule proclaiming your country the finest in civilisation, answer us!

“Can this difference continue? How long must we pass our lives in our own country as the slaves, attendants and servants of others? We have awakened. We demand freedom. We have sworn to dedicate our very lives to it. Somehow, in the end, we shall gain well-being.

“Our country has fine harbours. It has many people skilled in industry. Our country covers 241,100 square miles. We number forty lakhs. We are not dogs or pigs; we are the people of a nation. We have become impoverished. We shall establish good government ourselves. We have acquired the maturity required for it. This is the twentieth century.

“Self-rule is our birthright. No one has the right to obstruct or destroy it. This is our battle cry. Until freedom is obtained, this camp song will continue to resound.”

At the end of March last year, the freedom uprising on the island of Madagascar began to reveal itself. That land lies off southern Africa. It has an area three times that of the British Isles. Until 1890 it was under the protection of the French Empire; it later became a French colony and remains in the same grip today.

It is a rich and fertile country. Volcanoes and hot springs are found in a small part of it.

It has good harbours and a population of forty lakhs. Rice, maize, sugarcane, coffee beans, many kinds of timber, hides, mica and other products are exported. It is also renowned for precious stones.

The struggle for freedom that began at the end of March last year continued intermittently for three months. The aim of the uprising there was not “White men, leave!” The people rebelled merely seeking an elevated political status for their country while it remained a member of the French Union.

Did they receive what they asked for and desired? No. Rifle fire, artillery shells, aerial bombing and demonstrations by warships were all bestowed as gifts by French imperialism upon those who asked for freedom. It consumed the lives of twenty thousand people. The people of Madagascar sacrificed twenty thousand fighters in their effort to break the chains of slavery.

The repression did not stop there.

Finally, it has delivered to the gallows and the prison several people held responsible for causing and leading the freedom uprising.

As early as last December, ten people regarded as leaders were sentenced to death and many others to imprisonment.

Dr Joseph Ravoahangy, a member of the French Parliament, is to kiss the hangman's rope within days.

Joseph Raseta is another member of the French Parliament. He too is soon to hang.

The gallows awaits three more.

This is the priceless gift bestowed upon them for revealing to the world the flame of rights that rose in their hearts. And it is bestowed by crooked-minded men in power who claim that they continue to govern upon the lofty principles of liberty, equality and fraternity, principles for which many lives were sacrificed and a democratic assembly established.

When a sovereign became a tyrant, the eyes of the people spat fire. They rose fiercely to drive away the ruler's savage methods and expelled the worthless man. If the descendants of those heroes now act in this fashion, what can it be but a deed that stains the integrity and courage of their ancestors?

Before the other countries of the world understood the responsibility of a ruler, France understood it. Its people caused the head of an erring king to roll upon the ground, broke the people's chains, showed the whole earth the excellence of a life of freedom, and lived as victorious heroes whose fame spread abroad. Yet newspapers now report that in that very country their descendants commit the shameful act of cutting off the heads of heroes for the crime of asking for freedom.

That was the final honour paid by the people of France to King Louis XVI. They tore off and cast away his head and the heads of some who stood under his protection. The French people of that day gave the world the fragrance and philosophy of revolution. This occurred at the end of the eighteenth century.

But today, in the twentieth century, to crush the freedom uprising of the people of Madagascar, rulers with the mentality of Louis summon the same old gallows that Louis XVI used and upon which he failed. Louis's end is well known!

Joseph Ravoahangy is about to mount the gallows. His crime was demanding the freedom of his motherland.

If the people, the people of Madagascar, imagine this scene, he might address them thus:

“People of my country, mothers who gave birth to me, young bulls! Here I stand. Look at me! Think for whom, and by whom, this fate has come upon me.

“I could not bear to see you reduced to skeletons. I sought a life of dignity for you. Not for myself, but for you and for your descendants. Had I thought it enough that I alone should live, I could have borne witness for the rulers, sat in their embrace and obtained money, office and title. A mere gesture would have been enough for me to be made a despot over at least a few. I did not do it. I raged against those petty-minded men.

“Are we, who ought to be lions in our own country, to live like little foxes? Are the chains of slavery to be our ornaments? Is one people to suppress and rule another? Can this savage practice be tolerated? I tried to smash it. The rulers of the earth have given me this reward! Do you consent to it? Speak! Speak with open hearts!

“Why do you look at the earth? Do not rub your eyes. You are not cowards. Stand erect! Look at me! I knew the reward I would receive even before I entered the uprising. Knowing it, I set foot upon this dreadful path.

“If I alone hang upon the gallows, the loss belongs only to my parents. But if foreign rule, a rule that does not respect the people, continues, is that not an immense loss and suffering for all of us? Even if I alone die, it is enough if you obtain a better condition.”

British imperialism possessed the intelligence and foresight to understand the nature of the democratic awakening, the incomparable characteristic of the twentieth century. Therefore today that empire can say with pride and fulfilment, “We granted freedom,” while India can say, “Yes, we received it.” The two countries escaped the dangerous condition of ruler and slave and were able to enter a condition of friendship.

The French, Dutch and Portuguese empires do not possess this quality. Thus, even after seeing the judgment of Britain in coming forward to release from its bonds India, the subcontinent called the land where gold grows; fertile Burma; and Ceylon, that fortress of the sea, and even after seeing the results, these little foxes try on the disguise of lions.

Cultured people will not accept one country ruling another, whatever method may be employed, whether the instrument of rule is the sword, the rosary or the scales. They will inevitably resist it.

The conduct of these minor empires, which refuse to hear the voice of the age declaring that the era of imperialism has departed, destroys their standing. The foolish belief that they can preserve their imperial grip by hanging a few people will bear no fruit.

The blood shed by the heroes of Madagascar, the tears shed by their countrymen for those heroes, and the anger that rises in every person of intelligence who witnesses it will shatter these obstinate empires into fragments. Unable to recognise what is coming, French imperialism proceeds with a perverse mind that purchases trouble at a price. This performance will not continue for long.

The article discusses the 1947 Malagasy Uprising and the subsequent prosecution of Malagasy nationalist leaders. The OCR forms `ஜோசப்ராவோ ஹாங்கி` and `ஜோசப் ரசிடா` are rendered with the established English spellings Joseph Ravoahangy and Joseph Raseta.

The publication line was checked directly against Image 9 and corrected in both canonical Tamil copies from the damaged OCR date `10. 0.48` to `10.10.48` before translation.

The article's historical figures and casualty count are translated as stated in the Tamil source. They should not be treated as an independently verified modern historical assessment.
