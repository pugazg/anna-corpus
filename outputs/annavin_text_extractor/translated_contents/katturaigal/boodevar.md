## English Translation

*(Bharathan)*

Comrades know that Brahmins delight in calling themselves *devas* and *bhusuras*, and that Tamils, seeing them bear those titles, imagine they were born into that community through merit accumulated in a previous birth and are therefore worthy of reverence.

They supposedly call themselves “gods on earth” to display their superiority, ancestral greatness, intellectual power and spirit of service. Just as the puranas say human beings who perform virtuous deeds attain divine status in another birth, merit from former births supposedly gives these people Brahmin birth. Devas are the highest beings of the divine world; Brahmins are the highest beings of the earthly world. Thus they are presented to the people as *bhudevas* and *bhusuras*.

Ordinary Tamils hear puranas and epics supporting this claim, watch them enacted, believe them true, bend their bodies and fold their hands on seeing a Brahmin, and carry out whatever service he imposes.

Can the world function without the sun, moon, wind, Varuna and Agni? No. Who are they? Gods. In the same manner, Brahmins are said to be gods without whom nothing on earth can occur. Many puranas serve to implant this attitude in valiant Tamil minds.

Friends who delight in artistic beauty should also condemn the obscenity of puranic doctrine. Even if they cannot condemn it themselves, can they at least refrain from firing arrows of abuse at Self-Respecters who do?

Even in the age of the Nayanmars, to demonstrate the greatness and sanctity of Saivite texts, a manuscript supposedly had to travel against a flood and remain unburned when cast into flame. In this age, the anchor called faith has snapped. Human intelligence sails the vast sea of science with the rotating lamp of research. Will people now be enchanted merely by the majesty of words such as art, epic, Veda and Smriti? They seek evidence, test it upon the stone of reason, and reject it if its quality is deficient.

It is foolish to imagine that modern criticism can be demolished easily. It is also wrong to dismiss its speakers as a tiny group and assume a great multitude still listens obediently to puranic recitation.

As soon as the agitation to burn Aryan texts began, at least some Tamil scholars sincerely said: “We too condemn obscene ideas. We understand the obscenity of the puranas and how planting them in Tamil minds strengthens Aryanism. It must be abolished.” I rejoice on hearing them.

Some “great scholars,” however, continue arguing. They turn the lesson upside down, proclaim Ravana an Aryan and Rama a Tamil, and employ their vast learning to seek proof. At times their speeches entertain me like watching a circus performer walk a wire. Their activity resembles a trapped rat, blocked on all sides, leaping, jumping, biting with its teeth and scratching with its claws.

These scholars are exceedingly skilful at fastening the ideas of the modern world onto the language of old texts according to their own views, learning and predetermined conclusions. But their art of attachment is no more useful than the royal costume and ornaments worn by an actor on stage would be if he walked down the street in them.

Mention any new idea, wonderful science or modern principle of politics or economics. These scholars smile, nod, clear their throats, tap their sticks and wipe their spectacles. Then they take an ancient verse, read and interpret it, and announce that this wonder discovered in the West in 1848 was known here two thousand years ago.

Uncritical people rejoice: “Is that so? Is this a land of such greatness?” They continue performing ablutions for the Pillaiyar beneath the banyan tree, while the Westerner circles overhead in an aeroplane, sees the devotee through a telescope and exults: “India still belongs to us.”

A classical Tamil scholar may begin by asking, “Was Marx the first to say the world is common and all people kin?” He smiles, strokes his hair and lets the audience look at him. Then he declares: “Two thousand years earlier, a son of my sweet Tamil mother said, ‘Every place is our town; everyone our kin.’ Listen to it! It contains Marxism, Leninism, Bolshevism and everything else.” The audience cries, “Wonderful!” and in some places even raises devotional acclamations.

What does the hymn beginning “becoming grass, becoming a plant, becoming a worm” signify? What is its inner subtlety? The lecturer claims it contains evolution: by the Supreme Spirit's grace, life appears as grass, plant and worm, changes step by step, develops and finally receives human form. Darwin discovered evolution in the West, they say, but it was known here before him. The meeting ends pleasantly amid sacred cries of “Tiruchitrambalam.” Comrades will have seen such gatherings.

We see a small child put on his grandfather's long coat, toddle about, and receive kisses from a delighted mother. But if a boy wears his father's upper cloth outside, his mother rebukes him: “Why are you ruining his garment? Where is your own?” If a grown wastrel says, “It will become mine after you, so I shall take it now,” steals his mother's jewellery and goes carousing, she cannot refrain from weeping.

Actions are judged according to the actor's stage of life. Let readers decide whether certain great Tamil scholars who insert Western ideas into ancient texts resemble the innocent child, the schoolboy, or the wastrel who burns his mother's heart.

The people who established modern ideas such as evolution did not discover them by reading the ancient poems to which these scholars now attach them.

The Westerner did not build the aeroplane because he heard a roadside tale that Kubera possessed the Pushpaka craft. He observed floating objects and investigated their cause. He considered that if humans had wings like circling birds, they might move through the sky, and imagined a device that could swim through air like a bird. With reason as aid and science as companion, he now flies.

Likewise, Marx saw that most people throughout many lands suffered the same disease of poverty. He investigated its causes and declared social equality a remedy for the whole world. He did not derive that doctrine from the old line “Every place is our town; everyone our kin.” Darwin did not begin thinking about human development after reading the hymn about becoming grass and worm.

People who never consulted these texts discovered such wonders and made them available to everyone, without decreeing that only high castes might enjoy them. Those here who clung to the texts sang the hymn repeatedly, built devotional halls, performed milk ablutions, ate *panchamritam* and wore golden rudraksha beads. They never stated the theory of evolution. Only after Darwin made it accepted by intellectuals and studied by students did they attach it to their hymn.

People would laugh if a jasmine bud were grafted onto a cactus. The work of some learned sons of Tamil who pride themselves on despising others through their learning resembles precisely that.

Evolution and Marxism do not belong to religious advocates and did not bloom in the imagination of artistic connoisseurs. Honourable people moved by human tears discovered these truths after deep thought about what was required to end suffering and after enduring indescribable hardship. Their power did not arise from drinking divine milk or hearing a disembodied voice dictate a line.

If these doctrines were already known here, people may ask: “Scholar, why did your ancestors not give them to the world before Darwin and Marx?” I do not know what answer could be offered. The thought itself provokes laughter.

To call claims that every modern scientific marvel was already known here evidence of intelligence, scholarship, eloquence or patriotism is disgraceful. Everyone but the gullible will mock them. If a farmer says, “The title deed is mine, but the neighbouring village enjoys the field,” people call him a fool. The argument that all wonders existed here once but are now found there is the same.

It is even more degrading than pointing to a beautiful woman laughing beside a wealthy man in a motor car and saying, “Do you know who she is? She used to be my wife!” Seeing Westerners rule the world in intimacy with the woman called science should awaken self-respect. Instead, these people say she lived here in ancient times and point out her former house.

Whenever someone discovers a principle through immense effort, they immediately read two lines in an old text and announce that its essence was already there. This is a degraded mentality that keeps people foolish and entangled in antiquity. They must abandon it. Once discernment rises with strength, people will not praise such barren speakers.

Lovers of antiquity know that if people are attracted elsewhere and develop desire for science, they will no longer sing and dance to hymns proclaiming sacred ash a medicine or carry Brahmin feet. Whatever marvel is mentioned, they therefore say, “What is remarkable about that? It is in the *Ramayana*; see it in the *Mahabharata*,” guarding people from escaping unjust antiquity.

When criticism grows, they seek refuge in philosophical interpretations. If those are demolished, they say: “Leave the doctrine aside, brother; look at the artistic beauty.” The present struggle has reached this stage.

Whatever justification is offered, so long as a group protects texts that support communal degradation, Tamils will remain servants. Aryanism soaked into people's minds must be uprooted.

During the present war, Stalin stated that when a town was trapped, its people should destroy their own property so it could not serve the enemy. Countries now follow this principle. When German troops capture a city, they find devastated fields, collapsed homes and charred provisions.

The *Ramayana* and *Periyapuranam* are called art. Very well. When they serve the enemy, Brahminism, should they not be burned? The Dnieper Dam was among the world's greatest works, benefiting millions, a sign of science and labour and a strength of the Soviet order. As German forces advanced toward it, the Soviet leader's order came. Mines were placed; a mighty blast followed; the dam was destroyed. Why? Left intact, it would have served the enemy.

If artistic beauty and epic distinction strengthen a wicked system that blinds the people, what path can save them except destroying it? The heroic Soviet people say: “What if the Dnieper Dam is gone? We built it, and we can build it again.” Here, people say Kamban's art must not be damaged and fall into the Aryan net.

If this is the condition of the educated, and they argue that they cannot abandon texts which foster worship of Brahmins as gods on earth, need one describe the condition of ordinary people? When a scholar calls a Brahmin “Iyer-avargal,” an ordinary man calls him “Swami.” To both, the Brahmin remains a god on earth.

Only the Self-Respecter is free of this condition. Would he, who challenges even the gods, offer flowers merely because someone is called *bhudeva*? The Self-Respecter knows the character of the gods.

A young man with an attractive face, innocent heart and pleasing appearance approached a guru for education. Several other pupils studied there. The aged guru had a beautiful wife. The young student became a feast to the woman married to the old man.

At first he did not understand her sighs, smiles, rolling eyes, bodily movements, suggestive adjustment of clothing and affectionate words. He had approached the guru for learning; the guru's wife's “curriculum” was new to him.

One day she taught him its first chapter. Alarmed, he refused, saying he had come to study books, not find a mate. She was not discouraged. She reassured herself that he was moral before intoxication, but would surely be caught once desire was awakened.

The guru knew nothing of the illicit love moving in her heart. He had many concerns and rejoiced at the growth of his pupils' knowledge.

One day while he was away, his wife summoned the pupil and said in a low voice, “I shall give you an auspicious bath.” Without waiting for consent, she began rubbing oil upon him. He stared into the bowl of oil: first he saw two hands, then two desire-filled eyes, then more. As her clothing loosened, his mind opened. He straightened and saw her smiling face. “Stop; begin,” he said. She had won, and he learned the wisdom of the gurukula.

At that moment the guru arrived. Their pleasure appeared to him as a scene of disorder.

Do not ask what happened next or where it happened. Do you not know the story of Tara and Chandra? That is what has just been narrated. Tara, wife of Brihaspati, guru of the gods, rubbed oil upon Chandra, who had come as a pupil, aroused him and united with him. Such is the character of the divine guru's household.

The enraged guru supposedly cursed Chandra: “May your body wane and grow.” That is said to explain the moon's waxing and waning. What became of Tara, who gave the pupil a special gurukula lesson? She lives comfortably, and her name is engraved upon the list of chaste goddesses. This is the divine world.

Brahmins take pride in calling themselves gods on earth. What can I say? If sweet Tamil scholars wrote and spoke so that Tamils understood such divine obscenities and rejected Aryan imagination, the domination of the “gods on earth” would end. Where is that work taking place? If it did, we could hear their lament.

Instead, we still hear the sound of the Vedas and of puranas that elevate these gods on earth. What are we to do?

Published in *Dravida Nadu*, 23 May 1943.

## Translator's Notes

`பூதேவர்` and `பூசுரர்` literally mean “gods on earth” and were honorific descriptions of Brahmins; the title is deliberately ironic. The parenthetical `பரதன்` is retained as the printed byline or pen name “Bharathan.”

The English retains the article's historical polemical register, including its caste critique and sexual retelling of the Tara-Chandra myth. These are translated source claims, not independent endorsements.

The OCR contains a severely damaged clause in the question addressed to scholars about why ancient forebears did not disclose modern doctrines before Darwin and Marx. The evident question is translated, while the corrupt Tamil remains verbatim in the source block.
