## English Translation

*[The available source begins on printed page 197 and refers to words “printed above”; the preceding epigraph or opening page is not present in the image set.]*

At a public meeting organised last week by the Tiruppur Dravidar Kazhagam on the bank of the Noyyal, our leader Periyar Ramasamy spoke in the manner printed above. To anyone capable of serious thought, those words provide fitting work for the mind.

The rulers are the British: world-renowned, masters of the surging seas and possessors of exceptionally subtle commercial skill. These are not the only epithets applied to them. The literary creators of that country have fashioned many garlands of praise for the British: capable of conducting civilised government, upholders of human justice, rulers with an uncorrupted balance, and a people who labour to remove evil and make the country flourish.

The government's charters and the writings of its servants are not the proper measures for deciding whether rule actually proceeds according to civilisation, humanity and justice. Society itself must be measured with the yardstick of reason. Only if discrimination has declined since former times, social attachment has increased, differences have vanished, unity shines, those who destroy self-respect have fallen and the sense of liberation has risen, can civilisation and humanity truly be called attributes of the rulers.

As Periyar asks, is there among the British one class called Brahmins and another burdened with the abusive name “Pariahs”? Could there be? If there had been, could England have become a great power?

If India had fallen under rulers too ignorant to understand how society must be organised for a country to advance, spread its fame, hold an exalted place in the world and cultivate dignity among its people, the heart might yearn to ask whether it was just or proper, yet the tongue might remain still. One might conclude: “The rulers themselves are ignorant. How can we expect civilisation or good qualities from them? Will a leopard sing a *sindhu*? Will a rooster lay eggs?” Speaking would appear useless. In a country ruled by a dog, barking would be law and biting the reward; if a donkey began to rule, could kicks be avoided?

But it cannot be said of the British that they do not understand how to shape a social order, restrain religious arrogance or abolish discrimination. Everyone knows the social structure, intellectual clarity, ethical distinction and beauty of unity in their country.

Groups that came from many places seeking a home understood centuries ago that life depends upon unity. They abolished divisions, adopted a broad path, overthrew social corruption, removed religious pollution and became the indivisible people called the British. They did more than reform society and weed it. Through the virtue of unity, they transformed wanderers into a good people and demonstrated the capacity to make them distinguished throughout the world.

Do they not know that the source of caste arrogance here is the discriminatory Hindu religion and the Aryans—hereditary religious fanatics who cling to it and rule madly from the Vedic seat?

Do they not know that “forty crores” is merely a headcount of a marketplace crowd filled with innumerable divisions, deceits, selfish interests and darkened minds, and that India is not the home of the exalted ideal expressed by “forty crore bodies and a single heart”?

The English know what exists here: one person is called inferior and low-born; one group is said to have been created by God to perform servile work for another; one group is promised a place in heaven only by remaining beneath another's feet; and the labourer is told that religion consists in giving milk, lentils, offerings at the feet and more to the wearer of the five-fold waistcloth.

They also know that unity cannot live unless this social order is abolished and rebuilt—even if it is fed tiger's milk and rocked in a cradle by a horned horse. British public figures who frequently discuss India's deficiencies and obstacles to self-government do not fail to mention these matters. They know and say so, yet refuse to reform them. Why?

Periyar explained the reason that day. When British rule was established, Aryan agitation compelled the British to take power only after promising, “We shall not interfere with your religious and social institutions.” It was an age when Dravidam was weakened, Pakistan lacked strength and the country suffered under forest kings; an age when the thought of Aryan pandits corroded royal courts. The English who came to rule had to yield to Aryan clamour and promise not to alter the religious and social order.

Consequently political rule went to the English, social supremacy to the Aryans, and decline to the people of the country.

For many centuries, through Vedas, puranas, epics, rites and customs, the Aryans—subtle thinkers in the art of self-interest—had established a particular religious and social system here. Aryan religion made people learned fools who knowingly fell into the Aryan pit. Its social system arranged for the labourer to be ruined, crushed by stigma and afflicted by degradation, while the master ate six-flavoured meals, wore silk around his waist, received the title of teacher and lived in pleasure.

When a people sacrifices millions of warriors on many battlefields and conquers another country, it installs its domination there. That means the conquered people obey the victors, pay the demanded tax, follow the prescribed path and bare their backs to the whip. Warriors won such domination by spilling blood. Aryans won theirs by spinning thoughts of a spider-like character.

Who can deny that the Aryans' language is regarded here as the path to both this world and the next? A warrior-ruler compiles laws; the Aryans compiled Vedas and puranas. One who violates ordinary law is punished only in this world, but one who violates the Vedic-puranic scheme is promised many punishments in the next as well.

As public knowledge grows, ordinary laws change and are changed; the desire for reform is respected and permitted. The Aryan scheme remains today as it was then. No disposition to change it arises, and if one does, plenty of Aryan preachers trample it down. The Aryans themselves may, for some reason, make a few alterations, but if the people demand “Change it,” is that not an offence even against Maheswara?

A great power's tax law may be amended or abolished when people can no longer bear it. Some may refuse payment because they have nothing or question why they should pay. The tax system devised by the Aryans, however, is called “donation.” Even a destitute person must withhold what his mother or child needs and give it to the Aryan. If he does not, he is said to lose caste here and suffer further in hell.

Thus Aryan domination, which seized administration by corrupting people's intelligence, is stronger than the domination of one who won titles through war.

Understanding this, the Aryans reasoned when British rule began: “Let anyone rule; we suffer no harm. Let the religious and social structure we built and protect remain so that our supremacy in the country is permanent and our lives prosperous. If we make the incoming English promise not to alter or interfere with it, our dominance will not perish.”

They obtained that promise from the British, then went among the people proclaiming: “Do not be confused, good people! Kings are incarnations of Mahavishnu. The English who rule us too are an aspect of Vishnu!”

Clive began the work at Plassey; the Aryans completed it beside temple tanks. “What does it matter who rules?” had been an Aryan ideal since epic times. Otherwise, why would they have crowned even sandals?

Let us explain it in the manner of an acharya through a story.

Panibai was a virtuous woman, skilled in service as her name suggested. While she was in a doorless house in pitch darkness, Parangiyappan seized her. Panibai lamented. Srijat, who had received Panibai's services, saw what happened. He feared that because she had fallen into Parangiyappan's hands he would lose her customary service.

Srijat told him: “Parangiyappa! Panibai is with you, but it is her established duty to serve me. Give her freedom only to continue serving me.” Parangiyappan agreed. As before, Panibai performed the services owed to Srijat while also being used in Parangiyappan's bedroom.

This story illustrates the argument. Being used in a bedroom means, in brief, prostitution. Scholars call one country's rule over another political prostitution. Let Panibai stand for India, Parangiyappan for the British and Srijat for Aryanism, and understand the story's meaning.

Aryanism obtained a boon from the English for its survival. Whenever some Aryan agitation arises, close observation will show that it comes from fear that British rule may have forgotten its promise to Aryanism.

Because Aryanism received this promise, Varnashrama—devised by wandering Aryans beside riverbanks—still lives even under British rulers. It destroys people's intellectual, material and landed wealth. Though aware of it, the ruling English say, “What can we do?” Tens of millions are born to labour as servants of Aryanism and perish.

The taxes collected under the Aryan plan in the name of “donations” could make even the darkest tyrants jealous. Even a sovereign's law is only one human being's law, and another human has the right and intelligence to alter it. But the Aryan rules concerning donation, society and religion are said to have been made by God himself. Who can change them?

That is why the *cheri* and the *agraharam* have remained from the age of Rama of Ayodhya to the time of the American ruler; donations have continued from the age of Sanaka and Sanandana to that of Mr Churchill.

Tax paid to the ruler provides only town protection, lighting, roads, medicine and transport. A donation to the Aryan is said to purchase God's grace!

Just as those who deceive people by claiming that obscene and irrational things were spoken by God obtained a promise of non-interference in religious and social affairs from the English, they obtained one from the Congress founded for national liberation. The Karachi Resolution is that promise. Gandhi's talk of *Rama Rajya* rests upon it.

In ancient times the Aryans enacted a “ban on reason,” and it remains in force today. The government has felt urgency and necessity to repeal the liquor-prohibition law enacted by the Aryan Acharya today, but it does not recognise the need to repeal the ancient Aryan “reason-prohibition law” that has ruined a great people.

Why? The Acharya subsided after agitating for ten days against repeal of liquor prohibition. But perhaps the British believe he would not remain quiet if they repealed the “reason-prohibition law” enacted by his ancestors.

Periyar's agitation is precisely an agitation to repeal that ban upon reason. Everyone who desires reason must work to repeal the Vedic conspiracy and Vedic-age arrangement that excluded reason. Where is that marching force?

*Dravida Nadu*, 23 July 1944
