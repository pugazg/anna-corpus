## English Translation

Indians in South Africa are engaging in civil disobedience, demanding equal rights. In Ceylon, Tamils are undertaking strikes and agitation against the ruling dominance of the Sinhalese, demanding equal rights and equal opportunity.

Here, within our own country, the Adi Dravidars

live in segregated settlements,

are treated as untouchables,

and do not possess even common civic rights.

Yet there is no opportunity for an equal-rights agitation!

Why?

Here, except for Brahmins, others cannot enter the sanctum of a temple, touch the deity, and worship. Brahmins alone possess the right to perform the puja.

Non-Brahmins have neither the courage to demand equal rights in the temple nor the opportunity to agitate for them.

Why?

Even in eating houses, discrimination is practised by assigning one place to Brahmins and another to non-Brahmins. Here too, non-Brahmins have neither the courage to demand equal rights nor the opportunity to agitate.

Why?

Even now, orthodox Brahmins regard themselves as polluted if we touch them. The scheme by which everyone other than Brahmins is classed as an inferior caste has been accepted on the authority of the shastras. There is neither the courage nor the opportunity to oppose it, demand equal rights, or agitate.

Why?

It is easy to defy General Smuts's law; it is difficult to defy the shastras of the Sanatanists.

Why?

Smuts is a political leader; the Sanatanist is a "god on earth."

How much longer will the Dravidians in the Congress remain without understanding this subtle truth?

Civil disobedience brings only the punishment of the government.

They will threaten that rejection of the shastras brings the punishment of God.

One who practises civil disobedience will be praised as a hero; one who rejects the shastras will be reviled as a sinner!

Why?

If laws are defied and conditions are set right, the comfortable life of the Sanatanists suffers no harm.

If the shastras are rejected and conditions change, the comfortable life of the Sanatanists will be ruined. That is why Brahmin society, which assents to civil disobedience, does not assent to rejection of the shastras.

The South African government's degrading treatment of Indians is condemned by the government here and by every political party. An agitation is conducted against that law, and that agitation is praised. It is just, necessary, and important too.

But what would happen if the indigenous people living here in segregated settlements similarly demanded equal rights and conducted an agitation to "find a place" in the locality where the other, upper-caste people live? The law would rage, the shastras would curse, and bloodshed would occur throughout the country.

In this very month, an Adi Dravidar was sentenced to four months' rigorous imprisonment merely for cremating a body in the place reserved for caste Hindus. It was our country's legislature that imposed the punishment.

In the Tiruvannamalai temple case, it has been "decided" that Brahmins may stand in the ardha-mandapa and behold the deity, while others must stand outside to worship.

At the Sanskrit school in Tiruvaiyaru, Brahmin students declared that sitting and eating together with non-Brahmin students diminished the honour of their caste. Brahmin leaders supported them.

Even today, in some places, a bathing tank used by Brahmins is not made available to others.

Even today, people of other communities are not given a place in the agraharam where Brahmins reside.

All these practices are carried on in the name of religious custom, and the authority of the shastras is cited for them.

Just as courage has arisen to begin civil disobedience in South Africa for equal rights, if someone here comes forward to reject shastric traditions for equal rights, he will be called

base-born, a sinner, an atheist.

He will be reviled, condemned, and stoned by the people themselves, by the very people for whose equal rights we labour and agitate.

Civil disobedience is easy.

Rejection of the shastras is difficult.

Law is common to all.

The shastras are the exclusive privilege of the Brahmins.

Brahmins will exercise the utmost vigilance to ensure that the shastras, their exclusive privilege, are not damaged.

In South Africa, white people treat black people, including us, with contempt, offering as their reason that they are foreigners and belong to another race. We say that this is an affront to democracy, national honour, and humanity. True! For the people of one country to regard those of another country with contempt is an utterly barbarous idea. That arrogance must indeed be opposed. It is being opposed in South Africa, and we support that struggle for equal rights.

There, whites treat blacks with contempt; Americans treat Negroes with contempt. At a time when agitations are being conducted to abolish these cruelties, here,

Hindus do not grant equal rights to non-Brahmins, who are one section of Hindus.

They degrade and oppress the indigenous people, themselves one section of Hindus, by calling them untouchables.

Whites do not degrade or lower a section of white people.

Chinese people do not contemptuously call some among the Chinese untouchables. A Negro does not degrade another Negro.

But a Hindu degrades another Hindu. He calls him an untouchable and denies him rights in the temple, the bathing tank, and public places.

The Hindu divides Hindus themselves into four castes and perpetrates the cruelty of maintaining distinctions among them. He calls these practices the shastric order.

There are heroes for civil disobedience. Who comes forward to reject the shastras? Civil disobedience becomes possible; at the very mention of rejecting the shastras, people tremble in fear!

Why?

*Dravida Nadu*, 23 June 1946.

## Translator's Notes

“Shastras” is retained because the argument concerns scriptural authority invoked to uphold caste practices, not scripture in a generic sense. “Rejection” conveys மறுப்பு consistently in the contrast with civil disobedience. Historical community terms and the source's period terminology are translated or transliterated in their original argumentative context.
