## English Translation

Even during earlier days, many comrades now angered by the division complained that I was indifferent and out of touch, and that I came only once after being summoned ten times. When Needamangalam Arumugam, Nadigavel Radha, Natrampalli Venkataraman and many other friends wished to offer me garlands, happiness, affection and support, I was a person who almost slipped away from them.

Knowing this nature of mine, to imagine that I planned every second, counted supporters town by town and deliberately constructed an opposition front is to have known me without understanding me correctly.

The situation naturally created by Periyar's marriage arrangement caused the opposition front to form, not I.

One may think the situation arose because I wrote about the marriage. That too is not the cause. Even when I wrote, the statement did not take shape merely as my individual opinion or propaganda. It emerged as the collected thought of many comrades whose hearts seethed at Periyar's marriage arrangement.

In other words, by the time I issued the statement, the opposition front had already formed. I did not form it.

That front was not created by my deceit or wickedness, tactics or talent, tireless labour or enchanting plan. It arose naturally and democratically. That is why it possesses living force.

Only by understanding this can the situation be judged as it truly is.

It is precisely here that Periyar and his supporters adopt a mistaken view. They think that I remained obstinate, conducted poisonous propaganda, enchanted and corrupted many people, and thereby produced an opposition front. Wrong. Wrong, profoundly wrong. The situation developed naturally and democratically.

If a single statement from me could enchant so many comrades, my statement issued in August should also have created an opposition front. Did anything of that kind occur? No. I myself knew it would not. In that very statement I wrote that disciplinary action could be taken against me if necessary.

When the August statement appeared, did Asaithambi, Vallimuthu, Chitrarasu, Srinivasan and countless other comrades say, “Anna has written it, so it must be right”? Our comrades do not lack that much courage of heart. They said with dignity that they could not accept my view.

Should we not consider why my opinion on the marriage issue received support that my August statement did not? If this is considered carefully, it becomes clear that the opposition front was neither the result of my enchanting words nor the success of a selfish design. It was a natural result, a democratic sprout.

I therefore ask supporters not to give a false idea lodging in their minds and thereby allow many further errors to germinate.

Because the basic assumption itself is wrong, mistakes then grow rapidly. From that side now comes abuse extending even to charges of forgery and fraud.

Believing wrongly that this new organisation arose solely through my tactics and selfishness, they think the way to break it is to abuse and discredit me. They have made me the object of research. They examine my condition, thoughts and actions, saying, “I knew it even then! Does this not prove it? Was he not always a man who did such things?”

This mistaken method cannot destroy the new organisation called the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. If I alone had caused its formation, it might be possible to eliminate the organisation by condemning me. But it exists as a natural outcome, the voice of countless comrades' hearts, and a democratic sprout. No amount of degrading or blaming me can abolish it.

The organisation is not founded upon my ability. It is built upon the abilities of countless Dravidian comrades. I do not, and will not, rejoicefully claim that the Dravidar Kazhagam itself exists solely through the ability and labour of Periyar. Such a claim would not be true.

Even thus far, I proudly and joyfully acknowledge that others have performed, and continue to perform, more of the rare work required to establish the DMK than I have. I thank those friends engaged in that excellent labour.

Until today, letters continue to arrive from those dear friends saying, “This should be done; it should be organised this way,” and giving me encouragement and feeling. I have done nothing by methods such as recruiting people, testing the depth of their loyalties or sending emissaries. I have no habit or training in those practices. That is why I say the DMK cannot be destroyed by abusing me.

The Dravidar Kazhagam in North Arcot district was transformed into the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam as a result of a meeting at Tiruvathipuram, twelve miles from Kanchipuram. Tiruvathipuram Subbarayan took the lead and carried it through.

I had been in Kanchipuram for five days. Friend Subbarayan was very close to me, and Tiruvathipuram was only twelve miles away. Yet had I met and spoken with him about this, or conveyed any view even by letter? No.

He was a friend, close to me and geographically nearby. Still, I neither met him nor sent a letter directing how the matter should proceed. Why? He bore at least as much responsibility and concern about this issue as I did. I trusted that he possessed the capacity to do what was right. Events occurred exactly according to that confidence.

I mention this because every action undertaken thus far for the DMK has occurred like the Tiruvathipuram action: naturally and democratically, without my incitement or flattery. It is not the victory of one individual, selfish man or traitor. It is the ability and victory of countless Dravidian comrades whose hearts were inflamed.

Is abusing me the way to destroy it? No. There is no better way to make me grow!

Contrary to what he imagines and says, the present condition is not something I planned and built, nor was it my long-standing desire.

Had the Dravidar Kazhagam been a party seeking to capture political power and ascend the seat of government, there might at least be room to allege that I or someone else selfishly planned to seize the organisation and obtain office.

The Dravidar Kazhagam was not an instrument for hunting immediate political profit. I always supported that course and still hold the same view. In these circumstances, what need or opportunity could there be for selfishness?

Does Periyar imagine the public will simply believe him if he endlessly repeats “self-seekers” and “traitors”? Will people fail to ask what selfish interest can be found here and whom anyone has betrayed?

I understand well that, even after this great storm, Periyar speaks in this way to encourage those who remain with him and prevent their confidence from breaking. That is why I feel neither anger nor sorrow.

I explain this so that, if you understand the subtlety, you too will feel neither anger nor grief when he speaks burning words.

Along with accusing me of planning, working and enchanting the people, he hints that I have held treacherous thoughts for three years. He reportedly says he had spoken of this from time to time.

If he knew three years ago that I was a traitor, why did he honour me only a few months ago at Erode by saying he would hand me the “key to the cash box”? Was leadership of a separate conference being given to a traitor? Was it a gift, a bribe, or a hint that I would receive a share? What does it mean?

Today he calls me a “traitor.” Had he given me even the slightest indication of this through word or action in a way I could understand, I would not even have laid my head down to sleep in the direction where he lived!
