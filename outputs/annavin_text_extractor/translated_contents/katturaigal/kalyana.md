## English Translation

Thiru. Vi. Kalyanasundaranar, who participates in a special gathering of distinguished Jains!

You are a Saiva, one who wears sacred ash and worships the Nayanmars. Your Saivism tortured Jains. Your Nayanmars reviled them without restraint, and those very songs remain Saiva hymns of grace. Your Tirugnanasambandar is the “merciful benefactor” who impaled the Jains, and your Kulachirai stood guard beside the stakes.

You carry a Saivism that committed such cruelty and yet mingle with Jains. Are you now seeking expiation for your religious leaders having impaled them in the past? If so, condemn this cruel deed of Saivism. Bow your head in shame that the Nayanmars abused the Jains, ask their forgiveness, and teach Saivism to do the same.

We have shown the cruelty of impalement in the illustration above. Look upon it with tears. See the gulf between the words “Love is Siva” and the act of killing Jains; stand with head bowed in shame and sorrow.

Here is evidence from your own Saiva texts that the impalement occurred. Observe the course of your Saivism:

> Raising before him the manuscript taken from the fierce fire,
> setting it in the river so that it travelled against the stream,
> and impaling every Jain without leaving one...

This verse is from the *Tiruttondar Purana Saram*. Sir, those embodiments of grace who impaled people are your dignitaries who wear the ash of the cremation ground belonging to the Lord of the forest. Are you not ashamed to hear it?

The *Tiruttondar Tiruvandadi* says of Kulachirai:

> He caused the Jains to be raised
> upon the points of the stakes
> in the land of noble Tamil.

How, sir, can one belonging to such a sacred company show his head at a Jain conference? Are you seeking expiation for the impalement? Answer with your hand upon your heart.

**Dravidar Kazhagam, Kanchipuram**

On 19 March 1948, Thiru. Vi. Kalyanasundaranar came to Kanchipuram to preside over a special gathering of Jains. Members of the Kanchipuram Dravidar Kazhagam distributed leaflets carrying the illustration and text reproduced above. The Saiva Tamil gentlemen who arrived intending to say one thing at the gathering, heard his tongue say several others, and departed inwardly chanting “Praise the Lord's feet! Praise Namashivaya!” know what effect it produced.

Why was this method adopted? The Jains wished to hear Kalyanasundaranar, a supporter of Saivism, praise Jainism and delight their ears. Kalyanasundaranar gained an opportunity to proclaim, “Even Jains invite me; sound the conch that I am good to everyone!” Whatever the motives, the Dravidar Kazhagam distributed the leaflets so people would learn what Saivism had done to Jainism.

Some wilted, many writhed, and a few dragged out the response, “It is necessary, but...” There were angry Saivas too. As though a red scorpion had stung their hearts, they declared: “This is the work of the Self-Respect Movement!” Some asked whether it was proper to roam about hurling such abuse.

Whenever truth is stated openly in this way, sincere devotees chanting “Om Namashivaya” feel moved to say only that Self-Respecters abuse people indiscriminately. It never occurs to them to say, “We shall prove their claim false, extend evidence and drive away their question, remove doubt and plant Saivism's flag.” Only what exists in the pot can enter the ladle. Truth can shine outward only if it exists within.

Some friends offer advice: “Whatever else may be true, Self-Respecters harshly abuse those who differ from them. I do not like this. Opponents and those who reject one's principles should not be hurt with insulting language. Ideas should be explained pleasantly.”

In particular, there is a group that claims it is second to none in Tamil feeling but warns Self-Respecters not to involve themselves in Tamil religious affairs. Without answering the movement's questions or clarifying its doubts, they become distressed and angry while uttering labels such as impudence, agitation, abuse and provocation.

Anyone approaching intellectual inquiry will accept that arrogantly scolding others as dogs or spirits is wrong. Self-Respect comrades do not aim to wound others' minds. They do not treat insult as thought or abuse as a method of argument.

When they uproot doctrines that have flourished in thought for ages, some people will naturally suffer. To them, Self-Respect reasoning appears as abuse. That is the whole truth.

Nor can anyone claim that angrily shouting “dog,” “spirit,” “fool” and “savage” is a special trait of the Self-Respect Movement. Those described as men of virtue, religious teachers and counsellors of wisdom, including Nayanmars, Alvars, gurus and poets, have abused, pursued, raged against, persecuted and tortured people who differed from them to a degree beyond description. Their insults are so unjust that the ear can scarcely endure them.

In religious affairs, each denounced the other's faith as inferior while calling his own superior. Wherever they obtained support, these great men treated the humiliation of other religions as worship of Maheswara. Celebrated poets obtained shelter from a king or minor ruler, guarded the place so nobody else could enter, and tirelessly worked to eliminate anyone who did. How many abusive words they uttered!

Yet many still read those verses and call them sweet and excellent. Look closely at the jealousy, agitation and hatred swaying through them, and it becomes clear that Self-Respecters are not the masters of abuse.

Some poets became furious and abusive over even a very minor fault. Because the attack appears in classical Tamil verse, nobody examines it and asks why the poet showed such rage over so small an incident.

Imagine a communal meal. The topknot of one diner comes loose and falls upon the leaf-plate of the person beside him. Absorbed in eating, he fails to notice. What happens?

A cultivated person gently calls, “Sir!” The offender recognizes the situation and begins, “Alas, it happened unknowingly.” Before he finishes, the other says, “Never mind.” That is civilized conduct.

The next type operates with more anger:

“Who is this? Can you not look where you are eating?”

“I did not see. Would I have let it happen if I had?”

“Why become so angry over this?”

“You know enough to say anger is wrong, but lack the intelligence to know your act was wrong?”

“You should not show so much temper.”

“You should not have shown so much carelessness.”

Others intervene before the exchange becomes ugly.

At the lowest level, speech and conduct follow another course:

“Idiot! Do you not know the person beside you is human?”

“Did I think you were a cow instead of a human?”

“Rogue! Be careful!”

“Enough. Stop. A hair fell on your leaf by accident, and you jump like this? Ten people attend a feast; some small mistake may happen in the bustle. Must you stage a performance?”

“Had I known fools like you would attend, I would never have come.”

“Get lost, idiot! Do I not know your worth? You stand like a dog at every doorway decorated with a plantain tree.”

“Me or you? Even if they seize your neck and throw you out, you refuse to leave. Giving someone of your worth a place at the feast was itself a mistake.”

Words end and hands begin.

Do not many such types exist? Something like this happened to a celebrated poet at a feast. A man with a front topknot sat beside him. His hair came loose and fell on the poet's rice leaf; failing to notice, he shook his topknot. The poet grew angry.

In which class would you place him? Are patience, awareness and forgiveness not ornaments of poets? Friends say Self-Respecters speak wildly, utter intolerably harsh words and abuse others. I ask them to decide from his own verse in which class this poet placed himself.

The moment the topknot loosened and fell upon the leaf, he sang:

> O Choliya with loosened front topknot,
> mouth rising from scraps of rice,
> outcaste, dog of sacred Kumbakonam,
> fox! Did your mother not behold
> your dog's face and childish form?

The poet reviled him as dog, fox and degraded man. What abuse, and what harsh insult! Spoken by whom? Have Self-Respecters ever abused anyone in this manner? The man who received it was Savayappa Nayan. The speaker was the learned classical Tamil Brahmin poet Kalamegam. Observe the poet's conduct.

Kalamegam's calling someone dog and spirit is not the only matter I wish to show. He was a poet. The Nayanmars, praised as the refuge of poets, dwelling of divine grace, objects of reverence and homes of virtue, spoke with contempt and abuse about Jains who differed from the Saiva faith they supported. Even today devotees sing those words melodiously, believing divine grace will flow from them:

> Great sinners without Vedic custom.
>
> Men bereft of reason.
>
> Ruined Jains, like elephants dwelling in wasteland.
>
> Wearers of closed robes, old bald-heads, gross Jains.
>
> Jains who are liars.
>
> Jains who are cheats.
>
> Jains who are rogues.
>
> Jains without knowledge.
>
> Filthy-mouthed Jains.
>
> Jain sinners and Buddhists.
>
> Base men who eat standing.
>
> Filthy-bodied skull-bearers.
>
> Brutes without virtue.

Fool, sinner, bald-head, stunted mind, liar, fraud, villain, brute, ignoramus, cheat, filthy mouth: sincere devotees, hear these insults said to have blossomed from Tirugnanasambandar's sacred lips.

Because Jains belonged to another faith, Sambandar abused them furiously with the intention that the public would hate both them and their path. He uttered words unfit to come from the tongue of a virtuous person.

Sambandar was the child whose mouth had not ceased drinking milk, and not ordinary milk. Parvati came with the Lord who wears an earring, rides the bull and bears the pure white crescent, and fed him herself. See the character of the mouth that drank Parvati's milk. Was it right to revile another faith with such frenzy? Consider and say whether Self-Respecters have ever spoken indiscriminately of anyone in this fashion.

Did they stop after speaking abusively and making hatred of Jains grow like madness among the people? No. Saivas say they “won the debate.” What did they do after winning? They impaled eight thousand Jains. This was the conduct in those days of scholars who said love itself is Siva.

The reason it does not occur today is that English law obstructs it, not that their hearts have broadened like Thiru. Vi. Ka.'s. Yet such devotees complain that Self-Respect comrades speak indiscriminately. What a condition their thought has reached!

It would indeed be wise for them to seek expiation for the cruelty inflicted by ancient Saivas upon Jains. Abusive expressions should be removed from the texts. The habit of reading the impalement tale should cease. When people speak of Sambandar's history and Appar's greatness, they should stop maligning Jainism. The picture in the Madurai temple depicting the impalement should be removed. The festival reenacting it in Madurai should be stopped. In Tirunelveli, the street name commemorating the tale should be changed.

Thiru. Vi. Ka., who speaks of harmony among all religions, should devote his time and thought to these tasks. It is not right to declare at special gatherings, “To my eyes the Prophet and the Nayanmars are one; Hari and Hara are one; Saivism and Jainism are one,” and then sit chanting “Siva, Siva.”

I have heard many say that seeing everything as one is refinement. A failure to commit oneself to anything, lack of courage to condemn anything, indifference to how matters stand, and fear of what trouble a statement may cause make some claim that they see everything alike. This is not refinement. It is evidence that the heart lacks strength.

To a madman or drunkard all objects look alike, and sometimes one appears as another. That condition is not worthy of praise, nor a blessing one should pray to receive. I say this to the company of friends.

*26 March 1944*

## Translator's Notes

The title turns on `கழுவேற்றுதல்` (impalement on a stake) and `கழுவாய்` (expiation or atonement). `கலியாணசுந்தரர்` is Thiru. V. Kalyanasundaram (Thiru. Vi. Ka.), a Tamil scholar, labour leader and Saiva writer. The article addresses his participation in a Jain gathering at Kanchipuram.

The alleged impalement of eight thousand Jains in Madurai is presented as narrated and celebrated in Saiva tradition. Its historicity is disputed; this translation preserves Anna's argument and citations rather than independently affirming the event. `குலச்சிறை` refers to the Saiva minister Kulachirai Nayanar. `திருத்தொண்டர் புராணசாரம்` and `திருத்தொண்டர் திருவந்தாதி` are cited as textual sources.

The long verse attributed to Kalamega Pulavar contains caste and animal insults. `சோழியா` may denote a Choliya Brahmin, and `சவையப்பநாயன்` is OCR-sensitive. The catalogue or running-page numbers and repeated book header `நமது கீதை!` are not part of the article.

Several quoted devotional lines are damaged by OCR punctuation and word division. They are translated conservatively from their visible sense; the Tamil source remains unchanged. The article's religious polemic and characterizations are retained as historical argument, not adopted as editorial descriptions.
