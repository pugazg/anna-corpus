## English Translation

He is mute! He has no hands either! A man blind in judgment appointed him as a guard. Where, and over what? On a rock, over a ball of butter placed upon it! What kind of hour is it? The fierce heat of the day.

The man who has lost both speech and hands has not lost his eyes. He watches. As the sun's rays fall upon it, the butter melts and runs over the rock. He has no voice with which to explain; he can only howl. He sees the butter melting in the heat, flowing away and going to waste upon the rock.

He has the will to scrape up the butter and move it elsewhere, but no hands! He wishes he could tell someone with hands to move it to another place, but he has no voice with which to express the thought! What can the “handless mute” do?

From one side come cries and lamentation: “Hunger! Hunger! Famine, high prices, disease and suffering!”

From another come reports: “Profiteering! He has become a millionaire! He has built a mansion as tall as a temple tower!”

On one side there is anxiety: “There are no goods, no provisions, no rice, no ghee, no firewood, no nine kinds of grain.” On the other, people speak of offerings of ten thousand and one rupees, offerings at the feet of one thousand and one, and alms of one hundred and one rupees; and the spectacle unfolds of ten cartloads of samba paddy, twenty tins of ghee, ten tons of firewood and forty sacks of nine grains arriving and being unloaded!

The pauper's heart trembles, while the ostentatious man's splendour spreads everywhere. Among those tormented by this condition, one section has lost its tongue and cannot speak what its conscience knows. Another has a tongue and even the desire to correct and reconstruct this condition, but lacks the power to act. Seeing this and grieving, many crores of people become trapped in the mire of anguish and suffer mortal agony.

Just as the handless mute can only watch butter go to waste upon the rock and do nothing, here too there are handless mutes who see the enormous harm produced by inequality but remain unable either to speak or to remedy it. Unable to prevent the destruction of material wealth or remove the parasite that eats away the intellect, and without even a way to speak, our people languish in delirium and lamentation.

Like the butter melting before the mute man's eyes, wealth and knowledge are ruined before theirs. They see it, but suffer without any means to stop it. So long as the number of handless mutes remains large, pleasure-seekers who enjoy themselves by plundering the property of others, and hard-hearted men who trample and wash out other people's lives and make that very act their livelihood, will continue to live triumphantly.

Cries of pain fill the country. Food is scarce. Every person's mind is distressed. In people's homes, the fire of suffering caused by the absence of firewood heats the famine and serves it for them to drink.

At that very time, Vedic chanting resounds on the bank of the Yamuna. To ensure that the chanting does not diminish and that hunger does not surge there, foodstuffs lie in heaps! A company of Vedic priests has gathered for a sacrifice. There are said to be one hundred fire pits, with a crowd of priests surrounding them, at a cost of thirteen lakhs! A thousand maunds of ghee! Five thousand maunds of sacrificial firewood! And thousands of Aryan priests to burn them all!

New leaf-thatched hermitages have been built, sacrificial pits dug, cartload after cartload of firewood piled up and costly goods poured into the pits. A great sacrifice is being performed, and four Shankaracharyas are said to grace it with their presence!

What does it matter if people die in Bengal, if decay overtakes the land where the Chera once dwelt, if these are days of scarcity, or if famine dances with its hair unbound? In a place filled with handless mutes, scoundrels destroy wealth before their very eyes. What else can the handless mute do but babble and lament? The responsibility for protecting the nation's wealth rests with these handless mutes!

The government will not intervene. The Queen has declared, “We shall not interfere in matters of religion!” The world will not prevent it. It will remain silent, thinking that a country seized by such madness may serve as an object of amusement. Perhaps it may also serve people like Mayo as material for their written portraits.

In this country that claims to know everything, the activity that allows termites to consume its wealth proceeds without let or hindrance! The summit of greatness! A mountain of virtue! The pinnacle of wisdom! Here it is, this Bharata continent. Do you doubt it?

Consider how deeply pride, stupidity, indifference to the people and refusal to cooperate with reason must have seeped into society for such a meaningless waste of time, thought and material wealth to occur in the present state of the world, amid the suffering of the country, while the tears of the poor run like a river.

These are heartless people who destroy provisions that ought to be used for those writhing in misery. In a country filled with people who cannot even dream of ghee, these cruel men pour ghee into fire. They see the tears of the poor and feel no compassion. Such wicked men exist, and there is no one to restrain them!

Many lakhs go to waste during famine. It is a sacrifice, they say, a great sacrifice! The whole world will prosper through it, they say. What a wonder that the world of reason tolerates such babble!

Was it not in the days when there were a hundred sacrificial fires that self-rule was plundered and this country made a servile possession of a foreigner? Was it not while Vedic chanting struck the heavens that the report of the gun fired by Clive, the Englishman who came seeking employment, was heard in our land? The sacrificial fire that did not prevent slavery from arriving then, and the pot-bellied men who sat beside it feeding their bodies: what are they going to accomplish today by establishing a hundred fire pits?

For some reason our people give these things no thought. Even those who think remain handless mutes! Such ruinous festivals occur not only on the bank of the Yamuna but in many places. There is no shortage of temple consecrations or revelry accompanied by drums and clamour. While people languish without a means of livelihood, temple renovation proceeds without pause.

While the army of the starving suffers, Shankaracharyas continue to parade in palanquins and monastic heads rejoice in ceremonial entries into towns. Those engaged in these activities have no compassion. The intellectual who sees them but lets his mind wander to other matters has no indignation. Most of the people have neither speech nor hands, only breath and wit: we see the former curled up and the latter darkened.

Can there be a country like this? Is the name “holy land” anything but an affliction upon it? Was all this labour undertaken only to pour wealth into the obscene sacred games of the priestly class and then weep over its loss?

People overthrew those who, unconcerned by people trembling in famine, lay upon soft beds, drank the honeyed words of fragrant women, caressed their flower-like feet and stumbled through a city of pleasure; they hailed democracy. History calls those events the French Revolution and the Russian Revolution.

What difference can one find between the scoundrels who wallowed in luxury in those two countries before their revolutions and the sight here today of religious fanatics deceiving simpletons, living in intoxicated frenzy and standing with the support of the wealthy?

“My body thrills and my blood boils when I read those histories praised by the world,” comrades of May Day proclaim, shaking the platform. Yet see how they tremble to move their tongues even after witnessing this condition! The guardians of the sacrificial pits tell you to wring your hands and shed tears at being forced to live among handless mutes.

Offerings, honours, gifts at the feet and alms are showered upon Shankaracharyas who do not work, cannot understand the dignity of labour, know nothing of the cruelty of hunger, have never felt their legs ache from walking as wayfarers, have never wandered in search of clothing, and know nothing of the anguish that fills the homes of people who struggle desperately to sustain their bodies.

At Kanchipuram, the Shankaracharya pitches his camp and, with drums, a crowd and a retinue, displays loyalty to caste, places a bridle upon narrow-minded people and gathers wealth in splendour. There are noble profiteers who sold a thing worth five for fifty; slaves of desire who give a thousand so that alluring women may caress their flower-like feet; and honourable men who regard mansions built from the money poured out by fools and clowns as boons granted by God. With devotion they give money to the Shankaracharya, the embodiment that says the whole world is illusion, and to the Brahmin who says the inner self is Brahman; they drink the water from his feet and attain supreme bliss!

These wealthy people will not even raise their eyes to look at the poor person approaching them. Yet they stand at a distance, pour money onto a tray and imagine that they have paid an advance towards salvation.

What can the handless mutes do on seeing the procession of these figures in whom mean conduct and great wealth are joined? Even after witnessing cruelties committed in defiance of the course of the age, can they continue passing their days as handless mutes?

The reason the people remain handless mutes is that the people of this country cling weeping to Hinduism, which lacks explanation and a plan, sacrifices heroes to wastrels and embraces caste distinctions.

At the All-India Untouchables Conference held in Kanpur last week under the leadership of Comrade N. Sivaraj, Dr Ambedkar, sovereign of the oppressed people, declared: “Hinduism itself is the cause of our degraded condition and suffering. Only by casting it away can we obtain rights and attain an elevated condition.”

That is the path of liberation not merely for the oppressed people, but for the entire Dravidian race. It is Hinduism that betrays us to the ritualist priests and turns our people into handless mutes. Those who do not understand this understand nothing!

Our people were not born mute; Brahminism has sealed their mouths. Nor were they born without hands; the fetters of the destroyers' scheme have rendered Tamil hands powerless. They are not handless mutes by birth. By accepting Brahminism, they suffer with their hands bound and mouths stopped.

If a determined effort is made, the slavery of those who appear today as handless mutes will end, knowledge will rise and happiness will overflow.

So long as they pass their time as handless mutes, not only will the sacrificial pits continue to exist; we need not be surprised if, in those pits, the people's blood is poured in place of ghee and their hands and legs are thrown in and burned in place of firewood. After all, this is a country that has conducted human sacrifices!

*(Dravida Nadu, 6 February 1944)*

## Translator's Notes

The title invokes the image of a mute man without hands who can see butter melting on a hot rock but can neither move it nor call for help. “Handless mute” is retained throughout as the essay's governing metaphor.

Image 9 was downloaded locally but omitted from the OCR Markdown. Its concluding paragraph and publication date were manually transcribed from the scan before translation. The scan also confirms the corrected title `கையில் ஊமையர்!` and the opening readings `அவனைக்` and `கருத்துக் குருடனொருவன்`.

Several quantities in the sacrificial description are visibly damaged by OCR. The translation preserves their scale without treating unclear final digits as critically established; the Tamil source retains the archived readings.
