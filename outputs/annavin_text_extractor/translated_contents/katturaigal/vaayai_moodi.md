## English Translation

**Place:** The village rest house.

**Present:** The village priest and Kandan, a farmer.

**Entering:** Palani, returned from the army.

**Situation:** As usual, the Aiyar is seated in the village rest house for idle gossip. He is discussing world affairs with the farmer Kandan. At that moment Palani, the son of a villager named Muniyan, passes by, having returned to the village after service in the army. They begin speaking with him.

**Kandan:** Brother! Look, master! Do you know who it is? It is our Muniyan's boy.

**Aiyar:** Oh! Were you in the army? I am very pleased. Come, son. Which countries did you visit?

**Palani:** Me? Burma, Penang, Greece, Italy—everywhere.

**Aiyar:** You might as well say you have travelled around the whole world.

**Kandan:** It was all to fill that span of a belly, wasn't it?

**Palani:** Yes, of course. Isn't every kind of work done for that?

**Aiyar:** Was the fighting terrible?

**Palani:** Why ask? The aeroplane would come whirring!

**Aiyar:** Above you?

**Palani:** Yes, of course it came flying above us. The moment we heard the sound—silence! Without making the slightest noise, we would get down into a trench. Within a minute or two: boom, bang, rat-a-tat, crash! The hill would be blown to dust, buildings would collapse, and fires would break out. Would our men remain quiet? They would take the anti-aircraft gun—the gun used to shoot aeroplanes—and fire: rat-a-tat-a-tat! When a shell struck the plane, you would hear a screech. It would spin like a top, catch fire, fall to the ground, and break into separate pieces, axle from nail.

**Aiyar:** It is frightening even to hear about it.

**Palani:** Is that all? We would casually walk even over corpses. A man would sit with us in the morning drinking tea. In the evening, if we asked where he was—gone.

**Aiyar:** What do you mean?

**Palani:** Dead.

**Kandan:** Oh, mother! Half the people died in that wretched war.

**Aiyar:** Why ask about it? What was all this for? To capture markets, of course.

**Palani:** Yes. But the war in our *Mahabharata* was not even fought to capture a market. It was fought to capture cattle. Is that not the *Virata Parva*?

**Aiyar:** Yes! He is intelligent and has read. In those days, war was fought to destroy demons. Now human beings fight among themselves. The Germans are Christians and the white men are Christians too, yet they fight. Is that not an injustice?

**Kandan:** Yes, yes. A great injustice.

**Palani:** Did this happen only now? Who were the Kauravas? Who were the Pandavas? They were brothers, one family. The Germans and the British at least belong to different countries with a sea between them. Was not the *Mahabharata* a war within one family?

**Aiyar:** What the boy says is also true.

**Aiyar:** But in those days innocent people were not killed. Today, look: when a bomb is dropped, an entire town is destroyed and ruined.

**Palani:** Yes, but that injustice existed in those days too. Consider one example. Ravana carried Sita away. Hanuman went to Lanka as a messenger. What did he do? For Ravana's injustice, he burned the whole of Lanka!

**Aiyar:** Why, boy! Are you one of those Self-Respect fellows? All right, I am leaving. I have work to do.

*(The Aiyar leaves.)*

**Kandan:** Well! The Aiyar shut his mouth and left!

*(The soldier also leaves.)*
