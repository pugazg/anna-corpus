**18. Did other parties tremble at repression and abandon their principles in this manner?**

This is much the same question that was asked before. It is the question, “Should we fear the law?” repeated in different words. None of us has ever feared that the law would strike us; we do not fear it now, and we shall not fear it in the future. This is a prohibitory law, not an ordinary law. An ordinary law strikes at actions. A prohibitory law deprives people of rights; it strikes at the organization itself. This enormous difference must be understood.

No party has ever been subjected to a prohibitory law merely for the fundamental principle it held.

For example, no law was enacted to stop the Congress Party from demanding self-rule.

The British government never enacted a law declaring that a party must not function in support of the principle of self-rule.

Indeed, the first man to demand self-rule was himself a white man!

Direct actions such as making salt, the no-tax campaign, civil disobedience, picketing cloth shops, and burning foreign goods were made punishable.
