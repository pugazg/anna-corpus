## English Translation

Below is the pitiable account of Sayyid Mir Alim Khan, the former ruler of Bukhara, becoming an exile on the Kabul frontier.

He had been the husband of 110 beautiful women and the owner of three and a half crore in gold. Twelve and a half lakh people lived under his rule. The Russian Bolsheviks seized his country and reduced him to the condition of a beggar.

## An Emissary to the International Conference

He sent an emissary to the international conference at Geneva asking that Bukhara be recovered from the Bolsheviks and restored to him. A Soviet governor administered Bukhara. That governor looted all of Sayyid Mir Alim Khan's property and sent it to Russia.

## The Stone Fortress

He had lived inside a stone fortress eight miles long and twenty-eight feet high. The fortress had eleven gates.

About one lakh people lived in that city, which was an important trading centre in Central Asia. At the centre of the fortress, all of Mir Alim Khan's possessions were stored in a stone vault. He possessed gold coins worth three and a half crore pounds, as well as countless jewelled ornaments. He customarily opened the treasury vault only at night and inspected his treasure twice each day.

## A Strange Monarchy

Like the kings of the East, he ruled in a most peculiar manner. He ascended the throne after killing four brothers who claimed a right to it and twenty-five relatives. He impaled political rebels and conspirators without mercy.

## 110 Beauties

He kept 110 beautiful women as wives in his harem. He had also been a major general in the old Russian army and a bodyguard of the Tsar. In the Tsar's time, he himself ruled as an autocrat. His fortune vanished in August 1920.

For a long time, the Soviets had secretly provided financial assistance to the Bukhara Youth Association so that it would revolt against Alim Khan. The youth association began its revolution.

On 5 August 1920, the Soviet army entered Bukhara. All of Alim Khan's troops opened the fortress gates and fled. Alim Khan was shocked. He sent an emissary to Delhi, asking it to recover his three and a half crore pounds and protect him. The Delhi Government did not agree. He therefore fled to Afghanistan. Later, with the help of the British agent in Turkestan, he reached Kabul with five hundred attendants and some property.

Two of his sons were deported to Moscow. The Soviet Government had them educated at a college.

(*Dravida Nadu*, 6-3-49)
