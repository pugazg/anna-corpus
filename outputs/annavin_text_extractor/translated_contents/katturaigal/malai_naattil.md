## English Translation

Because the plays *Manohara*, *Thuli Visham*, *Velaikkari*, *Or Iravu*, and *Chandramohan* have been staged repeatedly in various cities including Singapore, Malacca, Kuala Lumpur, Ipoh, Penang, and Klang, the man who went there planning to stage only twenty performances has been obliged to remain for many more days.

Malayan newspapers report that immense crowds gather and that people enjoy the plays without regard for the lack of amenities.

It is heartening to hear that the scholar of dramatic music is doing more than giving theatrical performances: he is taking part in welcome meetings organised by the various institutions of Tamils living in Malaya, mingling with them, and conducting a campaign to explain conditions in Tamil Nadu.

Many associations are showing great enthusiasm in welcoming comrade Ramasamy.

We are especially delighted to learn that both the Dravidar Kazhagam and the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam in Malaya are welcoming and entertaining K. R. Ramasamy without distinction.

In many parts of Malaya, the Dravidar Kazhagam has given the scholar of dramatic music a warm reception.

It has been more than three years since the Malayan government banned the journal *Dravida Nadu*.

The Malayan government imposed the ban after *Dravida Nadu* published editorials and articles condemning its execution of Tamil youths on the allegation that they were Communists.

The bans on all the other Tamil journals prohibited for the same reason have been lifted, but the ban on *Dravida Nadu* alone remains in force.

It is noteworthy that K. R. Ramasamy is making efforts to remedy this grievance.

In this connection, K. R. Ramasamy met and spoke with the Chief Minister of Malaya, Tunku Rahman. We understand that the Chief Minister promised to examine the matter and do what was necessary.

A photograph taken when K. R. Ramasamy met Malayan Chief Minister Tunku Rahman was published here.

He likewise met and spoke with Singapore Chief Minister Lim Yew Hock.

Malayan government dignitaries have attended receptions and banquets held to welcome K. R. Ramasamy, adding distinction to them.

"I have not come to Malaya to make money and carry it away. I have come to spread good ideas needed by the country through drama and to gain the friendship of the Tamils here," K. R. Ramasamy is reported to have said with feeling at one reception.

We understand that the hundreds of thousands of Tamil plantation workers admire K. R. Ramasamy's acting ability and rejoice in his friendly spirit.

We commend our friend, who is engaged in the worthy work of advancing Tamil art and spreading Tamil culture.

Through K. R. Ramasamy, the affection of the homeland is being conveyed to the Tamils of Malaya.

We congratulate K. R. Ramasamy for undertaking this honourable work.

(*Dravida Nadu*, news item; date printed in damaged OCR as 14-10-26)
