## English Translation

That graceful young woman walked comfortably through the grove. At her waist was a pot filled with water. As she walked swaying her hips, the water in the pot shook and overflowed as though beating time for her dance. The grove's parrots performed their “music” as though accompanying the dance of that golden beauty.

Ananda, whose face poured forth peace and compassion, saw the woman and rejoiced.

“Mother, give me a little water to drink. My heart has dried up,” he asked kindly.

The young woman was alarmed. The whole sea is water, but can it be drunk? The entire forest may flourish, but if it consists of poisonous `etti` trees, whom does it benefit? What rested at her waist was indeed a pot, and the pot contained water from a pond. Yet that tender maiden stood bewildered, believing she could not give it.

“Sir, what can I say? I belong to a Chandala community. How can I give you water? Would it not be a sin?” she asked.

Ananda was a disciple of the Buddha, the teacher of harmony who transcended distinctions of caste and religion. He was therefore free from the madness called difference. He had understood how much foolishness distinction had created in the world and how many divisions it had produced.

Ananda said: “Lady, I asked for water to drink, not for your caste. Does water possess caste? Is the water poured by those called high-born in the town honey, while the water you give is salty? Noble woman, forget the world's foolishness and give me water.”

The maiden gave it. The Buddhist disciple drank and later told many others about the event.

## The Buddha and the Aryans

Many similar incidents occurred while the Buddha laboured to restrain Brahminism and abolish the superstitious customs introduced by it. The Brahmins' “high status” was attacked. The secrets of the sacrifices through which they deceived the town were exposed. The Buddha's new doctrines spread with fresh speed.

What did Aryanism do? Realising that the Buddhist movement would burn it away, it hid. Later it began praising the Buddhist movement. It pretended to embrace some of its ideas, associated with it, and through that association ruined the community. Images began to be erected even for the Buddha, who had denounced image worship. In the end, they turned the Buddha himself into a god, declared him one of the many incarnations of their Lord of the Supreme Abode, and converted the danger confronting them into an advantage. Such is the character of Aryanism.

Aryanism knows at what time and in what manner it can turn anything to its own advantage and profit from it.

Yesterday we said that only one idea remained from Gandhism and only that one idea had proved useful. In a game of chance, all the players may lose and many may become bankrupt, but the house alone makes a profit, because a fixed amount, the “house cut,” is paid to it for every game.

Likewise, the scheme called Gandhism created losses for Gandhi and hardship and loss for the people, but one party made a profit.

The Aryan idea introduced through Gandhi and the movement supporting him benefited the Aryans. Today, that one idea is all that remains of Gandhism. Aryans are those who benefited from it. Although they knew the uselessness of Gandhism's other parts, they continued blandly pretending to agree with them all, saying “Yes, yes,” because Gandhism contained one idea useful to them.

Before Gandhi entered politics, the mania of Aryanism had received blow after blow both from thinkers within the country and from Western civilisation. The secrets of Aryan fabricated stories began to emerge. Condemnation of the manner in which Aryans treated others gained strength.

## Aryan Strategy

After Aryanism entered this land, it divided the country's “strong people” through caste and religious differences and ruled them. To understand how deeply others were mesmerised by Aryan words, it is enough to examine the religious stories Aryans wrote and the social laws and schemes they created. Those immersed in superstitious customs through Aryan teaching fought head-breaking battles among themselves and lost their character.

At the same time, Muslims demonstrated fellowship through the foundational principles of one God and equality for all. Seeing this, Aryans trembled. They feared their trickery would be destroyed and therefore devised a stratagem. It succeeded to some extent.

They campaigned: “Muslims do not belong to this country. They are foreigners, opponents who have come to destroy our religion. Driving them away is sacred service to God.”

One such propaganda book was Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's *Anandamath*. The song in it is “Vande Mataram,” which became Congress's national song.

Among the various conspiracies Aryans devised to suppress Islam, one cultivated internal conflict and failed. Later, they claimed to believe whatever others found attractive in Islam and created separate movements that removed a few superstitious customs and doctrines from Aryanism. Movements created through such competition included the courageous and disciplined Sikh faith, the Brahmo Samaj and Arya Samaj. In the end, these served only to inflame communal hatred and could not prevent Aryanism's decline.

Meanwhile, Western methods entered and began destroying Aryanism still further. Reformers arose to wipe away innumerable disgraces such as sati, child marriage, polygamy and the maintenance of women dedicated as devadasis. The world blessed the reformers' efforts. Aryans alone knew that each reform deepened by another foot the pit being dug for Aryanism. The people's minds began travelling along the path of reform. The obscenity of Aryan religion was explained.

Aryans thought: “We defeated the Buddha. We defeated the Jina. We deceived the people by calling Muslims a different race. But now all these forces have gathered together and approach with Western brilliance. What shall we do? How can we escape?”

## The Nationalist Stratagem

They devised a strategy: if people thought about the condition of society, they would restrain Aryan domination. The people's attention must therefore be diverted. They began speaking of nationalism.

“Does not the white man rule our sacred land today? Should he not be driven away? How beautiful is our country! Was not the Buddha born here? Does not the crescent flag fly here? Did not Vishnu's sacred incarnations and Siva's divine sports occur here? We have surrendered such a country to someone else. Is that proper?” they asked cunningly.

“Yes! We must not surrender it. The foreigner must be driven away,” the people began saying.

“They are caught in the net,” the Aryans rejoiced inwardly. Every day they fed the people nationalism. The townspeople consumed it and slept.

“The white man has seized our country. We must drive him away. His new ideas do not suit us; those too must be expelled,” said the Aryans. The mesmerised others agreed.

“We must recover our ancient greatness. Therefore, the ways established by our ancestors must not be disparaged or spoken of contemptuously. They must not be opposed and destroyed,” the Aryans said.

“Yes,” others replied under Aryan instruction.

The Aryans said self-rule must be Rama Rajya. The others replied, “Yes, yes.”

“Therefore, we must not now speak of ourselves as Hindu or Muslim, Brahmin or non-Brahmin. We must not ask why one rose and another became low. The disputes among us will resolve themselves later. Our present work is solely to end British rule,” said the Aryans, and the others agreed.

Internal agitation and social revolution were thereby stopped. The Aryans rejoiced that the danger rushing towards their heads had passed.

They turned to the English. “Gentlemen, listen! The people are speaking of nationalism. If you give the country's people a share in government at this moment, the agitation will subside,” they said. The British agreed.

Immediately one section of the Aryans campaigned: “May we accept employment from the Englishman? Shall we perform slave labour for the enemy? Shall we wait for a scrap of bone? We must not.”

Our people joined the chorus: “Yes, we must not hunt for government employment. We must not become slaves to the white man.” Then they continued lying beneath the burden of labour with bent backs.

Aryans entered the sphere of government employment as dignitaries, diwans to maharajas, knights, Diwan Bahadurs, members of governors' councils, collectors and executive council members. Seeing them, others began asking, “Why should we too not request a place where they are?”

## Touch It and It Becomes Poison

“Do not hunt for government jobs. Do not divide people by asking, ‘If he may have it, why may I not?’ That is communalism. It is an enemy of nationalism and a friend of the white man,” said the Aryans. “Is that so?” the others responded.

Consequently, the country's great majority suffered without the time or position either to remove social decay or even think about it, because they spent their time and labour solely upon threatening and troubling the white rulers.

The Aryans coated the opium pill of Aryanism with nationalism, gave it to non-Aryans and subdued them. They bent the English to their will, preserved their customary supremacy in society, occupied leadership positions in nationalism, and secured every comfort upon the seats of government.

Nothing assisted the Aryans as much as Gandhism did. They accomplished this through Gandhi himself. For that reason, they pretended to accept even Gandhi's many impractical doctrines as correct, while preparing the way for those doctrines not to endure in society.

That is why we say that the idea still remaining from Gandhism today is Aryanism; that is what proved useful, and the Aryans were those who benefited. Are we wrong?

(*Viduthalai*, 3 August 1940)

The OCR title `வற்றியில் வீழ்ச்சி` is corrected in the display title to `வெற்றியில் வீழ்ச்சி`, supported by the filename and the article's conclusion that Aryans profited from an apparent nationalist victory; the Tamil source remains verbatim. `பிடி ஆட்டம்`, `போஸ்தாகி` and `மேஸ்` describe a gambling game, bankruptcy and the house's fixed cut. `ஜீனர்` refers to the Jina or Mahavira. The section heading `தீண்டினால் திருநீலகண்டம்` is an idiomatic warning that touching the issue makes it poisonous or dangerous. The article's historical and communal claims are translated as the author's polemical argument, not silently recast as neutral historical findings.
