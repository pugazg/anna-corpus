## English Translation

...that request was rejected. The South African government did not accept the Indian government’s request. The Indian government therefore announced that commercial relations with South Africa had been severed and recalled the High Commissioner.

Indians have begun a campaign of resistance by defying South African law.

It appears that a South African named Lieutenant Colonel Preston serves as Commander-in-Chief of the Mysore Government’s forces.

The Government of India and people belonging to the various sections of India are united in condemning the cruelty inflicted upon people from the Indian subcontinent in South Africa. Every political party has condemned it with one voice. Even after all this, the South African government has not changed its ways.

In these circumstances, why should the Mysore State retain a South African as its military commander? How can it be right to give the office of commander of a princely state’s forces to a citizen of a country that dares to insult people of the Indian subcontinent? Why should the Mysore Government not declare, as a sign of its condemnation of South African law, “We do not want a South African commander”?

If the Mysore Government acts courageously, foreign countries will understand that South Africa has received a fitting rebuke for its conduct. If such an event occurs, they will realize that a new awakening and self-respect have truly arisen in the Indian subcontinent. It will also teach the South African government a proper lesson.

Will the Mysore Government come forward to do this?

The Chamber of Princes has issued a circular expressing its bitterness over the South African government’s conduct and declaring that the princely-state governments are prepared to cooperate in the measures taken by the Government of India. In these circumstances, why should the Mysore Government retain a South African as Commander-in-Chief?

The Government of India has even prohibited South Africans from holding “shares” in the Reserve Bank of India. After the Government of India has gone so far in openly condemning South Africa and taking action against it, the Mysore Government must recognize that a South African should not occupy a major and important office in Mysore. It must act immediately and teach the South African government a lesson.

Will the Mysore Government dare to do this?

*Dravida Nadu, 23 June 1946*
