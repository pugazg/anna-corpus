## English Translation

Tamil title: “ஆனாலும்!”

Production is increasing day by day, not merely in one or two fields but in every field - and yet prices have not fallen; on the contrary, they are rising like poison!

We have poured out tens of millions and built dams. We have opened many factories. New schemes are being implemented every day - and yet the affliction of unemployment has not diminished and conditions have not improved!

Without even considering the people's standard of living, how much tax may be collected from them, or how much they can bear, new taxes have been imposed. And yet there remains the critical situation in which the government, saying that the money is insufficient, attempts to manage affairs through ever-new methods!

Countless measures are taken to expand trade and increase exports - and yet exports have not grown!

Every day, slogans are raised: "We are all brothers of this country. Can there be differences, divisions, quarrels and conflicts among us? Is this proper or tolerable?" And yet the spirit of unity has not grown; there is not even a sign that it will grow!

The song of socialism is raised everywhere, emphatically so by the rulers - and yet the strength of the moneyed sharks has not declined, nor has their influence faded!

Many precautionary measures have been taken to prevent disorder in the administration - and yet bribery and other degrading conditions have not been eliminated!

We continue preaching "Peace, peace" to the whole world - and yet we hesitate to reduce military expenditure!

Great dams have been built. We see irrigation schemes being completed - and yet the food-supply situation has come to be arranged in a gratifying manner!

* * *

Such political arguments, each joined to the refrain "and yet," are made daily by our rulers. They are not merely ridiculous; they are capable of creating confusion.

It is natural to explain that this happened for such-and-such reasons, that this resulted from a particular measure, or that this is the outcome of a particular scheme. Such explanations of cause and effect occur everywhere and are also appropriate to reasoned argument.

If today's rulers offered explanations of cause and effect in that manner, not only would everyone understand them, but many people in the country could justly accept and praise them.

If it were explained that production had increased and therefore prices had fallen, everyone could readily understand the explanation. If they said, "We implemented these schemes and built these dams; therefore prosperity has increased throughout the country and the standard of living has risen," everyone would understand and accept the claim.

But in today's politics, we repeatedly find the word "and yet," rather than "therefore," appearing in the rulers' statements.

This is the strange spectacle of present-day politics.

What has produced this curious spectacle? We must certainly understand why the rulers speak in this "and yet" style. Only then will our confusion disappear, clarity arise, and the country's true condition become plainly visible.

On what occasions are such amusing arguments and strange explanations offered? In what circumstances are they employed?

Whenever the people develop anxiety and mental confusion about the future, our rulers deploy this "weapon."

This tendency of the rulers shows nothing other than their desire to prevent agitation from arising in the people's minds.

But mental agitation will disappear and confidence arise only if the people accept the rulers' arguments and explanations. Where have the people accepted them? What evidence is there that they have?

Again and again, the rulers calculate wrongly that the people have accepted their arguments and explanations and have become satisfied.

Until this peculiar habit of making false calculations changes, no enduring and beneficial solution can be found for any problem in the country.

If the rulers sincerely believe that problems must be solved, and solved properly, there is no sense in relying on their skill at covering everything up. Problems will not be solved by attacking every opponent with the challenge, "See how I deal with you," or by somehow drawing a few people to their side and flattering them.

But what do our ministers do? They devote their attention to concealing problems, throwing dust into the public's eyes, abusing those who point out and condemn faults, and mocking them.

By doing so, they imagine that opposition has been destroyed and attain mental satisfaction. Yet, for some reason, they forget that these are not the methods rulers must employ to solve problems. Such methods may postpone problems, but they cannot provide permanent solutions.

Because the rulers possess an excessive numerical majority in the legislative assemblies and Parliament, and because they have the assistance of newspapers that somehow try to conceal their mistakes, they can easily suppress any opposition.

What good is there in concealment of this kind? To whom is it beneficial? It is not merely unwise; it is dangerous.

Let the rulers demonstrate their valour in whatever manner they please. We do not tell them not to do so.

But let them alone refrain from the false calculation that the people believe their explanations and arguments.

Let them answer the objections raised by the opposition parties. Let them demonstrate where the opposition's arguments are hollow and where they are mistaken. We welcome that and say it is precisely what is needed.

And yet they devote their attention and activity to dismissing and attacking the views of their opponents!

In our state, the ministers derive a certain pleasure without even saying what it is that is "needed."
