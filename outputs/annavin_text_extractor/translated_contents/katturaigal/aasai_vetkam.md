## English Translation

Tamil title: ஆசை, வெட்கம் அறியாது!

"Soldiers! You lie hungry, without even half enough clothing upon your bodies. The very sight fills me with pity. Must soldiers endure such suffering? Our government too is troubled, but what can it do? We can express our gratitude, but we are in no position to give you good food or sufficient clothing.

"Your courage and determination have made you this country's victorious sons. The people praise you, saying, 'What great warriors are ours!' I too swell with pride when I hear the tributes you have received. You have won fame and distinction, but not adequate reward or comfort.

"Your hardship and poverty stand visibly before my eyes, and I grieve. Nevertheless, I summon you to war, boldly and with the affection due to my comrades. Anxiety stands on one side and duty on the other. Soldiers, which is greater, anxiety or duty? Set forth! We shall cross the wealthy petty kingdoms.

"We shall conquer beautiful cities. We shall make the Indus and the Alps bow before us. Cast aside domestic worries. Mother France awaits you. We shall pour gold and glory at her feet. Let our enemies be bewildered by your great valour. O lamps of France's glory, must I speak to you about courage?"

* * *

This was the triumphant call that made the nineteenth century shine with heroic letters upon the pages of history. After assuming office, the great warrior Napoleon, who made the entire world tremble, raised this roar to summon his armies to war.

The cry filled weary and distressed French soldiers with martial fury. Forgetting poverty and disregarding hunger, they sounded the conch call: "War! War!"

The question, "Sons of France, must I explain courage to you?" caressed their hearts and stirred the frenzy and passion of "victory or a hero's death." They forgot hunger and poverty and made the entire European continent tremble.

Napoleon's voice kindled the fire of courage in suffering hearts.

There was hunger at home, but they did not falter. Their families were in turmoil, but love of country did not let those great warriors tremble. A wife's tears drove them to drink their enemies' red blood rather than restraining them. Their devotion to France grew so intense. The great warrior's heroic voice transformed affection and devotion into frenzy.

They fought with the passion, "We shall bring the French Empire into being." That single voice drove them to war.

"Must I explain courage to you?" This one sentence stirred the tireless desire that their country's glory should spread everywhere, and filled the soldiers with martial passion.

"We," "ours," "our country" and "our people": students of political history know the list of those who displayed such words of unity, aroused courage and frenzy, and accomplished their own purposes.

More often than bringing benefit to the people, this rhetoric of unity has subjected them to cruelty.

Many tyrants, like Hitler and Mussolini, used patriotism and the people's concern for their country as pieces in a political gambling game and emerged as its victors.

Many agents of destruction travelled the path of making patriotism in the public forum blaze forth and then fulfilling their selfish desires beneath its cloak.

Seeing his soldiers tormented by hunger, Napoleon threw out a single phrase: "Must I explain courage to you?"

That one sentence made them forget their pain and drove them into a frenzy.

It is an extremely cunning act to stroke a desire buried deep in the people's hearts and use its force to establish one's own autocracy.

Some call this method "statecraft."

This device has sometimes turned sleeping people into leaping tigers. Similar heroic calls have made stagnant societies rise in strength when they had lost the ability to protect themselves as their property was seized.

Many countries bound in the chains of slavery were inflamed by the words, "Are you slaves?"

"We are forty crores of people. The Englishman lives six thousand miles away. Must we live as his slaves?" The rising cry of freedom produced floods of blood upon Indian soil, bullets piercing human bodies, multitudes subjected to suffering, women who lost their husbands, and mothers who gave their sons to death. They were not few; they were many, very many.

The courageous realization "We are slaves" raised both storm and earthquake upon Indian soil.

The people treated the voices of the heroic leaders who aroused that awakening like divine music. They did not merely honour those voices; each person came forward to turn them into accomplished action.

The reason was that the calls raised by the popular leaders awakened a genuine feeling. The people saw the light of hope in their voices; their speeches sounded as a new music.

It was not simply because "the leaders say so" that people leapt into the fire of sacrifice.

The thought spread through every mind: "There is truth in what the leaders say. Under foreign rule, we experience only suffering."

The leaders' voices made the spreading flame expand many times over.

The country achieved victory. The people's affection and devotion towards their leaders increased, as did their trust and goodwill towards the leaders' words.

"We have obtained freedom, and our leaders were its driving force. They will turn our country into a land of gold," the people believed.

Because of that belief, they did everything the leaders said.

With guileless hearts they thought, "The leaders' word is law. Every act of theirs is without blemish."

When Napoleon summoned his soldiers to war, the French soldiers forgot their poverty and went to the battlefield because they trusted him.

Indian leaders had acquired similar trust from the people and could therefore proceed as they pleased.

"War in Kashmir," they said. The country said, "Very well."

"The refugee problem," they said. The people agreed.

"We need higher taxes to meet expenditure." The people merely asked, "Is that so?" and did not refuse.

At one moment came, "There is no money to provide the necessary benefits."

At another, "We can give only six ounces."

In many forms, troubled cries rose from those seated in government.

At first, the people believed them without question. Because their faith in the leaders had not diminished, they imagined cruelty and poverty would disappear within a short time.

Days became years. Grievances only increased; they did not decline.

A question mark arose in the country's heart. The question then grew into dissatisfaction and has now transformed into anger.

The former light of confidence in those called popular leaders has faded.

As the people repeatedly see these rulers behave chaotically and irresponsibly instead of as expected, conditions have changed until they think of them contemptuously and say, "Shame!"

This is not confined to the public forum. Even within the Congress Party, once regarded as a machine endowed with strength and power, the dissatisfaction of volunteers towards their leaders has grown and begun to radiate heat.

Newspapers that once praised and celebrated them have begun, at one moment, to offer instruction and, at another, to condemn them with pain.

*Vikatan* publishes cartoons about their rule.

*Mithran*, though not fiery, does not fail to condemn its disorder.

*Dinamani* occasionally exposes corruption with its pen.

*Dinasari* writes every day with ringing mockery and condemnation.

*Kalki* frequently offers advice and counsel.

And *Gandibam*? Do not even ask. It heaps forth the ministers' daily corruption unknown to others.

The newspapers do not merely write. No, no: they rain down a bombardment.

Whenever four volunteers meet, and in committee meetings, they release the storms within their minds.

That is not all. They oppose the leaders they once honoured.

Yes, they oppose them.

To that extent has confidence in the leaders collapsed. The conduct of Congress dignitaries has become disgraceful enough to make it collapse.

And "disgraceful" is no small word. Here are one or two examples.

A Madras minister inaugurated a hotel in the city. Within a few months, it reportedly became a brothel. It also became a place where smuggled gold, silver and other goods were stored.

The police arrested two employees from the house of a Madras minister while they were drinking illicit liquor.

What disgrace, what obscenity! The lives surrounding those seated as ministers have settled into such repulsive paths, and the ministers too have begun to live accordingly.

If one investigates the ministers of Madras State, as *Dinasari* says, one minister travels from one celebration of dancing queens to another; another labours to discover every possible form of personal benefit; and yet another devotes excessive attention to cinema studios and picture theatres.

They failed to understand the importance of governing and began dancing along a chaotic course.

The public's confidence in them has therefore been destroyed and shattered. Abuse and denunciation now abound.

Perhaps the approach of the general election has caused them to recognize this truth.

For some time now, wherever they go, our sagacious ministers have begun singing laments:

"Members of the public, you suffer so much. The government is not unaware of your difficulties. But what can it do? We too wish to remove them, but cannot. Economic hardship strikes like a spear upon a wound. Do not be angry. Show us sympathy."

Beginning in this way, and like Napoleon summoning troops to war, they appeal, "In the general election, vote only for Congress. Elect us!"

They then conclude in the style of the great French warrior: "The world knows that the Indian people do not forget gratitude. Need we say any more?"

Speaking at Pachaiyappa's College in Madras on the seventh of this month, our Finance Minister reportedly said, "What the government has done thus far must be appreciated sympathetically."

As the election approaches, this phase of prostrating at the sacred feet has begun to bud.

But, poor men, the people are neither deaf enough nor intellectually blind enough to believe what they say.

Had this occurred in earlier days, the people would have accepted anything they were told and set forth with martial passion like soldiers hearing Napoleon's call.

But now they have no confidence at all in Congress.

Desire apparently drives the ministers to employ every stratagem.

How loudly they roared until now! How many times did they send men to beat those who exposed corruption in their administration? The spectacles of rule by the gun, the occasions when they leapt up crying, "We shall suppress this impudence" - even if they forget, can the country forget?

Consider the dismissive attitude shown to those who approached them with advice: "Do this, and do not fail to do that." Consider their uncontrollable ferocity and outrageous demonstrations.

Forgetting all this, they now expect the public to show sympathy.

The moment they realize the people have lost confidence, they begin turning into singers who intone, "Your sacred feet are our refuge; protect us."

Suppose an unfaithful woman said to her husband, "Ayyo, my dear! I desired that man, and so I may have behaved wrongly. Desire and attachment are natural to human beings. Forget it and forgive me."

A man without honour might say, "Do not grieve, my doe. Let bygones be bygones." If he possessed a little anger, however, she would be swimming in blood that day or driven terrified into the streets.

Yet our ministers feel no shame in saying, "We may have been unfaithful, but show us sympathy."

They have begun speaking in this style: "Mistakes may have occurred; nevertheless, show sympathy."

They boldly demand sympathy.

People say desire knows no shame.

The occupants of the Congress seat have reached that excessive company. That is why they have begun singing, "At your sacred feet we surrender, O protector of the humble."

At one time, the refrain is, "Are we not all the people of India?"

At another, they use the unity slogan, "United we live."

At yet another, they say, "You have supported us; must we explain anything to you?"

After performing these varied dances of the tongue, they now set forth singing, "At your sacred feet we surrender; bestow your grace upon us."

Fortunately, neither the people nor the country are in a condition to believe it.

*Dravida Nadu*, 16 September 1951.
