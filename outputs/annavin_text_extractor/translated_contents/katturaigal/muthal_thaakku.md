## English Translation

"Born into the sacred Brahmin caste, you commit a great sin that even a lowly Chandala would shrink from. Wretch! Why were you born as our son? Have all the evil deeds of our former births gathered together and arisen as a son to us? Hinduism is an ancient path. Our Vedas and shastras are sacred books. Those who disregard them, you contemptible man, will enter a hell from which there is no return. Will society leave unpunished one who acts against scripture? If a man born a Brahmin violates the Vedas and shastras, how will all the other castes preserve their respective dharmas? Everything will be destroyed."

"You are afraid because you do not understand. I am not one who reviles the Vedas, and what I do is not a sin. Can the Veda be understood merely by chanting without grasping its inner meaning, like frogs croaking in a well? Only after deep reflection did I realize that *sati* does not possess scriptural sanction. You accept as spiritual truth the words of impostors who falsely claim to know the Vedas beyond all doubt. People throughout the world see this, mock us, and call us savages and barbarians who perform murderous acts without shame. I say that sati must end, not that the scriptures must be rejected. I say, do not push young widows into blazing fire and roast them alive; I do not deny the Veda."

"Who are you to offer a new interpretation of the Vedas? There are crown jewels of Vedic and Vedantic learning, scholars of grammar, and masters of every shastra to provide interpretation. What you utter is empty argument, nothing but atheism."

"No. I do not deny God, nor do I despise religion. Among those whom you worship as qualified to explain the meaning of the Vedas and shastras, some are impostors and many struggle without knowing the truth."

"You consider yourself a great genius. What immense arrogance fills your head! Did not our elders establish sati as sanctioned by scripture? Who gave you authority to criticize, condemn, or reject it? Brahmins are the guardians of Aryan dharma. Born in that caste, you have become the handle of the axe that fells it. Mother Earth herself will refuse to bear you; how can we accept you? You are no son of ours. This womb that carried a great sinner who would destroy a religion filled with divine greatness has become a pit of sin."

"This anger is futile; it arises from failure to understand."

The most critical stage in Raja Ram Mohan Roy's life came when his parents and caste elders called him a "great sinner" and prepared to excommunicate him.

He endured the opposition of his caste and the hatred of his parents.

He was driven from his family and denounced by his community. Even so, he would not abandon his principle. Determined that the burning of widows called sati must be stopped, he travelled across the country conducting a whirlwind campaign.

He did not hesitate to debate the pandits who boasted that they had mastered the Vedas and shastras, issued blind commands, spread fear, and dominated society. He used those very Vedas and shastras as the basis of his argument.

He studied not only Hinduism's foundational texts but religious works more broadly, probing again and again for the truth.

He did not confine his campaign of knowledge and moral struggle to the Indian subcontinent. He travelled to England, argued his case, and fought.

The British feared that intervening in religion was dangerous and would provoke major opposition to their empire. They therefore left intact many superstitions they encountered here instead of uprooting them. Though they knew that the cruelty of burning a widow with her husband existed nowhere else in the world, they did not prevent it in India.

Raja Ram Mohan Roy declared that this was the injustice of submitting to ignorance and the crime of complicity in murder.

He spoke in many forums in England.

The reformer's argument spread through the British Parliament, and sati was prohibited by law.

Raja Ram Mohan Roy, who had wept after witnessing with his own eyes the cruelty of widow burning, wiped his tears only after sati was legally banned.

What Raja Ram Mohan Roy delivered was the first assault upon a Hindu religious order saturated with superstition. It was also the first victory.

Was the opposition raised against him ordinary? He was forced to leave his own family, yet he did not fear.

In Raja Ram Mohan Roy's time the pandits truly were learned authorities. The Vedas and sacrificial rites had not yet become tales of a vanished past; the speech of the "cultured men" along the Ganges was then law within society. There was no one engaged in spreading rational thought. Nevertheless, he courageously performed that service. He accepted the slur "atheist" and achieved victory.

Society abused him as an atheist, his family turned against him, and his caste hated him. Incited by religious ascetics, they accused him of engaging in the sinful work of destroying Hinduism. Fearing nothing, he launched the first assault upon the fortress filled with blind doctrines and won.

14 January 1956.
