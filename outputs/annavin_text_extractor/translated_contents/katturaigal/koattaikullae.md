## English Translation

> Jeeva, A.S.K. Iyengar, K.T. Raju, and the J.P. Purushotham group have deserted the working-class battle front.

> They are the new traitors to Tamil Nadu's working class.

> Let us regard them and those who have joined them as class enemies, put an end to their activities, and save the working-class struggle.

> They are selfish men who entered the South Indian Railway trade-union movement and sought influence there.

> There is no difference between the agents of capitalism, the blacklegs who break strikes, and this treacherous gang.

> The K.T. Raju-Jeevanandam group is the blackleg force of Congress capitalism.

> This gang used the working class as a ladder for their own influence.

> When they uttered the names of Lenin and Stalin and cried, "Hail the Red Flag!", it was to deceive the working class and protect their selfish interests.

What harsh words and cruel spears have been hurled against communist leaders such as P. Jeevanandam!

One is tempted to ask: whose work is this? Who said these things? Was it at a Congress conference or a meeting of some rival party?

Yet these words, filled with such speed and anger, were thrown at them not by the Congress party, nor by its puppet, the Indian National Trade Union. What we have given above are portions of a long resolution passed by the South Indian Railway Workers' Conference.

Yes, do not doubt it! The very union in which these men participated and laboured read out this "certificate of honour" when it met in Madras on 28 and 29 January.

When one says "Jeevanandam" or "Jeeva," it is the Communist Party, rather than his physical form, that immediately comes to anyone's mind. Yet the South Indian Railway union angrily bestowed the titles

Traitors,

Self-seekers,

Blacklegs,

Hooligans,

not only upon him, but also upon K.T. Raju, Banu, Purushotham, Lingappan, Krupapuri, Manickam, Velayutham, Sethu, Bhaskaran, Variyar, Mari, Rajamani, Andi, Krishnasami, Thomas Masilamani, and others who had laboured beside him day and night.

The weekly *Porani*, which now serves as a *Janasakthi* and presents communist thought, published this news under furious headlines. Pens that once wrote proudly, "Our leader, our Jeeva," have now inscribed the words "traitor" and "self-seeker."

The same trade union that once sounded the trumpet declaring that his work would remove its difficulties has now passed a resolution demanding "action" against these men.

The South Indian Railway Workers' Conference that passed the resolution belongs to the communists.

The journal that printed it in bold type under furious headlines belongs to them too.

It is therefore evident that a split has arisen within the Communist Party of Tamil Nadu and that people such as Jeeva, disliking the party's course, have turned their thought in another direction.

The reason for the resolution pouring such flames of anger upon them and demanding their expulsion from the party is that, while in prison, they did not participate in the struggle conducted by the other communist prisoners.

It had long been said that two divisions or factions existed.

Recently, reports arrived that P.C. Joshi and several others had left.

At the same time, some newspapers reported that Jeevanandam was saying he disliked the Communist Party's course.

Now *Porani* has attached the word "traitor," crowned him with the titles "blackleg" and "self-seeker," and fired arrows of denunciation filled with agitation.

No details concerning these matters have emerged for us. Nor can the public learn them, because Comrade Jeevanandam, the accused man, remains in prison.

But one thing is clearly visible to thought.

Stabbing and slashing have begun within the fortress itself. The division between those who favour the path of violence and those who reject it has widened. Opposition has arisen even among people such as Jeeva, worn down by many years of labour. Darkness is closing in. That is what these events show.

*Dravida Nadu*, 26 February 1950.
