## English Translation

Whether one is a crowned monarch, a sage who has transcended the three impurities, or a villain who has committed many evils, anyone who sees that all must finally become a handful of ash is moved by many pure thoughts. “Was it for this, merely to become one with this earth, that we ruined the lives of many for the sake of our own, and willingly committed the five great sins?” Looking back, people suffer over the deeds they have done.

At the cremation ground they grasp a subtle truth: those who lived amid publicly displayed splendour and the pauper who wandered, crying “Hunger! Hunger!” with a begging bowl yet could not satisfy it, all finally mingle with the same earth. They then resolve to abandon their former ways, love everyone, and perform many good deeds for the welfare of others. But how long does this noble disposition remain? Within a few minutes that clarity changes. The transformation produced by seeing a corpse does not endure.

Once the corpse is out of sight, thoughts of home and one's own life seize the mind. The sight of wife, children, and other relatives, joined by self-interest, makes one forget the lesson learned at the cremation ground. As before, the struggle of life creates a setting in which one must seek prosperity by taking deceit as a sword and selfishness as a shield, cheating the credulous and bringing down opponents.

If the detachment felt at the cremation ground is to remain permanently among the people and humane qualities are to grow, the social foundation and its institutions must be reconstructed accordingly. Without successfully completing this preliminary task, one may recoil from and become disgusted by the consequences of the present social order. But to imagine that success can be achieved merely through eloquent sermons on good conduct will end only in disappointment.

Even when those capable of preaching pure virtues live with purity in life and heart, people merely love them, declare their devotion, and feel satisfied. Unable to practise their teaching exactly, they sigh, “Could ordinary people like us ever follow the path he showed?” They define their own helplessness and continue as before.

Buddha was born, renounced royal office, and showed the way to a pure life. He was designated an incarnation and placed among the gods. He became an object of worship and festivals are celebrated for him. Some have made comfortable lives for themselves by telling the country of his teachings and noble ideas. Yet the condition of the people remains what it was before Buddha was born!

Jesus appeared and delivered many counsels. He tried to create a realm of happiness in this world. He embraced non-killing to the extent of sacrificing his own life and shed his blood. Many people are said to follow his path. Yet his ideal has not been fulfilled. The people have been unable to accept his words and act accordingly.

Though people proudly call themselves followers of Jesus's path of love, the struggle for existence has not allowed them to walk that path. They recognise the condition, yet refuse to conclude that a new society can arise only by transforming the conditions of life, that only in such a society can the teachings of Jesus take root and flourish, and that they must work toward it. Jesus therefore remains confined to the church. His ideas remain on paper. Instead of occupying people's hearts, they are displaced by the many other thoughts and decisions demanded by life's struggle. Like duckweed that parts and closes again before an eyelid can fall, Jesus enters the human heart and immediately disappears.

We saw Gandhi's path and his doctrine of nonviolence ignored, even during his lifetime, by people enslaved to other ideas; he too saw it and grieved. In the end he gave his life for the principles he held.

It is one matter that not all people can follow his lofty teaching. But would anyone dare say that the Pandit and Patel, who invoke his name without pause on every occasion and gained elevation, popular support, and power through him, are following his path? Scholars must consider whether, even if they cannot practise his entire way, they act according to his teaching in the one or two matters that were questions of life itself to him.

Jawahar proudly declared at the United Nations: “Behold, people of the world, this wonder! The mighty British Empire bowed solely before soul-force and our refusal to submit to evil. Behold the power of the path shown by our master!” Yet after testifying verbally to Gandhi's way, he nurtures in practice an ambition extending even to the making of the atom bomb, and proclaims, “Behold Delhi's greatness! See our military organisation!” By whatever Gandhian standard it is judged, is conduct that disgraces the soul-force said to have humbled imperialism not an offence?

Jawahar advised the delegates of many nations gathered at the United Nations to follow the path shown by our master so that the world might be set right. They listened with pleasure. Many thoughts must certainly have arisen in their minds. They may even have felt a momentary, cremation-ground detachment. But their thinking would not have stopped there.

They will compare the power of soul-force and the eloquent speech praising it with the deeds that took place, and continue to take place, in India under Jawahar's authority. They will analyse carefully where word and deed part company and why. They will weigh the actions of the hero who explains his country's freedom as a victory of soul-force against their own actions, for which they are accused of disbelieving in it, and ask where the two differ and where they agree. What conclusion will they reach?

They will conclude that the soul-force celebrated for defeating imperialism is used only for beautiful speeches, while the country that speaks of it also relies on violence and modern weapons to win further victories and fulfil its aims. They will say that in practice there is no difference between the birthplace of soul-force and their countries, which are said to lack it. For other reasons they may lack the courage to say this publicly and keep within their own minds the contradiction between the Pandit's words and deeds.

The scholars gathered at the United Nations will compare the war in Palestine, the civil disorder in Greece, the alignment in Berlin, and the measures taken to suppress labour unrest in France with events in the East: the past and continuing Sino-Japanese War, the Chinese Civil War, unrest in Burma, the war in Java, disorder in Malaya, and the uprising in Indochina. They will reflect upon both the strength of the Red Army of atheist Russia and the military power of Christian America.

India speaks ornamentally of soul-force as greater than the atom bomb. Yet in Kashmir, under rulers who shine as heroes of soul-force, that force has been abandoned and new weapons used to repel an invasion. People will speak of this puzzle among themselves. India places exactly as much faith in military victory and brute force as other nations do. There is not a particle of difference between their actions and India's. They will ask why the Pandit seeks to cover all this with the veil called soul-force. They will think, and laugh inwardly.

## 8. Art and Mass Murder!

Here is the sinner who killed sixteen people! He poisoned sixteen people to death!

He made sixteen people corpses and carried away the money he had robbed!

That is what the court said. The murderer did not deny the charge.

“It is true! It is true that I poisoned sixteen people to death! It is also true that I stole the money and carried it away!

“But do not merely call me a sinner and a mass murderer! I am an artist, not only a killer. I committed mass murder; yet when those sixteen fell as corpses, I became not only a murderer but also a devotee. I prayed that the souls of the dead might find peace.

“I became a killer so that I might live as an artist and obtain love! Even after the mass murder, I did not fail to pray.”

The accused said this and reportedly submitted a fifty-eight-page confession.

Hirasawa was the murderer's name. Disguised as a health inspector, he entered a bank in Tokyo. Claiming that dysentery was spreading in the city and that he had come to administer preventive medicine, he gave poison as medicine to the sixteen clerks working there. Calmly and without the slightest agitation, he administered the poison. After sixteen bodies had fallen, he prayed to God for peace for their souls, gathered up the available money, and fled. Once captured and brought before the court, he admitted the crime and also declared, “I am an artist!”

He was reportedly the head of an art association in Tokyo. The association, devoted to the development of art, needed funds, and in his enthusiasm for art he had accepted responsibility for finding them. He too needed money, and not for an ordinary purpose, but for love! Thus the criminal said that he murdered for art and for love. “The act was indeed criminal, but look at my purpose. Remember that this was a murder committed for art,” said Hirasawa. This event took place in Japan in October: murder for art!

No one would dare commit so wicked a deed, it may be said. He did so only because he was a thoroughly hardened criminal; only because he was extraordinarily callous did he dare declare that he had killed for art.

Our question here is not whether murder may be committed for art. We ask whether anything at all may be done for art; whether every act performed in art's name may be considered just; and whether it is right to justify anything by saying it was done with an artistic purpose. No one here has carried out mass murder and robbery for art. But many murders of knowledge and plunderings of self-respect have been carried out in art's name.

Suppose an artist genuinely wished to display his skill by painting the anguish on the face of a tiny infant dying without its mother's milk or any substitute food or drink. Suppose he stole a baby from its mother, laid it alone without milk or drink, stood beside it observing, dipped his brush in the colours, and painted. Suppose he then convened the town council, displayed the painting before connoisseurs, delighted in hearing everyone exclaim, “Ah! Wonderful! Wonderful!”, accepted the title of sovereign of art, and began to thank them. If he then said, “Hear the reason this painting possesses such artistry,” and revealed the infanticide he had committed, what would happen? What fate would befall that sovereign of art?

The Japanese murderer advanced the argument that anything may be done for art, and put it into practice through a cruel deed. Although they have not committed such mass murders, there are people in this country who, in art's name, have poisoned the lives of the people. Some have massacred the qualities of the community for art. Saying, “See how I have added artistic beauty,” they have destroyed people's reason and reviled knowledge. There are also art lovers who celebrate those who committed intellectual murder for art.

Someone asks, “What is this picture? It is unbearable to look at: women in naked poses and a man playing the flute as he watches them. Why was it painted this way? What state of mind will it produce in the men and women who see it?” At once comes the attacking reply: “Ignorant man! Man without artistic feeling! You do not understand its artistry. You think it arouses lust, but that was not the purpose for which it was painted. It is for art, to display artistic beauty!”

Erotic play and obscene acts have been turned into stone images and installed in temple towers, precincts, and halls. If one asks whether such obscenities of lustful sport should occupy a place meant to teach purity, the answer is angry: “Small-minded man! You speak without knowing the excellence of sculpture. It is stone, carved only with a little chisel held in the hand. But look at the expression! See the face of the mother in labour. It is for art, brother, for art!”

If one asks, “The song and course of the story are unfit for the home. They do not suit devotion or lead to good conduct. Is this an epic?” the art lovers become fiercely angry. “Do you dislike the story? Throw it aside! Do you dislike its religious teaching? Throw that aside! Look at the art in it, the art! Even if the tale is unbelievable, read it for art. Even if its religious teaching does not follow reason, read it for art!” they say.

They have committed intellectual murder for art, and some still defend it in art's name.

In Tokyo an artist committed murder for art, and the court convicted him. Here, those who committed intellectual murder in art's name, and those who spread that “art,” ought to be made to stand before the tribunal of reason and questioned if knowledge is to be saved from destruction. Instead, in a strange and painful reversal, those who expose intellectual murder committed in art's name are dragged forward, placed not before the tribunal of reason but upon the artistic stage, and accused!

Yet the day when the tribunal of reason will examine those who believe that anything may be done for art and anything depicted in its name is rapidly approaching.

Even now, under the rule of Ramana's disciples, orders have been issued to remove obscene stone images from temples and destroy obscene paintings. In many temples one can see repellent obscene scenes carved in stone, deeds carried out in art's name. Now that “art” is being destroyed. The arrogant belief that anything may be done in art's name and accepted however it appears is dying. If, even during the rule of administrators so infatuated with antiquity that they grant a holiday for Avani Avittam, an order has been issued declaring that obscenity can no longer be permitted merely by invoking art, then...!

*(Published in Dravida Nadu, 28 November 1948.)*
