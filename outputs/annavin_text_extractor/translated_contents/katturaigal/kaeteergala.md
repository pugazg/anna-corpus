## English Translation

...that the appointment had not been made with the consent of those concerned; that Delhi had decided to make him Governor of Madras and, before issuing the announcement, had not consulted the Chief Minister of Madras about it; that Pandit Nehru later asked the Chief Minister by trunk telephone whether there was any objection to the appointment; that the Chief Minister replied that he knew nothing about the Maharaja and therefore had nothing to say; that he further asked Pandit Nehru whether the incoming Governor would be able to assist in conducting the administration; and that Pandit Nehru answered that he did not know whether the new Governor would be of assistance to the ministry, but that the Maharaja possessed a good and egalitarian disposition.

This report appeared in *The Mail* of 8 September 1948 under the heading “Press Report.”

If the authority above exercises arbitrary power without even informing the Chief Minister responsible for a province who is coming as Governor, what kind of person he is, whether he possesses administrative ability, or whether he can at least assist in conducting the administration, what greater method could there be of humiliating a province? We ask this question.

Nor is that all. It is said that the central authority did not even inform the Chief Minister of Madras beforehand whom it had appointed Governor of Madras. Another amusing feature is that even Pandit Nehru apparently knew nothing about the Bhavnagar Maharaja’s administrative ability. Apart from saying that he was “a pleasant man to know,” Nehru reportedly knew nothing else about the new Governor.

Given this state of affairs, it is not known through whose favour the new Governor obtained the office. The Chief Minister of Madras knew nothing about the appointment and was not informed. Pandit Nehru knew nothing even of the new Governor’s administrative ability. Who, then, is responsible for the appointment? What truth can we infer other than that the private effort of some individual made the Bhavnagar Maharaja Governor of Madras?

A man unknown to the Chief Minister; a man whom Pandit Nehru could not explain; a man not desired by the people of the province; a man introduced over the telephone with the words, “This is your Governor”; a man who is not a resident of Madras Province: such a man has come as Governor of Madras Province.

If the public says that it cannot accept such a person as Governor, it is punished with imprisonment! What kind of justice is this? Is this democratic government? Let the public answer.

*Dravida Nadu, 12 September 1948*
