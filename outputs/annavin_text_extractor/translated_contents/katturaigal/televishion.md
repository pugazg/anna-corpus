41. டெலிவிஷன்
விஞ்ஞானம் தரும் விருந்தோ,: உலகத்தை வியப்பில்
ஆழ்த்தி விடுகிறது!
சில விநோதங்களை நினைத்துக் கொண்டாலே, நெஞ்சு
வீங்கிவிடும்-ஆச்சரியத்தால்:

விஞ்ஞானத்தின் அறுவடையைக் கண்டு ஆனந்தம்
கொள்ளாதவர் எவருமே இருக்க முடியாது!

விஞ்ஞானம்-ஓரு வளரும் நட்சத்திரம்; நட்சத்திரம் வளர
வளர, அதன் ஒளியின் அளவும்-பரவும் பரப்பும் விரிந்து
கொண்டுதானே போகும்!

இதுவரை, விஞ்ஞானத்தின் விருந்தை நாம்
சுவைத்தவர்களே தவிர, சமைத்தவர்களல்லர்!

விருந்தை வெளிநாட்டார் படைத்து அழைத்தால், போய்
அருந்திவிட்டுக் குறை: பேசியிருக்கிறோமே தவிர,
வேறெதையும் சொல்லக் கற்றுக் கொண்டோமில்லை!

அகல் விளக்கைத் தாண்டி அரிக்கன் விளக்குக்கூட,
சொந்த மூளையிலே வந்ததாகக் கூறமுடியவில்லை; இது
குறித்து வெட்கமோ-வேதனையோகூடத் தோன்றவில்லை

யென்றால் நாம் என்ன செய்வது?

விஞ்ஞானம்-நம்மிடம் மாற்றாந்தாய் . மனப்பான்மையிலே
நடந்துகொள்ளாவிட்டாலும், நம்மில் சிலர் அதனை மாற்றாந்
தாயாக்கிவிட்டு - மனதில் வந்தபடியெல்லாம் கற்பனை
செய்து - கண்டபடி பேசியிருக்கிறார்கள்! பேசும் பிரமங்கள்
இருக்கத்தான் செய்கின்றன!

சூரியனைத் தேவனாக்கி-தேரைக் கொடுத்து-சாயாதேவி
யையும் .பக்கத்தில் அமர்த்தி-ஓற்றைச் சக்கரத்தையும்
பூட்டிவிட்டு வணங்கியிருக்கிறார்கள்!

சந்திரனையும், தாரையுடன் “தாறுமாறாக' நடக்க
விட்டுத்தான் தொழுதிருக்கிறார்கள்! :

நட்சத்திரங்களைத் தேவர்களாகக் கண்டனரே தவிர,
அவைகளை, “பூவுலகைப்போல் பன்மடங்கு பெரிய அளவில்
உள்ள உலகங்கள்” என்பதை உணரவில்லை!

விஞ்ஞானம்-ஒரு கால வேகத்தின் கண்ணாடியென்பதை
மறந்து, விஞ்ஞான வளர்ச்சியை விபரீதக் கண்ணாடி
மாட்டி-விளங்காத அர்த்தங்கள் கூறி-எகிர்க்கிறார்கள்!

ரேடியோவைக் காலையிலும்: மாலையிலும் திருப்பாத
கரங்கள் இல்லையென்று சொல்லலாம்; காலையிலே
ரேடியோவைத் திருப்பிவிட்டு விட்டு, பிறகுதான் விபூதி
மடலைத் தேடியிருக்கும்-திருவாசகத்தைப் புரட்டியிருக்கும்-
“தேவார ஏட்டைப் பிரித்திருக்கும்-அந்தக் கரங்கள்!

விஞ்ஞானம்-இவர்கள் வீட்டுக் கிள்ளுக்கீரை போல
நினைத்துக் கொண்டிருக்கிறார்கள்; ஏனெனில், அயல்
நாட்டார் சிந்தனை தரும் விருந்தாக அது போய்விடுவதால்,
அதில் புதைந்து கிடக்கும் புதுமைப் புதையலும்-அதனைத்
தோண்டியெடுக்க மனித மூளை விடும் பெருமூச்சும்

அவர்களுக்குத் தெரிவதில்லை; இல்லாவிட்டால், “ஆகாச
வாணி” என்று பெயரிட்டு, புதுமையை, பழைமைப்
பெயரால் அழைத்தாவது திருப்தி அடைவார்களா?

அதுபோலத்தான், பறந்து செல்லும். விமானத்தைக்
கண்டதில்லை, இவர்கள்; ஆகாய விமானம் வந்தால் மட்டும்,
“ஆகா - அது இராமாயணத்திலேயே, வருகிறதே; சீதையைக்
கவர்ந்த இராவணன் எதில் சென்றான் - புஷ்பக விமானத்தில்
அன்றோ; அதைவிடவா இது?” என்று நா குழறாமல் பேசுவர்!

இவர்களாவது பாமரர்; இவர்களைப் பரிபாலிக்கும்
சர்க்கார், பறந்திடும் விமானத்திற்கு, “அனுமான்” என்று
பெயரிட்டனரே - இதனை எங்கே போய்ச் சொல்வது
எப்படிக் கூறுவது?

இந்த நாட்டில்தான் விசித்திரத்தைப் பார்க்க முடியும்!

புதுமையைக் கூட, பழைமைத் திரையிட்டுப் பார்த்து
மகிழ்வதிலே ஒரு மகிழ்ச்சி காண்கிறார்கள்!

ரேடியோவை, “ஆகாசவாணி” என்று கூப்பிட்டு
ஆனந்தம் அடைகிறார்கள்!

விமானத்தை “அனுமான்” என்று அழைத்து - அதனால்,
“பழைமை பாதுகாக்கப்பட்டது” என்று தப்புக் கணக்குப்
போடுகிறார்கள்.

விஞ்ஞானம் - இந்த வீர்களுக்குப் பயந்து கொண்டு
தனது புதுமைக் கரத்தை விரிக்காமலிருந்து விடவில்லை;
அது, தன் விநோதச் சிறகசைத்து - விசித்திர வானத்திலே
புதுமைக் கீதம் பாடிப் பறந்து சென்றிடும் வானம்பாடி
யாகவே இருக்கிறது!

புதுமைப் பொம்மையானாலும், அதற்குப் பழைமை

விடவா இந்தக் காலத்தில்” என்று பேசி - காலமாற்றத்தால்
உலர்ந்த பழைமை நாவில், பசை தடவிக் கொள்கிறார்கள்!

“உண்ட வீட்டிற்கு இரண்டகம் செய்யாதே; உப்பிட்டவரை
உள்ளளவும் நினை” என்பவர்கள்தான், விஞ்ஞானத்தின்
பயனை விதவிதமாக - வகைவகையாக அனுபவித்துக்
கொண்டு வசை பாடுகிறார்கள்!

அவர்கள் வஞ்சக நெஞ்சம் கொட்டும் வசையைக்கூட
இசையாக ஏற்று, விஞ்ஞானம், விருந்தளித்தபடியே உள்ளது!
துரோகிக்கும் அன்பைப் படைக்கிறது!

விஞ்ஞானத்தின் சமீப காலக் குழந்தை டெலிவிஷன்!

“டெலிவிஷன்-இளைய மகள்” என்றாலும், எல்லாரும்
பாராட்டும் அழகும்-அறிவும் கொண்ட மகள்-விஞ்ஞானத்
தாய்க்கு!

## English Translation

The feast that science provides plunges the world into amazement!

Merely thinking of some of its wonders makes the heart swell with astonishment. There can be nobody who does not rejoice at the harvest of science.

Science is a growing star. As a star grows, must not the intensity of its light and the extent over which it spreads also continue to expand?

Thus far we have only tasted the feast of science; we have not prepared it. When foreigners prepare the banquet and invite us, we go and consume it and then complain. We have learned to say nothing else.

We cannot claim that even the hurricane lamp beyond the simple earthen lamp arose from our own minds. If that produces neither shame nor pain in us, what can we do?

Although science has not treated us with a stepmother's disposition, some among us have turned it into a stepmother, imagined whatever occurred to them and spoken as they pleased. Talking Brahmas do exist!

They made the sun a god, gave him a chariot, seated Chaya Devi beside him, fitted it with a single wheel and worshipped him. They let the moon behave “waywardly” with Tara and worshipped him too. They saw the stars as gods but did not understand that they are worlds many times larger than Earth.

Forgetting that science is the mirror of an age's speed, they put on a distorting lens, attach incomprehensible meanings to scientific growth and oppose it.

One might say that there are no hands that do not turn on the radio morning and evening. In the morning those hands turn on the radio first and only afterward seek the packet of sacred ash, leaf through the *Tiruvachakam*, or open the volume of *Tevaram*.

They think science is as commonplace as greens growing in their own backyard. Because it reaches them as a feast furnished by foreign thought, they know neither the treasure of novelty buried within it nor the great exertion of the human mind that excavated it. Otherwise, would they be satisfied merely by naming the radio “Akashvani,” calling a novelty by an ancient name?

They have not truly seen the aeroplane flying either. The moment an aircraft appears, they speak without faltering: “Ah, does that not occur in the *Ramayana* itself? In what did Ravana travel when he abducted Sita? Was it not the Pushpaka Vimana? Is this greater than that?”

These people at least are ordinary and uninformed. But the government that looks after them named a flying aircraft “Hanuman.” Where can one report this, and how can one describe it?

Only in this country can one see such a curiosity. People find pleasure even in viewing novelty through a curtain of antiquity. They call the radio “Akashvani” and rejoice. They name an aeroplane “Hanuman” and make the false calculation that antiquity has thereby been protected.

Science has not been frightened by these heroes into withholding its innovative hand. It remains a skylark that beats its wondrous wings and flies through the remarkable sky singing a song of novelty. It delights in giving a special pleasure even to those who mock it.

Yet the bearers of old texts have not abandoned their double-tongued ways. Even when the toy is new, they dress it in ancient clothing, proclaim the glory of the past and deceive themselves into believing that their hold has not yet weakened.

Without reflecting for a moment, “This instrument is the living pulse of science; how greatly it helps us and how much benefit we enjoy from it,” they say, “What is all this? Did the olden days lack anything found today?” Thus they smear moisture on an ancient tongue dried by the change of time.

The very people who say, “Do not betray the house that fed you; remember throughout your life the one who gave you salt,” enjoy the benefits of science in every form and variety while singing abuse against it. Science receives even the abuse poured out by their deceitful hearts as music and continues to serve its feast. It offers love even to its betrayer.

Television is a recent child of science. Though television is the younger daughter of Mother Science, she is a daughter of beauty and intelligence praised by everyone.

Arrangements have been made to exhibit television in India for the first time. Television is to be displayed at the international radio and electronic exhibition to be held in Bombay next February.

Like radio, television does not merely enable us to hear programmes spoken or sung by people many miles away. It enables us to see the speakers and singers directly as well.

Radio is a feast for the ear. Television is a feast for both ear and eye. It is to be shown in India for the first time in February.

Science has advanced to this extent. Yet in India, people perform worship when a ship is launched and wave sacred lights when an aircraft takes off, turning the calendar back to the Stone Age.

Leave aside how useful television is. It is a marvellous endeavour that has turned into reality a dream project beyond human imagination. Many people here possess neither the culture to appreciate it nor the generosity to speak of it with wonder.

Attlee speaks in London, six thousand miles away. Kumaran, standing at the doorway of the “Kumbeswarar Cafe” in Kumbakonam, hears his speech and sees his form.

Kailasanathar and the dweller in Vaikuntha may have appeared so that only their devotees could see them, but they never contained themselves inside a small box and presented a wondrous vision to everyone gathered around it.

Siva would have searched for his bull, and Vishnu would have sent Adisesha to summon Garuda, whenever they heard their devotees' cries. Even to say, “Kannappa, stop!” Lord Siva, beloved of Mother Sivakami, came riding the bull. When Gajendra cried “O Primal Source!”, Vishnu may have rushed from his game with Lakshmi, his garland disturbed and upper cloth slipping, but still he had to search for Garuda.

Attlee needs none of this. Turn the switch and at once he speaks and appears. That is television's wonder.

Science is the world's mother in this century, and television is the jewelled garland that lets us delight in the dance she joyfully performs. Through television, science gathers our greetings and praise.

Even so, certain voices will certainly reach your ears.

“What great thing have they supposedly discovered? Is projecting a picture of what is happening such a grand achievement? The sages and divine seers who lived here knew not only the present; they knew the past and foretold the future,” some will say.

They will ask, “What can this atheistic method accomplish in a land that possessed great men with the eye of wisdom?”

There are people who will declare: “Television indeed! It is all mere disguise and deadly poison, a venomous serpent invented by atheism to mock the sages who knew the three divisions of time.”

It means that the intoxication of antiquity has not cleared from them.

Science does not become idle on hearing these voices. Like one who does good even to an opponent, it continues to benefit them. They forget the many benefits and expanding conveniences by which science reduces human labour.

The scales they use are faulty, and their weighing stones were picked up in the street. Nobody can accept the measuring rod in their hands.

The chief reason offered by those who hate science is this: “With its assistance, numerous instruments of war have been made terrifying.”

Opponents of science are of many kinds, and the intensity of their hostility varies. But those who advance this reason are a curious class. They are educated and know the world. They are neither simply lovers of antiquity nor entirely free from hostility toward it. Yet they are the ones who make this accusation.

It is true that weapons of war have become monstrous through scientific aid. We accept that. But is science the cause? No.

Science is prepared coloured paint. With it one may paint the form of a beautiful woman or draw the shape of a hideous monkey. If a monkey is drawn, does the fault lie with the bowl of colour or with the artist who paints? Think about it.

Those who belittle and malign science under the name of “lovers of peace,” in the manner of lovers of antiquity, must not be allowed to multiply.

With the colours available, one may create beautiful curtains and paint a fine garden; one may also depict a stinking sewer. The fault belongs neither to the colour applied nor to the brush. It belongs to the artist who paints.

Likewise, friends who raise angry cries while pointing to the atomic bomb must understand that it is not science's crime but the crime of the human being who draws science into that disastrous course.

One could tell the owner of a wounded dove: “Do not blame the arrow; look at the bow. Do not blame the bow either; question the hunter who bends it, strings it and releases the arrow.”

In the same way, those who seek peace must condemn the destroyers who use science's fiery point to annihilate human society. Those who sing, “The world's happiness is our happiness,” like heirs of worm-eaten books, should not speak otherwise.

Science has not given us only the atomic bomb. It also gives us television. Science is like a woman's eyes in Valluvar's verse: it gives the affliction and itself becomes the great medicine that cures it.

If one screams at the atomic bomb and garlands science with abuse, what is one to do upon seeing television? Those who criticize must therefore consider where the fault truly lies.

An honest mind can only say, “Science has given television; for that we marvel and offer thanks.”

Television is greatly needed in this country. Not merely this television; we need an even more powerful television.

If a radio station stages the drama “Sita's Wedding,” what would appear on television? We would see Sita seated in a cane chair, careful not to crumple her velvet slippers or parrot-green silk sari with its “Rajakumari” border. We would see Rama wearing a silk *jibba*, a neatly fan-folded Jawahar coat and suit, together with emperors gathered in the wedding pavilion in still stranger costumes.

The speech would be one thing and the visible forms another.

In the same way, we could see scenes in which speech is one thing and conduct another.

Young Mahalingam might be studying intensely for his M.B.B.S. examination at medical college, financed by his father Subramania Sastrigal's earnings as a priest. Yet the father would complain: “The times have gone bad. Whether it is a headache or anything else, they immediately pick up a needle and inject you.”

A son may work as an electrical engineer, while his father praises the radiance shining from the face of an idol in a temple.

And that is not all. At school a teacher explains Newton's research, speaks of Thomas Alva Edison and sings Marconi's praise. On returning home, however, he consults the almanac and will not take a step without examining omens.

In public he says, “God is one,” but he observes Vinayaka Chaturthi, devotion to Murugan, the sight of Nataraja and the Mariamman festival alike.

He says, “There are no castes other than two,” but will he give his daughter to a man of another caste?

They sing, “Even if one be a Pulayar who eats goats, if he is Siva's devotee, behold in him the god we worship.” Yet when they see an oppressed-caste person in the street, they leap about and shout threats, springing like frogs.

How amusing it would be if this entire country were turned into a radio station and the television method let us hear people's voices while also seeing their actions!

Y. A. Fazalbhoy, secretary of the forthcoming Bombay exhibition, recently returned from Europe. He has announced that television is included in the arrangements for the exhibition.

If a “social television” like that television could be invented, the Ardhanarisvaras roaming this country would be exposed before the public. It could project the corrupt character of those virtuous people who maintain no connection whatever between tongue and hand, and make the people clap and laugh. We need television for that!

That is why we need television: not only to display the greatness of science, but to introduce to the country those who wander about turning it into a realm of absurdity.

*Dravida Nadu*, 1 July 1951

## Translator's Notes

`ஆகாசவாணி` (Akashvani, “voice from the sky”) is the Sanskrit-derived name used for Indian radio. Anna contrasts it with the modern technology and satirizes attempts to absorb inventions into claims of ancient precedent. `அரிக்கன் விளக்கு` denotes a hurricane lamp. `திரிகால ஞானிகள்` are seers claimed to know past, present and future.

The reference to a woman's eyes giving both illness and cure adapts *Tirukkural* 1091. The line about there being only “two castes” invokes a well-known verse attributed to Avvaiyar, dividing people morally into those who give and those who do not. `அர்த்தநாரீசுவரர்கள்` literally invokes the composite Siva-Parvati form; here Anna uses it satirically for people whose words and deeds are contradictory or divided.

The OCR has several damaged expressions: `விஞ்ஞானத்தின் ௧௬ உயிர்ப்பு`, the beginning of the quotation on Image 7, the description of science on Image 6, and part of the casteist street outburst on Image 11. Their surrounding argument is recoverable, but unclear wording has not been silently reconstructed in the preserved Tamil source. The numbered `41.` is a catalogue number, not part of the printed title.
