## English Translation

*(Bharathan)*

"Even if a difference of opinion or a breach arises between the Governor and the ministers, the ministers must not resign from office." This is what Pandit Jawahar recently said at the place printed as Jakkar.

On hearing this, the editor of *Navasakthi*—from the Sadhu Press—wrote, or rather fashioned, an editorial. Its title was "Wise Counsel." Thiru. Vi. Ka. fashioned many things in refined Tamil within it. The substance of them was as follows:

"A struggle begun by ministers without leaving office will have more life than a struggle they begin after resigning. For ministers to resign would strengthen the bureaucracy... it would mean voluntarily abandoning a fortress already captured... At this time the Congress ministers must not resign; they must not resign for any reason whatsoever."

Praising Jawahar's words, Kalyanasundaranar, celebrated in poetry, wrote in his classical Tamil style: "We honour it as wise counsel indeed. We expect Haripura to proceed according to his [Jawahar's] wise counsel." This was on 11 February 1938.

What happened at the conference? Neither the country, nor politics, nor the Congress ministers followed the wise counsel of *Navasakthi*. What could Thiru. Vi. Ka. do? On 18 February 1938, he simply wrote, "Everything is God's doing."

In the United Provinces and Bihar, differences of opinion arose between the ministers and the governors over the release of political prisoners. The Viceroy intervened. Disregarding the wise counsel bestowed by *Navasakthi*, the ministers resigned. What a cruel turn of time! The elder's maxim went unheeded. What can one say? "Everything is God's doing."

So Thiru. Vi. Ka. once again took up his pen; that was his weapon. With sorrow dripping from every line, he wrote about "The Crisis." Was it about the failure of events to proceed according to the advice he had offered the previous week? No. That was a *kalambakam* on "Wise Counsel." This is a Mukhari lament concerning "The Crisis."

Here, the elder gives several reasons to show that the ministers had acted properly:

"In certain places, in the name of law, the ministers imposed Section 144 upon patriots; they arrested some people; they also tried to suppress the peasant movement and even opened fire."

What a splendid picture of the proper manner in which the ministers acted! True devotees, I, your humble servant, have merely repeated what the heroic orator of the Sadhu Press declared. It is not merchandise of my own making.

Think for a moment about what the elder has said. It is plain as daylight that, to preserve their offices, the ministers suppressed civil liberty, crushed labourers, and repressed peasants. Therefore, what does it matter whether the Governor drives out those who did such things or someone else rolls them away? You decide.

If I said that Thiru. Vi. Ka.'s writing looks like someone rubbing his eyes and weeping before the British, he would certainly become angry. But you who reflect upon it, tell me: am I wrong?

"Sirs, you British, why do you distrust our ministers? Did they regard civil liberty and such things as important? Did they not impose Section 144? Did they not suppress the peasant movement? Did they not even demonstrate their willingness to open fire? Do you suspect such excellent disciplinarians? Why this needless suspicion? They are very good men. Are they not noblemen who will act exactly as you command?"

Would it be wrong to say that this is the purport of the elder's exposition?

He says that British statesmen appear to have developed a disease of the brain.

Whose brain is diseased, sir? Please think. On 11 February 1938, our Thiru. Vi. Ka. offered the "wise counsel" that ministers must not resign whatever happened. On 18 February 1938, he wrote that all Congress provincial ministers should resign with one accord. Why? You may understand if you think for a moment. If you have neither the time nor the desire to think, conclude that this too is "God's doing," in accordance with the elder's method.

(*Kudi Arasu*, 20 February 1938)

## Translator's Notes

The place printed as “ஜாக்கர்” is uncertain in OCR and is transliterated conservatively as Jakkar. “அரிபுரம்” is rendered as Haripura from the 1938 Congress context. The essay is satirical throughout and contrasts two positions attributed to Thiru. Vi. Kalyanasundaram and his journal *Navasakthi*.
