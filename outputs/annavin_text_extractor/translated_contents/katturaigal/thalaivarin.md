## English Translation

No one knows what occurred to that eleven-year-old girl, but she boldly wrote and sent a letter.

The leader was a good man and a friend of the poor. Yet why was his appearance not one that remained in the mind the moment it was seen? That small concern arose in her.

While the girl was thinking about what might make the appearance of the man who had become leader more attractive, Abraham Lincoln, who had been willingly elected President, was considering the programmes needed for the people's welfare and how they might be put into practice.

At that time the girl knew nothing about what defect there might be in Abraham's appearance. She reached a conclusion.

Abraham Lincoln, too, reached a conclusion, but his concerned the country: slavery must end and the dignity that all people are equal must endure. He began firmly to believe that learned people would not accept America as a well-governed country while the slave trade continued as a stain and a blemish upon it. The stain must be removed, the blemish wiped away, and America's dignity must shine with beautiful light, he thought.

“Yes, only then will his face become attractive. Now the bones stand out in his face; that is why it lacks appeal,” the girl discovered. Just as she had observed, Abraham Lincoln had always been a gaunt-faced man.

Born into a poor family, he had worked his way to high office. Yet because he had not immersed himself in wealthy surroundings, he possessed neither glitter nor polish.

One might say that he looked like a farmer who, after tying his cattle in the shed, put on his master's coat and went to see the town.

The forms of some people become fixed in the mind as soon as they are seen. The reason is that their appearance bears some special mark not found in others, or that such distinction is visible in their deeds.

The girl wanted Abraham Lincoln to possess something of that kind.

“Yes, that is right,” she concluded.

“Dear leader,

“I wish to see a new radiance in your appearance. For that purpose, I think it would be good if you grew a small beard. Please fulfil my wish.”

How Abraham Lincoln must have laughed when he saw the letter that this little girl had sent him! “How many problems confront the country, and how very many duties I must perform! Yet this amusing girl tells me to grow a beard, as though that were the first task to which I should attend! Mischievous child!” he must have thought.

If, after all these years without one, he suddenly began growing a moustache and beard, what would the townspeople say? Some might even scoff, “Oh! Abraham has become conceited.” “No, no! Why do I need a beard and moustache?” he told himself.

It was a time when the scent of civil war was already in the air. Lincoln faced many important problems. Hundreds waited in his office to hear his counsel and carry out their tasks. Even in such circumstances, Abraham did not fail to reply to the letter from the girl, Grace Bedell, who had asked him to grow a beard and gain a new lustre in his appearance.

“If I were to grow the beard and moustache I have never had until now, the townspeople might think it rather strange. Would that be proper?” he wrote.

Though his letter said this, within a month Lincoln began growing a beard and moustache.

Just as the girl had said, a new radiance appeared in Abraham Lincoln's appearance. Not only did he acquire a form that remained in the minds of those who saw him; even today, whenever anyone thinks of Abraham Lincoln, it is the bearded and moustached Lincoln who appears before the mind's eye.

Among the Presidents of the United States, Abraham Lincoln alone had a beard and moustache.

The affectionate letter of an eleven-year-old girl was the reason.

The day on which that letter arrived became the day a new phase began in Lincoln's life. The leader's new appearance had begun.

In Abraham Lincoln's life, each event similarly arose as an unexpected occurrence. Yet every one became a turning point in his own life and, as a result, in the lives of the American people.

Who could have imagined beforehand that a man with a beard and moustache would be enthroned as President?

Who could even have thought that Abraham Lincoln was capable of becoming President?

He was born into a humble family and had possessed only the modest aim of learning a little and earning a living through hard work.

Lincoln was not born in the mansion of a wealthy lord who hosted political leaders at banquets and celebrations.

He was born into a family that endured a difficult livelihood, thinking, “What connection have we with the subtleties of politics?” Political questions therefore did not attract Lincoln's thought. It is doubtful whether debates about political issues had even reached his ears in childhood. Yet a life that began in such circumstances later became a chain of events of immense importance to American political history.

Abraham Lincoln attained a stature that allowed the world to celebrate him as the fearless man who abolished slavery.

He achieved a valiant victory in the terrible civil war that shook America.

There had been many compassionate people, people who spoke of righteousness, and people who preached about mercy.

There were churches, and priests in abundance. They spoke pleasantly about the path of truth.

Yet the practice of turning human beings into beasts, enslaving them and inflicting cruelty upon them continued.

Even those who spoke beautifully of innumerable forms of charity could neither ask why nor voice opposition.

They would donate costly bells to churches. Yet those same “philanthropic lords” enslaved fellow human beings and whipped them!

They would thrill on hearing the sacred story of the “Son of God” who showed compassion even to a leper. At the same time, seeing Black slaves continue their labour while wiping away blood flowing from flesh torn by the lash, they would smile faintly and say, “The fellow will be properly submissive hereafter!”

Abraham Lincoln was a man with the strength of mind to change that condition. Yet, strikingly, he had not spent his childhood engaged in a great deal of political talk.

When a rare opportunity came to him, he could have abandoned his long, simple life and rolled in luxury. Lords were ready to smile upon him, and fine ladies to kiss his hand in affection and, if he wished, offer their cheeks. He could have decided that he had wandered through villages long enough, put on splendid clothes and become a man of the city. Instead, he resolved and laboured to abolish slavery, which had remained a stain upon American history and a great disgrace that destroyed human dignity during his administration. He also achieved victory.

How deeply the problem of Black people and the institution of slavery had gripped America may be understood from several incidents that still raise their heads today.

Not even a month has passed since a thousand white students, seeing a Black woman go to study at a college, raised frenzied cries and drove her away.

If this was the condition in 1956, how much courage of heart must have been required in those earlier days to speak against slavery, meet opposition, enact a law and make it succeed?

Turn back once more to the cover of this journal and look now at the portrait of Lincoln with his new, bearded appearance. That courage of heart can be seen clearly.

He did not possess this appearance at first. A young girl perceived the deficiency, and the leader gained a new appearance.

14 October 1956
