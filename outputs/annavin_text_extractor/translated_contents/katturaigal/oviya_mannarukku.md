## English Translation

"Do you know where the flowing river, the singing cuckoo, the flying sparrow, and the colourful parrot are? They are in his fingers!" said a friend while speaking about K. Madhavan.

Madhavan is foremost among the master artists who have given life to many artistic treasures that were lost yet not entirely gone, and who have kept them alive. Last week in Madras, the Artists' Association honoured such a great genius by draping him in a golden shawl and crowning him with fame. The function was presided over by Finance Minister Subramaniam. T. K. Shanmugam, a luminary of the acting world, joyfully draped the golden shawl and praised Madhavan's service to the theatre. Friends M. Karunanidhi, T. V. Narayanasamy, K. A. Thangavelu, Sakthi Krishnasamy, G. Umapathi, and others spoke in appreciation of the artistic genius. Finally, Annadurai, delighted in heart and countenance, conferred upon that incomparable artist the title "King of Art."

There are people here endowed with skill and creative power worthy of the praise of the entire world. Yet, confined within an unfeeling northern rule, they have been unable to receive the recognition that is their due. It is no falsehood to say that another part of this "India," proclaimed to be one country, still knows nothing of the paintings of people such as Madhavan. In the North, where people ask who Bharathi is, how could our purest gold ever receive its proper value?

Even so, lest we too forget our own people while brooding over this, adorning them with golden shawls and crowns of honour does more than confer distinction upon them: it is also a great deed that gives encouragement and enthusiasm to our other jewels of art. Especially in these times, with their ugly habit of calculating whether one's own prestige will diminish if one praises the excellence of another in the same profession, it is worthy of admiration that all those engaged in art should gather and honour one among them. We commend every artist who took part in organising this celebration, as well as R. Madhavan, vice-president of the Artists' Association.

Long live K. Madhavan, King of Art!

May a life free from poverty flourish for him!

(*Dravida Nadu*, news item; date printed in damaged OCR as 9-8-29)

The source alternates between K. Madhavan, the honouree, and R. Madhavan, vice-president of the association. The English preserves that distinction. The publication date is visibly doubtful and has not been silently repaired.
