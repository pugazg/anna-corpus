## English Translation

Comrades! A rare opportunity has arrived for your ardour, which has long been throbbing for action.

The occasion is near when we must demonstrate both our courage and our wisdom to Delhi, where the court of authority holds sway.

Until now you have sighed with bursting hearts, your eyes and speech pouring forth valour. Here is the moral struggle. Form your ranks and set out to warn that arrogant regime.

On July 15, a distinguished moral struggle worthy of a place in the history of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is to begin. The DMK has sounded its drum against Delhi's domination, which, armed with an arrogant voice, conceit and obstinacy, has become the rule of tyrants.

“Fools! Madmen! Idiots!” Though we pay taxes in abundance and receive no life of prosperity in return, Delhi's leader has hurled these words at us. We proudly proclaim, “Sound the conch that the people of the South are indomitable heroes!” We cannot forget the days when Rome and Egypt marvelled at our splendour. We are a people unafraid of the battlefield. Courage is our sport; sword and spear are the emblems of our fame. The banks of the Ganges and the northern mountains once trembled at the South's voice. Today, however, that bank of the Ganges mocks the bank of the Kaveri.

The Chola who conquered the Ganges was the foremost of our lineage, yet Delhi's seat calls his descendants rowdies. Kanaka Vijaya no longer roars but fires shafts of ridicule: the Chera's descendants, it says, are fools, and an attempt to recover lost well-being is madness. A few seated in Delhi, which bent before every invader, mock the descendants of Kannagi, who lived with courage and dignity. According to Delhi's chief, it is foolish for a deer caught in a tiger's cage to struggle for freedom, idiotic for a poor man trapped beneath a deceiver's feet to try to stand upright, and madness to escape being crushed between a fox's teeth.

Such words come from the proprietor of Swaraj Bhavan, who sleeps amid silk mattresses, ostentatious comfort, chilled water, fruit and milk. When we fought Hindi, a dead language which even its admirers ridicule, Pandit Nehru called it “nonsense.” Now that same hard-hearted Pandit has added new titles: we are fools, the Tamils are madmen, and all our demands are idiotic. He bestowed these titles upon those who joined the Tiruttani moral struggle.

The late Mukherjee called Pandit Nehru a little child; his American friend Stevenson calls him impulsive. Whenever that “little child” and “impulsive man” grows angry, he snarls at us. Are we cowards or a nerveless multitude? Those descended from people who carried grass mock the community of the *Purananuru*, from whose verses Tamil valour drips. Their mockery is of a kind that even a degraded person would not utter, yet the Pandit does.

Had he remained merely the Pandit of Allahabad, he could readily have been made to experience the consequence. But he is now a political Pandit, blessed with the fortune of governing thirty crore people. For a person in such a position constantly to consort with such words is shamefully bad and will invite the world's ridicule. Since he mounts the political stage without abandoning ugly associations, it is our responsibility to teach him sense. He repeatedly mixes indecency into politics. We who struggle for political dignity must demonstrate that this is wrong. The day for it is July 15.

If a man who flees with his heels striking the back of his head shows his tail to a warrior who charges into battle with sword in hand, that tail will not survive: the sword will come down. Unaware of this, they repeatedly insult and revile us. Where is our courage? Proud people of stately gait and commanding gaze, Draupadi spits upon Kannagi! Are we blind simpletons, nerveless dolls or a wandering rabble, that we should see this and act as though we have not? No. We were bound and we endured. Now the hour calls: where is your voice? Let the lion's roar rise and the northern quarter tremble. July 15 summons us.

That day will defend the honour of the sweet Tamil land. It will be the day when the community of the immortal *Kural* strikes down the cruel beak of the eagle that comes to peck at it.

Our peaceful moral struggles are described as madness and stupidity, and we as a crowd of fools, by one who uprooted railway tracks, overturned trains and wrought destruction. The python mocks the cow; Draupadi mocks Kannagi. This has happened not once or twice, but whenever an opportunity arises.

What does it mean? The extent of our patience appears to have led them to mistake us for cowards. They seem unable to understand the depth of the goodwill with which we have borne peace and tranquillity.

We requested with affection. We presented demands through resolutions and sent petitions through the people. More than fifty panchayat boards sent requests. Having received them all, the northern regime still displays contempt over changing the name Dalmiyapuram: “Must we listen merely because they speak?” It provokes us, while Nehru abuses us as rowdies, hirelings and idiots. A demand angers him; a moral struggle makes him hurl uncivil words.

Such is the North's swelling arrogance and pride. They do not regard us as human beings, but treat us as lower than earthworms. We descend from great monarchs, yet a lineage that never learned to wield a righteous sceptre snarls and springs at us.

We asked humbly that the northern magnate Dalmia's name be removed and the place renamed Kallakudi. We used petitions from the people and resolutions adopted before tens of thousands at a conference, without diminishing political civility or dignity. The central authority paid no heed. It gave not even a brief answer, while the railway board bearing the name Dalmiyapuram laughed at us.

Students who sing that Mother Tamil rejoices adorned with *Chintamani* and the wealth of the *Silappathikaram*, look: that town appears as a saw cutting through your mother's heart. Its own people labour until their bodies become bone and their blood becomes sweat, but Dalmia, who came from elsewhere, mocks them. “I am trampling you; see what you can do,” he roars. Every year half a crore rupees enters his cashbox, while our soil becomes cement.

The northerner becomes a lord while we decay. Is there no end to this torture? Must our Dravidian land disintegrate so that little foxes may grow fat? Must a deceiver enjoy the flower of the pleasure garden while all of us stand yearning? Is this not degradation? Will others not laugh to see us lose our own possessions and then extend our hands for a quarter-anna in alms? Must the sad tale continue in which the visitor prospers and the native becomes a simpleton?

Dalmiyapuram lies in a land where Tamil flourishes, but not even the smallest Tamil name exists in Dalmia's own land. Bulls who roar that Tamil fame once spread across the earth, behold this hideous sight. It is a weed in a wet field, a thorny euphorbia in a fine garden. It must not be; it is intolerable. Come, therefore, calls July 15.

“Beloved friends, comrades of the motherland that bore me! I am coming. I go to Dalmiyapuram, the emblem of northern domination. If I return victorious, let us conduct a victory procession. If Dalmiyapuram swallows me, conduct my funeral procession.” Standing on the soil of Tiruchirappalli, Comrade Karunanidhi sends this message to the people of the sacred Dravidian land. Under his leadership thousands upon thousands of heroes stand in formation for the Dalmiyapuram moral struggle. The day is July 15.

What will you do that day? Karunanidhi and those young bulls may suffer the government's blows; they may face bullets. To them blood is commonplace and principle itself is life. So roar the heroes of the Dalmiyapuram struggle.

Where is your salute to the sacrificial spirit of these splendid young lions? They do not desire written tributes or words of praise. Strong-shouldered heroes spread throughout Dravidian land, how will you show that their demand has your support? By remaining at home and shedding tears along the way? No. Set out. This uncivil government does not value the people's tears.

You may hesitate: are Karunanidhi and the others entering the field merely to change a foreigner's name? What can be done? Our mother is trapped in the grip of those who build mansions from meanness, erect pavilions through deception and live by cheating. Sighs and tears will not rescue her. Something must be done, and it must be done without harm to public property or the people. July 15 calls with just such a plan.

Our peaceful supporting struggle must trouble the mind of the Pandit, Delhi's nursemaid, who repeatedly throws uncivil words at us. He must realize: “We babbled while ruling the country; now these people are teaching us a lesson.” The DMK has announced the form that struggle will take.

July 15 is the date when our dear comrades will join the campaign on the soil of Dalmiyapuram to change its name. On that day trains running throughout the South must be halted here and there. Throughout daylight on the fifteenth, moving trains must suddenly stop. You know the methods. Those who need instruction may read the words “Emergency Notice” written in every railway carriage.

Let trains stop everywhere. If officials arrive, answer: “Sir, the name of a northerner stands in our land as Dalmiyapuram. It is a sign of northern domination. We asked that it be changed, but Delhi ignores us. We therefore issue an emergency warning and register our condemnation.” Let affection be in your reply, not anger.

Maintain discipline while engaged in this work; do not behave as impulse dictates. Expressing our condemnation is the central purpose. The passengers on the trains are all our own people, and the railways too will one day belong to us. There must therefore be no inconvenience, and no passenger should be made dissatisfied. Many may attempt the supporting struggle by lying on the track before a train departs. Those who do so must act without transgression.

July 15 seeks to demonstrate that, if we resolve upon it, we can prevent the central government's trains from running according to schedule. Today it is the train; tomorrow we can, if we so resolve, prevent the central government itself from running smoothly. July 15 is intended to deliver that warning. Set out with this victory alone in mind.

When you halt a train, officials who serve as northern emissaries may come and threaten to beat you. Give them a smile. They may command you to prison. Reply that prison has become for us a grove where the mango cuckoo sings. Make July 15 victorious through peace and nonviolence. Let us wash away with our sacrificial blood the stain of humiliation upon the honey-sweet Tamil land.

July 15 is a day of warning.

July 15 is the day that beats the drum for the destruction of the lust for domination.

At Dalmiyapuram, let the capital of domination hear the cry of moral struggle. Let the cry of support resound throughout the country, and let the Pandit be given the opportunity to learn of it.

Beloved heroes with fearless hearts, the occasion summons you. Prepare yourselves. This is the hour when the North that torments us and Achariyar, who would imprison us within hereditary vocation, must witness the strength of our voice. Wherever we look, dark clouds appear, threatening to engulf our future. Is it not our responsibility to dispel them? Prepare for all these tasks. Nehru stands on one side and Achariyar on the other: two governments, and we between them. Consider what a disgrace this is. Delhi's arrogance dances upon the motherland, while Achariyar seeks to render its people mute.

Come to sound the death bell for this injustice. Prepare. July 15 calls. It is a day of sacrifice for us, an important day in the history of liberation, and the day on which the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam sounds its clarion call toward the North. Do your duty that day. Our mother is distressed, her feet in fetters. Young lions eager to cut them away and valiant women of sweet Tamil, rise in your multitudes. Make the voice of our people heard in every direction.

July 15 approaches. Hurry and prepare. Subdue the arrogant revelry of the insolent.

The July 15 railway-stoppage agitation must remain wholly disciplined. Railway property must suffer no damage, for that property is ours. There must be no shouting that disgusts the public; remember that we labour on the public's behalf. Take part only during daylight. Explain to the public why the trains are being stopped. Tell them that Nehru, today's head of government, speaks so as to humiliate Tamils, and that a campaign has begun to remove the name Dalmiyapuram, an emblem of northern domination.

By halting trains we can make Nehru understand the country's condition. We can make him realize that the country has noticed his disparagement of Tamils. Only if the stoppage on July 15 succeeds and reports pile up before him will he recognize Dravidian awakening. How else will he learn of it? Will he watch such gatherings as a television display, or will illustrated reports appear in the newspapers he reads? The Pandit must therefore be made to understand the conditions here.

From the Chidambaram conference.

*Dravida Nadu*, 12 July 1953.
