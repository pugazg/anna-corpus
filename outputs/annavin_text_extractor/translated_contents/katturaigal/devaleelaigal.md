## English Translation

Day after day we hear devotees loudly worshipping: “O God of the devout! Lord of the gods! One of the Trinity, foremost among the three!” It is like calling a deranged man a genius, celebrating a drunkard as a man of virtue, or calling a cur a lion. So it is with the custom of addressing men crazed by lust as “God” and “the deathless one.” For the exploits of the very figures whom the simple-minded followers of Aryan religion worship with such devotion are shown, in the Puranic volumes recited by those same devotees, to be filled with blind lust, deceit, baseness and savagery.

Under the title “Divine Exploits,” I offer a small sample of the revelry of these performers in the theatre of lust. Let thoughtful people reflect; let lovers of antiquity hang their heads in shame; and let the youthful world clap its hands and laugh!

* * *

“Lord Indra! This is the proper moment. Do not delay. Leave at once!”

“Messenger! What is the news? Where do you ask me to go?”

“A rare opportunity has arisen for you to fulfil your long-cherished desire. Set out.”

“Where?”

“To King Parikshit's palace.”

“Ah! Messenger, why did you raise that subject? The beautiful Vapushtama there continues to torment me! Though I pleaded, she refused to yield. Though my heart melted like syrup, that maiden would not consent to this sinner. What am I to do? How am I to be saved? I tried every possible thing and displayed all my skill. Nothing worked. My infatuation with Vapushtama has not subsided, and she will not yield. My heart languishes.”

“Why test yourself like this? What about Ahalya...”

“That ended easily. And not only easily, messenger: when she learned that I was Indra she became delighted and proud that so eminent a person had come to her. But Vapushtama is not like that!”

“That is precisely why I speak. If you miss this moment, another will be hard to find. Leave for Parikshit's palace.”

“Why is this the right moment? What has happened?”

“An Ashvamedha sacrifice is taking place there.”

“What is that to us? I want the woman whose beauty puts the apsaras to shame.”

“In your haste you will not let me finish. The Ashvamedha is taking place, and its horse has died.”

“It has died? So what...”

“Why are you still wondering? Has desire for Vapushtama dulled your customary cleverness? Do you not know that, according to Ashvamedha ritual, the queen must spend a night with the horse?”

“Excellent, excellent! Messenger, you reminded me at just the right time. You have a fertile brain.”

* * *

The story says that Indra and his messenger once held a conversation of this kind. King Parikshit had an exceedingly beautiful wife named Vapushtama. Indra became infatuated with her. Her charm must have exceeded even the beauty of Indrani, Menaka and Rambha, for somehow this desire took possession of him. He tried all manner of devices, but the woman gave him no opening.

Meanwhile Parikshit performed a sacrifice. By its custom, the king's wife had to spend a night with the horse. At that moment the horse died. Learning this, the messenger ran to the lust-stricken Indra and announced that the proper opportunity to fulfil his desire had arrived. Delighted that his design would succeed, Indra went to the sacrificial hall, entered the dead horse's body with his life-force, joined Vapushtama and enjoyed the night. This is not found in some ordinary book. It appears in the sacred tale of the *Siva Maha Purana*.

What a noble quality: taking another man's wife! What refinement: entering a dead horse's body to enjoy royal pleasure! Such were Indra's traits. Yet this Indra, the embodiment of blind lust and savagery, is said to be the foremost and the leader among those who suppressed lust and anger, controlled desire, performed severe penance, obtained divine grace and attained the rank of gods. This is the deed of one who supposedly conquered lust and anger: not merely desiring another's wife, but committing an uncivilized outrage to possess her. If the sexual conduct of one who attained divine status is like this, one can infer the worth of the ascetics and rishis ranked immediately below him.

Just as Indra delighted himself with Ahalya, Vapushtama and the apsaras and committed many forbidden acts for sexual desire, Mother Indrani too did not fail to perform some “good deed” according to her power.

Once, the story says, Indrani conceived a desire for Mahavishnu and disclosed it to him. Anyone might expect Mahavishnu to threaten her: “Foolish woman! Is it not a woman's duty to live as a queen of chastity? Should you not guide divine and earthly women toward virtue? You are the wife of Indra, handsome, immersed in pleasures and a connoisseur of delight. Why did this evil thought arise in you? Sinner, is it proper that such a wanton should live in heaven? And what audacity! You direct your lustful gaze at me, one of the Trinity, husband of Sridevi and Bhudevi. Am I a libertine who enters another man's home? Is it proper to desire one of the great gods? Take this curse!”

But that is not what happened. Mahavishnu reportedly told the enamoured Indrani: “Desirable Indrani, I cannot fulfil your wish here. I am going to incarnate on earth as Krishna. At that time you too should be born on earth as Radha, and your heart's desire will be fulfilled.” The Purana accordingly says that Indrani, bearing an adulterous intention, was born as Radha and lived in amorous pleasure with Krishna, Narayana's incarnation.

It is such figures as Indra and Indrani whom self-styled champions of Hinduism have entered in their register of gods. The *Rig Veda* contains many hymns about Indra. Put aside even the mentality of the ancient Aryan group that shamelessly called a libertine who entered another man's household and whose wife went astray the king of the gods. Even today teachers dare to preach that people should worship Indra and the other gods as deities who grant wishes and thereby obtain blessings.

Their audacity makes us angry. Yet when we see the credulity of our ordinary people, who believe these degrading stories and regard fantasies devoid of humanity or holiness as gods, we feel pity.

Nor should anyone suppose that Indra alone has such characteristics. The qualities of the other gods in the Aryan catalogue are not an atom better. One god defeats another. In what? Purity? Truth? Divinity? Justice and integrity? Compassion and righteousness? No, no: in blind lust.

Brahmaloka is depicted as a sacred land reached by virtuous people disciplined in the Vedas: filled with Vedic sound, Narada's music and the voices of the good; surrounded by sacrificial smoke; adorned with lotus ponds where swans move beautifully; and blessed by a gentle breeze. Enthroned there is Brahma, the creator. His exploits are endless.

The most distinguished of the creator's exploits is that he married the woman he himself created. Brahma is both Saraswati's father and her husband! Perhaps Brahma pressed the maxim that bodily contact with one's children gives physical delight into the service of his lust. Could not the man who invented stories about the creator have endowed him with character, peace and discipline? After composing a story in which the lustful madness of the one titled Father of the Earth went so far as to take his own daughter as wife, how fraudulent is it to say, “Such a Brahma must be worshipped; Brahma's command is the law of caste”? And how degradingly blind is the belief that accepts it?

Our people refuse to think. They cultivate foolishness, become victims of cruelty and suffer. Is it right to believe notions filled with folly and fabricated stories, sink into degradation, and then lament that degradation, like someone touching fire and dancing in pain, or stepping in filth and crying out in disgust?

Another of Brahma's celebrated deeds was his desire to possess Parvati. Even the demons condemned by the Aryans did not commit such outrages. Brahma is one of the Trinity, while Siva is foremost among them. Both reportedly had five heads. One day Mother Parvati entered a pleasure garden where Brahma was strolling. Seeing five heads, she mistook him for her lord, walked toward him with a swan-like gait and embraced him.

The lady's haste caused her mistake. But did the lotus-born Brahma not know the truth? As soon as Parvati's hand touched him, could he not have cried, “I am Brahma, not Siva”? Instead, the source of the Vedas and creator of the universe delighted in the pleasure that had come to embrace him of its own accord.

At that moment Hara arrived, searching for his beautiful wife, and saw the scene. He grew angry. Thinking that Parvati had embraced Brahma only because Brahma too possessed five heads and she had mistaken him for Siva, he seized Brahma and plucked off one head. Though Siva thus beheaded and punished Brahma for an improper act, people on earth continued to worship that same Brahma as before, giving him the new name “the four-faced one.”

Can a dictionary furnish a sufficient term with which to condemn the religious advocate who calls the traitor who embraced Goddess Parvati a god and one of the Trinity? Even a savage would refuse to look upon a person who did such a thing. Here people fold their hands and worship a libertine, a scoundrel and a betrayer of Siva as God. This, they say, is devotion!

One section of the religious will ask: “What great sin is there? The lady embraced him in haste, and he too became somewhat transported with delight. Must he be condemned completely for that?” But Brahma's exploits did not end with his pleasure with Uma and the loss of one head. Brahma, supposedly forever occupied with creating the universe, continued to perform many more exploits of this kind.

We did not discover these things through detective work. We merely point to what exists in the Puranas described by teachers as books of praise. Brahma served as priest for Parvati's wedding. On seeing the beauty of the veiled bride's big toe, he reportedly thought, “Ah! If the toe itself is like this, how beautiful must the foot be, and the rest...” Blind lust seized him. The sacrificial fire kindled for the wedding reportedly went out as a consequence of his evil thought.

What worthiness! What auspicious marital virtue in the wedding priest! What a wicked thought in the mind of the lord of creation at the very hour of marriage! Yet this possessor of evil thoughts must be praised, and this is called devotion.

Did Brahma stop there? No. Once Brahma himself reportedly performed an Ashvamedha sacrifice. The wives of the gods came to witness the ritual. Seated beside the sacrificial pit, Brahma looked at the divine women who had come to view it. That was enough. His desire refused restraint; lust flooded over him. As a result, the holy men Angirasa, Atri, Marichi and Pulastya are said to have appeared there beside the sacrificial fire. Observe Brahma's exploit at the ritual pit. He is the creator; he must be worshipped; and that worship is called devotion! Would anyone with a drop of honour, self-respect and rationality accept it?

The *Brahma Purana* records another exploit. Brahma reportedly attended Gauri's wedding. He saw Gauri; desire arose; and its consequence immediately manifested itself. The Valakhilya rishis were thus said to have appeared. What greatness in Brahma! The religious advocates recount the lust that grips this great person the moment he sees a woman and its consequences, and then declare through their Puranas, “We shall praise and worship such a one again and again.”

What do these people respect? Divinity? One who commits evil is no god. If a story first says that a person performed an evil deed and then calls the same offender divine, who except those bereft of reason would accept it? Whether Urvashi dances, Tilottama sings, Parvati meets his gaze, Gauri appears before him, or even his own daughter stands in front of him, this great being is seized by sexual frenzy. We ask: who has any need for “gods” possessed by lust like this?
