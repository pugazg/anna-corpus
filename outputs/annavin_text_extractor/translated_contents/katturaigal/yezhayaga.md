## English Translation

"Look at my bags! Look at my hands! I brought nothing, and I am taking nothing away.

"I wrote books and earned money. I have given it all to worthy causes. Do you want the details? There is the charitable committee that distributed it all. Ask them! Its chairman is no ordinary man: he is our former Chief Justice!

"Many gifts arrived, including gifts from foreign countries. There they are. I have given everything to the government and my party. I have taken nothing.

"I have a few suits left. They were tailored while I was Prime Minister. I no longer need them. If editors or reporters want them, let them take and use them.

"Finally, I make one request.

"Appoint a commission to investigate and report whether, during my administration over these ten years, either I or my wife committed any wrong, or whether we used office to benefit relatives or favourites. Do this so that I may examine my own soul."

One's skin tingles on reading it. U Nu, who occupied the seat of power for ten years and shone as Burma's uncrowned king, has relinquished office! And he has issued such a statement!

A party within the party; fights and disputes; taunts from opposition parties. In these circumstances, when information arrived that someone intended to seize the government through the military, he voluntarily handed his office to General Ne Win and withdrew.

In the political world, it is not surprising for those once enthroned to fall face down, or for the man who waved the ceremonial fan to find himself seated in government. If we drew up a list of all who came and the various ways in which they departed during the past ten years, throughout the world and even within the Indian subcontinent, it would present a mixture of pain and strangeness.

We have seen a man who served as minister one month become forsaken the next, and men who seemed to proclaim that none could equal them left without a place even to stand. From the tyrannical King Farouk to Omandur Ramasamy Reddiar, praised as an upright man, political downfall has passed by leaving its marks.

When the cruel fell, the world rejoiced. When good men were driven out, it clicked its tongue in sympathy. U Nu has gained the second kind of place in the world's heart. Moreover, the statement he issued upon relinquishing office is capable of attracting many eyes and minds.

Inventories said that two thousand wristwatches and three thousand walking sticks were found in the palace of the deposed King Farouk, and that packs of playing cards lay inlaid with gold. In Pakistan today we hear accusations against various ministers: "This man bought a Chevrolet car"; "that man brought goods into his shop at reduced prices." In our own subcontinent too, although matters are not exposed so openly, the public gives some description or other to every person who becomes a minister. We remember how the late Prakasam publicly accused several ministers still alive of bribery and corruption, making the marketplace laugh. Even now we hear people who have left Congress call others by names such as "Onion Permit," "Pepper Permit," and "Contract." Even serving ministers are nicknamed "Ten Lakhs," "Storeyed House," and many other things.

Burma is not a place that possessed a Mahatma Gandhi or a preacher of the righteous path, nor does U Nu belong to that lineage. Yet upon leaving office today, he fearlessly asks that his actions over ten years be investigated. He says: "I entered poor; I leave poor!"

Political life today is regarded as a means of exercising force and obtaining benefits and advantages. The legislature: somehow form a group there; through it obtain a ministerial post; then... then... Under Congress rule, matters have reached the point where they can be imagined in this way. The idea that a minister is a servant of the people has disappeared; things have reached the point where ministers are described as "men wearing shirts with two pockets."

There may be pure people and people with a spirit of service. Yet somehow, during these ten years of Congress rule, such disgrace and notoriety have arisen. Many who were seated in responsible positions have conducted themselves in ways that bring disgrace and blame upon the office itself.

Some lovers of integrity within Congress have even introduced resolutions saying: "This must be investigated! How many who were ordinary men before independence have become wealthy? What was the extent of their property before and after they became ministers? All this must be discovered." For some reason, despite the presence of great paragons of virtue, they have not submitted themselves to this examination of the soul!

U Nu makes his declaration. We do not know him clearly or fully. Yet the very fact that he has made such an open and candid declaration is something new.

That is why, when we read, "I entered poor; I leave poor," we instinctively feel wonder. We also feel admiration.

*Seithi, Dravida Nadu, 2 November 1958*

## Translator's Notes

The first surviving scan begins inside U Nu's quoted departure statement and omits the printed heading; the descriptive title repeats its central declaration. The OCR form நூவின் refers to General Ne Win. The closing year appears as 28 in OCR; historical context places U Nu's transfer of power to Ne Win in 1958, which is used provisionally in English while the Tamil source remains unchanged.
