வகையிலும் பெற்றவர், அரங்கண்ணல் என்பதை நாடு

“அறப்போர்” உள்ளத்துக்கு, துணிவும் வேண்டும்;

## English Translation

Madras  
7 March 1961

There is no need to search for a reason why *Arappor*, launched by younger brother Arangannal, will serve as both an ornament and a weapon for our movement. The country knows that Arangannal has remained by my side, understood my thoughts, and acquired a maturity and training whose quality and extent I can commend with pleasure.

*Arappor*, a righteous struggle, is the method our movement has undertaken with unshakable confidence.

The spirit of righteous struggle requires courage; it requires purity; and above all, it requires selflessness.

It is easy to strike; it is difficult to endure.

When he attacks, a human being descends to the level of an animal; when he endures, he becomes a higher being.

When this is merely stated, putting it into practice may seem easy. But in practice, one is astonished to discover how necessary it is to withstand a thousand thoughts boring into the mind and to guard oneself from becoming agitated upon hearing abuse and accusation.

Nevertheless, those who have dedicated themselves to noble work must acquire the spirit of righteous struggle.

The first stage of righteous struggle is for each person to fight and overcome the thoughts, impulses, anger and anguish, desires and attachments that arise within. The immeasurable strength that victory confers cannot even be explained; it can only be felt. They say that a piece of coal buried beneath the earth for an immensely long time becomes a radiant diamond.

Younger brother Arangannal took responsibility for giving life to the journal *Dravida Nadu*, conducted it, and delighted the Tamil people.

By offering many verbal portraits in which pleasure and benefit are interwoven, he has secured a worthy place of his own.

I have no doubt that *Arappor*, with beauty and an elevating method, will become a fine journal admired by the country. Since the people share this view, I am certain their support will be abundant enough to gladden me.

*Arappor* will chart a good path of its own, present portraits of ideas with light that gives no heat and explanation free from hatred, and contribute to the realm of knowledge.

*Arappor* will also possess colour. The younger brother, being young, can seek and display colour; indeed, it is necessary.

This beautiful journal must reach every home and enable everyone to acquire the spirit of righteous struggle for the liberation of the country. *Arappor* needs your generous support to undertake that work. Trusting that you will provide it, I greet and welcome the journal.

Yours affectionately,  
Annadurai

(*Arappor*, publication date printed in OCR as 10 February 1967)
