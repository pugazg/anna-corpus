## English Translation

"Is this not Tamil! Listen, my friend, listen. Observe the beauty and richness of that sequence of words as it rolls forward and pounds the arguments of its opponents into sheets! A breeze is blowing now! Here is the storm! Did you hear that ancient Tamil verse? Is it merely the sweetness of its sound that we perceive? What use is learning Tamil if one does not speak fine Tamil in this fashion! Acting is blended with speech! Look there; see the radiance upon the face, like proof of Tamil valour! Here is the appearance of Alagiri! How beautifully this young man employs Tamil!"

For ten years Tamil Nadu has offered loving praise of this kind to one of the foremost fighters of the Dravidian movement! Tamil Nadu knows well that this bull has worked for the Dravidian movement without regard for forest or hill, never seeking places of pleasure or days of rest.

The stamp of a university degree gives him abundant opportunity to seek employment and conduct a settled life. Yet even when opportunities came to him of their own accord, the young man dedicated himself to spreading the Dravidian movement in every nook and corner of Tamil Nadu, proclaiming, "Long live Dravidam! May Tamil flourish!"

Tamil Nadu praises him, and throughout Tamil Nadu his work proceeds without obstacle. He mounts the platform and, in beautiful Tamil, presents rare...

---

...I explain. "Guide" is a word carrying the orthodox odour that came through religion.

During the crisis two years ago, everyone expected the Dravidian movement to be shattered and scattered, its strength ground to dust. But your faith has triumphed. The unity you maintained has developed today into this great power. I am filled with joy when I see the attachment and affection you bear toward me. I am one bound by that affection, not one who commands you or acts as your guide! Until now I have conducted myself without betraying the confidence you placed in me. I pledge that I shall do so in the future as well.

I am a believer in democracy. I desire dignity in politics; I am not one who issues commands! In this land where democracy has grown, leaders who command cannot arise, and must not arise. Without democracy, politics yields no benefit. I do nothing merely on my own, nor will I. I listen to the views of many among you, bring them together and consider them, and then reach a conclusion accordingly. It is by trusting your immense strength that I have accepted and discharged the great responsibility entrusted to me. I work with boundless patience.

Hundreds of thousands of us have gathered beneath this vast pavilion. I would say that all this feeling, impetus, and awakening are gifts bestowed upon us by our former leader, Sir P. Theagaraya Chetty. Had that man, born into a wealthy family, not roused the declining Dravidian community, we could not have grown so greatly. Had he not raised his storm-like voice not only in India but also in England, we could not have grown. Many leaders fought for the Dravidian community in those days. At this moment I offer all of them my heartfelt respects.

At this time I also remember our leader Periyar. Our comrade Jeeva is present on this same platform. Today we work in different movements. Yet I have faith that a day will come when Periyar, Jeevanandam, and I will work from one place. I am not a shopkeeper in a marketplace of principles; neither are they. I shall not abandon my principle, and must not. But a camp in which all three of us meet will certainly arise. Let it be a communist camp; let it be called a socialist camp. I do not mind. But the qualifier "Dravidian" must be added. Call it the Dravidian Communist Party: I will be its first member. Call it the Dravidian Socialist Party: I will be its first member. Let it be the Party of Dravidian Radicals. Let it be the Dravidian Revolutionary Movement; I have no objection. In those...

---

...the explanation he has given of the reason it remains so is like the pupil within the eye.

The excellence of this book tells us that he devoted a long period to this rare work.

It is not enough for this rare book merely to adorn every home; it must also be installed in every heart.

We commend our friend's worthy effort. The People's Forum of Coral Merchant Street, Madras, has published it in a most excellent manner.

This book is a fine mine that explains the growing thunder of rights.

*Dravida Nadu, 19 June 1951*
