## English Translation

The whole city gathered and stood watching that strange spectacle! It seems even the officers charged with keeping the peace decided, “Why should we invite this trouble?” They too stood watching!

With a battle song, the force arrived before that shop!

The shopkeeper understood the situation. For a moment he struggled, not knowing what to do; then he himself came outside boldly!

The woman commander roared and issued a warning:

“Do not come near me! Do not become prey to my axe! I am not responsible for the consequences!”

The proprietor stepped aside. The women’s force received its order from the commander: “Go on! Smash everything! Leave nothing behind!”

The order was carried out!

Next came the neighbouring shops; they met the same fate!

Anyone would be moved to ask: Who was this noblewoman, appearing in the form of Kannagi who burned Madurai? What caused her to set out with such a large force? What cruelty had those people inflicted upon her?

That woman’s name was Carrie Nation. The incident occurred in the American city of Wichita on 21 January 1901!

The places plundered by the force she led were shops selling alcoholic drinks!

There were many such incidents in her life!

It is said that she disliked smoking and drinking alcohol. That was the reason for this kind of campaign!

She went to the city of London in 1909. In one part of the city, a large cigarette advertisement caught her eye. She had no axe in her hand then, but she did have a walking stick! The advertisement was torn to shreds!

A case was brought against her. A fine of five shillings and damages of twenty-five shillings were imposed!

She was delighted! The reason was that, in America, she had received a prison sentence for a smaller offence. This was less severe, was it not?

In 1880, the American state of Kansas passed a prohibition law.

The law came into force, yet alcohol did not disappear. The number of people distilling it secretly kept growing. Gradually, some began selling it even in public places, while large quantities were imported from neighbouring states.

The authorities’ actions produced no result. Selling liquor remained a highly profitable major trade.

It was in that situation that this woman began her campaign. The authorities did not merely watch; they also assisted her in many ways!

She accumulated abundant funds to conduct that great campaign.

The axe was the emblem of that struggle. Small axes were sold for a price!

It is said that the mayor of one city not only gave her funds but also presented her with an axe eight inches wide!

She was born in 1847 in the state of Kansas.

She married at the age of twenty-one. Her husband was reportedly a heavy drinker. Within a year and a half of the marriage, he fell victim to alcohol and died.

It was to prevent other women from experiencing the disappointment and catastrophe that had befallen her own life that she began campaigning against alcohol. Many people there honour her even today!

(*Dravida Nadu*, Pongal Annual, 1963)
