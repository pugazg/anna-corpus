தூபமிடல்! இவ்வளவும் நடைபெற்றுக் கொண்டிருக்கிறது.
கையைப் பிசைந்து கொள்கிறார்கள் - சிலர் கண்களைக்
கசக்கிக் கொள்கிறார்கள் - வேறு சிலர் விம்முகிறார்கள் -
சிலர் ஆத்திரத்தைக் கொட்டிக் காட்டுகிறார்கள் - மக்களோ
- காண்கிறார்கள்! களிப்படைகிறார்கள்! மீண்டும் மீண்டும்
பார்க்கிறார்கள், மேலும் மேலும் மகிழ்ச்சி அடைகிறார்கள்
- குடும்பம் குடும்பமாகக் காண்கிறார்கள், வீடுதோறும்
வியந்து வியந்து பேசுகிறார்கள்- நெஞ்சு நெஒிழ்கிறது
உருக்கமான கட்டங்களைக் காணும்போது, கண்களிலே
கனலும் புனலும் கொப்பளிக்கிறது பல கட்டங்களில், வேறு
கட்டங்களில் விலா. வலிக்கச் சிரிக்கிறார்கள், சில
. கட்டங்களில் கிழவன் குமரனாகிறான், மொத்தத்திலே
புதியதோர் மகிழ்ச்சி. ஆனால் நீண்ட நாட்களாகத் தேடிக்
கொண்டி ருந்த மகழ்ச்சி பெறுகிறார்கள் - மக்கள் - கண்டனம்
மடிகிறது, படம் ஓடுகிறது, ஓடிக் கொண்டே இருக்கிறது,
உலுத்தர்கள் . குரலை அடக்கியபடி, ஊராள்வோரின்
மிரட்டலைக் கண்டு அஞ்சாமல் நொந்து போனவர்களின்
தூற்றலைக் கண்டு புன்னகை புரிந்த வண்ணம், பராசக்தி
பவனி வருகிறாள் - பதர் காற்றிலே. பறந்துபோகிறது,
செந்நெல் குன்றெனக் குவிகிறது - வெற்றி, படம்
பிடித்தவர்களுக்குக் களிப்பூட்டும் விதமாக மட்டுமல்ல,
பகுத்தறிவாளரின் மனம் குளிரும் விதமாக மட்டுமல்ல,
படஉலகினருக்கே புதியதோர் பாதையைக் காட்டும் விதமாக
வெற்றி கிடைத்திருக்கிறது. வீணர்கள் சொல்லம்பு
வீசுகிறார்கள், மக்கள் வெள்ளித் தோட்டாக்களை வீசியபடி

## English Translation

Condemnation! Mockery! Threats! Incense offered to the government! All this is taking place.

Some wring their hands, some rub their eyes, others sob, and still others pour out their fury. But the people watch, rejoice, watch again and again, and take ever greater delight. Entire families see it; in every home they speak of it in wonder.

Their hearts melt at the moving scenes. In many scenes both fire and water well up in their eyes. At other moments they laugh until their ribs ache; in some, an old man becomes young. Taken altogether, it is a new happiness, yet one the people had sought for a long time.

Condemnation withers, while the film runs and continues to run. Silencing the voices of the worthless, unafraid of the rulers' threats, and smiling at the abuse of the aggrieved, *Parasakthi* advances in procession.

The chaff flies away in the wind and red paddy piles up like a hill. Its victory does not merely delight those who made the film, nor merely cool the hearts of rationalists. It is a victory that shows the entire film world a new path.

Futile men shoot arrows of words while the people fire silver bullets. Mischief-makers continue to spew perverse accusations, while the people adorn *Parasakthi* with a garland of joy and admire her beauty.

Jackals howl; the lion roars, not in response to their howling, but to issue its heroic cry.

*Parasakthi* runs triumphantly. Wrongdoers and people with nowhere else to go continue saying whatever enters their mouths. There is the crowd waiting to enter; here are young people recounting to others the delight they felt after seeing it. There stands a helpless woman comparing herself with the maiden she saw in the film. Here is a hypocrite who looks exactly like the one on screen. Such is the conversation of the town.

The worthless ask, "What injustice is this? These sinners are degrading Parasakthi! Is this a film? Is there a government at all?" They do not merely speak but abuse, though only after looking around, because day by day the ranks supporting *Parasakthi* grow while the camp of those who loathe it disintegrates.

*Parasakthi* is not only a successful film; it is also a school that gives a fitting lesson to the useless and fanatical.

The person who laid the foundation for all this success, for a victory that brought condemnation from one side and immense support from the other, is a worker in the world of reason named Karunanidhi, still at the age when a moustache has only begun to sprout, and indeed scarcely even that. He is one of my brothers, dear to me as medicine.

"One who has a younger brother fears no army," says the wisdom of the elders. My brothers are people of this kind. The *Parasakthi* they give will inevitably nourish the people with welfare, the deficient in understanding with fear, and those of poisonous thought with anger. It is doing so already.

By creating *Parasakthi*, Karunanidhi has given me even greater happiness than he has given the people. I ask everyone concerned with art and rationalist work to bless the growth of his artistic talent. Tamil Nadu must honour and nurture such ability.

The arts need ever more new light of this kind. Karunanidhi's talent is a good flower of the growing renaissance. I desire many more flowers. No, my desire does not stop there: I wish to see an entire flower garden.

Nor does my aspiration end with increasing the number of films that offer reason. I wish to see the film world itself put entirely to the service of rationalism. That can indeed happen.

How did a world of cinema become available to us? How did we obtain the method and instruments that weave light, sound and shadow into a film? Through fasting for divine sanction? Through God's grace? Through a boon from Ayyanarappan? No. It was because rational thinkers developed the field of science.

Therefore, when I say the film world must serve rationalism, I express not merely my desire but my right.

Our friend Perumal, who produced *Parasakthi* and gave it to Tamil Nadu, consulted me in the earliest days of the film. I welcomed his idea. I too bear some responsibility for the arrangement by which comrade Karunanidhi wrote the screenplay.

I knew well that Karunanidhi's "portrait" would be of a kind in which comrade Perumal could take pride. Today the people of the country have accepted it.

By the grace of *Parasakthi*, the anthem of Dravida Nadu now resounds throughout Tamil Nadu.

We see *Parasakthi* attacking worldly hypocrites and religious impostors with the arrows of reason.

*Parasakthi* smiles at young men who touch the heart of a new woman.

Nor has *Parasakthi* stopped there. She brings a new actor before the film world and asks, "What do you say?"

That actor too is a younger brother. Friends know that this young man, affectionately called Sivaji Ganesan, played Sivaji in the drama *The Hindu Kingdom Seen by Sivaji*, now called *Chandramohan*, staged in Madras some years ago.

We rejoice to see that Sivaji Ganesan has contributed his talent with consummate skill to the success of *Parasakthi*. The film world has gained a new man of talent.

When Ganesan laughs, the people laugh. When he grows angry, they grow angry. When he weeps, they shed tears. When he performs his furious dance, they do not dance, from embarrassment!

The finest actor is one who carries the minds of his audience into his own emotional state through his performance. No one fails to praise the elegance with which Ganesan has accomplished it. No one fails to salute that "maharaja beneath the tree."

As a fragrant bouquet rests within the ornamented braid of a beautiful woman, so we see *Parasakthi* alongside Ganesan. Through *Parasakthi*, Sivaji Ganesan's fame spreads; Ganesan's artistry gives *Parasakthi* an enchanting form.

My happiness doubles when I remember that I was the one who told comrade Perumal that Sivaji Ganesan was a fine actor capable of winning success in this manner. There is little need for me to insist that Ganesan, who has secured a good place in the acting world, should continue to move within the camp of rationalism.

The film won its proud success because no one behaved in a manner that invited dismissal as rotten or inferior. Every actor and actress performed with skill.

The elder of Udumalai, Narayana Kavi, has given songs that ensure Tamils can never forget the film. Every evening the people hear a song in their ears calling, "Come! Come! Come!" They go, taste the flavour of classical Tamil, hear the language of reason and gain inward strength, and rejoice in the excellence of acting.

Then they look back to find the "eminent persons" who went about calling it an atrocious film. Alas, those people are nowhere nearby. Like a snake within its anthill or a tiger hiding in the thicket, they remain in corners and crevices, veiled and muttering.

They mutter. The victory cry of *Parasakthi* makes Tamil Nadu rejoice.

(*Por Val*, *Parasakthi* commemorative issue, 20 December 1952. The issue commemorated the film's diamond-jubilee celebration on its seventy-fifth day, 30 December 1952.)

## Translator's Notes

The final citation prints the years as “20-12-22” and “30-12-22.” Since *Parasakthi* was released in 1952 and the note identifies its seventy-fifth day, English resolves both as 1952 while retaining the Tamil verbatim. “வெள்ளித் தோட்டாக்கள்” is a pun on audience spending, rendered “silver bullets.” “மரத்தடி மகாராஜா” recalls Ganesan's memorable destitute character in the film and is retained literally.
