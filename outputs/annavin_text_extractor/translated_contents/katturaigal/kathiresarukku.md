## English Translation

"Devotee, arise!"

"Why are you tormenting me like this, woman? I did not begin sleeping until midnight. You wake me again and plague my life. Living with you is difficult."

"Jewel among devotees, it is not your wife who wakes you. The poor lady has fallen asleep from exhaustion. Open your eyes and look! I am Paramasivan, Lord of Alavay, the Three-Eyed One. Do you not recognise me? Look here."

"Who is it? What a life, what misery! Someone comes even at this hour to torment me. Who are you? I cannot even open my eyes; they burn."

"Open not the eye of flesh but the eye of wisdom."

"Oh! A snake! Akilandam, get up! Light the lamp and bring a stick. A snake!"

"Do not fear."

"A tiger has come too! And a fellow with beard and moustache! Akilandam, wake up, wretched woman. How can you sleep like this?"

"Do you not recognise Paramasivan, clad in tiger skin and adorned with serpents? Devotee, do not fear. The snake will do nothing. Even if it bites, what harm? Do you not know the hymn declaring that sacred ash is a spell?"

"Lord, is it you? In my sleepy confusion I babbled. Forgive me.

Praise to the One with an earring-bearing ear;
praise to the One whose locks bear the moon;
praise to the Burner of the Three Cities;
praise to the Dancer who dwells in Tillai."

"Stop, man. You have sung from morning to evening until my ears were cooled; that is enough. Delighted by your praises, I have come to take you to Kailasa. Rise."

"Lord! What are you saying? Are you going to take me away?"

"Yes. Why are you agitated? Is not snow-filled, fragrant Kailasa the place we grant a noble devotee? You are about to receive a place unavailable to anyone in this age. Grasp my sacred feet; let us depart."

"Moon-crested Lord, one petition. If I gain entry to Kailasa so soon, Saivism will not flourish."

"This is a strange thing for you to say."

"It is true, my Father. Have I praised you from heart and tongue like the Four, composing honey-flowing songs? Have I plucked out and offered an eye like Kannappar? Am I Siruthondar or Iyarpagai? What have I, a humble man, done to merit this great blessing? If so ordinary a Saivite enters Sivaloka, everyone will practise Saivism only to my modest degree, and Saivism will cease to flourish. Let me therefore remain here many more years, wandering while singing your praise. Do not take me to Kailasa now, O bearer of Ganga, destroyer of Death, father of the elephant-faced one!"

"You speak cunningly so that you may remain here a little longer. But if I do not take you, my fame will fade and Saivism will suffer."

"How will Saivism suffer?"

"People may say: this great devotee melted like syrup, sang many songs, sought the Supreme, and languished with many pleas; yet Hara neither came nor took his servant to Kailasa. Saivism has no substance, so let us seek another path. That could destroy Saivism. Therefore, beloved of Siva, depart quickly."

"Impossible! I will not come! Absolutely impossible!"

Hearing this dreadful cry, the wife sleeping nearby awoke in fear, sprinkled water upon her delirious husband's face, and roused him. Returning from his dream world, the great Saivite scholar laughed.

"Laugh away! You frightened me. I have told you for days: at least tomorrow show yourself to a doctor," she said. He laughed and replied, "Foolish woman, it was only a dream. Lord Siva himself appeared before me..."

Before he could finish, she folded her hands and said, "Then our troubles will end today. Would he not grant us land and wealth like your younger brother has? Did you ask? Of course not!"

"He came of his own accord and invited me to Kailasa."

"What will become of me?" she cried. Embracing her, he consoled her: "I said I could not come now. He set the snake upon me; I cried out in fear, you awakened me, and the dream dissolved."

I asked Veeran, "What wild fabrication is this? Which Saivite ever had such an experience?"

He rebuked me: "Bharatha, you are as hasty as those who oppose Tamil music. Did I say this happened to a named Saivite in a named town? Do not interrupt before hearing the whole story." He continued:

"Do you imagine that merely because devotees seek Siva's grace, any Saivite elder would be ready to go if Siva himself appeared and invited him to Kailasa?"

I pondered this difficult question, as our Saivite friends too surely would. Some would ask to remain on earth until their sixtieth-year ceremony, as happened to Thiru. Vi. Ka. Others would seek an adjournment until completing a new commentary on the *Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam*. Still others would promise to come only after announcing their vision to the world and converting everyone to Saivism. How many would run behind the Lord saying, "I have come"? I, a small man ignorant of the nature of the Saivite path, do not know.

Veeran said that at the meeting of Siva-loving devotees held in Tuticorin on the final Sunday of the previous month, they offered a safe prayer to prevent such a predicament.

"What new variety of prayer is that?" I asked.

Fearing that Siva might decide their devotion had matured and come to take them to Kailasa, they sought protection: "Lord, grant long life to our society and to its members, the devotees of Siva."

This is entirely new. The old prayer was, "Grant freedom from rebirth; unite me with your sacred feet; let me too come there; grant me liberation." The new prayer asks for long life. Present-day Saivites have no desire to abandon wife and cattle, son and jewels, books and inkpot, platform and scented garments, sacred mark and women named Tilagavathi, wealth and sacred dance, to live in Kailasa. Thus they prayed for long earthly life. That is the safe prayer, Veeran explained.

I understood. At the annual meeting of the Saiva Siddhanta Sabha in Tuticorin, chaired by Professor, or in his preferred style Mahamahopadhyaya Pandithamani, Kathiresan Chettiar, he concluded his address by asking Siva to grant long life to the devotees, according to the report in *Mithran*.

As Veeran observed, I wondered why Saivites who thoroughly understood life's impermanence, the sorrows of this world, and the greatness of the next should desire long life in this illusory, transient world ensnared by lust and anger. Their safe prayer itself inspired his satirical picture.

Even Siva, if he had no other work, might wonder why all earlier devotees prayed to join him while these modern Saivites seek grace to remain long upon earth.

They also specified how long. Like an old man who says, "I am weary of the world and do not fear death, but I should like to see my grandson married before peacefully closing my eyes," the Tuticorin devotees wish to remain until certain "pitiful people" reform. Until those who have lost reason, Tamil, and the Saivite path become human beings, these "great souls" wish to remain on earth, not for themselves but to reclaim the erring.

Those assembled under Kathiresar are lords of compassion indeed. Who are the people needing reform? Hear his own words: although our ancestors gave us great treasuries of religious books and truths, some among us fail to benefit from them, forget that great debt, revile both the elders and their truths, and draw people from humanity toward animality. Kathiresar reportedly prayed that these deviations disappear and that the Sabha and its members live long enough to let people gain the religious eye, greater than education, and clearly behold truth.

Let Siva grant grace and the devotees live long. Let the Lord send some of us pitiable people ahead to inform him that they remain here; I shall not complain. But I want to know: what are these great textual treasures? Do the meaningless and obscene stories draw people toward animality, or do those who seek to remove obscenity do so? If the latter, whom do they draw? Are Siva's devotees themselves susceptible? Rather than ask Hara to give wisdom to the pitiable, is it proper to ask him to prolong the devotees' lives so that we may behold truth? Is this preferable to the bliss of beholding the divine parents?

Kathiresar, hear without anger. You declared religion necessary for morality. Has religion established morality? Self-Respecters, the people you call pitiable, appeared only a few years ago. Before that, were most religious exponents paragons of conduct? Are there not wealthy experts today who possess playful religious learning? Has morality been established in temples, monasteries, halls, society buildings, and homes fragrant with Saivism?

You said our Saivism accepts other religions without hatred. Is that true? How contemptuously have the religious teachers spoken of Jains, even singing abuse before God! Is that non-rejection? They did not stop with abuse; Jains were impaled. Does that accord with belief in God, religious character, humanity, rational discernment, or ordinary compassion? Saivite dignitaries even conduct festivals for it.

Professor, many years ago we pitiable people addressed a list of questions to your people. You prayed for long life; I too desire that you live long, especially since you say it is to reform us. We do not want the devotees who have long left these questions unanswered to depart for Siva before answering them. Therefore, teacher and friend, whether from attachment to the Saivite path, compassion for our supposedly low kind, or love for the Lord of Chidambaram, attempt an answer before the people's forum.

## Questions

1. Did any religion exist in our country before Saivism?
2. Which religion was it?
3. When did Saivism arise?
4. Who was its founder or religious teacher?
5. What doctrines unique to Saivism are absent from other religions?
6. What is the authority for them?
7. Does Saivism worship a personal god named Siva?
8. Or is it founded without a separate deity, upon doctrines or qualities alone?
9. Is Siva the name of a god, a condition, or a quality?
10. Why is Siva given a form?
11. Why is he represented as having a wife and children?
12. Why do the many Saivite gods have thousands of Sanskrit names?
13. Why do thousands of gods and their wives and children have separate names?
14. What differences separate Saivism from Jain and Buddhist doctrines?
15. Does Saivism accept or reject the Aryan Veda and animal-sacrifice rituals?
16. Do the Saivite teachers accept or reject the Aryan Veda and its sacrifices?
17. When Jains condemned Vedic sacrifice, which Veda and sacrifice did they mean?
18. Are Saivism's Siddhanta and Agamas Sanskrit or Tamil?
19. Why does every town associated with a Saivite god possess a distinct sacred greatness?
20. Why are only towns and gods sung by the religious teachers accorded special value?
21. Why did Saivite teachers and devotees persecute Buddhists and Jains?
22. If they did not, why are those communities indiscriminately reviled in the *Tevaram* and similar works?
23. If Sanskrit stories and Puranas are rejected, do Saivites have any god left?
24. Does Saivism teach worship of one god or many gods?
25. How many gods have been discovered so far?
26. Is the number complete, or will more arise?
27. Does Saivism prescribe temples?
28. Does it prescribe idol worship?
29. Must a person worship God only through another acting as priest?
30. What authority supports these practices?
31. Does every Saivite have the right personally to worship the god of his or her choice?
32. Does Saivism recognise caste distinctions?
33. Are caste distinctions now observed in Saivite temples?
34. Is that contrary to Saivism?
35. If so, have Saivites yet remedied the contradiction?
36. Does any Agama require camphor to be burned in worship? Which one?
37. If an Agama says so, is it an Aryan or Tamil Agama?
38. Since when has the custom of burning camphor been practised?
39. Does the god Pillaiyar have a place in Saivism?
40. Do Saivites accept the *Kanda Puranam*?
41. Do they accept the *Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam*?
42. If so, is it compatible with Saivism?
43. Do Saivites accept the *Siva Rahasya*, *Siva Mahapurana*, and *Siva Parakrama*?
44. Did the Four Teachers accept Brahma and Vishnu?
45. Are those separate gods?
46. If the Four sang disparagingly of Vishnu, is that religious hatred or not?
47. Do Saivites accept the *Manusmriti* and *Parashara Smriti*?
48. Do Saivites approve the impalement of Jains?
49. Do they deny or approve the continued staging in temples of festivals based on the *Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam* story?
50. Why is sacred ash applied?
51. Do they accept the story told about the lingam and its pedestal?
52. If not, what answer can they give to the Sanskrit authority that states it?
53. From what language is the word *lingam*? What does it mean, and on what authority?
54. What is Kailasagiri; what is Subramanian; what is Kandan; what is Kumaran?
55. What does "daughter of the mountain king" mean?
56. What benefits have so far resulted from existing temple, worship, and festival systems?
57. Must they continue unchanged?
58. Is it just that many lakhs of rupees are spent in their name?
59. May that expenditure and time be redirected, or must matters remain as they are?
60. Do Saivites accept a heavenly world?
61. Do they accept rebirth?
62. Do they accept ancestral rites?
63. Do they accept Brahmins?
64. Do they accept every miracle of the religious teachers as literally having occurred?
65. Do they likewise accept every miracle in the Saivite Puranas as historical fact?
66. Do they accept as true the local legends of place, deity, and sacred water attached to every temple and god?
67. What marriage system belongs to Saivites?
68. Do Saivites or Saivite gods have a devadasi system?
69. Is there a varna-ashrama system?
70. If so, what is its authority?
71. If not, what is the authority for denying it?
72. Why is it presently in force?
73. What purpose do Saivite monasteries serve?
74. What have they accomplished so far?
75. Must those monasteries continue unchanged?
76. Has any Saivite yet attempted to reform them?
77. Among present-day Saivites, who is qualified to express an opinion about the religion?
78. Who possesses the necessary competence?
79. Do Saivites accept the Siva depicted in Vaishnavite Puranas such as the *Mahabharata*, *Ramayana*, and *Bhagavata*?
80. Can Saivism be explained without Sanskrit and the books written in it?

## Translator's Notes

The title addresses Pandithamani Kathiresan Chettiar, who chaired the Saiva Siddhanta Sabha meeting discussed in the essay. `Veeran` and `Bharatha` are retained as names within the satirical narrative frame. The Tamil OCR extraction lost several visible question numbers because the source was printed in two columns; the English list restores the scan's continuous numbering from 1 through 80 without changing the verbatim Tamil block. Period religious titles and work names are transliterated where a generic translation would erase the reference. The final publication line was restored from the scan.
