உளறினான். பதவிப் போதையும் அப்படித்தானோ என்று சந்தேகிக்க
வைக்கிறது, காமராசரின் வெற்றிப் பேச்சு!

மீண்டும் வெற்றி பெற்ற காமராசர், வெற்றிக்களிப்பில் விவேகத்தைத்
தள்ளி வைக்காது, நாட்டு மக்களின் சிந்தனை வளர்ந்துள்ளது என்பதை
நினைவில் நிறுத்திப் பேசிடவேண்டும். பதவியாசையே இல்லாத காமராசர்,
துஷ்ட நித்ரக சிஷ்ட பரிபாலனம்' புரியத்தான் மீண்டும் தலைவரானார் :
என்பதை நம்பிடும் காலம் போய்விட்டது. அதனைக் கவனத்தில் வைத்து,
போட்ட கணக்குப் பிழையுடையது என்பதை யுணர்ந்து புதுக் கணக்குப்
போட்டுப் பேசிடவேண்டும்.

ஆனால், காமராசராகட்டும்; சுப்பையாவாகட்டும்- எவர் வந்தாலும்,
இடிந்து வரும் காங்கிரஸ் கோட்டையை எத்தனை நாள் தாங்கி நிற்கமுடியும்
என்றுதான் மக்கள் கேட்கின்றனர். மதியுள்ளோர் கேட்கின்றனர். நாட்டு
நலம் விரும்பிகள்யாவரும் ஒருமுகமாக, ஒரு குரலில் கேட்கின்றனர் -
மீண்டும் தலைவராக வந்துள்ள 'பதவி. ஆசையே இல்லாத' காமராசரை!

- (திராவிடநாடு - 10,9.50)

## English Translation

"Why have you come?"

"It is all for your own good!"

"Do you not understand? After forcing your way inside, you pose riddles too?"

"Do not make a noise, Sarasangi! I have not the slightest desire for any of this. I spent ten years enjoying it and grew tired. But Ekambaram from the house opposite competed with me. I became angry, gathered all the strength I still possessed, leapt over the wall, and came here. Should I be sacrificed merely because he chose to compete?"

"No, my dear, I came only at your mother's request."

"Do not sulk. How beautiful is Muthamma from the third house! She pined away for me, yet I rejected her. If I wished, Muthammal would be mine even today!"

A man entering late at night, speaking in a trembling voice with no connection between tongue and words, then throwing down money and leaving, was nothing new to her. The man who came today was known to her: Velappan, who had once shouted that he would never return and gone away.

It seems he returned for her welfare. The man opposite had competed with him. Her mother had requested it. She must not be stubborn. Though he had seen Muthamma's enchanting smile, he had resisted it and come here. Hearing all this, she laughed. He, however, leapt with delight, imagining that she had believed the tale he had invented.

In the Tamil Nadu Congress election, Kamaraj was once again elected president. Comrade Subbiah was defeated once again.

Kamaraj had already served for many years as president of the Tamil Nadu Congress. Yet that desire did not leave him. He entered the contest and won.

There is nothing surprising in his victory. He has long enjoyed the Congress presidency as a monopoly. He could therefore manufacture a group devoted to supporting him. Even in Congress panchayat and delegate elections, he worked with close attention to field and elect comrades who would stand on his side. That is how Kamaraj became president.

Today, there is not a trace in Congress of dignity, a spirit of sacrifice, or the good intention to work for the people's welfare. Men crazed for office have calculated that organizational posts within Congress are steps by which they may climb, and they have gathered there seeking places.

Through small favours and the use of his influence, Kamaraj gave them a taste. Once a cat has acquired the taste, will it cease circling the hanging food pot? That is why they elected Kamaraj president.

Though Kamaraj had served as president for ten years, his thirst for office was not quenched. Unwilling to descend from the presidential seat, he performed many deceptions and returned as president.

The speech delivered by the victorious Kamaraj is highly amusing. One can also hear in it a painful song with a perverse note. Kamaraj speaks like Velappan entering a prostitute's house:

> The office of Congress president is not some great position.

> It would be wrong for anyone to think that it is a foundational post from which one can proceed to successively higher offices.

> I was only wondering when I might set down this burden and leave.

> Friends insisted. Other Congressmen insisted in the same way. The people of Tamil Nadu too requested it.

> Some opposed me. I entered the contest only for that reason.

> I have absolutely no desire for office.

> Had I wished to become a minister, I could have done so long ago. That post was waiting for me.

When one reads these pearls scattered by Kamaraj, one remembers Velappan entering his "old chaste woman's" house at midnight.

If the presidential seat is filled with trouble, why must he remain hanging from it like a baya weaverbird's nest? He says this presidency is not a route to higher office. Yet he himself says that a ministerial post waited for him and that he alone pushed it away.

Why was that office waiting? Through what qualification or ability did it approach him, kneel before him, and wait? We do not understand. Was there any reason other than that he was president of the Tamil Nadu Congress?

This man says he longed to cast aside the burden and that he had held the office for years amid troubles. Yet if he contested the election saying that some people opposed him and therefore had to be suppressed, does that not show that he lacks even the slightest concern for Congress's future?

Had he thought great divisions might arise within Congress, he could have withdrawn honourably. Not only did he fail to do so; by lecturing those who opposed him, he stirs sleeping anger. He has incited the defeated as though inviting a new faction to arise.

Kamaraj must understand that the times have changed. Instead, it is neither good nor useful for anything except the country's laughter to speak after wrongly calculating that the people's minds remain dark.

Kamaraj says his friends requested him and the whole country asked him to return. But he received 155 votes, while Comrade Subbiah, who opposed him, received 99. Will people not ask why someone who returned at the request of the Tamil people encountered so much opposition?

Kamaraj's calculation is wrong. The people will not be deceived. He may lengthen his list of qualifications by saying that he has no desire for office, that office brings him no benefit, and that he even rejected a ministerial post. People will laugh, especially when they hear the reasons he gives for having contested.

Velappan too spoke that way: "I have no desire. I am not interested in the pleasure you provide. Your mother called me. Ekambaram opposite competed with me. Muthammal in the third house invited me..." He babbled whatever came to mind to conceal his lust.

Kamaraj's victory speech makes one wonder whether intoxication with office works in the same way.

Having won once more, Kamaraj should not cast discernment aside in the joy of victory. He should speak while remembering that the thinking of the country's people has matured.

The age when people would believe that Kamaraj, who has "no desire whatsoever for office," became president again solely to "punish the wicked and protect the virtuous" has passed. He must keep that in mind, understand that his calculation was mistaken, make a new calculation, and speak accordingly.

But whether Kamaraj or Subbiah comes, the people ask only this: how many days can anyone hold up the collapsing fortress of Congress? Thoughtful people ask it. Everyone who desires the country's welfare asks with one mind and one voice, directing the question to Kamaraj, who has become president once again despite having "no desire for office."

*Dravida Nadu*, 10 September 1950.

## Translator's Notes

The canonical Tamil OCR block is preserved verbatim by the bilingual builder. The article's extended sexual and brothel analogy is translated as its deliberate satirical rhetoric, without treating it as a factual claim about Kamaraj. The OCR-damaged Sanskrit-derived maxim “துஷ்ட நித்ரக சிஷ்ட பரிபாலனம்” is read in its conventional form as “துஷ்ட நிக்ரஹ சிஷ்ட பரிபாலனம்,” meaning punishment of the wicked and protection of the virtuous. Vote totals and political claims are translated in the source's voice and have not been independently verified.
