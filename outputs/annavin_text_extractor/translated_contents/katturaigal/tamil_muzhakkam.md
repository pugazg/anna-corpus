## English Translation

"To those who say that I am raising an empty cry, I issue this challenge. I echo the voice of the people who elected and sent me as a member of Parliament. If you consider this an empty cry, here is my resignation! Accept it today. You will hold another election in my constituency, will you not? I shall contest that election on the very issue you deny today! Let us put it to the test. At least then, let the whole world realise the truth; let everyone know that you are committing an injustice!"

With these words, a man named Sundaralingam resigned his seat in the House of Representatives.

Where did this happen? In Tamil Nadu? His name sounds Tamil, you may think. He is indeed a Tamil, but this happened not here, but in Ceylon!

Sundaralingam had earlier flung down his letter of resignation in order to expose the dishonourable refusal to respect Tamil.

And now, on 14 February, news of victory arrives! He, who issued a challenge for a worthy cause, has won. The Ceylon government that rules through arrogance has been defeated! In the by-election held in the same constituency, this advocate of Tamil rights received 8,995 votes. The candidate of the opposing UNP, the ruling party, received 2,003.

The by-election demonstrates not only how just the Ceylon Tamils' demand for their rights is, but also how intensely Tamil representatives have committed themselves to securing them.

A general election is to take place in Ceylon in another week or two. Sundaralingam, though victorious, has not even had time to take his seat in the legislature! The House is to be dissolved and another election held. If he is to take office again, he must contest that election too.

Neither Kakkan nor Kamaraj, nor anyone else here, would consent to place himself in such a predicament!

But because the Ceylon Tamil people can see the great issue of securing Tamil its rightful place, courage shines in their hearts: they regard office as trivial and say, "We shall fight for the people's voice; however many elections may come, the people will never fail to support us when we fight for justice."

This incident shows the strength and brilliance attained there by the rallying cries: "Tamil must be accorded its rightful place! Tamil, like Sinhala, must be recognised as an official language! We shall not permit in the slightest any attempt to change this and make Sinhala alone the language of government!"

Ceylon's total population is estimated at 8.1 million. Of these, 2.4 million are Tamil-speaking people: thirty per cent. Yet the Kotelawala government has "filtered" them and granted the vote to only one and a quarter million! This is injustice and wrongdoing. May they take revenge through such filtering? Is it right to drive away those denied the vote? The Tamil people have been fighting over these questions for many years. The honest voice of the Tamils who went there from here in search of a livelihood has gained no strength in Ceylon because of the indifference of the seat of power in Delhi, which has a responsibility to recognise their wretched lives and provide a remedy! Kotelawala frequently meets Nehru, and Nehru repeatedly dines with Kotelawala.
