## English Translation

The beautiful skylark that flew across the heavens was V. R.

In the sky of writing, he was a skylark that fastened wings of thought and flew at heights of ideas beyond reach.

The vulture of orthodoxy continually circled, seeking to bring down this skylark. The eagle of antiquity too remained in flight. The priestly hunter's arrow waited for its opportunity.

But none of them brought the skylark down. They could not.

That beautiful skylark continued to fly according to its own will.

By birth V. R. belonged to that community, but in every other respect he was one of us, a good man.

He was a danger signal to the *agraharam*.

To the stronghold of orthodoxy, he was a walking warning board.

In the priestly world, he was called a wicked man.

But to us, V. R. was a rare arrival, a fine gift, and a delightful feast.

His thought suited the age, benefited the country, and was necessary to us.

Caste did not bend his pen.

Clan did not scorch his thinking.

Scripture did not overthrow his ideas.

His writing was neither slippery ground nor mud filled with hazards.

He stated everything clearly, said as much as was needed, and never stopped midway.

Because he spoke what was in his heart exactly as it was, many bore hatred and hostility towards him.

We have lost such a skylark of the literary heavens.

The loss caused by his death cannot be made whole.

V. R. was the one who warned the country that everything which ought not to cry out in certain places would gather there.

He was fresh nectar found in a poisoned pool.

He was a wild rose found among thorny cacti.

He was an oasis amid a desert of language.

Yes, he was an unexpected treasure found for us in the stronghold of priests.

He was a wondrous birth that arose in an *agraharam*, but the country honoured him as a necessary birth. Today it writhes and weeps at his departure.

May our swollen hearts too accompany that funeral procession through the streets.

(*Dravida Nadu*, 2-9-51)
