## English Translation

...a scholar who had worked for twenty [years], and one of the foremost Indian political experts. He was acclaimed as an outstanding thinker on the Constitution and methods of administration. The following are passages from a recent article entitled “Indian Administration” by this experienced man, whose capacity for inquiry matched his experience:

“Government secretaries and the heads of every department have been reduced to dancing to the tune played by ministers! If a minister says something somewhere in the course of a speech, it has become the task of the government secretaries to search for evidence and statistics to prove the statement. Even in the daily matters they must examine and dispose of, the head of every department has been placed in the difficult position of having to accommodate and act in accordance with the wishes of ministers.

“Even in ordinary matters such as granting promotions to subordinate officers and making transfers, departmental heads have been compelled to act according to ministerial intervention.

“Government secretaries with twenty or twenty-five years of work and experience now see their advice reduced to cries lost in the wind. They must close their eyes and silently carry out the ministers’ orders. All the administrative experience they have acquired is being squandered and wasted!

“Because ministers intervene even in routine work, the enthusiasm and initiative once found among departmental heads responsible for administration have diminished. They have developed the attitude, ‘We shall do something for the salary we receive. What does it matter how anything turns out?’ If this condition continues, the administration will fall into disorder and be shattered.”

The article was not written with the object of blaming the rulers or condemning Congress administration. He wrote it with concern and an impartial frame of mind, and with the sorrowful thought that the country was suffering.

*Dravida Nadu, 9 April 1950*
