## English Translation

Some people are now worried: “Where have these Saivites gone? Nothing can be seen of them!” They lament that the Madras Government, while proposing amendments to the Hindu Religious Endowments Protection Act, has made no arrangement for singing the *Tevaram* and *Prabandham* in temples. Just as temples provide for Vedic study and teachers who recite the Vedas, they grieve that the government has done nothing to provide a place for reciters of the *Tevaram*.

They point out sorrowfully that properties endowed for *Tevaram* recitation exceed those set apart for Vedic recitation. They sing a lament that, even before Brahmins reciting the Vedas became temple priests, Oduvars served as temple functionaries and worshipped God through musical hymns. Their hearts ache as they say that the Nayanmars and Alvars who sang the *Tevaram* and *Prabandham* possess a distinction not enjoyed by the rishis who sang the Vedas. They criticize the government, saying it is neither proper nor honest to spend on ablution and worship for Nayanmars and Alvars installed beside the gods as gods themselves, yet spend not a paisa on the hymns those saints composed.

The very people making all these criticisms wholeheartedly support and welcome the Madras Government’s present amendment bill.

They say:

“Opposing this bill will not benefit Hindu endowments. Anyone who honestly weighs the country’s present condition and that of Hindu endowments will welcome it and never think of opposing it.”

“Hindu endowments receive an annual income of thirty-five million rupees.”

“This exceeds the revenue of our country’s princely states.”

“Though so vast a sum is spent each year, religious feeling and religious discipline have not advanced even slightly.”

“On the contrary, disbelief in religion and disgust toward religious institutions have increased.”

“The public has also clearly understood that a large share of this amount is spent for the benefit of a notable few.”

Through these statements, they ask everyone to praise and welcome the bill.

It is therefore evident that these supporters wish one more amendment added to those already made by the government. They say the bill now defines nine classes of expenditure and ask that a tenth be added: reciting the *Tevaram* and *Prabandham* and training people to recite them.

They do not appear to have considered whether this addition is indispensable in present circumstances. Long ago a Siddhar sang:

> No front veranda, no house of one’s own;  
> What need is there for Tevaram, Kudambai?

Today we see countless people, including thousands of Oduvars, suffering without even a street veranda upon which to rest after the exertion of singing to the Supreme. Not only that: before the eyes of those who wish to delight God by singing hymns in melody, countless people totter without food. The *Tevaram* that supposedly obtained coins by singing to remove famine still exists, yet famine has grown until even the Supreme One’s temples must receive rationed food.

Do we need a God of such “broad outlook and abundant compassion” that he says, “Sing of me and I shall give you a coin to remove your hunger”? Do we need a perfectly selfish God who demands songs about himself before removing famine and disease? Or do we need a selfless and compassionate God who regards all life as one and spontaneously sends rain, the root cause for relieving hunger?

Rationalists who follow nature need not wrestle with those questions. But should not those “men of wisdom” drinking the imaginary milk that rain falls only through Maheswara’s grace consider them? From Kudambai Siddhar’s age to that of Kuppusami Oduvar, has the *Tevaram* benefited any sphere of human life?

Apart from demanding a share by asking, “When money is spent on Brahmins reciting Sanskrit Vedas, why should money not be spent on Tamils singing Tamil *Tevaram* and *Prabandham*?”, will this petition bring any benefit to the country’s people? Vedic reciters use their payment to eat until their sides bulge and, having no other work, sit speaking empty Vedanta. Likewise, money given to *Tevaram* reciters may fill their stomachs and allow them to sit on verandas singing in melody that life is illusion and only earth is certain. Will it serve any other useful purpose? Why lie brazenly that those who ask are atheists harming the country?

Their petition raises a doubt. Do they suppose that the Supreme will be pleased only when a hymn is sung at public expense and become hostile if it is sung without cost? The arrangement by which one person sings a hymn for payment and the Supreme accepts it in perfect satisfaction and grants benefit to all is harmful, resembles obtaining profit through bribery, and contradicts divinity itself. Why regard a rationalist who says so as God’s supreme enemy?

Saivite friends concede that despite spending thirty-five million rupees annually on temples, religious feeling and discipline have not grown. How, then, do they imagine those qualities will grow if a small amount is allocated to sing the *Tevaram*?

Although Agamas and Smritis do not permit *Tevaram* recitation according to temple rules, and although Brahmins dismiss the *Tevaram* and *Tiruvasagam* as songs of non-Brahmin mendicants, our Oduvars continue singing them in temples. Many *Tevaram* schools also exist. Yet even these have produced no religious feeling or discipline among the people.

The *Tevaram* and *Prabandham* presently sung in temples are already paid performances. Though temple public funds may not cover them, private individuals and lovers of the *Tevaram* spend money. Still religious feeling and discipline have not arisen. If those hymns actually contained such feeling and discipline, people hearing them would inevitably acquire both. Have people complained that they failed to practise the moral paths in the *Tevaram* only because temple money was not spent on its singing?

The arrangement forbidding *Tevaram* during temple rites was not created yesterday; it has existed for a long time. To enrich and honour a country’s mother tongue, and in opposition to Sanskrit Vedic recitation in Tamil Nadu’s temples, Tamil verses ought to be recited there. Though the hymns are said to have been composed by Saivite teachers blessed by Siva and to praise and marvel at him, Brahmins classified them as “Pandaram songs” and prohibited them during temple worship and festivals. That arrangement remains in force in Siva temples throughout Tamil Nadu, while all acts of worship are conducted with Sanskrit mantras.

We have long said that if Siva were truly the Tamils’ god and loved Tamil, he should by his grace set fire to the belly of the Aryan doctrine that forbids the *Tevaram* and *Tiruvasagam*, which he himself inspired and which praise him, from being sung in his presence. He should strike Sanskrit mantras upon the head and cause all his rites to be performed by Tamil people in Tamil. Had he done so, the honour of at least those Saivite Tamil elders who wander deludedly speaking of “our Siva” and “our Saivism” might have been saved.

We also say that if these Saivites truly love Tamil and wish it to flourish, they should not accept as their God one who drinks Sanskrit like honey, treats Tamil as poison, and delights in turning Tamil people into Sudras. We are not greatly troubled even by their failure to honour and develop Tamil. Our doubts arise because they freely welcome Sanskrit mantras in Tamil Nadu’s temples.

They ask for money to sing the *Tevaram*, yet do not ask that Tamil worship replace Sanskrit ritual. They seek only a share beside the existing arrangement, not its removal. We grieve that despite so much experience, they still lack knowledge, dignity, and Tamil consciousness. We grieve that the lamp of reason and feeling of self-respect have not arisen in them and that they do not act remembering they are Tamil people. We are not villains who abuse God or worshippers without cause.

We understand what kind of God and arrangement would be beneficial. That understanding may differ from the God presently in practice. Does that make us revilers of God? If someone removes stones and thorns from a walking path or asks that they be removed, should he be called a sinner who says there should be no path at all?

We say only that present arrangements and laws concerning God and religion do not suit people’s well-being and must be corrected and altered. The government is now doing this little by little. Friends who daily abuse us as atheists should compare our ideas with the reforms being made by the government. They will then understand that calling us atheists was merely the product of an itching tongue.

For years we condemned the cruelties and excesses committed by heads of monasteries using the great sums set apart for temples and mutts, and demanded their abolition. We were abused then. Today the situation has changed so greatly that all these are regarded as matters requiring legal correction, and those undertaking such correction are considered good people. Let those who revile us forget us and our party if they wish, but remember whose labour brought about this condition.

Should they not at least consider whose work enabled the secret abuses long hidden within monasteries to reach the press?

The March 29 issue of *Dinasari* reported that a Dharmapuram Mutt ascetic had committed an improper act against Vairathammal, wife of Arumugam, a cook in the mutt; that four others assisted him; and that a case had been filed against five people, including the Pandara Sannidhi, under Sections 22, 497, 366, and 229.

Before the Madras Government introduced its amendment bill after recognizing abuses in monasteries, many such private “miraculous divine sports” were committed by Pandara Sannidhis, but did not come to light. Who knows how many Vairathammals suffered? Fear of the temple helped conceal the sports performed by “Pandara Sannidhis” with women. Now circumstances have changed. The games played by monastic heads who treated public money as personal wealth can be exposed. Newspapers, without fear or favour, boldly reveal these secret “miraculous sports” so that ordinary people recognize the daytime disguises of the Pandara Sannidhis.

In this situation, can anything be more comic and painful than Saivites still working to allocate public money to sing a *Tevaram* that praises the story of Pillaiyar’s birth after Paramasiva assumed a male elephant’s form and coupled with a female elephant? Paramasiva’s sportive pleasures were performed openly after changing form. The Pandara Sannidhis perform theirs secretly without changing form. That is the difference between the God of Kailasa and monastic heads of his lineage.

There Parvati assumed the form of a cow elephant and Paramasiva that of a bull elephant to satisfy desire. Here Pandara Sannidhis retain their costume, make public money a fence, and conduct the games of Manmatha behind it. The *Tevaram* and *Prabandham* protect both groups. These hymns cast the net called devotion among the people and seize the wealth they earned through labour.

Saivite true devotees who want this work to continue petition for public money so the story of Siva assuming a great elephant form when his consort became a female elephant may be proclaimed, and so Oduvars may continue through the *Tevaram* the Nayanmars’ abuse of Vishnu, Indra, Brahma, Jains, and Buddhists. They shamelessly say religious feeling and discipline will thereby grow. Let that rest.

Do all Saivites here support the present amendment bill with a few changes? No. Saivites in Tirunelveli support it with one or two amendments, while those in Thanjavur oppose it with all their strength.

At Kumbakonam on March 25, 1949, the Saivites of Thanjavur district resolved:

“The present bill will certainly destroy our dharma.”

“It will produce no benefit whatever.”

“All Hindus must oppose it as one community.”

“We ourselves are attempting to degrade our incomparable dharmas.”

“This law will destroy Hinduism.”

“We must not permit it.”

“We must vigorously oppose the bill.”

“And this must be done in time.”

But on March 2, 1949, Saivites of Tirunelveli district, who spread Saiva Siddhanta throughout South India, adopted the statements quoted in the first part of this article and resolved to support the bill with two amendments.

The course of Tirunelveli Subbiah Pillai does not please Kumbakonam Palaniappa Mudaliar. One supports; the other opposes. Both sections are mature Saivites, but their camps differ. We see a little reformist tendency among the Tirunelveli supporters. Among the Kumbakonam opponents, because the guru’s seat is at Kumbakonam, we see opposition born of fear of that seat. Therefore we consider the Tirunelveli group better than the Kumbakonam group: they are better than these people. Whether Kumbakonam or Tirunelveli wins depends upon the government’s decision on the bill.

Our view is that the government’s present effort should succeed. Yet we firmly say that those supervising arrangements for spending money on God, instead of refusing all such expenditure, are likely to indulge in extravagant games with it. We say money should not be wasted by confining a God said to fill everywhere without exception to a few specified places, building temples and monasteries, and creating priests and monastic heads.

Do not spend cash on God. God will not be deceived by money; if he could be, he would not be God. When we offer explanations of God, they appear atheistic to many for some reason. Here, therefore, are some views expressed by the universally loved and honoured Bengali poet Tagore. Perhaps afterward the friends who insult us will recognize and correct their mistake:

“God is present everywhere without exception.”

“He is not useful in our worldly transactions.”

“He cannot be our partner in efforts to obtain wealth or pleasure.”

“We cannot use him in politics, accumulation of wealth, warfare, or other undertakings.”

“He wholly transcends all these.”

“There is no need to seek in one place the God who pervades everywhere.”

“That attempt itself is wrong.”

“It is wrong mentally to imagine that the God who shines beautifully everywhere resides in one particular place.”

“He is not one who can be caught by us.”

“Attaining God should not be our aim.”

We have been expressing these same views, yet we are placed upon the list of atheists. What can one call this except one of the world’s wonders?

Devotees explain that those who said God does not inhabit stone or bronze, temple or tank, and that one should not call a planted stone divine and offer four flowers to it, were Siddhars and ascetics detached from this world, whose teaching cannot aid worldly people. But can they call the poet Tagore a Siddhar or ascetic who transcended this world and wandered another? Tagore said what Siddhars and ascetics said, and what we say. Siddhars and ascetics are worshipped, Tagore is praised, and we are abused!

We do not grieve for that. Our wish is only that people of deficient understanding who, when we say “Do not go to a temple imagining God resides there,” accuse us of atheist propaganda and demand our imprisonment should at least now compare Tagore’s views with ours and acquire the mature understanding required to recognize truth.

3-4-49
