காம:- (வெறுப்புடன்) தேடிப் பகைவளர்த்துக் கொள்கிறீர்.
தி.மு.க.வைக் கண்டபடி ஏசினா, எதிர்ப்புக் கிளம்பாமலா
இருக்கும்! உனக்கு இருக்கிற அபாரமான செல்வாக்காலே,
அவர்களை ஓட ஓடவே அடிச்சி விடறதாகவே வைத்துக்கொள்ளு,
பிறகு அந்த எதிர்ப்பு உம்மோட முடியுமா - என்னையுந்தானே
வந்து சேரும். புதுசா பலம் சேரும் உமக்கு மந்திரிவேலை
கொடுத்தா, நீர், சும்மா இருக்கிற தி.மு.க.வைக் குத்துவிட்டு,
வேடிக்கை பார்க்கிறீர்.

ராம:- (சலிப்புடன்) என்னைக் கண்டித்தா நான் எப்படிச்
சும்மா இருக்க முடியும்?

காம:- (கோபமாக) என்னய்யா கண்டித்துவிட்டாங்க.
மாணிக்கவேல் நாயக்கரு, மந்திரியான போது, நீர் அவரை என்ன
கண்டித்தீரோ, அதையே இப்ப உமக்குத் திருப்பறாங்க -
ரோஷமும் கோபமும் பொங்குதே! மாணிக்கவேலர்
விஷயத்திலே மட்டும், குலம் மறைந்து போவுதா? அவரும்
வன்னியர்தானே...

ராம:- (சோகமாக) பழைய விஷயத்தைக் கிளறினா, பேச்சு
ஒரு முடிவுக்கு வராது.

காம:- (சலிப்புடன்) பழைய விஷயத்தை நீர்
விடவில்லையே. இப்பவும் திண்டிவனம் மகாநாட்டிலே கூட
வன்னியர்களுக்காகத்தான் நான் மந்திரி சபையிலே சேர்ந்தேன்
என்று பேசினீர். காங்கிரஸ் புனிதமான ஸ்தாபனம், நான் அதிலே
சேர்ந்துவிட்டேன், வீணாக ஜாதி, குலம், இதை எல்லாம் பேசிக்
கொண்டு தேசத்தை நாசமாக்கக் கூடாது, எல்லோரும்
காங்கிரசிலே சேரவேணும், என்று பேச உமக்கு மனம்
வரவில்லையே. பழைய பேச்சுத்தானே நடந்தது.

எனக்குக் கவலையா இருக்கும். மாணிக்கவேல் நாயக்கர் போல்
பக்குவமாக நடந்துகொள்ளத் தெரியவில்லையே, உமக்கு,
அவருக்கும்தானே, வன்னியர் சங்கம் இருக்கு.

ராம:- (சிறிது கோபமாக) அடிக்கடி, மாணிக்கவேலர்
பேச்சை எடுத்துக்காட்டறது என்னைக் குத்திக் கிண்டல் செய்கிற
மாதிரியா இருக்கு. எனக்கு ஒன்றும் இந்தப் பதவி பெரிதல்ல.

காம:- (கோபமடைந்து) ஆமாம் ஆமாம்!... மகாநாட்டிலே
கூடத்தான் பேசி இருக்கிறீரே. காமராஜர், தேடித்தேடிப் பதவி
கொடுத்தாரு, எனக்கு ஒண்ணும் பதவி பெரிதல்ல! கிடைக்கிற
சம்பளம் என் செலவுக்குப் போதுமா, மாதம் ஆயிரம் இரண்டா-
யிரம் என் கைப்பணம் செலவாகுது, என்றெல்லாம். இது,
எங்களை எல்லாம் சேர்த்து அவமானப்படுத்துகிற பேச்சு
அல்லவா...

ராம:- (திடுக்கிட்டு) ஐயயோ! உங்களை அவமானம்
செய்தேனா... நானா...

காம:- (கோபம் குறையாமல்) பதவி எனக்குப் பெரிதல்ல,
பணம் முக்கியமல்லன்னு நீர் பேசினா எங்களுக்கெல்லாம்
பதவிதான் முக்யம், பணம்தான் பிரதானம், என்றுதானே,
மறைமுகமாகப் பொருள்படும்.

ராம:- (சமாதானம் கூறும் பாவனையில்) தப்பா அர்த்தம்

மகாநாட்டிலே, நான் தேடித் தேடிவந்து உமக்குப் பதவி

உங்களையே இடித்துப் பேசறதுக்கு இடம் கொடுத்தீங்க,

காங்கிரசுக்கு சரியான மெஜாரிடியும் இருக்கு. நீரும், உம்முடைய

மந்திரியானேன் என்று மார்தட்டிப் பேசறதும், அதுவும் நானாகப்
போகவில்லை, காமராஜரே கூப்பிட்டுக் கொடுத்தார் என்று

## English Translation

The Honourable Kamaraj and the Honourable Ramasami meet!

An imaginary scene!

What do they talk about? This and that! It is pure imagination!

But if one examines the situation, it will become clear that this is how they could speak!

Please do not suppose that this conversation actually took place.

This imaginary conversation has been composed only because, if such a conversation were to take place, a little honesty and clarity might enter politics.

**Ramasami Padayachi:** (Eagerly) Did you hear? I gave those D.M.K. fellows a proper thrashing. They called me a firebrand. I was furious; I said I would chase them down and beat them.

**Kamaraj:** (Mockingly) I hear Vinayagam spoke even more fiercely!

**Ramasami:** (Dismissively) Yes! That man is always like that. He loses all sense of proportion... But... it was only after hearing me speak that everyone was astonished. After all, I occupy the position of being the "leader" of one crore Vanniyar people.

**Kamaraj:** (Teasingly) Our Manickavelar, too, still calls himself the leader of the Vanniyars.

**Ramasami:** (With a careless laugh) Only in North Arcot! He has no influence in the other districts. I have one man in Tiruchi district, Subramaniam, M.L.C., in Salem district, and Sambandam, M.L.A., in Thanjavur district. I have people like that.

**Kamaraj:** (His attention seemingly elsewhere) So I was told!

**Ramasami:** (Delightedly) Even the Dravidian legislative party supported me. You read it in the paper, did you not!

**Kamaraj:** (Frowning) I did! You still have closer ties with the enemies of the Congress. Ramasami! Do not be angry; at that conference, you did not include Congress leaders even as a token presence. Many comrades asked about it. Some even condemned it. You had said that if S. S. Ramasami were given a ministerial post, the entire Vanniyar Sangam would join the Congress. But at the Tindivanam conference, Ramasami Padayachi staged his grand display by gathering opponents of the Congress and gave no place to Congressmen. Some angrily asked why such a man should be made a minister.

**Ramasami:** (Worriedly) What can I do! I cannot tell them to merge into the Congress all at once. Our people would say, "See, see, what the D.M.K. said has come true."

**Kamaraj:** (With a touch of authority) But the arrangement was to bring all Vanniyars into the Congress and dissolve the Toilers Party, was it not?

**Ramasami:** (Worriedly) Yes. Am I saying it was not?

**Kamaraj:** (Eagerly) Did you see what happened in North Arcot! Manickavelar has announced that his party will not contest the District Board election. And mind you, he did not obtain his ministerial post after I came to power; Rajaji himself gave it to him.

**Ramasami:** (Proudly) His case is different. What influence does his party possess! It managed to capture four or five places. Besides, the Vanniyars will not listen to him.

**Kamaraj:** (Mockingly) Yes, yes! And do Gopal Gounder, Tirukkural Munusami, Kacharayam, and the Vanniyars in the D.M.K. all listen to your every word and act accordingly? Why this empty pride, man? You took so much trouble to conduct a conference, yet in that very town lives Gopal Gounder, who condemns your course, and you could not pass even one resolution against him...

**Ramasami:** (A little angrily) We did not pass one. Why do you say we could not pass one?

**Kamaraj:** (Angrily) What use is this lawyer's talk, man? I can see that my calculation was wrong.

**Ramasami:** (Angrily) You too listen to this D.M.K. talk and believe it. If you speak without knowing my influence, I am compelled to explain it myself. Then you will think I am boasting. Do you know that Periyar Ramasami did not issue even a single statement condemning me!

**Kamaraj:** (Laughing) Good heavens! What is so remarkable about that? Do you know that he has issued a statement supporting me?

**Ramasami:** (Boldly) I saw it. But the Dravidar Kazhagam, the D.M.K., the Communists, none of them can do anything against me. Did they show me a black flag? Did they manage it? Could they?

**Kamaraj:** (Angrily) Jawaharlal Nehru, the Light of Asia, had black flags shown even to him, did he not? What would you do if the D.M.K. showed you one? Is saying, "I will stab, I will cut," political speech? All the D.M.K. leaders were locked in jail. Those who remained outside showed Nehru black flags in Madras itself and in Madurai. Do you possess influence in Cuddalore and South Arcot district that Nehru does not possess in Madras? Yet you talk as though they could not show you a black flag!

**Ramasami:** (Sadly) If even you speak like this...

**Kamaraj:** (Sarcastically) I am not speaking; you are making me speak. They showed Manickavelar a black flag at Walajah. Does Manickavelar lack in Walajah the influence you have in Cuddalore? You talk as though...!

**Ramasami:** (Humbly) That is not what I mean... Generally, no one can work in South Arcot district while antagonising me...

**Kamaraj:** (Majestically) I myself have worked against you, and my head is still on my shoulders...!

**Ramasami:** (With a faint smile) It seems the very hour in which we began talking is inauspicious...

**Kamaraj:** (With disgust) What is wrong with your hour! Yours is fine; it is my hour that is bad. Considering the circumstances I am in, I work on the belief that there must be no opposition and that we need everyone's cooperation. Rajaji is waiting for a gap through which he can get inside, and even within the Congress a "faction" waits for a chance to trip me. So it is best to keep the D.M.K. people placated and carry on. I gave you a ministerial post with that very aim. What you are doing is ruining my entire purpose...

**Ramasami:** (Sadly) What harm have I done?

**Kamaraj:** (With disgust) You go looking for enemies. If you abuse the D.M.K. indiscriminately, how can opposition fail to arise! Let us suppose that, with your tremendous influence, you chase them down and beat them. Will that opposition end with you? Will it not come to me as well? I gave you a ministerial post so that new strength would join us, but you provoke the D.M.K., which had been keeping quiet, and stand watching the spectacle.

**Ramasami:** (Wearily) If they condemn me, how can I remain quiet?

**Kamaraj:** (Angrily) How have they condemned you, man? Whatever you said in condemnation of Manickavel Naicker when he became a minister, they are now returning to you. Yet you overflow with wounded pride and anger! Does caste alone disappear when it comes to Manickavelar? Is he not also a Vanniyar?

**Ramasami:** (Sadly) If we rake up old matters, this conversation will never end.

**Kamaraj:** (Wearily) But you have not abandoned the old matter. Even now, at the Tindivanam conference, you said that you joined the ministry only for the sake of the Vanniyars. It did not occur to you to say: "The Congress is a sacred organisation. I have joined it. We must not ruin the country by talking needlessly of caste and clan. Everyone must join the Congress." It was the same old talk, was it not?

**Ramasami:** (In a pleading tone) At a Vanniyar conference, that is how one has to speak. In other places, I have spoken only in support of the Congress.

**Kamaraj:** (Angrily) I do not need your favour! It is among your own caste people that you must speak and seek support for the Congress.

**Ramasami:** (Humbly) As time goes on...

**Kamaraj:** (With disgust) As time goes on, I worry only about what further troubles you will create for me. You do not know how to conduct yourself with the maturity of Manickavel Naicker. He, too, has a Vanniyar Sangam.

**Ramasami:** (A little angrily) Your repeatedly holding up Manickavelar feels like a taunt aimed at me. This post is not such a great thing to me.

**Kamaraj:** (Growing angry) Yes, yes!... You said the same thing at the conference: "Kamaraj searched me out and gave me the post. The post is nothing great to me! Is the salary I receive enough for my expenses? I spend one or two thousand rupees of my own money every month." Is that not speech that humiliates all of us together?

**Ramasami:** (Startled) Good heavens! Did I humiliate you... I?

**Kamaraj:** (Still angry) If you say the post is not important to you and money does not matter, does it not indirectly imply that, to all of us, the post alone is important and money alone is paramount?

**Ramasami:** (In a conciliatory manner) Is it right to give it the wrong meaning and then speak...

**Kamaraj:** (Still angry) You declared publicly at the conference that I had sought you out and given you the post. When you meet your caste people privately, you may have to embroider the story still further and say that I fell at your feet and begged you to save me.

**Ramasami:** (Placatingly) Would I say anything like that! I spoke that way to silence those who condemn me for changing parties for a ministerial post...

**Kamaraj:** (With disgust) If so, will my party men not question me? Will they not condemn me by saying: "We already have a proper majority. Why did you make Ramasami a minister and now give him room to speak slightingly even of you?" I gain prestige only if I can point out that I gave you a ministerial post, dissolved the caste association, and brought everyone into the Congress. Now your speech is costing me my honour.

**Ramasami:** (Clearly) I did not speak a single word of condemnation...

**Kamaraj:** (With disgust) It is a Congress ministry that is in office, and the Congress has a proper majority. You and your colleagues have taken your seats on the Congress side in the legislature. In such a situation, is it proper for you to preside over a caste conference, beat your chest and say that you became a minister for the welfare of your caste, and boast that you did not go of your own accord but that Kamaraj himself called you and gave you the post? If Rajaji's faction reports this to Nehruji, what will he think of me? Will he respect me?

**Ramasami:** (Sadly) I did not think that far. I was angry with the D.M.K. fellows...

**Kamaraj:** (Mockingly) Why would you think! You want the ministerial post, and you want the caste association too!

**Ramasami:** (Frowning) You took me into the ministry only after considering the caste association and its influence.

**Kamaraj:** (Dripping sarcasm) Yes... Only now is its true quality becoming evident. It is also becoming evident that you are spoiling a peaceful political situation by abusing the D.M.K....

**Ramasami:** (With a faint smile) What can the D.M.K. possibly do? Why do you regard them as so important and talk this way?

**Kamaraj:** (Still more angrily) Enough, keep quiet, man... Are you now asking what the D.M.K. can do, after all those days when you were on intimate terms with them?

**Ramasami:** (Dismissively) They came and cultivated me...

**Kamaraj:** (With disgust) Yes, yes! Tomorrow you will say the same of the Congress: that the Congress itself surrendered to you. Why tomorrow? You are already saying it. Did you not say the same at the conference? The Dravidar Kazhagam conference passed a resolution that our ministry should be supported. You are a minister, yet at your conference you had no wish even to pass a resolution supporting the ministry.

**Ramasami:** (Placatingly) It slipped my mind.

**Kamaraj:** (With disgust) Such a trivial matter to you, is it not! The conference was held only to attack the D.M.K. Was there even the slightest exposition of policy in it? While sitting in a Congress ministry, your mind permits you to preach caste doctrine. I, too, have a caste, man. Rack your brains: have I ever once said that I became a minister for the advancement of the Nadar community?

**Ramasami:** (Angrily) Who told you not to?

**Kamaraj:** (Still angry) That is my principle! I have never imagined it a great distinction to speak of my clan, my caste, and my influence within it. Look at the constituency from which I am now standing for election: Gudiyatham, where Senguntha Mudaliyars are the majority. It is not a Nadar constituency. Yet that is where I stand, and I am going to win. That, man, is what influence truly means.

**Ramasami:** (Disgustedly) It is not right to prolong this conversation. I...

**Kamaraj:** (Mockingly) Are you saying you will resign your ministerial post?

**Ramasami:** (Taken aback) I will come back and speak later.

**Kamaraj:** (Mockingly) I was terrified that you might actually resign! Let our conversation lie. Do not abuse the D.M.K. needlessly and bring trouble to my door, man. You will earn a crore blessings from me.

(The Honourable Ramasami takes his leave with a slight smile.)

4 July 1954

## Translator's Notes

The article is a fictional political dialogue, not a record of an actual meeting. The source itself explicitly says so.

“கனம்” is an older honorific used here before the names of office-holders; it is rendered as “the Honourable.” “மகாநாடு” is rendered contextually as “conference.” “கொலு நடத்தினார்” is figurative and is rendered as “staged his grand display.” Party abbreviations are expanded only in English where their identity is clear: தி.மு.க. as D.M.K. and தி.க. as Dravidar Kazhagam.

The corrected Tamil transcription was checked against all nine scans. Scan-confirmed repairs include the title, fragmented opening lines, speaker labels, party names, personal names, page joins, the badly corrupted closing exchange, and the printed date. The source's period colloquialisms and political invective are retained.
