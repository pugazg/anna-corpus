## English Translation

For some reason, the quarrel within the Madras Congress is growing stranger by the day. What began as an auntie's whispered gossip in some inner corner has grown and spread until the neighbours can hear it and the whole town has begun to laugh. So far it has remained a war of speech and writing. Who knows whether it will end in blows and some further disgrace! No, surely they would never do that: are they not all champions of non-violence? At most, the two factions will demonstrate which can abuse its opponent more impudently and contemptuously.

I know that among those ornaments of nationalism are people steeped in classical Tamil, people accustomed, one might say, even to sleeping in classical Tamil. They have studied Vedanta too!

They begin, "True devotees, we have taken this human birth, the finest of all births," and in one stroke descend into the language of the gutter: "Yes, I am a great rogue! Don't you know? Your head will split open. The yellow rice in your chest will spill out!" Such gems and jewels pour forth this rain of oratory and simultaneously gladden the hearts of both Mother India and Mother Tamil. I have heard with my own ears how they gladdened them. Let that pass. In the land of the Taj Mahal there are crumbling little walls too. In a garden with a five-coloured parrot, there are stinging wasps and carrion-eating vultures as well. Our country is vast, very vast! It contains all sorts of characters, and now it contains this commotion too.

Conflict between one party and another is natural in politics; indeed, it is the food on which politics grows. But factions, sub-factions and captious factions within a party are something I dislike wherever they occur. It is not merely I: who wishing to conduct politics in a civilised manner would welcome them? This was one of the chief reasons for the decline of the Justice Party.

Following the proverb, "Fool, do not ruin yourself," Congressmen concealed the corruption smouldering within for a time. How long could they do so? It has now burst into the open. Each has taken up a measuring rod and begun measuring the others: Who is the greater man? Who is the true nationalist? Who is the honourable Congressman? This is the sacred farce now being enacted.

You know that story. A courtesan walking down a street saw three fools and casually saluted them before going on her way. The three immediately began a battle. "That salute was for me," said one. "It was for me," said another. The third fellow, being very clever, began to run; the other two chased him. The three worthies reached the woman and asked, "For whom was your salute?" Thinking, "What an unadulterated selection of first-rate fools!" she replied, "It was for whichever of you three is the number-one fool." This quarrel has come to resemble that story.

They paid prize money and acquired the name *Dinamani*. I do not remember the sum. Seeing how the name obtained in that fashion is now being dragged about, I wondered at its plight. In a newspaper that appeared on auspicious yellow paper, adorned by Mother India and a song of Bharati, itself a nationalist paper, *Dinamani* and its editor have received such an "offering of worship" that, I tell you honestly, although I belong to another party, I briefly forgot that fact and wondered whether I ought to arrange a mediation to reunite these two groups. Matters have become that bad. If both sides fling whatever comes to hand, stones, sticks or anything else, I suffer no loss; but it is an ugly sight.

"A newspaper of wicked character, whose editorials and articles are written in so low and degraded a manner as to disgust educated people and true Congressmen; a foolish newspaper." That is the description of *Dinamani*.

Of its editor it says: "A person so utterly unworthy is in no way fit to edit a nationalist daily."

He is described as one who "attends meetings convened by insignificant men, mixes and talks with a few petty persons, and then grandly publicises those very things in his newspaper."

And further: "He stood as a candidate for a place on the Working Committee and received only fifty-five votes. The woman who opposed him received 110."

Who, you may wonder, is portrayed with all these glories? None other than the editor of *Dinamani*. Whose affairs is the new newspaper airing in public? Poor thing! I suppose it has no other work. But *Dinamani* deserves this, and more besides. Does it not howl incessantly, without any restraint upon its tongue? Whatever anyone gives it can hardly be enough. We are also told that the *Dinamani* faction has a leader. His own "glories" are now being poured into the street. That too is a form of public service, is it not?

The condition of Congress resembles schoolchildren dividing themselves into teams for a game. The printed passage beginning "Dinamani, Bhaktavatsalam, Bashyam, Muthurangam" is damaged, but its point remains clear: these people bring forward no plan even to eradicate illiteracy.

Meanwhile, the Congress ministry proposes to abolish colleges, close higher-grade schools, eliminate schools with fewer than sixty pupils, reject compulsory free education, and reduce educational grants. In what way, then, has the present Congress government helped us advance?

## Hindi

In such a state, what is the purpose of the Hindi scheme? We possess pure Tamil, good and beautiful Tamil, our Tamil by birth, the language we speak every day and find convenient. When only eight persons in a hundred are educated even through it, what will happen if Hindi too is imposed? Our people still do not properly distinguish between the Tamil letters *la*, *zha* and *lla*. Our children come to school only to be told, "You cannot learn. Go home: if you are a cart-driver's son, drive a cart; if you are a ploughman's son, plough," and are driven away. If Hindi is now added and made compulsory, how can our children learn it?

People say that to ruin a poor man, one should give him an elephant. Likewise, even if elephant-like Hindi is considered lofty, can it come within reach of our people, who are very poor in education? Can they get their mouths around it? Even one letter is difficult, and there are many letters. Can our children study two foreign languages between the ages of twelve and fourteen? Can they obtain sufficient marks in them? By the time a person learns Hindi and passes it well, he could instead become a B.A. graduate.

## English

When introducing this Hindi scheme, they offer the reason that English is an education of slavery and that they are therefore abolishing it and reviving Hindi. Our Congressmen, from Comrade Gandhi to Comrade Murthi, now cry, "English is slave education! Slave education!" But can we view it in that way? The coachman speaks English; English is current even in the red-light street. So long as we did not think of competing with Brahmins for government employment, English remained a fine language. The very day we began to compete, they began calling it a slave language and a degraded tongue.

Without knowing English, how would Comrade Gandhi have acquired the title Mahatma? Did that honour come from reading the *Ramayana* of Tulsidas, which he is always talking about? (Laughter.) How would Comrade Rajagopalachari have acquired so much prestige and such capacity for intrigue? Did all this come from not knowing English? Consider whether this is merely a cunning campaign intended to lay a hand upon the heads of others.

Until Muslims and Tamils began asking for their share, no calamity was discovered in English. How greatly Congress leaders have praised it! Leaving the dead leaders aside, Comrade Malaviya has thanked the British for English education. Prominent Congress leaders such as Dadabhai and Surendranath Banerjee have said many times, "Our progress in every field is due to the education given by these benevolent white men."

Within Tamil Nadu, it is in your own Tanjore district that Brahmins have received higher education in numbers unequalled by any other district and have pressed most heavily upon your heads. The reason is the two colleges in this district. When Sir C. P. Ramaswami Iyer, Mani Iyer, Muthuswami Iyer, Krishnaswami Iyer, Srinivasa Sastri, Venkatarama Sastri and the other men of your district studied English, it was a national language; when we study the same thing, it is called slave education and a degraded language.

Because people other than Brahmins were prevented from studying; because in places such as Mysore and Travancore Brahmins were given both money and education; because, if by chance one of our people went to study, Brahmins humiliated him with words such as, "How could education ever come to you? Go and carry sacks somewhere"; and because our wealthy magnates, without concern for our community, donated their fortunes to Brahmins alone, the Brahmin community has been able to achieve universal literacy. A priest's son has been able to enter the I.C.S. Otherwise, how could their community have fifty-eight out of every hundred in the I.C.S. class? Are Brahmins all so wealthy? Are non-Brahmins all such destitute mendicants?

When the Justice Party held office, it created facilities enabling every community to study. Comrade Nadimuthu opposes Hindi; Ramalingam Chettiar objects to it; Comrade V. Saminatha Iyer objects too. Ignoring all of them, why does Rajagopalachari display such obstinacy over Hindi? Belief in the Puranas, the Vedas and God has declined in the present age. Rajagopalachari appears to believe that Hindi will help revive those beliefs, and that is why he acts in this manner. Otherwise, which country has been ruined because Hindi was not its common language? People are being imported from North India for this Hindi propaganda. What does Comrade Gandhi know of Tamil Nadu? What has he done here? What connection is there between him and us?

*Viduthalai*, 5 February 1938
