## English Translation

### Image 1

33. The Play “The Gallows” in Tiruchi

Our country greatly needs plays of this kind, containing progressive ideas for the nation and society. Since this play is filled with the principles of the Dravidar Kazhagam, it may be regarded as a propaganda play of the Kazhagam. To some extent, that must indeed be conceded. But it is my desire that plays should be fashioned not merely to explain the principles of one party, but to remove the defects and cruelties found in society generally, and that comrades of every party and of no party should stage them. It is clearly evident that plays containing such ideas of social reform enjoy great support in the country and that people expect such plays. Yet some companies still stage only the old Puranic dramas; in doing so, they invite harm both to themselves and to the country.

In today's newspaper I saw an advertisement. I read that a troupe called the “Netaji Dramatic Society” was going to stage *Krishna Leela*.

### Image 2

I believe many Congress sympathizers have come to this gathering. I wish to bring Netaji's heroic life briefly to their memory. Should Congress sympathizers not feel ashamed when a society bearing the name of that great martyr and hero, who dedicated his entire life to the country's liberation, stages a play called *Krishna Leela*? Should they not become angry upon considering this condition? Should they not stand before that company, conduct a non-violent picket, and advise its members: “Either change the company's name or stage plays that elevate society”?

The Netaji Dramatic Society need not stage *The Gallows*. Could it not stage the story of the Rani of Jhansi? Could it not depict the Dandi March? It could stage a play called *The Blood Shed by Bhagat Singh*. It could stage *The Murder of Kumaran*. It could dramatize the Salt Satyagraha. If all these subjects had been exhausted and no other story remained, it could at least devise one about Sardar Vedarathinam. Instead, should a dramatic society named after that great hero stage *Krishna Leela*?

There is, however, a reason why they do so. They know that the name Netaji is a marketable label. They also know that performing *Krishna Leela* will bring in money from ordinary people. This is merely an attempt to combine the two and make a profit; it demonstrates neither devotion to Congress nor the growth of art.

### Image 3

Until now, we have never shown “real men” upon our stage. We have shown only imaginary forms invisible to the eye and beyond the reach of reason. Indra has appeared. Kubera has appeared. We have seen Hanuman. We have seen Alli and Pavalakkodi. We have seen Mother Sita Devi in the Ashoka grove. In most plays we have met Narada. But have we shown the country's great heroes, those who invaded countries, those who stood against them, and the great figures of the world?

Have you seen the great Alexander or Purushottaman? At one point in this play, our friend Karunanidhi referred to “Socrates, who died after drinking poison.” Have you seen that figure onstage? Two thousand years ago, that old man stood in the marketplaces of Greece and taught the truth. Listening to the conspirators of the town, they imprisoned him and gave him poison. The whole world knows this. Have we introduced him to the country through our stage? Why speak only of the stage? Even in our history books it is rare to see pictures of these people.

Instead, in our history book we see Rama standing with his matted crown, Sita beside him, his brother Lakshmana four feet away, and a deer concealed behind a bush, with hesitation on Rama's face and longing on Sita's. How long must such a condition continue?

### Image 4

Why should this reform of drama be regarded as the work of one particular party? I tell my Congress friends: take up this task and carry it forward. There are countless scenes that ought to be shown upon our stage. I am not asking you to depict an unveiling ceremony for Periyar's portrait, as this play does. I ask my Congress friends why they should not stage plays that explain a widow's tears; why conflict arises between elder and younger brothers; what causes estrangement between father and son; why a good man becomes bad; and how compassion exists even in a murderer's heart.

Just as today's government has established a “Censor Board” for cinema, it has dared to control dramatic art and thereby obstruct the growth of knowledge. You may have seen in the newspaper two days ago the report that it intends to establish a “Drama Scrutiny Committee.” Restrictions have already been imposed. It has banned *Sivappi Who Guarded the Red Flag*, a play by Communist Party comrades. It has stopped a similar Telugu play. It has prohibited plays of our movement, including *Hiranyan, or the Peerless Hero* and *The Sword*.

Congress friends know of the “Song of the Crane” that Viswanatha Das used to sing onstage, how British imperialism banned it, how the actor defied that ban, and the consequences that followed.

### Image 5

Should Congress friends not feel ashamed to see a democratic government behaving in the same imperial manner? Should people with artistic hearts not consider where it will lead if the government ruling the country is allowed to keep imposing such bans? Are these really the plays that ought to be prohibited?

The story *Sabapathi* has appeared both as a play and as a film. How disgracefully the Tamil teacher is portrayed in it! Does showing the Tamil teacher falling asleep as soon as he enters the classroom, while children tear up paper and throw it about, contribute to the growth of Tamil? Such stories escape the government's eyes. But it sees *Hiranyan*!

What reason did the government give for banning *Hiranyan*? This play, written fifteen years ago by the revolutionary poet Bharathidasan, has been staged many times in many places. Ministers of that period, such as P. T. Rajan, presided over and enabled its performances. Why ban such a play now? They may say that in some places the play disparages and condemns Aryans. Even supposing that it does, why should this government become angry about it? Or why should anyone else become angry?

Whenever we discuss the Aryan-Dravidian question, they say, “What Aryan and what Dravidian? Everything has become mixed.”

### Image 6

If there is honesty or truth in the statements of Brahmin intellectuals who say there is no longer any separate Aryan race, why should Brahmins become angry with the play? If it is true that no Aryan race now exists, no one has reason to become angry when Aryans are criticized!

If a play were written disparaging or condemning the Japanese, a Japanese person watching it might become angry, or his agent might become angry. Would anyone else? Likewise, if a play condemning Aryans is staged, Aryans or their agents may become angry. They banned the play.

News arrived today that the ban has been defied and the play staged at Tiruvathipuram near Kanchipuram.

Friends! Stage reformist plays that advance the country. Do so not only in sheds such as this, but also in open spaces where people may watch free of charge. They need not all be propaganda plays, one hundred percent, like this one. Friends such as Karunanidhi may stage plays of that kind. Members of other parties and lovers of art need not stage even such plays; they may present “light comedy.”

Five or six years ago, I wrote *Chandrodayam* expressly as a propaganda play. I have now written the lighter-coloured *Or Iravu* and *Velaikkari*. Such plays, when staged by everyone,

### Image 7

will make it possible for people to see them and for their principles to spread widely. I request our friend Karunanidhi to write plays of this kind and provide them to theatrical companies.

## Translator's Notes

`தூக்குமேடை` is translated literally as *The Gallows*, the established title of M. Karunanidhi's reformist play discussed in the speech.

The source uses `பிரசார நாடகம்` positively for a play written to propagate political or social principles; “propaganda play” retains that period meaning.

Named plays, political organizations, and historical figures are retained rather than silently normalized. `லைட் கலர்`, glossed in print as “light comedy,” describes less overtly propagandistic social drama.
