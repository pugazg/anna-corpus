## English Translation

“Heroes stand with swords in their hands and protect country, home and honour.”

“Deceivers stand with *darbha* grass in their hands, bewildering, intimidating and subduing those without firmness of mind.”

“And you, Bharatha, stand with a pen in your hand. What use is it?” Veeran asked in exasperation.

I asked him why he had suddenly become interested in investigating the condition of people's hands.

“The work performed by a hand reveals the thought behind it,” Veeran said.

“True. The hand's condition accords with the nature of the thought. But what is special today? Why have you embarked upon this investigation?” I asked.

“Those hands grasp and guide aircraft controls, drive tanks, make cannon speak, hold rifles and thrust them at the enemy's neck. They bear the flag of freedom, wield the sword of inquiry, carry the flame of knowledge, restrain rioters and govern nations. Those are hands; the rest are mere trees!” Veeran declared enthusiastically.

I asked whether praising foreigners' hands was his only occupation and whether our own country's hands were so insignificant.

“Hands folded in supplication; hands striking the head; hands rubbing an empty stomach,” Veeran replied in disgust.

When I helplessly asked what could be done, he was not satisfied. “Bharatha, it is not enough to blame the foreigner alone because ours are not hands that steer ships, raise flags before forts or hold books of poetic speech. Did he pass a law compelling our people to remain with clay in their hands? Did the white man order them to use their hands on useless labour or command them to toil for idlers?”

I did not understand why he said we stood with clay in our hands. He explained that on Monday I would see the handiwork of our incomparable heroes: it was Vinayaka Chaturthi, when every household would handle wet clay, shape the elephant-faced, pot-bellied son of Maheswara, offer sesame balls, *appam*, *kozhukattai*, beaten rice and puffed rice, knock their knuckles upon their heads, perform squats and worship him.

Only then did I laugh and agree that they would indeed have clay in their hands. “Not only in their hands,” my angry friend said. “The same clay is in their skulls.” I warned him not to abuse them or blaspheme; it was merely an old custom. But he would not be restrained.

“Observe Vinayaka's form: a human body, elephant face, drum-like belly and single tusk. Tell civilized people that this is our god's shape and they will laugh until their bellies shake. His vehicle is a bandicoot. Hearing this, who would dare pronounce these people fit for self-government? If representatives of the world's peoples assembled and several figures arrived with topknots, garlands of skulls and buffalo faces, would the other civilized forms not laugh? If a monkey-faced woman entered while beauties danced gracefully, would they not clap and laugh? Be honest. See the compassion in the eyes of an image of Jesus and the radiance and peace of the Buddha's face; then place beside them the image of the rider of a bandicoot. In a pageant of gods, Ganapati would be treated only as the clown.”

Veeran was not wrong to say that our people would have clay in their hands. Many among us spend Vinayaka Chaturthi in precisely this futile activity. It is also true that foreigners would ridicule us if they heard of it. And what of the strange stories of Vinayaka? Friends handling clay need only examine them to recognize the truth in Veeran's words.

People still believe that one god has an elephant face, another carries a goddess upon his tongue, another rides a dancing peacock, another sleeps upon a banyan leaf on the rolling sea and another bears a woman upon his head; that these and similar gods live while arranging the stations allotted to different people. They remain attached to days, stars and practices suited to this belief. Yet leaders speak to these same people about the Constituent Assembly, Karl Marx's thought and revolution.

Can one obtain a sweet-fruit tree by sowing seed and pouring honey upon an uncultivated field? Can embracing an impotent person yield pleasure merely by dressing and adorning him and making him sing and dance? The abyss called Aryanism lies concealed beneath green leaves called the Puranas. Aryans know that common people, attracted by the greenery, will certainly fall into the pit. Waiting like cranes and fixing their aim deceitfully like little foxes, they have made prisoners of guileless people. The captives' flood of tears irrigates their selfish grove, and their sweat and blood flow into the Aryan farm. Even those who know this are afraid to explain it; what need be said of those who do not know?

Had leaders possessed even a particle of concern for knowledge, firmness in integrity, regard for justice and faith in humanity, would they not, long before burning foreign cloth, have explained to the people and burned the obscenities measured out in God's name? Before picketing liquor shops, would they not have picketed institutions that invoke God to seize money?

Even in this age when the lamp of reason shines, they neither ask whether such things ought to continue nor take the nation's pulse. In dark India, holy men hold courts without light; Aurobindos establish ashrams amid cool sea breezes; maharishis build hilltop fortresses and rule like kings amid great wealth; Sankaracharyas accept refreshments from simpletons in several districts and boast of being gurus to the world; and monastic chiefs enjoy every pleasure and possession amid the delights of Saivism. How many stones and words are hurled at those who dare to question this? How many pits have ritualists dug for those who escape those attacks?

God is described as a flame showing a path to the pathless and light to one who cannot see it; a preceptor teaching purity in an impure world and civilization in an uncivilized one, producing compassion, honesty and love in hearts filled with deceit, treachery and feverish passions. God is said to pervade everywhere, possess every power, embody limitless bliss, be truth itself, manifest directly, protect every soul and stand as its lord.

Many speak tearfully of God in this manner. They teach that guidance, light, justice, truth, purity and love themselves are God; that knowledge is divine, the all-pervading reality, love is Siva and truth is the Lord.

Yet alongside this philosophy stand other stories of the gods: the schemes of arrogant heroes who caper in a ritual dance. People accept both as true. They hear a philosophical explanation for “O deity who dances standing with the left leg raised,” then watch a particular pontiff, on a particular day and in a particular monastery, gaze without blinking at that lifted leg, his large belly shaking with laughter, before giving specified gifts. Seeing the incompatibility between philosophy and conduct, they do not writhe like worms in flame but remain frogs in a well. So do many of the people and their leaders.

I am convinced the day will soon come when, if a ritualist points to Vinayaka's image and says, “This is what your god looks like,” the rationalist's hand will play upon his cheek. Those without another course may listen when today's Puranas are recited as the history and character of their god. Those without minds of their own may tolerate it. But will people capable of thought accept it? The day is not far when they will rule: “I want a pure God, not an evil one.” A little clarity and sensitivity, a little more vigour in the pulse, concern in the mind and care for knowledge must arise.

Who is the Vinayaka whom you bow to with fearful devotion? He is the son of three-eyed Siva, whom Saivites proudly call foremost among the Trinity. The word God is said to mean one who has transcended everything, yet here God has a family, children and even a mistress, lacking none of those attachments.

One story says Siva and Uma once walked through a forest by the seashore. Like lovers today, they had the ocean before them and an ocean of joy within; waves surged in the sea and thoughts in their minds. The transcendent god saw two elephants mating, and desire rose within him. Saivites proudly say their Mahadeva burned Kama with his eye, yet also recount this Purana with delight and smear themselves with sacred ash afterward.

The sight of mating wild elephants caused sexual feeling to roar within the god who had burned Kama. He wished that he and Uma should become elephants and enjoy that pleasure; Uma agreed. They took elephant form and their divine sport occurred in the forest. The result of that enjoyment was elephant-faced Ganapati. This is one account of Pillaiyar's birth.

Consider its obscenity. Are there greater revilers of God than devotees who call such obscenity a divine act? They describe him as compassion embodied, beyond bonds, free from the three impurities and untouched by the play of the five senses, then say the forehead-eyed god lusted after seeing wild elephants mate and begot Ganapati. Have you no right to ask whether this is foolishness or madness?

Hear another history. When Parvati went to bathe, she supposedly rolled together dirt from her body, made a boy and posted him at her doorway. Ordinary women carry a child ten months; Parvati rolls bodily grime into one. She is a goddess, after all, and the fabricators of Puranas perhaps thought that failure to do even this would disgrace the wife of the lord of the Himalayas.

While this child of grime guarded the entrance, Siva tried to enter. The son stopped his father, who angrily cut off the boy's head with a sword and went inside. The son's neck must have been like a palmyra fruit: one stroke and the head rolled away.

Behold the deed of the embodiment of compassion. The boy merely said that his mother was bathing and asked him to wait, but Siva erupted in anger and beheaded him. What justice and civilization! Learning what had happened, Parvati wailed for her child. Siva consoled her, saying he would restore him, but outside they found only the body and could not locate the head. For some unexplained reason a god able to revive the dead could not find the missing head. An elephant happened to pass, so its head was cut off and attached to the body. Ganapati then arose with an elephant face.

I ask devotees who tremble at the word atheism whether this story reveals any divinity, divine principle, humanity, purity or other virtue.

Hear still another account. Our Puranic practice provides at least nine stories for one event, as though the fabricators competed at their trade. One says that while Vinayaka was in Parvati's womb, a demon entered in the form of air and severed the child's head. Siva blessed him with an elephant head instead, and the child was born elephant-faced. The *Vinayaka Purana* says that Vinayaka, sent by Siva to destroy Daksha's sacrifice, was beheaded; Siva saw his headless son, fitted an elephant's head and restored him. The noble being who could restore life apparently did not know how to restore the former human head. What folly!

A northern Purana offers a particularly strange Vinayaka story. Brahma once yawned, and a sacred child suddenly dropped from his open mouth. As Brahma held and admired it in his palm, the child leapt into water and became a demon.

What marvels: a baby from a yawn and, upon entering water, a demon. One must pity the gods that their lives are so perilous. Brahma granted the child, Sindura, a boon: anyone he embraced would die.

Narada passed by, and Sindura approached to test the boon upon him. Understanding the danger, Narada craftily suggested that he test it by embracing Brahma, who had granted it. Sindura went to Brahma, who grew furious and cursed him to be killed by Ganesha, then disappeared. Searching for Brahma, Sindura went to Vaikuntha. Vishnu called himself an ordinary being and directed Sindura toward Siva as a worthy opponent.

Sindura reached Kailasa, but Siva was absent. After a fierce dispute with Parvati, Siva returned at the right moment, fought Sindura and fainted. The distressed Parvati ordered Vishnu to be born from her womb and kill Sindura. Mahavishnu appeared elephant-faced in the mountain-daughter's womb and destroyed him. That elephant-faced being was Vinayaka: Parvati's son and an incarnation of Mahavishnu. One disastrous yawn from Brahma caused all this.

Babu Bhagavan Das, who explains Hindu philosophy, says Vinayaka was unknown to Aryans before the age of Yajnavalkya; that four Vinayakas later appeared as destructive and evil beings demanding blood-soaked rice and animal sacrifice; and that these four were subsequently transformed into one Mahaganapati.

Whichever Purana you believe, friends, consider whether it contains anything free of obscenity, compatible with reason, grounded in love or civilized. Should you not calmly ask whether it is right to fashion such a protagonist from clay, set him up and worship him with folded hands?

These and similar things are merely the mire of Aryan religion. What progress or life can Tamils have while they wallow in that sludge? Friends with clay in your hands, so long as you remain dupes who accept stories fabricated by rogues as tales of God, our country will be crowned the clown of the intellectual world. The day these are abandoned, the moment self-respect is born, will be the people's day of liberation.

Hearing Veeran speak, I began to yearn for the arrival of that day. I have therefore told you the same. Think and understand without anger in your hearts.

## Translator's Notes

The scan confirms `களிமண்ணும்` where OCR had `கணிமண்ணும்`; an OCR-only line produced from the closing ornament was also removed from both canonical Tamil copies before building the bilingual file.

The title literally means “With Clay and Hands,” but the idiomatic “With Clay in Their Hands” conveys the repeated image of Vinayaka worshippers moulding clay figures. `தர்ப்பை` is *darbha* (sacred grass), while `தோப்புக்கரணம்` denotes the ritual squat performed while holding the ears.

The article presents a polemical rationalist critique and retells multiple Puranic traditions in the author's own hostile language. The English preserves that argumentative stance without presenting the stories, chronology attributed to Bhagavan Das, or the article's claims about Aryans as independently verified history.
