## English Translation

If the Congress ministry today enacts laws establishing "Brahmin domination," they can be destroyed in half a second tomorrow when Tamils become courageous and conduct the government. But the indirect harms produced by this rule are far more dangerous than the direct harms it causes.

For ten years, we have analysed the Tamil mind and brought it to a particular condition. The Congress is destroying that work and drawing our people back towards the ancient age. Is that not the great harm? Is that not what we must eradicate completely?

When we say scientific development is necessary, Rajaji says that one may behold God's "vision of divine light" in electricity. We preached that people should learn and understand new ideas. They tell them to read and enjoy Tulsidas's *Ramayana*. We labour for rationalism. The Congress director says, "Do not disobey commands or analyse them. Close your mouth, fold your hands, stand, and work."

We say caste distinctions must be reduced to dust. They say it is enough to visit the *cheri* one day, give an oil bath, and bestow a new title upon the enslaved.

By pointing to ancient glories and preaching that a simple life is superior, they leave the people in poverty. In brief, we seek progress, while they call us back to the primitive age. They do not call us there ordinarily. They invoke the name of a Mahatma and call us in the name of a national movement.

Everything we explained as foolishness and threw into the rubbish heap has been newly polished and made valuable again. If we do not prevent this, Tamils will lose the benefit of our work.

Therefore, it is essential that we fight the Brahmin domination arising in the name of politics and fight superstition with greater strength than before, with discipline, method, courage, and the feeling of being true Tamils.

I believe no one can now doubt the necessity of our future struggle. I have also explained its cause. I do not think comrades who understand it need to be shown the point from which we must wage that struggle.

No one will deny that Brahmins who once suppressed us through Manu have now begun to suppress us through the Mahatma's Congress. Therefore, I believe there is no justification for us to join either that Congress or another group that hides in its shadow, survives in seclusion, and sings its praises.

Leaders may be startled by electoral defeat. Pretenders may become frightened. Political opportunists may leap from one nest to another. Those desiring garlands of fame may begin worshipping "new deities." Those who gain no advantage may seek to survive by approaching another "leader."

But those inseparably united with the movement will not seek another place. They will instead try to draw here the many people elsewhere who cry out because they cannot endure oppression.

The domination we wish to abolish is not ordinary. To protect its position, that community has seized all the vital arteries of society and uses them to make us dance.

Readers of German ruler Herr Hitler's description in his autobiography of Jewish domination in Germany will, the essay argues, understand the social harm caused by Brahmin domination in South India.

It says that the great factories belonged to Jews, that Jews controlled the universities and institutions of art, that scholars were in their hands, that the press belonged to them, that the ministry was in their grasp, and that science and socialism were theirs.

Wealth was with them and poverty with Germans. Domination was with them and enslavement with Germans. Happiness was with them and exhaustion with Germans.

Hitler wrote in his autobiography that he therefore hated Jews and that, if he obtained political authority, his first task would be to abolish Jewish domination. He then carried out what he had written.

History demonstrates that, in any country and at any time, when one community seizes society's vital arteries, expands its domination, enslaves other communities, and deepens divisions within society, the other communities unite and revolt to abolish that domination.

(*Kudi Arasu*, 29-8-1937)
