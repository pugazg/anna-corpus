பதில் அனுப்பிவிட்டார். மரியாதையான பாஷை; அர்த்தம்
“காஷ்மீருக்குள் காலடி எடுத்து வைக்காதே!” என்பதுதான்.
மன்னரின் பதில் மட்டுமல்ல, பல ஸ்தாபனங்களும்
தனிப்பட்டவர்களும், பண்டித ஜவஹரை இப்போது காஷ்மீர்
வரவேண்டாம் என்று தந்தி மூலம் கேட்டுக் கொண்டுள்ளனர்.

(திராவிட நாடு 23-6-46)

## English Translation

### Image 1

31. He Congratulated Him!

He has offered congratulations. He congratulated the Maharaja who made blood flow in the streets, imprisoned hundreds, used everything from baton charges to gunfire, and suppressed the people's liberation uprising. With inward delight he proclaimed: “Emperor of emperors! Protector of the realm! Courageous hero who crushed the revolt conducted by the treasonous Pakistani Muslims, long may you live!”

In Kashmir, on the snowy slopes of the Himalayas, a great disturbance arose. The people's uprising advanced so far that there was fear it might shake the king's palace. It was a liberation uprising, a struggle demanding responsible government. The system under which the people were sacrificed to the king's autocracy had to change; the king must govern for the people. This was one among the freedom movements arising in various princely states, a part of the general movement for liberation. The courage opposing autocratic imperialism reached Kashmir with the same speed. The king's rule was mighty. Leaders of the people's organization launched an agitation, demanding removal of the many defects in government and insisting that in an age filled with republican principles kings must regard their people's welfare as paramount.

### Image 2

The princely Government employed repression. It called the freedom uprising a conspiracy to overthrow the regime. A man honoured by the people as their leader was imprisoned as one who had arisen to undermine royal rule. The king's army and entourage energetically suppressed the people. Sheikh Abdullah, arrested by the ruler, now stands before the court.

We do not know whether the king strolls in his garden thinking the revolutionary storm has vanished, or sits in the women's quarters surrounded by maidens. But Hindu Mahasabha leader Savarkar overflowed with even greater joy than the king on hearing of the Kashmir ruler's valour.

The king may have worried that the outside world would hear of his severe methods and condemn him. Pandit Jawaharlal, president of the organization of the princely states' peoples, issued a fiery statement that may have caused the king some distress. Savarkar, however, felt untroubled delight at the Kashmir events and sent congratulations to the royal lord. Why? The king was Hindu and the people Muslim.

### Image 3

When people rebel against a king's autocracy, people naturally support the people and kings support the king. Savarkar is not a king, yet he condemns the popular uprising and congratulates the king who crushed it severely. Why? There is no other reason: the king is Hindu and the people Muslim.

Ninety-three out of every hundred people in Kashmir are Muslims. Sheikh Abdullah, one of Kashmir's foremost leaders and a friend of Pandit Jawaharlal, argues that the white East India Company sold Kashmir to Maharaja Gulab Singh for 400,000 sovereigns, and that this alone brought them under Hindu rule.

Hyderabad, with a large Hindu population, is under a Muslim ruler. Likewise Kashmir, with a large Muslim majority, is ruled by a Hindu king.

Mr Jinnah, who demands a separate Pakistan for Muslim-majority regions, has said nothing in opposition to Kashmir being governed by a Hindu ruler. He has not intervened in princely-state affairs.

### Image 4

Although many people, including Congress leaders, supported the movement against the king as a liberation uprising of a princely state's people, Savarkar treated it as a racial struggle by Muslims against a Hindu ruler. He praised the king who suppressed the people as the man who crushed a revolt of Pakistani Muslims, supported the repression, and sent the ruler his congratulations.

Honest people must consider whether this conduct is right. Sheikh Abdullah is Pandit Nehru's friend, a comrade of Congress, and not a member of the Muslim League. If the popular liberation uprising led by such an Abdullah appears to Savarkar as an Islamic revolt, indeed a Pakistani Islamic revolt, what would Savarkar's statement have been had Abdullah truly belonged to the Muslim League, or had the League participated in this liberation movement? Alas!

Even if the ruler is Hindu and the people Muslim, Savarkar might have had reason for anger had the agitation declared, “The king is Hindu, so we refuse to live under this rule.” But the people fought for their rights and asked for relief from poverty. Why must Savarkar turn this into a Hindu-Muslim question?

### Image 5

For the sake of a Hindu ruler, Savarkar abandoned concern for liberation and democratic principle, declared the suppression of the people right, supported the king, and congratulated him. When Jinnah hears that in a state where his community forms the majority, not only is rule held by a Hindu but the people's rights have been seized and they have been subjected to repression, why would he not speak for Muslims?

This is an example showing who begins what is called “communalism.” Jinnah knows the country has a Muslim majority, that a Hindu king rules it, and that the people face repression. Yet he refrains from intervention lest he be accused of inciting Muslims against Hindu rule. The people's struggle was not conducted as Hindu against Muslim but as ruled against ruler. Was it proper, just, or politically righteous for Savarkar to congratulate the king who suppressed the people and rejoice that an Islamic revolt had been crushed?

### Image 6

This very mentality has enlarged the Hindu-Muslim division to its present extent.

Poverty dances among Kashmir's people, while the king's entourage lives in fragrance and luxury. His retainers and jagirdars are Hindus. Not even four in a hundred among the Muslim community possess education. Under the Kashmir ruler's law, only Rajputs may bear arms. Until recently all government employment was held by Kashmiri Brahmins; only lately have others entered the official sphere. Most people are poor cultivators. The Brahmins are educated, and “Pandit” itself is their title. Jawaharlal belongs to this Kashmiri Pandit class.

Savarkar congratulated such a kingdom. His greeting reached the Kashmir ruler somewhat late, but the blessings of the “best of Brahmins” had arrived earlier. While a powerful struggle was under way, while Kashmir's people conducted their liberation uprising, while Sheikh Abdullah was arrested, while repression raged and the people trembled, some Kashmiri Brahmins shamelessly wrote upon the walls: “Long live the Maharaja!”

### Image 7

While they wrote “Long live the Maharaja,” the blood of Kashmir's people was flowing. At the moment they should have shown love to those being beaten, these “best of Brahmins” wrote that slogan on the fortress wall.

While Sheikh Abdullah was arrested and tried for sedition, Savarkar telegraphed: “Long live the Maharaja!” The Maharaja will live, no doubt. With so many crowned scholars and Savarkar blessing him, what could his life lack? Even if Brahmin blessings lack such power, the guns of British imperialism certainly possess it. Let the Maharaja live by seeking their aid. But did these great souls give even a little thought to the people's fate?

Without shame or concern for the people, they congratulate him and praise the bravery of unleashing repression upon an unarmed population. Is this conduct right? Is the action of these “best of Brahmins” just? Have you no courage to say no? How could you, when “the Brahmin is my deity”? Congratulate those great souls who congratulated the king for suppressing the people's uprising!

### Image 8

The priestly group did not stop with congratulating Kashmir's king. They came forward to propagate the view that the Maharaja was right to suppress Sheikh Abdullah's movement. Siva Narayan, secretary of the Kashmiri Pandits' Brahmin association, visited New Delhi and spoke with Gandhi and Pandit Jawaharlal. As a result of this priestly visit, Pandit Jawaharlal lowered the pitch of his statement and said the Kashmir movement had been begun hastily.

Here, a minority is treated like insignificant greens to be plucked! These Pandits are a minority in Kashmir, but their influence does not end at the palace; it extends to New Delhi. They issue statement after statement declaring Kashmir's liberation movement disastrous. This country's “national newspapers,” with “reverent loyalty,” never fail to publish those statements that injure the people's freedom. How could they fail, when “the Brahmin is my deity”?

The Italians voted on whether their country should retain a king. They showed greater support for having no king, and the Italian monarch left the country.

### Image 9

In Greece, whether the king may return depends upon the people's consent. In many European countries the people have prepared the death warrant for monarchy. Here the opposite occurs. Through their diwans, the rulers bargain with British imperialism to prevent outsiders entering the internal affairs of the princely states, while courting Congress and strengthening their position. British imperialism has found a way to prolong its time by treating these states as favoured children.

At such a moment, why should genuine seekers of popular liberation not propose that the people of each princely state decide by vote whether their ruler should remain? If they fear angering the emperors, let the rulers remain if they must; why not support movements demanding that principal responsibilities be entrusted to people's assemblies? Those who possess a spirit of liberation would do so.

But the Brahmins of Kashmir and those conducting the Hindu Mahasabha across the Indian subcontinent congratulate Kashmir's king for the “great” achievement of crushing a movement that fought for popular freedom. What hardness of heart that requires!

### Image 10

Pandit Jawaharlal telegraphed the Maharaja for permission to visit Kashmir. The Maharaja replied: “There is no benefit in Pandit Nehru coming to Kashmir on this occasion; please cancel the journey.” Polite language, but its meaning was: “Do not set foot in Kashmir!”

Not only the king but many organizations and individuals telegraphed Pandit Jawaharlal asking him not to come to Kashmir now. Who were they? Who else could they be but the priestly group that congratulated the Maharaja?

*Dravida Nadu*, 23 June 1946

## Translator's Notes

The title's subject is deliberately unstated. “He Congratulated Him!” preserves the source's accusatory suspense before identifying Savarkar and the Maharaja.

The historical currency பவுன் is rendered “sovereign.” The source prints the sale price as 400,000 sovereigns; it is retained as Anna's figure and not independently corrected.

பூசுரர் and விப்பிரர் are polemical Sanskrit-derived labels for Brahmins; “priestly group” and “Brahmins” are used according to context. பிராமணா மமதேவதா is rendered “the Brahmin is my deity,” preserving Anna's repeated sarcasm.

அடாணா refers metaphorically to pitch or vocal register; “lowered the pitch” retains the musical image for Nehru moderating his statement.

The translation preserves the article's 1946 political framing and demographic claims as source content. They should be checked against primary historical records before being cited independently.
