## English Translation

However unjust it is to discriminate and offer justifications on the ground that colour differs, it is a thousand times more unjust to discriminate by birth and treat people like cattle. To label some people "untouchable," prevent them from walking in the streets, and refuse them entry into places where God is said to reside is especially unbecoming of a country described as a holy land capable of giving spiritual light to the entire world.

How are temples being opened today? Even that appears to be done for some ulterior purpose. Whatever the circumstances, we must nevertheless rejoice to the extent that a great stain is being removed.

Comrade Sivas Shanmugam himself says that temple entry still remains at the stage of inviting people in authority such as himself to "come inside." He has also pointed out that temple entry alone is insufficient and that many other benefits must be provided. He made these observations while speaking at the annual celebration of the Tiruvarur "Harijan" Seva Sangh.

That association too has adopted two resolutions. One is to make the places where those called untouchables live their own property; the other is to provide them adequate education. Both are vital necessities.

Creating the facilities that would enable every person to attain economic security like Comrade Sivas Shanmugam is the next stage after temple entry. Our oppressed comrades must unite and speak loudly with one voice in a manner that draws the government's attention to this task. They must remember that it is the crying child that receives milk!

(*Dravida Nadu*, 28-12-48)
