## English Translation

The gentle breeze, one of Tamil Nadu's distinctive glories, has become the name of one of our organisation's journals, which works in a manner that makes revolution blossom with the speed of a storm.

What is even more amusing is that the friend who serves as its editor is one of my younger brothers, an orator, writer, poet, and actor who vigorously explains that the puranas and epics constructed and steeped in poison for our country are what jointly corrupt ancient Tamil culture and the ideas of a new world. Yet his name is Kannadasan. You may think it incongruous.

I would say that the nature of his name demonstrates where the Dravidian youth began and where he is going.

Only our opponents are bewildered, wondering whether our work is a great storm, a thunderclap, or a calamity. If its purity and benefit are considered, our work offers the delight of a gentle breeze. I would say that this classical Tamil journal therefore moves among us bearing the name *Thendral*, the Breeze.

Let these small matters rest. The great task before us is to acquire and provide, day after day and in many ways, the strength needed to wipe away the degradation that has befallen our mother. The journals and writers engaged in that work must gain the character always to devote their abilities to the advancement of the homeland.

Is not Pongal new year the day on which we see farmers who say, "We labour precisely so that we may give to others"?

When we see what was once such hard, barren ground, we exclaim: "Ah! How intensely the great farmers must have laboured to produce red paddy and grain, beautiful fragrant flowers, and many fruits that make the mouth water at the mere thought!"

Our goal is to bring advancement into their lives. That is why we honour this golden day as a good day for offering them thanks.

Our country must reach a condition in which all its distress has vanished and it has gained fresh splendour, so that visitors from every land may joyfully praise it: "Everything good is found in this country; it is difficult to find anything bad. All the people live with dignity. Every home wears a festive appearance, every heart is joyful, and every town celebrates a sacred day."

Our homeland contains abundant resources for attaining that condition. We must be filled with the intelligence and ability to use them.

What is needed is a clear plan, organised labour, the skill to avoid diversions, and a heart that does not weaken before the hardships of the journey.

All these, and the good ideas that bring still greater advancement, seem to be offered by the ginger and turmeric there, speaking tenderly to our living portraits and making joy overflow in our hearts.

Long live Dravidam! May the character of its people flourish!

Let the breeze blow! Let the forum blossom!

Affectionately,

Annadurai

(*Thendral*, Pongal Special Issue, 14-1-56)
