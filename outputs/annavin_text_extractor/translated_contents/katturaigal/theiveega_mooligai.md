## English Translation

Corpses throughout the streets!

The end of the fraud.

Conspiring scoundrels!

Corpses!

Along the roadsides, at railway stations and bus stations, wherever one looks, there are heaps of corpses!

For a distance of ten miles, it is impossible even to travel beside the roads. A horrifying sight!

Five hundred people died in three days. Men, women and children all collapse in heaps. It is unbearable to witness: wherever one looks there is a corpse; whichever way one turns, a wilderness of corpses!

The man who walked about alive yesterday, the man who left in the morning saying, “I am going there,” and the man who boarded a train promising to return in the evening: none has returned. They keep becoming corpses!

The man who reached the railway station and curled up there, unable to bear the night's cold, becomes a lifeless body. Many die and slump while still seated. Those who come running stagger and die in the middle of the street!

A tiny village, and a sight impossible to endure!

The provincial government is shaken and struggling.

The Chief Minister of the province has hurried towards the place where this is happening. The government is making special arrangements to remove the heaps of bodies immediately.

Special magistrates and police officers have all been stationed there.

Section 144 has been imposed to prevent anyone from outside entering the area.

Requests have been sent to the governments of West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Madras and several other provinces to stop anyone from travelling there.

Numerous special trains have been brought to that remote village. Even so, it has not been possible to remove the many thousands gathered there.

A crowd of more than 50,000 people still remains congested at the place.

The government has asked all newspapers to discourage people from going there.

Despite every special arrangement within its power, the government has been unable to cope. It cannot manage!

The provincial government is consumed by worry; the matter has become a great headache.

This heart-rending scene is not our imagination. It is actually happening, and nowhere else but in the province of Orissa.

The description above was supplied by newspaper reporters who visited and observed the spectacle surrounding events in the remote Orissa village of Randali.

Our hearts are shaken when we read reports of this dreadful scene in Orissa. We have seen and heard of many dying from the cruelty of hunger, of human beings collapsing and perishing in famine.

But the horror unfolding in Randali village is not caused by hunger. It is not caused by famine.

Nor are bullets turning people there into corpses.

It is superstition!

Yes. The Orissa government itself has now issued a statement in anguish: “Driven by a frenzy of superstition, many thousands gathered in the remote village of Randali. Sanitary conditions deteriorated and cholera broke out. People are consequently dying like worms. We are struggling desperately to remove them from the village.”

Superstition, says the government.

Many are falling dead because of superstition among the people, says the government.

It is a painful and unbearable sight, announces the government.

“A miraculous herb!”

“Divine medicine!”

“God in the form of a cowherd boy!”

“The occult wonders of a twelve-year-old child!”

“It cures every incurable affliction.”

“Every disease that refuses to heal flees before this little herb.”

“A marvel! A marvel!”

“The marvel of Nepal Baba!”

This is the very event our newspapers sensationalised in such language, and which the government, unable to bear the distress, has sorrowfully declared to be “the result of superstition.”

The public knows how the national newspapers greatly magnified the story that a thirteen-year-old boy called Nepal Baba distributed a small herb which cured every incurable disease, and that people from throughout India were making pilgrimages to Randali, where the boy was staying.

The national journals and the “holy fraternity” that nurtures Brahminical deception published these reports in many forms and under many different headlines.

“The sacred incarnation of the God of the Kali age!” wrote one newspaper.

“God has come and united himself with that boy,” it wrote on another day.

“Many thousands are travelling to behold Nepal Baba; the government therefore appears to have been compelled to operate new trains and special transport,” the papers reported on yet another day.

As though some new wonder were occurring, the story was repeatedly published in novel forms calculated to create excitement among the people.

The publicity surrounding the Nepal Baba of Randali village was so intense that it could confuse even intelligent people.

These reports appeared in banner type! Each journal treated them in its own fashion. Even publications such as *Ananda Vikatan*, though they did not write extravagant praise like *Dinamani*, regarded the matter merely as something humorous and did not come forward to condemn it!

The newspaper stories thoroughly stirred everyone. The Orissa government was forced to take a special interest in Nepal Baba.

Because of the reports spread by the newspapers and the resulting public excitement, the Orissa government appointed a committee to investigate Baba's miraculous herb.

The affair even led the Orissa government to seek the advice of several scientific experts.

The Indian scientist Sir C. V. Raman was the first to express his view: “Matters of this kind are incompatible with scientific reasoning. None of this can be believed.”

After a thorough examination, the government-appointed committee delivered its conclusion: “The miraculous herb is a fraud.”

It was a conspiracy by a few self-interested men to collect money, a poisonous scheme promoted by bus owners so that they might earn substantial revenue.

Through their agents, this fraudulent group had skilfully spread the story in other provinces as well.

More than five lakh people had been affected by the episode.

The whole affair was an act of great madness.

The Orissa government issued these findings after the committee's report.

It also prohibited Nepal Baba, under Section 144, from selling the medicine.

The alleged divine herb did not cure even an ordinary illness!

Not only did it fail to cure disease; conditions became so terrible that people were dying and becoming corpses every day.

A situation that made an entire government cry out! An event that left it writhing in distress!

No thinking person can avoid agitation when considering how painful and how disgraceful this was.

After the government reported Nepal Baba's conduct as “madness,” our deceitful Brahminical national newspapers began giving equally prominent coverage to these developments too.

What petty intelligence for newspapers circulating in a society!

These are the same papers that first published it extravagantly as a marvel.

And these are now the papers reporting it as an outrage.

What else can one say on seeing the mentality of that Ardhanarisvara lineage, except that it entertained the petty thought of selling more newspapers by calling it a “marvel” one week and a “miracle” the next?

This “company of liars,” which set afloat ever-new stories about Nepal Baba, now publishes the exposure of those deceptions just as prominently.

What can one call this “sacred fraternity” with its forked tongue?

It first gives prominence to reports steeped in superstition. Then, when the point arrives at which its nose is cut off in disgrace, it transforms itself into an elder statesman!

This is the horror and cruelty produced by the conduct of these orthodox Sanatana ritualists who cultivate Vedic orthodoxy and superstition. Alas, the heart boils merely to think of it.

People are said to be dying and lying in the streets like worms!

It is not enough merely to condemn the conduct of newspapers that confused the people with stories about a superstition-filled “miracle.”

Newspapers that spread such superstitious reports must be put in the dock. Otherwise, this game of madness will never end.

## Translator's Notes

`தெகிடுதத்தம்` is a colloquial expression for fraud, trickery or sleight of hand. `நேபாள பாபா` is transliterated as “Nepal Baba,” and the village name printed as `ரந்தாளி` is rendered “Randali.” `அர்த்தநாரிசுவர பரம்பரை` and `திருக்கூட்டம்` are used sarcastically for the religiously orthodox press fraternity. The article alternates between “twelve-year-old” in a quoted publicity slogan and “thirteen-year-old” in its narrative; both are retained because the Tamil source itself contains the discrepancy.
