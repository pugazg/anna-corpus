## English Translation

Panchali is said to have vowed, "I shall not comb my loosened hair until the head of the man who committed the outrage has been severed." According to the mythologists, even that lady, wife to five men, was enraged by the dishonour another man inflicted upon her in the royal assembly. Need one describe how others would feel? Panchali's vow is an old fabrication. Today a real event, a *Punjab vow*, is unfolding.

Congressmen won votes by telling the people how the Englishman General Dyer fired upon and killed people in Punjab until his ammunition was exhausted. They then forgot Punjab's suffering and became servants of the white rulers. We do not believe the present incident will likewise be forgotten.

An act has taken place that wounds the hearts of ten crore Muslims. Sardar Shaukat Hayat Khan, son of Sir Sikandar Hayat Khan and a Punjab minister, has been removed from office, dismissed by the Governor of Punjab. Sardar Hayat Khan is said to have dismissed a woman from employment. On beholding this "injustice", His Excellency the Governor reportedly dismissed the minister himself.

On seeing the Governor who established such justice, the Chola king famed for Manu's justice, who placed beneath his chariot wheel the son who had killed a calf, might rise from his grave to sing a sacred hymn in praise. What kind of scales does the Governor possess? If a woman was dismissed from employment and considered it unjust, she could have stood forth, spoken, and sought justice. Whether moved by knowledge of the woman's sorrow or by righteous anger that such injustice should occur while he was present, we do not know; the Governor dismissed the minister.

When the Chief Minister and the other ministers heard this, what procedure should they have followed upon seeing what befell their colleague? They should themselves have resigned immediately. What did they do? They remain ministers even today. Why? Office contains an adhesive. Whether one is named Baladeva or decorated as Veerasingam, it presses down and holds fast whoever sits upon it.

We do not argue that Sardar Hayat Khan ought to remain in office because his father rendered immense service to the Allied powers and gratitude must not be forgotten. Nor would he desire that. The Islam he observes would not permit such a course. Above everything stands *izzat*, that is, self-respect: such is Islam's command. Therefore neither he, nor other Muslims, nor we who understand Muslim character grieve over the loss of office. We ask only why the nationalist heroes who always condemn imperial conduct have not opened their mouths about what happened in Punjab.

At a regional Muslim League conference in Punjab, Mr Jinnah declared that the circumstances of this dismissal must be clarified and that, if necessary, the other ministers supporting the League should resign. We trust that the echo of his declaration will soon take form.

Meanwhile, Khizar Hayat Khan, the present Chief Minister there, has become a nodding puppet of certain Hindu and Sikh leaders. He disregards the League, denounces Mr Jinnah, and prevents the formation of a League ministry in Punjab. We remind Punjab's Premier that there is a lesson to be learned from the present condition of Mr Fazlul Huq, who, while Premier of Bengal, cultivated quarrels with his tongue.

The Governor's action and the Premier's course have now created a situation in which Punjab's Muslims must take a vow. That vow is that a League ministry must henceforth be formed. The present Premier may cling to stale old elements and prolong his time in office, but not for long. Let an election be held, and the truth will become evident: Punjab's Muslims will support none but the League that strives for the birthright called Pakistan.

Not even a month has passed since our friend Acharya marvelled at the supposed perfection of Punjab's coalition ministry and the distinction of those who formed it. Today there is this much disorder within that very ministry!

7 May 1944.
