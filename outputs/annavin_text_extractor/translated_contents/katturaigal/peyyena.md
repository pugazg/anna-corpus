## English Translation

Rain falls! Not through the power of women's chastity; that exists only in our country's songs.

“Very well. The misery you cause me and the anger that rises whenever I see you tempt me to do all manner of things. It is written upon my head that I must cling to you and weep. What can I do? Only because I do not want to behave in a way that makes the whole town laugh do I lie here tolerating all your crooked antics. And in this fine condition, have you come to preach philosophy to me? Philosophy! Am I to touch your feet and worship?”

The angry wife crows in this fashion.

“You mad woman! I merely repeated what the Bhagavathar said in his discourse about how a chaste wife ought to behave. This is neither my command nor my wish. It is the word of the elders,” the husband explains.

“Does the fellow who tells stories for money know only the duty of a chaste woman? Has he forgotten the duty a husband must perform?” she asks with justice.

“Foolish woman! Even what he said was not intended to improve the worthiness of men. The earthly world needs queens of chastity who worship the lotus feet of their husbands. Why? Because if such women open their mouths and speak, rain will fall immediately. There is a verse saying, ‘Rain falls when she commands it to fall.’ Does the world not need rain? Through chastity, women acquire the power to summon that very rain and say, ‘Come!’

“If women wish to gain that chastity, glory and power, a force capable of benefiting the whole world, they need not even worship God. It is enough to worship the sacred feet of the husband,” he continues.

“They could not have said that about useless husbands like you. It must have been something learned men said about noble men,” she objects.

“Even after I explain it so gently, you speak against me!” the husband roars and beats her severely. Unable to bear the blows, the woman cries and tears begin to pour.

If desired, rain does fall at command in this fashion in many families in our country. Only in the verse written in the book have they portrayed it elegantly as “rain that falls at the word ‘fall’!”

Today Westerners, who are neither inhabitants of the holy land, followers of the sacred Vedic path nor practitioners of image worship, have made this miracle ordinary. Soon “rain” will become a commodity in the market.

Rain will fall when required, in the required quantity and at the required place. Not through singing hymns, nor according to the word of a chaste wife, but by scientific method.

Because our people forgot science and tightly embraced something or other called spiritual knowledge, they forgot even that rain is one of nature's delightful activities and invented Indra. He supposedly drives the clouds, which flee in terror and collide with one another. From time to time Indra throws his thunderbolt. The immense sound produced is thunder; the flash of the thunderbolt is lightning. They spent their lives inventing such tales.

If rain did not fall in season, they concluded that they must have harmed some deity. To appease that deity's anger, some festival had to be held. What should be done? Offer Pongal to Mari? Slaughter goats and chickens as an offering? Summon Mannar? Sacrifice a buffalo? Drag an effigy? Dance a kummi for Gopalan?

They established many such rites and many forms of worship. They did not spread even the elementary science that rain results from cloud, cloud from heat and heat from the action of the burning sun.

Generation after generation they asked: “How many eyes does Indra have? What is the relationship between Varuna and Vayu? How many horses draw the sun's chariot? Is there a white horse among them?”

They never asked: rain is an element of nature, but can humanity bring it under control? Can human intelligence help us govern this natural force according to our will?

How could courage for that question arise? We had already created a separate god for every form of nature. The mind gave room to thinking and speaking about those divine sports and to writing stories and poems, leaving no room for science.

Westerners too worshipped gods in the beginning. Whether they wanted rain or sought to prevent torrential floods from rolling through and destroying towns, they too invented deities like Indra for their countries, built temples and danced. Many Kambans there also composed poetry rich with sweetness.

But they passed beyond that childhood, became rationalists and today learned to understand clearly every quality of nature and bring it under control. There, “rain that falls at the word ‘fall’” has advanced beyond the measure of song to the stage of practical experiment.

To the shame of religious believers, at least those among them who are honest, ordinary people rather than saints, people without divine grace, summoned science to their aid and demonstrated how to make rain fall.

Like a mother carrying a child in her womb, a cloud travels the avenue of the sky. Australian scientists discovered a way to make that cloud rain when people desire rather than according to its own will.

For many centuries, or for age after age in mythological language, a hen has incubated an egg and hatched a chick. Today a chick is brought out of the egg through scientific means without a hen incubating it.

In the same way, instead of waiting for a cloud to ripen by itself and release rain, they cause the cloud to ripen and make it rain. Is this done by Ramana Rishi? No. By Aurobindo? No. By Australian scientists.

They fly into the cloud belt and cast solid blocks of hard ice. Struck by those pieces of ice, the mass of cloud carrying rain loosens and releases it.

A century ago this could not have been imagined; it lay beyond fantasy. Today it has become an ordinary sight. More than that, it is becoming an easy undertaking. America has begun applying this scientific method of producing rain on a large scale.

In our country the poor farmer may not even possess a pair of ploughing bullocks. In America, some moderately prosperous citizens are said to own aircraft. Farmers there have flown in aeroplanes, struck clouds by the method described above and made rain fall upon their own farms. Their knowledge has advanced to that extent. There, rain falls at command, and we merely read about it.

Our scientific knowledge need not immediately rise, like theirs, to the cloud belt or the lunar sphere. But should it not advance at least to some defined degree? Should there not at least be an effort towards it?

When one observes the condition of the people and the conduct of those who lead them, sorrow and shame cannot help pouring together. Thought has still not emerged from the mire of Sanatana belief. To many, even today, that mud is sandalwood paste.

There, human beings govern moving clouds through knowledge. They do not stop at governing them; they calculate the benefits and difficulties that may arise from this condition and act accordingly.

A cloud can be brought under control. But is it proper? The travelling cloud might tomorrow provide rain to another farm. Science drives it away and obtains water for this farm today. Does that not amount to stealing a cloud? Should this theft not be prevented? Westerners have advanced so far that proposals are discussed for an international organisation to address the matter.

We remain at the stage of merely hearing that the atomic bomb is a force of destruction. At the same time, they have begun examining constructive works that atomic energy can produce on a scale beyond our imagination. Pilotless aircraft fly there. Wonder has become an everyday commodity.

In this global environment, at this very time, newspaper reports say that the wise men governing newly independent India have supported a rare piece of research. What research? You will be startled. The world will clap and laugh, not in front of us but behind our backs.

The *Shanti Parva* of the Mahabharata has apparently not been published in a proper form. The source manuscripts have not been found. To remove the great deficiency caused by the absence of this magnificent “Parva,” great scholars and historical experts researched until exhausted. At last they learned that source manuscripts were held by the king of Nepal. They sent him a message, obtained his consent and are travelling there to turn the pages.

The government has agreed to spend money upon it, and wealthy patrons have agreed as well. Soon this *Shanti Parva* research will be completed successfully and the book published. This is the newspaper report.

There, with the help of science, they summon rain. Here, to explain the grandeur of the Mahabharata, research is conducted into the *Shanti Parva*.

Fortunately, they have stopped our country's research at that point. We have not yet reached an announcement that six lakh rupees have been allotted for the rare investigation of how many yards of cloth Dushasana might have stripped away, with Dr Alagappa donating half a lakh. They remain with the *Shanti Parva*.

The *Shanti Parva*! In 1947, the news that pilotless aircraft fly there and the news that *Shanti Parva* research is conducted here appear in the same newspaper.

Without feeling even a drop of regret or shame, without reflecting in the least whether a country can remain like this or whether such a condition will earn it respect before the world, students throughout the province read both reports and then turn to cinema news: “What is playing at Roxy? What is showing at Casino?”

They have made science the people's companion. We continue to trust the old almanac. If the Dravidar Kazhagam boldly comes forward to say, “Is this not wrong?” the Education Minister says, “Take this curse!” and flies into fierce anger, almost the kind of anger said to seize a person suffering from piles. The minister's conduct is this.

The students remain in a mood of “the evening hour when the cuckoo sings.” The people remain at the level of familiar devotional cries: “Mahesa! Chitsabesa! Natesa!” This is the condition here.

There, “rain that falls at the word ‘fall’” is demonstrated in the country rather than remaining only in song. When one considers this condition, what can one do but draw a long sigh?

12 October 1947

## Translator's Notes

The title alludes to the celebrated Tamil formulation that rain falls at the command of the perfectly chaste wife. The essay first turns that claim into a domestic satire and then contrasts it with scientific cloud seeding.

Image 6 clearly contains the word `எண்ணினால்...`, omitted from the OCR immediately before the publication date. It was restored in both canonical Tamil copies before translation.

The source describes early dry-ice cloud seeding and contemporary scientific claims as reported in 1947. Those claims, along with its descriptions of artificial incubation, atomic energy and pilotless aircraft, are translated as period commentary rather than independently verified technical statements.
