## English Translation

## A Withdrawn Case Is Investigated Again! The Rulers' Arrows Target Sivasamy and Thangapazham!

Wilkes was prosecuted once, twice, and many times. But he won again and again, while the rulers embraced disgrace.

This is an interesting detail from English history.

Now our government too is reviving a case it had abandoned and dismissed. A case was brought against Sivasamy, who wrote the book *Savukkadi*, and Thangapazham, who published it. In the end, it was announced that the case had been dismissed. Tamil Nadu commended the magnanimity shown in correcting the mistake.

But suddenly, a government summons again knocked at our comrades' doors. They did not understand why. They went to court to learn the truth. The case, it was said, had been dismissed by mistake. The Sub-Divisional Magistrate explained that the order had been issued because of the carelessness of his subordinates. Therefore, he announced, the abandoned case would continue.

The government is digging a pit for itself. What a strange thing! The rulers have set out to prosecute once again a case they had withdrawn.

The Congress Government, which talked endlessly of freedom of expression before attaining power, now stages a dance of domination, imposing prohibition and denial upon everything and forgetting its own past. It filed a case over the writing and publication of *Savukkadi*. Very well. It pursued the case, and even that was not wrong. But after everything had ended and the case had been withdrawn, it revived it on a bizarre pretext. Learning this causes distress to all who desire the country's welfare.

After the Restoration in English history, they reportedly dug up Cromwell's skeleton and hanged it to satisfy themselves. Is the government similarly attempting to establish its valour by reviving an abandoned case? Has justice fled and hidden? Have integrity and honour died and disappeared? We do not understand.

They say their employees made a mistake. Why can they not accept that mistake and leave the case withdrawn? The splendour of the administration's perfection is visible with complete clarity. We understand the rulers' competence very well indeed!

The comrades concerned have obtained an order preventing the revived case from being heard in the same court and have appealed in Madras.

Let them file case after case, and may our comrades attain the position of Wilkes. But prosecuting a withdrawn case on an insubstantial pretext is strange, an incomprehensible puzzle and an unsolvable riddle.

If the *Savukkadi* case was truly dismissed for the reason given by the rulers, it must be called a lash upon their administrative corruption.

Let the case proceed and judgment be delivered. We have no anxiety about that, nor time to oppose or ridicule it. But justice laughs at their revival of a withdrawn case. Time waits to perform its duty. We remind them of that.

(*Dravida Nadu*, 6-8-50)
