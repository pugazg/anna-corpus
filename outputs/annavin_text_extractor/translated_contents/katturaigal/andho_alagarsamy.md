## English Translation

A flame of the Self-Respect Movement! An incomparable commander of the movement for dignity! An open enemy of Brahminism! A frontline leader who broke down superstitious customs! A foe of Vedic orthodoxy! An opponent of *varnashrama*! A champion of reform! A man rich in words! A tireless worker! A person of character who forgot self-interest and served his country! A rationalist who filled the pits and chasms scattered along the path of knowledge! A courageous man who travelled the land with a fearless heart to remove ignorance!

"Stones thrown at you? Do not worry! An enemy army? Do not fear! Opposing parties? Do not be deceived! Sanatanism? Do not yield to it! Conspiracy? Destroy it! Religion? Cause its hold to fade! Wipe away blemishes! Shine as a jewel of humanity! Gain respect! Strive to obtain self-respect and live a life of well-being! Let this be your objective."

Alagarsamy, wherever you went you sounded this trumpet and raised the beat of the drum in every corner. You stood as a frontline leader of the Self-Respect Movement and carried out the work of enlightenment with a fearless heart. Is it you who has died? Even when the people forgot you, you did not forget them and continued your rare service. Hero of action, who performed his duty without expecting recompense, is it you who has died? Noble man who demonstrated in practice the principle, "My duty is to labour and lie down only after the work is done," is it you who has died? Leader who treated opposition as a feast and opened the eyes of knowledge of countless people, is it you who has died? Commander of the rationalist camp, is it you who has died? Commander who came to protect the dignity of Tamil people suffering like a child separated from its mother, is it you who has died?

Alas, Alagarsamy, have you died? The mere thought makes the heart shudder. It is an unforgettable event, an occurrence that confounds the mind.

Although comrade Alagarsamy had been ill and confined to bed for some time, we never expected him to leave us so suddenly and plunge us all into inconsolable grief. Reports that his illness was diminishing day by day comforted us and gave us hope that Alagarsamy would return among us and resume his service. But that hope has rewarded us only with longing. When the shocking news arrived that comrade Alagarsamy had died on 28 March 1949, we were stunned.

Imagine how people aboard a ship that has lost its direction would panic when, unable to see the shore, they watch even its mast break and fall. Many times greater was the agitation that overwhelmed all Tamil people when they heard the heart-stopping news that the rationalist ship named Alagarsamy, who had served as a guide to the Self-Respect movement, had fallen ill and died.

Must the reward received by a true worker who served Tamil Nadu ceaselessly with an untiring heart be a life-consuming disease brought on by that very labour? Alas!

Must tuberculosis be the reward for a man who shouted until his voice rang out and poured forth masses of ideas to open the eyes of people lying bereft of thought?

When we remember that anxiety over not obtaining money in time to treat the tuberculosis seems itself to have helped the disease grow, everyone in general, and the Self-Respect Movement in particular, cannot help reflecting upon and grieving over why this condition arose.

After comrade Alagarsamy became ill and bedridden, the Madras Self-Respect Association provided one sum of assistance, and small amounts were given from a few other towns. Although this offered him some comfort in his final days, it is our heartfelt wish and appeal that everyone who has seen the fruits created by his incomparable service should at least consider how the family that depended upon him will now live and do what is necessary.

The departed warrior's wife, three daughters, two sons, and aged mother now look to the assistance of Tamil Nadu's Self-Respect Movement.

We must ensure that the anguish caused by the loss of their head of household, joined by poverty, does not further torment and destroy them. This alone can serve as atonement for the duty we failed to perform for him.

We convey our boundless and profound grief to his family and to the comrades of the Self-Respect Movement who mourn his passing, and offer them consolation.

Yes, Alagarsamy is gone. His name, his fame, and his service have taken up residence in all our hearts. Let us now continue the work he left behind, attain victory, and perpetuate the fame of valiant Alagarsamy.

3 April 1949

## Translator's Notes

The source is an obituary for Self-Respect activist Alagarsamy and uses movement-era polemical language. OCR “இசை தவறிய கப்பல்” is translated as the clearly intended “திசை தவறிய கப்பல்,” a ship that has lost direction. A later damaged phrase is read contextually as the Self-Respect Movement; both source forms remain verbatim above.
