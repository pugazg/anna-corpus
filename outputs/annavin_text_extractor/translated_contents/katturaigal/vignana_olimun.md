## English Translation

Professor Julian S. Huxley writes in the *London Literary Guide*:

I cannot believe that a God exists, because the hypotheses that created God have become useless.

Hypotheses are necessary in every field. Only through them can a particular matter be explained. Here, however, “hypothesis” must mean only something written as a conjecture. Nothing can be done without conjecture, but one must not imagine that every conjecture will prove correct.

For example, only by forming some hypothesis about the forces and properties of the world can we bring each under control in a manner suited to its nature.

Through certain hypotheses and suppositions, people in many parts of the world have advanced. Some still remain in an old, uncivilized form of life. But the supposition called God has not aided human progress in any way; it has remained a great obstruction.

Because people could not discover the causes of events in the world, they created God or gods as their makers. They made God responsible for every occurrence and attributed certain traits and deficiencies to that God.

Believing that sacrifices, worship, and prayers to the deity would remove the suffering and disasters that arise in the world, people have always maintained many kinds of divine conceptions. Some attributed wholly animal qualities to God. Others established monotheism, which came to be regarded as the possession of a particular community.

Muslims conceived that there is only one God. Christians conceived Christianity. Buddhism was conceived as a religion not involving God, but in time it too sank into the atmosphere of other religions and introduced that idea.

This conception may perhaps have helped some people keep others in ignorance, deceive them, and live in ease and luxury. But if we examine whether it has truly provided the people in general a means of deliverance, we find that vessel empty.

For my part, I must say that today we can see nothing in the conception of God except its reduction of human beings to beasts.

I cannot believe anything apart from propositions hypothesized and discovered by scientists. The darkness of ignorance cannot stand before the light of science. The scientific world makes no room for the conception of God or for the fraud that God hears prayers and penances and bestows grace. It burns away all such fabrications.

The arguments of theists pierce my ears like red-hot iron.

If people had merely imagined that someone created this world, or that some divine being existed, and had left it there without establishing a relationship between that God and human life, neither I nor anyone else would trouble about the deity. It would make no difference whether there were one God or millions.

I do not believe in immortality either. Religion continues to wander through the world today because it offers consolation. Many embrace it and weep because of the imagined supposition that immediately after death a person will rise again or be reborn.

Who wishes to die? Even after an old person has passed seventy, that person wishes to live a little longer. Who would not desire to remain alive forever?

People generally wish not to die. Because religions contain suppositions suited to that desire, people continue to embrace them. How many among us would not wish to see dead friends alive again?

To sustain the conception that the departed remain alive, people created another world and invented hell. These have faded and disappeared in the radiance of science. Science cannot tell us what form, life, or power we acquire after death.

If the idea of God is a fabrication and immortality an illusion, what remains in religion once they are removed?

Distinguished theists frighten rationalists and atheists with the spectre that morality in human society will disappear and be destroyed if people cease to believe in God, immortality, rebirth, and resurrection.

First, we must examine in what respect agnostics, atheists, and rationalists who do not hold these beliefs appear inferior to theists.

Second, we see today that people raised without belief in God, rebirth, resurrection, and similar ideas live as courageous and healthy human beings.

Belief in God causes a person to think chiefly of selfish interests and makes personal salvation the goal. It teaches one to despise others and live apart, creating the desire to enjoy a happy life in another world. It can even make a person abandon children who placed their trust in him, and it prevents him from seeing the suffering of his brothers and sisters in this world.

This alone is enough to reveal who labours for the welfare of the world, the theist or the atheist.

There is no religious scripture that does not say, “He exists in every person; He shines in every heart.” Yet who can claim that atheists are responsible for the suffering found among people today?

Human beings possess the capacity to recognize good and evil, knowledge and strength, beauty and ugliness, nobility and violence, self-interest and the welfare of others. They can weigh the nature of their own actions and calmly assess their consequences.

Ordinarily, good qualities first appear in a human being. The structure of life is what causes them to be altered.

Through experience, a person prefers to do what produces good and yields beneficial results. It is therefore clear that humanity develops through experience from day to day.
