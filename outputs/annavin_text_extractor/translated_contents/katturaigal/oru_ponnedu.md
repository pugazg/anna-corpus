## English Translation

Sardar Patel—the lion of Gujarat and Deputy Prime Minister of India, a man of action who gave lustre to public service through firmness of heart, faith in his own actions, confidence in victory, speed in public work, eagerness to bear responsibility, wisdom sufficient not to become a slave to haste, affection capable of defeating even opponents with a smile, and selfless sacrifice—has died.

His final breath, that of the man who pointed his finger and subdued every schemer and stood as a symbol of courage in the Indian subcontinent, mingled with the air at 9:37 on the morning of the 15th. He was a great jewel. Sardar, the discerning flame upon the path to freedom, has been cremated and reduced to ashes.

His body, born seventy-five years ago on 31 October 1875 in the village of Karamsad in Gujarat, is now a handful of ash. The heroic figure who made kings and emperors bow with a nod of his head has disappeared. We can no longer see him: that broad face upon which resolve played; those eyes radiant with confidence; that wide forehead declaring that one must possess a heart capable of bearing anything; that heart which offered profound ideas joined with experience. It is no longer here. It has gone.

Nehru reportedly wept. C. R. is said to have shed tears in silence. When they saw in death the man who had stood shoulder to shoulder with them in public work for thirty-five years, the thought of their anguish would move anyone's heart.

When we heard of the death of that great man praised as the pillar of India, the hero of Bardoli, Sardar, and Vallabhbhai, we were shaken. Our hearts throbbed. Some great shock had struck us.

When the news "Patel is dead" reached us, we recalled what he had said at a public meeting in Madras on 23 February 1949:

"I am one who has always respected the South. I always wish to meet all of you and rejoice. But the responsibility I have accepted gives me no rest. I too have grown old. In all likelihood, this may even be the last time I see Madras."

Many may dislike Patel's policies and course because of political differences. But for those engaged in political life, his life is a golden chapter; for those who wish to accomplish a task, it is a picture to contemplate.

In 1916, at the age of thirty-nine, he became a Gandhian. During the no-tax campaign of 1928, he rallied the cultivators of the Bardoli region and conducted the struggle skilfully. Imprisonment and the repression of British domination sent storms through his life many times.

He received those storms with a smile and demonstrated the hardships and losses of public work. He bore new responsibilities and showed the sacred path of public service. Capturing the people's hearts, he became an imperishable jewel.

He was born merely as one among the many millions of human beings born in the world. Yet his life ended in such a way that many millions grieved at his passing.

He has died; birth and death are natural. But his death plunges everyone into sorrow. The enthusiasm he possessed for public service made him the "uncrowned king" of this country and installed his name in the hearts of the people.

Patel was the supreme enemy of false political actors who moved about showing one thing in their hearts and another outside. It is said that this man of action would rage when he saw them.

His distinctive quality was to keep his eye fixed upon the task and employ every strategy needed to complete it. The appearance of such human jewels among those in public life is itself the well-being of a country.

The enthusiasm and ability he displayed in the freedom struggle were immense. They deserve the thought and action of all who are engaged in struggles for liberation.

He completed what he intended, departed, and left every heart disturbed by his absence. His name now bears the fame of history. He has died; his body has become a handful of ash. But his name, sacrifice, and greatness will neither disappear nor perish.

Long live his greatness!

(*Dravida Nadu*, 24 December 1950)
