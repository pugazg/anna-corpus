## English Translation

Two victories from two fronts demonstrate that the clamour of the forces of domination cannot endure. One is a language struggle; the other, a struggle for rights. Though those engaged belong to two different camps, their victories inspire us, the members of a rights movement.

"We do not want an alien language; we want our mother tongue!" is one voice. "We will not fear repression; we will secure our rights!" is the other.

It may even be surprising to see the fronts from which these two voices arose. Those who waged the language struggle were Muslims. Those who began the rights struggle were Sikhs.

One took place in Pakistan; the other in Hindustan.

Both non-violent struggles have succeeded.

The people living in East Pakistan opposed the Urdu language imposed by Pakistan's Central Government. They asked, "Bengali is our own language. Why Urdu instead?" In Dacca this agitation grew into a great movement. No one can have forgotten the students' protests or the batons and tear gas used by the East Pakistan Government to suppress them.

The Central Government employed conciliatory words, threats, repression, and many other methods: "We are giving the order. How can you, the people of a province, reject it? Does not a united Pakistan require a single language? How can you oppose it?" But now it has decided that, if its tottering position is to be restored, it must act according to the people's wishes. The order has been withdrawn. Urdu will not roam there as a compulsory language.

The Akali agitation in East Punjab, which continued for the past seventy days and sent more than eight thousand people to prison, has also ended. To satisfy Delhi, the East Punjab Government had prohibited even the raising of slogans such as "Punjabi Suba! We want a Punjab province!" Tara Singh continued the non-violent struggle, declaring that this deprived the people of their rights.

People mocked it: "Shame! Is this even a struggle? It will fizzle out. Will people rally behind the same Tara Singh who entered Nehru's meeting and created a disturbance?" Yet the non-violent protest began every day before the Sikhs' holy Golden Temple. Eight thousand prisoners are no small matter in Punjab. They filled the prisons like swarming insects. The situation reportedly created for Chief Minister Sachar the kind of crisis Rajaji faced and grew to the point where the government itself might fall. Therefore, the government withdrew the prohibitory order it had imposed. A victorious withdrawal!

These victories in non-violent struggle bear witness to the truth frequently proclaimed by liberation movements: justice will triumph, and the impulse to dominate will perish.

They demonstrate that, wherever people may live, the right concerning their language and the fundamental demand for civil rights will inevitably arise.

The victories won by Punjab and Bengal give us confidence that the Julys and Augusts in Dravidam too will become victories. We rejoice and call out: Dravidam, behold!

(*Dravida Nadu*, news item, 17-7-55)
