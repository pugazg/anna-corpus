## English Translation

For roughly twelve years, friends who had adorned the Ripon Building without needing to look back at the voters who elected them finally found themselves obliged to face those voters on Monday the 30th. Some former members did not contest the election; many did. Among those who did contest were certain notable people: some who believed the municipal council was a charitable rest house built for them by their ancestors, and others who failed to understand that the council's permanent duty was to provide many benefits to the city's residents and instead treated it as a splendid hall in which to rest and enjoy themselves. Their defeat at the polls, to the point of losing the deposits they had paid, offers an excellent lesson to anyone who acts without respecting the voters.

Because of the division between the Andhra Congress and the Tamil Nadu Congress, it became impossible to contest the election officially on behalf of Congress. But would electoral opportunists let such a rare opportunity slip from their hands? They slapped their shoulders and thighs and set out on the great mission of saving Madras. They even bestowed upon themselves the name "Tamil Nadu Border Committee."

Although they did not intervene in the election under the name of the Tamil Nadu Congress Committee, the very same power brokers who pulled the strings within the Tamil Nadu Congress Committee appeared in leading roles in this Border Committee too. There is not much difference in colour between orange and yellow.

The Border Committee did not field candidates in advance in every division of the city. Instead, after people had stood as candidates according to their own wishes, the committee claimed a few of them as its own.

Most of those who received the Border Committee's blessing belonged to the Congress Party and no other. Of the fifty-five candidates given certificates of approval by the Border Committee, thirty were defeated. Through this defeat, the public influence enjoyed by Muthurangam, who stood as the principal force behind the Border Committee, has become abundantly clear.

It is noteworthy that workers gave labour leader comrade Anthony Pillai—who had suffered under the repressive measures of the Congress rulers—the largest number of votes and secured his victory, while the influential Congress candidate who stood against him was made to lose even his deposit.

Several leading members of the Dravidar Kazhagam contested the election. Despite malicious campaigning against them, voters were not deceived by false statements and elected Dravidar Kazhagam comrades Jeevarathinam, Ramalingam, Munisamy, and others. This deserves praise.

The election has made one thing clear: whatever party candidates may belong to and whatever policies they may personally follow, voters prefer—and do prefer—those who possess the resolve to act with the welfare of the city as their foremost concern, without attaching excessive importance to all else.

One or two people treated the municipal council as their private property and fielded close relatives and friends in several places. The voters ensured that their petty scheme did not succeed. We do not wish to expose them by name.

While greeting and welcoming the comrades of the Dravidar Kazhagam, we wholeheartedly commend the voters for properly teaching the electoral gamblers that, whatever disguise they may adopt, the voters will no longer be deceived.

(*Dravida Nadu*, date printed in damaged OCR as 10-19-46£)

## Translator's Notes

The title is ironic consolation for defeated entrenched candidates and political organisers. The publication date is malformed and has not been guessed. The source's “தேர்தல் சிகண்டிகள்” is a disparaging political label; English renders its functional meaning as electoral opportunists rather than importing its mythological and gendered insult.
