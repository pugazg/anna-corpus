## English Translation

The following is a summary of the speech by comrade Sithaiyan at the public meeting held under the chairmanship of Mr V. Kannappar on behalf of the Dravidar Kazhagam of Periya Kanchipuram at six in the evening on Sunday, 29 August 1948.

"Beloved chairman and comrades, my heartfelt thanks to you all.

"When we examine the present political situation, one matter stands fully before us: we must consider the contradictory and mutually inconsistent speeches of Indian Prime Minister Jawaharlal, who recently visited Madras.

"The important statement in his speech was, 'There must be no provincial fanaticism. If it exists, we must first remove it.' Yet the same Nehru, besides saying that a provincial commission must be established, also declared: 'The distressed people of Kashmir stand before my eyes. I cannot forget it, because it is the province where I was born.'

"Jawaharlal, who tells us that we must not possess provincial attachment, immediately reveals how much of it he himself possesses. This reflects like a mirror the direction in which present-day politics is moving.

"Moreover, the disturbances in Kashmir and the conflict in Hyderabad have brought the country to a condition in which more than half its income must be spent upon them. It is while the country is thus weakened and confused that the government has hurriedly introduced Hindi as a compulsory subject.

"There is confusion even within the government. At first it said Hindi would be compulsory in Kerala, Kannada, Malayalam and Andhra regions, excluding Tamil Nadu. For some reason, the rulers altered that decision the very next month and devised a scheme making it compulsory in Tamil Nadu too.

"It applies from the first to the third form. If a child in the first form is eleven years old, what will become of children when the government stuffs three languages into their brains at that tender age? In a country where ninety people out of every hundred are uneducated, where ninety are illiterate, what need is there to force three languages upon them?

"Does the government at least insist only on Hindi? It says children may choose whichever they like: Hindi, Arabic, Malayalam, Telugu or Kannada. But the teacher available at school is, and will be, a Hindi teacher. Government representatives say at meetings everywhere that Hindi must be the common language. It is therefore perfectly clear that the government is compulsorily imposing Hindi.

"Yet it evades the issue by saying that people may study any language they wish. There is confusion even among the leaders about Hindi itself: should Hindi be the common language, or Hindustani?

"The present government thus remains in confusion. The Dravidar Kazhagam's decision is intended to change this confused condition and lead it to a sound one. Let us therefore all oppose and drive out Hindi, and bring the confused government itself to a settled position."

Comrade R. Nedunchezhiyan spoke next. A summary follows:

"Affectionate chairman and comrades, my thanks to you all. Under the title 'The Present Political Situation,' we may speak about any issue.

"So far as the South is concerned, the condition of today's government is that it has introduced Hindi as a compulsory subject. When we of the Dravidar Kazhagam oppose it, government supporters say: 'The Dravidar Kazhagam wishes to oppose everything Congress does, and so it opposes this too.'

"What measure did we oppose merely for its own sake? When they introduced the bill abolishing zamindari and inam tenure, we held a conference in support and cooperated with them. When they enacted prohibition, we stood behind it. When they abolished the devadasi system, we praised the measure and supported it in our newspapers. When they said untouchability must end and Harijans must enter temples, we ran ahead and worked vigorously.

"Even now, temple entry has not abolished the exclusive right of one section to touch God. I ask whether Kamaraj, Omandurar or Kuppusami Naidu can touch Ekambaranathar here. Even today they are in fact regarded as unfit to touch him. We demand the right for everyone to go near God and worship.

"We supported all these things, assisted them and worked alongside them. Our Congress comrades call us people determined to oppose everything. Let them now say what we opposed. The government speaks this way only because it somehow wishes to suppress the Dravidar Kazhagam. Consider for yourselves whether even a sesame seed's worth of truth exists in what it says.

"We of the Dravidar Kazhagam did not begin opposing Hindi today. Ten years ago, when the Honourable Rajagopalachariar was Premier of the Madras Presidency and imposed it compulsorily, we opposed it and won.

"We do not oppose Hindi alone. Some may wonder why we also enter politics. Hindi is not merely a language question; it is also a political question. They are not introducing Hindi alone. Along with it they impose northern culture, customs and other things. That is why the Dravidar Kazhagam also enters the political field.

"Today's government says India is one nation and therefore requires one national language. We say India is not one nation but a subcontinent. At no time was this land united. It was not one even in Akbar's age or in Aurangzeb's. The Puranas themselves say it consisted of fifty-six countries.

"Only after the white man used the point of his gun to seize and combine the small countries, making Delhi the capital for administrative convenience, was it fashioned into one country and one nationality. At no other time does history show India as a single country or nation.

"That is why we say it is not one nation and does not require one national language. The South was never joined to the North. Northern culture differs from southern culture. We consider the imposition of Hindi an invasion launched by the North upon the South, and therefore we oppose it.

"Certain nationally minded Dravidian comrades here evade the question by saying Hindi will not damage Tamil. Was it not after northern Sanskritic learning entered this country that Kannada, Malayalam, Telugu and the others emerged? Is not Sanskrit the reason a language once spoken by five crore people has been reduced to one spoken by two and a half crores? Who can deny it?

"There need therefore be no doubt that Hindi will further injure Tamil. Nor will Tamil alone be harmed; Tamil character, art and culture will be ruined. That is why we shall fight Hindi to the very end and win."

He recalled the revolutionary poet Bharathidasan's song, "Shall Hindi dominate Tamil Nadu? Come, all of you, comrades," and concluded his address. The chairman offered thanks and the meeting ended pleasantly.

(*Dravida Nadu*, 5 September 1948)
