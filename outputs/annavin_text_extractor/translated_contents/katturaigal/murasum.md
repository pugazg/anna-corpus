## English Translation

I am happy to offer it. Since it comes through a “flower,” it is natural that it should be filled with fragrance.

The “drum” and the “flower” are objects that have given Tamil inspiration and delight from remote antiquity. The former belongs to the outer sphere, while the latter concerns chiefly the inner sphere. They served to signify the two noble qualities of valour and love.

Even drums and flowers existed in many varieties. The Tamils assigned a distinct one to each situation. Their art of living had been fashioned with such refinement.

Today, Thambi Karunanidhi’s *Murasoli* serves as a drum of awakening for the liberation struggle being conducted so that the Tamil people may once again gain self-rule and live with heads held high upon the earth.

Everyone has known for many years that the *Murasoli* annual brings sweetness and awakening to Tamil homes and gives them joy.

Those of us engaged in the liberation struggle rarely find time to add colour and gather in celebration. Opportunities to assume a festive appearance are rare. Yet the new day of Pongal somehow makes us seek colour and discover sweetness.

In those days when we had not learned to live by supplication or lose our country, it was the flower plucked by the hand of the victor wearing the *vaagai* wreath that brought joy and pride to the noble maiden! If one surrenders the country to an enemy, turns every house into a garden, plucks many kinds of flowers, strings them with golden thread, and offers them to the maiden, there will be neither fragrance nor joy.

Today our country is not a flower garden, nor do we govern ourselves.

That is why we need the “drum,” the “war drum,” and the awakening it gives us. It has been giving us the ardour to gain a liberation through which every home may truly be said to celebrate.

The growth of that ardour will bring us victory. Only after that victory will we be able to sing joyfully that the flower we sought and brought belongs to the honey-voiced maiden.

We journey toward that future. Gratefully accepting the help we receive and enduring the hardships that arise, the *Murasoli* annual gives us happiness during that journey and helps remove our weariness. I welcome it. I know that you too will welcome it. Long may you live! May virtue flourish! May the liberation struggle triumph!

Affectionately,  
Annadurai

*Murasoli Pongal Annual, 14 January 1960*

## Translator's Notes

The available first scan begins after an earlier sentence, so the object of “offer it” is absent. The recurring முரசு/மலர் contrast is translated as drum/flower and its classical puram/akam associations are retained as outer/inner spheres.
