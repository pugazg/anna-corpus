## English Translation

### Image 1

22. The Tiruttani Picket and the Nationalists!

Tamil Nadu knows of the peaceful struggle conducted for the past few days at Tiruttani in Chittoor district under the leadership of Comrade K. Vinayagam, M.L.A.

The struggle protests Delhi's announcement that certain parts of Tamil-speaking Chittoor district are to be included in Andhra Province.

Places such as Tiruttani, Podatturpet, Nagari, and Puttur are inhabited predominantly by Tamil speakers. Nevertheless, these areas are apparently to be included in Andhra.

Every party is supporting the picket against this announcement. We learn that L. C. Krishnasami, secretary of the North Arcot district DMK, directly participated one day with about two hundred volunteers and conducted a token picket.

### Image 2

Our friend Vinayagam is staying in Tiruttani and conducting pickets at the railway station controlled by the Central Government and at Provincial Government offices.

On 23 April 1953, DMK General Secretary Annadurai went to Tiruttani with Comrades M. Karunanidhi, Arangannal, and Rasagopal to observe this peaceful struggle. He met its volunteers and Comrade Vinayagam. Vinayagam explained its purpose and requested the DMK's assistance.

Following Potti Sriramulu's death, the Andhra people rose in fury, and Delhi issued its statement on the separation of Andhra Province.

As soon as the announcement came that “Andhra may become a separate province,” movements for linguistic provinces sprang up everywhere. The disturbances caused by Kannada people at Hubli last week were no ordinary events. Seeing the people's agitation, the Karnataka Congress Committee resolved that “a united Karnataka is immediately necessary,” and Karnataka Congress leader Nijalingappa hurried to Delhi.

At the very moment the Kannada speakers' demand for rights resounds, Bengal raises a lion's roar. The Bengal Congress Committee has resolved that a Bengal Province uniting all Bengali speakers must be formed.

### Image 3

Before Andhra's headache has been cured, these newly arisen cries have disturbed Pandit Nehru.

“Why do you torment me so?” he messaged Bengal. All-India Congress Committee Secretary Agarwal carried another message there: “Do not pass any resolution; this is Pandit Nehru's request.” The Bengal Congress paid no heed. It resolved that Bengal Province should be reorganized on grounds of administrative convenience and language, and sent the resolution to Delhi.

Karnataka! Bengal! Both travel the same road taken by Andhra. As Potti Sriramulu began a fast, one or two people in Karnataka and Bengal have begun fasting. Following their fasts, people in various places employ the “August method,” looting and damaging Central Government property. Delhi is therefore terrified; the Pandit is disturbed!

Had the demand for linguistic provinces been created by the Congress party, the Pandit could stop it with a few words. But it rises from the people's forum. Provincial Congress committees, seeing the people's passion, are themselves compelled to defy the Pandit's directive.

### Image 4

To create a Karnataka state, portions of Bombay Province, one or two parts of Hyderabad, some taluks of Andhra, and Mysore's Kannada region must be joined together.

Likewise, Bengal Province has areas to be incorporated from other provinces.

Linguistic regions hitherto governed by different provincial governments must unite to form new provinces.

This is a just issue, but it gives Delhi a great headache.

Justice requires that wherever speakers of a particular language live, they should be included in the government established for that language.

That is why Karnataka's people claim Bellary district, now included in Andhra. It is said that most people in Bellary speak Kannada.

### Image 5

To inquire whether this is right or wrong and decide the matter, Delhi has appointed Misra, a judge of the Hyderabad High Court.

But Chittoor has not received the same consideration!

Bellary is a disputed district. Misra is sent to examine the dispute.

Chittoor too is a disputed region. Yet no one is sent to investigate it. Whatever Delhi decrees becomes law!

It is against this “injustice” that the peaceful struggle proceeds in Tiruttani.

Its aim is not merely, “Do not join Tamil-speaking areas to Andhra!” Its cry is: “Do not act as you please. Give us justice too. Hold an inquiry by a commission or arbitrator, or conduct a public referendum!”

But the Tamil Nadu Congress Committee, which met and dispersed at Tranquebar, has reportedly resolved that even this peaceful struggle must not occur.

### Image 6

The Tamil Nadu Congress says the struggle must not take place now: “Let Andhra separate first; we can fight over the border afterwards. Why now?”

Former minister Bhaktavatsalam, who thundered, “I will not surrender it! Tiruttani belongs to the Tamils,” and who is a member of the Border Protection Committee, reportedly introduced this resolution.

Reports say Bhaktavatsalam is practising a regal gait in expectation of the “October surprise.” Before that “green light” appeared, he seems to have desired peaceful struggle and picketing. Now he condemns them and says they must not occur!

Though the Tamil Nadu Congress is so indifferent, the Andhra Congress Committee is arranging numerous activities everywhere to annex Tiruttani and similar places.

The Kamaraj Congress lacks their concern. We have heard it said that Comrade Kamaraj always lets go of the tuft and catches the tail. It appears to be true!

### Image 7

The Karnataka Congress prepares for battle over Bellary.

The Kamaraj Congress says there must be no struggle for Tiruttani.

The Bengal Congress says, “We do not care even if Nehru says otherwise!”

The Kamaraj Congress squares its shoulders and asks, “Is this Vinayagam to oppose Nehru?”

The more one thinks of the Kamaraj Congress, which hopes to buy a picture after losing both eyes, the stranger it seems!

Chief Minister Achariyar reportedly wrote to Comrade Vinayagam asking him to abandon the picket. Vinayagam replied: “This picket is not a struggle against the Provincial Government. It is a struggle against the Nehru Government. Let the Provincial Government explain the situation here to the Centre. We cannot stop until the Nehru Government heeds our just demand.”

He told us this in person.

The Tamils of Tiruttani struggle against the Nehru Government. The Tamil Nadu Congress stabs them in the back!

### Image 8

It urges them to abandon the peaceful struggle. Achariyar, who claims to fight for Tamil rights, writes a letter saying “No.” The Congress Committee too drafts a resolution. Yet these people supposedly fight for Tamil life!

Bhaktavatsalam, who until yesterday sounded the conch of battle, turns a somersault and drafts the resolution.

The reason is nothing else. Congress President Pandit Nehru sent Agarwal as an emissary to the Bengal Congress Committee. Here too, an emissary or letter must have arrived. Upon seeing it, the “somersault” episode began. The Tiruttani struggle suddenly appeared unnecessary!

Young people in Congress must consider the position of the Tamil Nadu Congress, which raises its hand in submission to Delhi. A cry of “Jai” is not the great thing; the future and a good life are. Today's nationalist leaders do not consider them. The Tranquebar resolution is the echo of that failure.

* * *

Pandit Nehru's agitation and confusion over the movement for linguistic provinces are reportedly immense.

One may ask: “Was that not an earlier Congress resolution?”

### Image 9

True! He himself argued for it. But today he is terrified to see the feeling that each region should separate into its own province growing stronger.

“One India,” “Mother India,” “Undivided India”: his great desire to rule the same “India” left behind by the British through such enchanting phrases is now collapsing, and that causes him anxiety.

Even when linguistic provinces separate, they will remain under Delhi's domination. Yet he is disturbed.

If separation grows, might not the concern to protect oneself also grow? Pandit Nehru surely knows where that could lead.

Not only Andhra, Bengal, and Karnataka, but Maharashtra too demands a separate province. Two days ago the Poona Municipal Council passed a resolution on the matter.

Seeing all this, the Pandit is disturbed at one moment and angry the next. Speaking on 28 April at Belgaum in Maharashtra, Pandit Nehru revealed his inner irritation: “If we keep dividing people as Andhra, Kannadiga, Bengali, and Maharashtrian...”

### Image 10

“...what becomes of the Indian? What becomes of the phrase ‘Indian nation’?” he asked in anguish. Seeing this development, the Delhi durbar now trembles.

Those who agitate for linguistic provinces, even when they are Congress members, strike at Delhi because Delhi has retained the authority to decide the matter.

If matters stand thus while the fruit is still tender, is it surprising that Delhi's throne fears the anxieties that may arise tomorrow when it ripens?

The Pandit's anxiety and confusion are the result of intoxication with “One India.”

That is why, to prevent such questions from growing stronger elsewhere, he tries to close the matter by “giving husk when beaten rice is requested.” Alas! For some time from the year 1800, the German philosopher Fichte likewise cried, “Europe is one nation!” He said certain mischief-makers were creating divisions in Europe and described all believers in Jesus as one country's people. But what is Europe's condition today? The lineage of Pandits who flatter us by saying, “Are we not children of Mother India?” must consider this.

### Image 11

An agitation for rights cannot be suppressed forever. It will grow stage by stage until the cry for complete liberation inevitably arises. Those who know political history can understand this.

Maulana Muhammad Ali, then a Congress leader, expressed this thought in *Comrade* on 4 January 1911: “India's problem is virtually an international problem.” He explained it again in his presidential address to the 1928 Kakinada Congress:

“Within the territory called India are many different classes. An effort is underway to paste them together and display them as one group. It will not succeed.

“India is not one country. The problem here resembles an international problem.”

After his time, we saw the truth of his words before our eyes. We therefore know well that the work of pasting things together under the name “India” cannot endure. That is why we support today's growing demand for linguistic provinces.

Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Andhra, and Karnataka should each obtain its own government and establish a federation based on unity and friendship.

### Image 12

We say this is possible.

Four quarter-annas make one anna. That example is enough.

That is why we desire decisions concerning each linguistic region to be made with justice and integrity.

We and the Andhra people must live tomorrow too. It is best to place our disputes openly between us and settle them. This is what the Tiruttani peaceful struggle declares.

For that reason, we too support the Tiruttani struggle.

Long live the heroes of the Tiruttani peaceful struggle! The Tamil Nadu Congress, which enters the field only to stab them in the back, will one day realize its error. Let the protesters therefore forgive it and continue their peaceful struggle.

On the very day the Congress passed its Tranquebar resolution, the DMK Tiruchi District Conference meeting at Lalgudi passed the following resolution concerning the Tiruttani struggle:

(a) In accordance with the resolution already adopted at our State Conference, this conference urges the Government of India to annex to Tamil Nadu the portions of Chittoor district extending as far as Tirupati, and requests the immediate appointment of a boundary commission or commissioner to investigate and decide the question of that district.

### Image 13

(b) This conference supports the Tamil peaceful struggle that has proceeded at Tiruttani for the past two weeks over the Chittoor question. It commends Comrade K. Vinayagam, M.L.A., who leads it, and greatly appreciates the cooperation of the DMK branches in Chittoor district.

(*News*, *Dravida Nadu*, 3 May 1953)

## Translator's Notes

`அறப்போர்` and `மறியல்` are rendered as “peaceful struggle” and “picket”; in this context they denote non-violent direct action rather than armed conflict.

`ஆகஸ்டு முறை` refers to the methods associated with the August 1942 movement. `அக்டோபர் அதிர்ச்சி` and `பச்சை விளக்கு` are retained as Anna's pointed contemporary political allusions.

The old-currency analogy `நான்கு காலணாக்களைக் கொண்ட ஓரணா` means that four quarter-annas form one anna; Anna uses it to explain a federation of four self-governing linguistic regions.

The scan prints the philosopher's name as `பிச்சட்`; the historical reference is to Johann Gottlieb Fichte. The translation identifies him as Fichte without changing the corrected Tamil source.
