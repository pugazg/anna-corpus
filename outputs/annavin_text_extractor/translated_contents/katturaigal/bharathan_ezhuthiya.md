## English Translation

## To Thiru V. Kalyanasundaranar, Editor of *Navasakthi*

Dear elder brother,

Do not become angry and wonder, "What is this? Do I have a younger brother without even knowing it?" This humble man is indeed your brother.

In your *Navasakthi* you have portrayed in great detail the "Fall of Subhas Bose episode" enacted at Calcutta. Every word in it is emotion embodied. As in your speech, a force arises from it.

"Murder! Murder! The murder of democracy!" you wrote of the Calcutta event. Yes, it was murder, a murder India can never forget. Autocratic fanatics murdered democracy. There is no denying it.

You say that conspiracies to overthrow the president had proceeded from Tripuri onward. Entirely true. The conspiracy had begun even earlier.

Was this not the second time Subhas Babu was elected, this very year? From the moment he was first made Congress president the previous year, the Patel group began conspiring to overthrow him.

Tell me yourself: when Pandit Jawaharlal was president, reports appeared in his name about his duties, his wit, his courage, valour and majesty. There were pictures from the scene of his meals to the scene of his boarding an aeroplane, greetings, receptions, whirlwind tours and publicity. Did any such thing take place for Subhas Bose? Think about it. It did not.

Why? The Patel group hesitated to bring Subhas Bose into closer contact with the people. It called Subhas Babu president in name while directing its worship and offerings elsewhere.

The conspirators in Congress joined together and finally overthrew Bose at Calcutta. As you say, a great struggle may now arise within Congress. Subhas has raised the flag and let it fly.

In these circumstances, however, you say Gandhiji should leave politics and enter the field of spiritual virtue. Why? How can that happen? That is my question.

The reason you offer is strange. You ask why Gandhi Adigal should roll in the mud of faction that has arisen in Congress and say he should withdraw. But was not Gandhiji himself the cause of that mud?

The moment Subhas Babu won, it was Gandhiji who tied up his loincloth and began the battle. He declared, "Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat," and said that he did not like Subhas Babu becoming president for a second term. From that day the trouble increased and the Bengali hero was brought down.

Why then do you ask Gandhiji to withdraw? It is natural that he should be caught in the destructive whirlwind he himself produced.

Even if, according to your view, he ought to withdraw, how can he? Was not today's Working Committee formed by his own guards? Who is Babu Rajendra, the treasure of Bihar? Is not President Rajan Babu Gandhiji's hand-reared child?

How, then, can Gandhiji leave Congress? He will remain commander of the Working Committee. Under the leadership of the Bengali hero, the radicals will oppose the old tea-shop worthies. That is what can happen.

Do you not know it? It is precisely because you know it very well that you ask Gandhi Adigal to renounce politics.

If Gandhiji remains in politics, he will remain commander of the Working Committee and fight to crush Subhas's revolution. Your devotion is to Gandhi Adigal, but your analysis follows the path of Subhas Babu.

If you enter the battle, you cannot support Patel. The Patel group murdered democracy. You must oppose that wickedness. To oppose it, you must stand beside the Bengali hero. When you stand beside him, you will see Gandhiji opposite you.

Alas, what a sight! Must I oppose my Gandhi Adigal, the avatar, India's peerless yogi? The heart trembles merely to think of it, and the tongue may begin to falter. To prevent that trouble, you ask Gandhiji to leave politics.

Good! Good! Your devotion to Gandhiji is very good. But there is something better still: what Krishna told Arjuna. "Do not fail to perform your duty."

What is your duty? It surges through your own editorial: to uphold democracy and support Babu Subhas Chandra Bose.

Let us see, elder brother, whether from now on you come forward to perform that duty. If you set out for battle, do not wonder whether you possess an army or doubt whether I, your younger brother, will accompany you. If you enter this struggle to perform your duty and defend democracy, you will find me already upon that road, bearing scars upon my chest.

Will you come to that battle?

Your affectionate younger brother,  
Bharathan

## To Comrade Nadimuthu Pillai

Bravo! Pattukkottai elder brother, I hear that you spoke at Nannilam. It even appeared in the newspapers, and I hear you issued a "challenge" too.

Good heavens, brother, how did you set out with such force after taking up Achariyar's brief? Has that old dispute been resolved? They reportedly told you to go outside and you went; it appears that as soon as they called you back inside, you returned.

Like the brother-in-law who returns fragrant with flowers after having gone away, you departed angry with Congress and, upon returning, seem to have begun speaking with extra spice.

I hear you twirled your moustache and said: "What can be done except impose the sales tax? The government has expenses. Can some other tax be imposed? If someone points one out, I shall tell the ministers and, if necessary, have the sales tax stopped."

A challenge indeed! But if you speak, will Achariyar listen? Has such "affection" now arisen between you?

Let that be. Suppose your farmhand spoke boldly: "My wage is insufficient and there is no other way, so I shall take six kalams from the harvest." What would you say?

If he said, "Master, you tell me whether there is another way to earn. Show me one and I shall follow it," would you answer, "Yes, poor fellow. Why take only six kalams? Take seven"?

Or would you say, "What, Kuppa! Do you come and ask me to find you some other 'way'? Remain under the old arrangement if you wish; otherwise put your pots and pans on your head and get out"? Think it over calmly.

I do not even answer your "challenge" in this fashion. Someone has written in the *Madras Mail*, listing taxes that could replace the sales tax. Pick it up and read it. Then show it to Achariyar and try to have the sales tax stopped.

If you do stop it, I shall call you the Pattukkottai lion, undiminished in courage, valour, dignity and bearing, even if no one else does.

The moment you asked what other tax there was, that gentleman listed them one after another: here they are. Will you read the *Madras Mail*? Why impose that trouble upon you amid all your work? I shall tell you what he said.

Nor did he invent them from his imagination. He cites provisions in the Government of India Act and classifies them category by category:

- A tax on officials earning one or two thousand rupees a month.
- A tax on foreign merchants.
- A tax on leading lawyers and doctors.
- Income tax on agriculturalists possessing mittas and similar estates.
- A tax on luxury goods used by the wealthy.
- A tax on large farms.

I shall add one too: a tax on large hotels!

Answer, Pillai! Will you prevent the sales tax from entering? Can you do it? Come, stir yourself a little. Tell Achariyar and see. Only then will your speech possess meaning.

Your younger brother,  
Bharathan

*Viduthalai*, 10 May 1939
