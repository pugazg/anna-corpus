## English Translation

The darkness that had surrounded the world of art in Tamil Nadu has departed. In this small book entitled *The Door Opened!*, our friend Pitchamurthi announces this beautifully.

Krishnan and Bhagavathar, those jewels of art, created novelty and revolution in an artistic world that had lain neglected and had been regarded by many merely as a means of entertainment. They drew the people's attention to that field and succeeded, thereby winning a lofty and noble place in Tamil history.

There is no house or small village where their names are unknown, and no person who has not rejoiced and benefited through their music and acting. Various circumstances brought adversity upon these two men who had won an honoured place in the people's forum, and they had to languish in prison for more than two years.

The imprisonment was not theirs alone. It was imprisonment for every lover of art in the Tamil land. Tamils waited in anguish, asking when the hardship suffered by men who had stood at the summit of fame would end, when they would again move through the world of art and delight the Tamil people.

Then came the news: "The door opened!" That day was a festival day for all Tamils.

In this volume, the distinction of these two sacred figures has been made into a portrait in words. It also celebrates the life history of the noble artists who brought joy into Tamil hearts. This book possesses the dignity to give happiness to every lover of art.

I rejoice inwardly at the affection of the friend who, through this work, serves art and offers felicitations to artists. If art is to advance and we are to prosper through it, we must learn to praise and honour the artists of our land.

We must not hold the mistaken idea that they are a separate kind of people, mere objects of spectacle. The wholesome understanding must spread that they are friends working through art for us and for the country.

In Western countries, people engaged in the arts are treated with dignity and the government too shows concern for them. Here, the prevailing attitude has gone so far as to degrade them. Some believed that this dangerous course was merely a means of destroying the fame of one or two artists. But did its consequences stop there? Could they? No. Interest in art itself began to diminish, and people dared to demean it.

This condition was created precisely when a sound relationship had begun to arise between artists and the general public. Had it continued to grow, interest in art itself would have been destroyed within a few years.

But the frenzy has subsided. Judged not guilty, the artists have emerged. Today they come in a procession of victory, and Tamil Nadu lovingly embraces her two precious sons, kisses them upon the crown of their heads and rejoices.

At this moment when delight overflows, the friend who prepared this book about the artists has done something suited to the time. This is a beginning, not an end.

The relationship between the public and artists must grow ever stronger. For that to happen, the attitude that issues a prohibition order declaring their field to be one thing and everyone else's another must change.

A friend writes: "Actor-comrade, keep acting. Do not enter other affairs. Do not join parties. Do not adopt principles. Move only in the worlds of cinema, song and drama!"

Why? Does this writer think an actor is a doll that dances and sings according to another's movement? It is wrong to imagine that all the actor's time outside acting belongs only to food, sleep and pleasure. Nor is that a path or means for enriching either art or Tamil Nadu.

Whether it is the arts or any other field, if prosperity is to grow within it, its practitioners must understand the people's mind and work accordingly. Only then will their activity possess life.

For this, artists must maintain contact with the public and with the principles and parties among them. Even singing birds must see the net cast at them. They must know the path of escape from the hawk circling overhead. There is no benefit in being a caged parrot.

The actor must therefore become a full human being; it is not enough to remain merely a doll moved by a mechanism. I mention this only to say that the path shown to them by that friend is wrong.

At the outset, fame and wealth may become the aim of anyone, artists included. But once they are obtained, is there such a thing as satisfaction? Thirst does not subside.

One possesses fame and wealth, but what is their use? Our service has given us silk garments, but what has it given the country? This question will rise and rage within the mind. Only then will an actor resolve to spend his talent and light for the progress of the general public and the reform of society.

The king of comedy has reached that stage. Yet a friend orders him and others like him, "Remain only an actor," thus showing them the wrong road.

Actors! Do not merely continue acting. Serve the country as well, along the path and according to the principles that seem right to you. That is what I wished to say.

The two jewels of art have come out through the opened door. Not only the door, but their minds too stand open. Hereafter they will perform service to the country alongside their service to art.

May their fame grow; may the country advance through them. I welcome this little book that portrays their excellent character and commend the friend who prepared it.

May art flourish! Long live the artists!

Affectionately,

C. N. Annadurai

## Publisher's Acknowledgement

We are delighted to offer this book to the world of art as the second publication of *Arivuchudar*.

As scholar Anna says, we dedicate this compilation to you from our firm belief that the country can prosper through artists.

Our friend Pitchamurthi, who accepted the task of writing and arranged it beautifully, is a newcomer to the literary world. His effort blossoms with the scholar's blessing. The Tamil Nadu of the future wishes to see countless more works of thought from him. Though asked at short notice, he did not lose heart; he wrote, helped the work take form, and thereby earned our enduring gratitude.

Our thanks also go to C. N. Annadurai, M.A., who supplied the foreword and encouraged the work, and to all those who helped this publication appear attractively.

The OCR is severely degraded throughout this old typeset source. Context establishes the celebrated artists as N. S. Krishnan and M. K. Thyagaraja Bhagavathar, though the damaged line prints forms resembling “சோழர்கள் கிருஷ்ணன்--பர்கவ”. The prose refers to their release after acquittal in the Lakshmikanthan case. The source heading appears as “அண்ணாவின் ஆசி?”; the publication context supports “அண்ணாவின் ஆசி,” while the Tamil is retained verbatim. The acknowledgement's initials before Roy's title are damaged; the historically recognisable author M. N. Roy is used cautiously in English.
