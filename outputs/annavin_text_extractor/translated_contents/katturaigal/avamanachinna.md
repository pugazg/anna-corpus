## English Translation

The Supreme Court has cited the Constitution of India and ruled that communal representation is invalid.

Law is the cage; social justice is imprisoned within it. Like a scorpion stinging a man who went in search of honey, this event has fallen as a thunderbolt across the path of progress of non-Brahmins, who had begun to rebuild their broken lives through social justice.

The bewildered and struggling Dravidian community looks sorrowfully towards its leaders. Its blood boils and its thoughts rise in revolt. Through eyes whose light has dimmed and which now brim with tears, and in voices that have lost their strength and sound with pain, it asks: “Has our backbone been broken? Have the deceivers torn out our very heart? Why has this catastrophe fallen upon us?”

It is an agonising sight. The people of this land, heirs to a living tradition, the noble community that laboured until its body was worn away to improve both field and life, is being rendered mute.

It is a treacherous deed that makes the heart boil. Yet what can be done? They have fashioned a cage called law and thrust social justice inside it.

Merely hearing of it makes every memory breathe fire. Thinking of it makes speed, courage and pride rise. Those who practise the craft of the jackal planned and inflicted this injury. The very man who helped frame the law went to tell the court what “the law” said in the communal-representation case.

Today everyone in the country knows how this strange disaster occurred and why.

When the faction that “twists rope upon a sleeping man's thigh” first dared to carry a dispute of this kind into court, many of us suspected a conspiracy designed through legal arrangements to strike down social justice.

That suspicion has not merely proved true. It has now opened the eyes even of the rulers who condemned us.

As soon as social justice was struck down in the Madras High Court through a legal question, we all cried out. Across the country, we assembled the people's condemnation beneath the slogans: “We demand social justice!” and “Amend the Constitution of India!”

Hearing the country's voice and our slogans, the rulers asked us: “Do you imagine that we alone have no concern? Are we people who do not understand the value of the Communal Government Order? We have appealed to the Supreme Court and are awaiting its decision.”

Their party emissaries too spoke in many places with anger, sympathy, emotional force and pain:

“Do you think we will simply abandon it? Are we so helpless that we would lose the Communal G.O.? We too are as concerned as the Dravidian movement.”

The question of social justice is a fundamental question in constructing social life. It cannot be said to belong only to one party or to be unacceptable to another. The Justice Party performed the great work of creating and protecting this worthy arrangement for social justice. Yet even the opposition Congress came to recognise its necessity. That is why Congressmen too were startled by the Madras High Court judgement and, like us, spoke from platforms of their concern.

We have always welcomed cooperation for the development of our society, wherever it came from. We did not fail to appreciate their emotional response merely because they belonged to another party. We desired that it should bear fruit and that they should work to make it do so.

“We have gone to the Supreme Court. If a favourable judgement is not obtained there either...”

“It has not been obtained. What will you do?”

“What will we do? We will cause the Constitution of India itself to be amended.”

Such were the questions and answers we received from them.

Now that the Supreme Court has not delivered a judgement favourable to us, ministers and members of the Legislative Assembly have met and reached a decision in accordance with those assurances.

We understand that the Congress Legislature Party has decided to introduce and pass in the Assembly a resolution calling for amendment of the Constitution. We also hear that this resolution will be introduced on behalf of the Madras government.

All well-wishers must be pleased to see that Congress members have acquired the courage to fight for social justice and the good intention to demonstrate in action what they proclaimed from the platforms.

Yet *Dinasari* has published a report explaining why members at the Legislature Party meeting insisted that the Constitution should be amended:

> With the general elections approaching, the Supreme Court judgement has created an embarrassing position in the State. It has given other parties an opportunity to use it as a pretext for propaganda against the Congress. Therefore the Government of India should be requested to introduce an amendment so that the Communal G.O. is not affected.

This is reportedly what was discussed at the meeting.

Those who hear this report will naturally ask, “Is even this being done only for the election?” They may also ask, “Whenever they do anything, is it always for electoral purposes? Do they never regard it as service they owe the people?”

What can the poor fellows do? They have poured out what lies in their hearts. They are the ones eating the sugarcane of office; they fear losing it and worry that this issue may become the cause.

Whether fear or weakness is responsible, the report makes one thing clear: they have understood the support and respect the Communal G.O. enjoys in the people's forum.

That is why we are pleased. These rulers ordinarily show no respect or regard for public opinion and do not even know how to do so. Yet on this vital question they have not slept and drooled; they have come forward courageously to perform certain acts.

Whatever hidden purpose may lie within the Congress Legislature Party's decision, we must praise the Congress for not running away from it.

The government has begun to treat the Communal G.O. as its own problem and take action. This clearly shows that, even while calling us communal haters and sectarians and casting arrows of grief upon us, they have understood the suffering of a vast community and its cause.

There is more. *Dinasari* provides another report:

> Some circles say that if the resolution is not passed, the ministry will not hesitate to resign.

“Some circles” are said to make this claim. *Dinasari* does not reveal which circles or who belongs to them. Whether it lacked the courage to say so or whether the information was not to be disclosed, we do not know.

Nevertheless, ministers who ordinarily think “office is everything” are showing concern in this life-and-death question. That is something to be welcomed.

We are not unaware of the difficulties facing this government in adopting a resolution to amend the Constitution of India and fighting accordingly to secure the right of social justice for the people.

Whether a resolution introduced in the Indian Parliament would receive support and welcome is a fair question.

But if the provincial government insists upon the amendment and the Central government itself introduces it, there should be no great difficulty in passing it. If the Central government itself amends this provision and introduces a new rule, the members will, as usual, not withhold their support.

Even so, those who have watched four years of this government's rule cannot avoid wondering whether the Madras government possesses the determination to carry the matter past the “images” seated upon the Central government's pedestal.

The question of social justice concerns a vast community that has lost its livelihood and lies in torment.

Whether victory can be achieved through stormy speed or loud clamour alone is a matter for reflection.

Our entire desire is that the “lifeline” bringing welfare to our community should not be snatched away.

Therefore, in goodwill, we trust that the Madras government will pursue the necessary effort with courage.

Victory can be obtained only if the government acts. Moreover, only if it presses the Central government and has the legal amendment introduced through it will “nothing” obstruct passage in the Indian Parliament.

If some private member brings an amendment instead, it will be very easy for the deceivers who possess the “images” to defeat it.

We therefore desire and expect the Madras government to treat social justice as its own question of life and death.

Our unceasing desire is that the Communal G.O. must not be taken from us.

The responsibility and the means to secure it lie with the government.

Let “Madras,” therefore, fight courageously to end the anguished cry of the poor.

We trust that these rulers understand the speed of the people's hearts. Let them take courage as their companion and strive for victory in this undertaking. If they labour sincerely, victory is certain in one way or another.

We shall wait and see whether they walk that path or become indelible symbols of shame.

“Ministerial offices are specks of dust before the people's storm.” We remind them of this and show them the path of duty. A mighty flood is surging against them.

Embrace the danger; do not become symbols of shame.

*Dravida Nadu*, 8 April 1951.
