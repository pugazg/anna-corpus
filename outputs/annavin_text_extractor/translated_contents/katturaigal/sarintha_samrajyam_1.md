## English Translation

A small island, yet its dominion had spread across every part of the world. It possessed abundant hunting grounds throughout the globe and therefore maintained military strength, a naval establishment, and air power on a sufficient scale. It did not rule the world merely for hollow prestige. Economic exploitation was the foundation of its power.

It learned the needs of the countries held in subjection, obtained their raw materials cheaply, transformed them into finished goods, and sent them back to those same countries. The truth concealed within its dominion was that this process supplied immense wealth to the rich of its own country and a first-rate life to its workers.

For a century, that little island carried out this work of exploitation more successfully than any empire previously known to history. The proceeds prevented the domestic suffering and eruptions caused by economic disorder in countries with similar forms of life—and the terrible experiences they brought upon their peoples—from appearing in Britain. Conditions permitted noteworthy progress in every field.

Democratic principles and the system of government connected with them have become inseparably mingled with the people’s blood. The crowned monarch is merely an ornamental figure without power. Yet the people’s affection, esteem, and respect for the monarch do not contradict democracy. Long habit has implanted an unshakable belief in their minds that someone wearing a crown, though powerless, must remain at least the nominal head of state.

It would be impossible there for either a crowned tyrant or an uncrowned brute to seize the seat of government. The people’s consciousness of their rights would not permit it. To that extent, democracy flourishes there with a distinction of its own.

Although Britain emerged victorious from the war, it suffered economic decline. Its overseas capital began gradually to melt away. A condition arose in which it had to borrow from others. The grip of imperialism too was loosened. Whether it wished to or not, Britain could no longer follow its former course. It had to adopt a new course and actions suited to the changed situation. Once it understood, without hesitation, that the old method would no longer yield results, it began to embrace a new one.

Britain has voluntarily granted political freedom even to countries held in its grip where liberation movements had not yet developed sufficiently. By creating a new relationship with them, it is today engaged in the constructive task of repairing its weakened economy.

Having clearly understood the great truth that capitalism can no longer build the future, Britain is moving along the path of social justice. One by one it removes the barriers obstructing that path, without violently contradicting the habits of its people, and advances toward its chosen ideal. Its success is certain.

After the war, many prophesied that Britain would never again be able to raise its head. All those predictions have proved false. Production continues to increase in every field. British motorcars are novel enough in appearance and construction to be willingly purchased even in the land of the dollar. Britain is approaching the day when it can export every kind of machine required for industrial development.

“Export” has become a word that gives life there. Groundnuts are being produced on a suitable scale in Africa not only to meet the needs of Britain’s people but also to enable the sale abroad of many manufactured products. The British people show even greater eagerness to repair their economy than they displayed in making sacrifices during the war. Britain was almost alone in having little black-market activity. That is a fitting example of the people’s sense of responsibility. They understand both rights and duties.

The leaders who guide the people have broken, one after another, obsolete treaty provisions unsuited to the times. The democratic powers’ hatred of the Soviet system blinded them so completely that they could not see the road to war hidden in Hitler’s course. War erupted. The early display of prowess did not endure to the end. Blitzkrieg appeared and vanished like lightning. Defeat produced a result from which Germany cannot raise its head for a long time.

What the German people gained from Hitler’s system was death then and death now. Their stomachs were not filled then, and no means of filling them exists now. He promised them life but sacrificed their youth at the battlefront. Instead of using productive machinery to increase food and clothing, he placed gleaming weapons in the hands of hungry young men and sent them to war.

To satisfy one man’s passions, he planned that a single idea—one suited to his war objective—must prevail throughout tens of thousands of institutions in art, religion, and every other sphere, and enforced it at gunpoint. He mercilessly destroyed opponents and established that no other party had a place in his scheme. He intoxicated the people with the claim that national honour must be defended, declared that valuing human life above it was treason, and led them onto the battlefield. He disappeared after bringing them collapse instead of victory.

It cannot be said that people accustomed to fighting abroad would happily support the foreign victors who now create conflict among themselves within Germany and increase German suffering. Having lost the power to resist, the German people see their country plundered. Anger may fill their hearts and grief cover their faces, yet they cannot openly express their honest views. With tears in their eyes, they stand waiting for decisions made by people devoid of concern.

Instead of leaving to the German people the responsibility of choosing a good opportunity to remove the distress that has befallen them, and instead of approaching the problem humanely, both America and Russia have selected Berlin as the arena in which to secure victory for their own ideals and demonstrate their diplomatic methods to the world.

If negotiation produces no result, the next stage will be a test of strength. If another war breaks out, it will mean that the end has drawn near for all the civilization humanity has so painstakingly developed.

All this is the fruit of the seed Hitler sowed. The German people still suffer from the cruelty of the fire he left behind.
