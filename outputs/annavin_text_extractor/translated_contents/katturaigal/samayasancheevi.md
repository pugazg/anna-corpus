## English Translation

*(The trouble caused by political opportunists exists everywhere and in every country. It exists in Punjab too. Such opportunists caused the crisis in the Punjab ministry. Explaining this, a writer named "Sayada" has written in the newspaper *Dawn*. We give its substance below.)*

For some time, a dispute had existed in Punjab between the League and the Unionist Party. Mr. Jinnah wished to investigate it personally. For that purpose, he went to Lahore last March and met and spoke with people holding various opinions. In the end, he correctly concluded that Sir Chhotu Ram, using a handful of his own men, was deceiving and defrauding innocent Muslims.

When Mr. Jinnah saw that Sir Chhotu Ram, through his nine Hindu Jat men, was rendering the Assembly's eighty-eight Muslim members powerless, he was genuinely enraged. He decided that such deception could no longer be permitted and that it must be brought to an end.

People outside Punjab may regard it as something impressive that the Unionist Party holds the ministry there. But if they knew the true condition, they would exclaim, "Is this what is called the Unionist Party?" In reality, the party called Unionist consists of eighty Muslims and Sir Chhotu Ram's nine Hindu Jat members.

Neither Sardar Baldev Singh nor Sir Manohar Lal belongs to that party. Though they offered no support, the party did not include them either. It never attempted, according to Unionist principles, to unite non-Muslim members and communities. They stood apart from this fraud. Poor Muslims alone were trapped within it.

Mr. Jinnah conducted discussions for weeks with Premier Malik Khizar Hayat Khan. Finally, at the close of the Provincial Muslim League Conference in Sialkot, the Quaid-e-Azam presented his final proposal to Malik Khizar Hayat Khan.

It was simply that he should maintain a connection only with the Muslim League, not with the Unionist Party or any other political party; that the name "Unionist" should be changed; and that what was called the coalition ministry should be called the Muslim League coalition ministry.

Anyone can easily understand how a person who refuses to accept these ordinary proposals can be a genuine Leaguer. Even so, Malik Sir Khizar Hayat Khan listened to the advice of his non-Muslim colleagues, rejected the proposal, and even dared to issue a statement. This must be called a misfortune for Muslims.

He was leader of the Muslim League Party in the Punjab Assembly, and it was his party members who raised him to that position. Even so, he did not convene his party or ask its opinion about what should be done with the Quaid-e-Azam's proposal.

Following either his own strategy or some concealed prompting, he issued on April 28 a statement wholly incompatible with reason and truth. Does not that statement reveal his true form? To this day, forgetting that he is the leader of a communal organisation, he says that communal organisations should not exist.

He regards the Quaid-e-Azam's proposals as "outside interference." He seems to have forgotten that, under the rules of the very party to which he belongs, he is obliged to follow all-India discipline and act according to the Muslim League's decisions.

He says that he must not cast to the winds the pledge he gave his non-Muslim colleagues. According to his own statement, that pledge was given to them, through him, by the Quaid-e-Azam and the Muslim League. Even if it were accepted that the pledge was not honoured, we do not understand how Malik Khizar Hayat Khan himself would thereby be harmed.

He sheds false tears, alleging that the Quaid-e-Azam exercises dictatorial authority.

It has become doubtful whether he knows what dictatorship means before speaking in this way.

Does he know how the dictator Hitler spoke when he met Austria's Dr. Schuschnigg and the leader of Czechoslovakia? It is said that the shock those two men suffered then has still not subsided. That is a true dictatorial method. The method we follow is not dictatorship.

Did the Quaid-e-Azam not meet Malik Khizar Hayat Khan and speak with him before presenting his proposals? Even after Malik issued his statement, the Quaid-e-Azam did not attack him directly but left the matter to the Muslim League's decision. Though he possessed full authority to exercise power in such circumstances, he did not use it.

Malik Sahib, however, issued a call to the Muslim community without even consulting his party members. This is the same Malik Sahib who speaks of democracy. What did he do when the Governor removed one member of the ministry? Did he at least submit his resignation? Did he regard the Governor's intervention as outside interference?

4-6-1944
