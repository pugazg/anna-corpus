“என் காலத்தில் ஏற்பட்டுவிடுமா? என் கண்ணால்
பார்த்து விடுவேனா? அதற்குள், அமைந்துவிடுமா, கல்லூரி -
சொல்லுங்களேன் ஒரு வார்த்தைதுணிவாகச் சொல்லுங்களேன்!"

“கட்டடம் அமைக்க மட்டும் ஆயத்தமாகி விடட்டும்.
நான் அங்கேயே போய்விடுகிறேன், என் மனைவியோடு! சிறு
குடிசை போதும் - என் ஜீவியத்தை அங்கேயே கழித்து
விடுகிறேன்!"

கொஞ்சங்கூட ஓய்ந்ததில்லை - எப்போதும் எந்நேரமும்
சதா சிந்தனை! இன்று இத்தனை ரூபாய் வசூல் - நாளைக்கு
எங்கெங்கு செல்லவேண்டும் என்று சொல்லித் திருப்தி
படுவதிலே ஒரு ஆனந்தம். அல்லும் பகலும் அலைந்தார்,
வெறிபிடித்தவர் போல அலைந்துகொண்டே இருந்தார்.

கல்லூரி ஒன்றைக் காஞ்சியில் உருவாக்க வேண்டும் என்பதற்

அடடா! தள்ளாத வயது. வீட்டிலே கிடக்க வேண்டியகாலம்.

ஒரு அறிவாலயத்தை அமைப்பதில்!' என்ற எண்ணத்தால்!

## English Translation

"Will it come into being during my lifetime? Will I see it with my own eyes? Will the college be established before then? Tell me—say one word with confidence!"

"Let everything be ready just to put up the building. I shall move there with my wife. A small hut will suffice; I shall spend the rest of my life there."

He never rested even a little. At every hour, there was constant thought. He found joy in reporting with satisfaction how many rupees had been collected that day and where they must go the next. He wandered day and night, roaming as though possessed. Having given the fifty thousand rupees that constituted his own property, he laboured to create a college in Kanchipuram.

"If we do not collect one and a half lakhs within this month, the longing alone will be enough to make me die suddenly!" he would say aloud. Seeing him brought a feeling of elation, immediately followed by sympathy: "Alas! He is of an age when he should no longer exert himself and ought to remain at home. Yet he wanders like this. What a longing he has to establish a temple of learning in this town!"

He was a rare jewel. The name "Kandasamy Mudaliar" was being engraved in the hearts of the people of Kanchipuram.

Such a philanthropist has suddenly gone. Time has taken him from us. Unexpectedly, at 12:15 in the night on 9 March 1950, he died of a heart attack.

At about nine that night, he came to the office, discussed whom they should meet the following day, and left saying, "I shall come in the morning."

Morning dawned, but he did not come. Only the heartbreaking news of bereavement arrived.

Among the wealthy, it is impossible to find people like Kandasamy. It is difficult to find "gentlemen" willing to give money for a college, because there are more "maharajas" devoted to ritual ablutions at the Kali temple and to the sacred work of restoring another eye to the "one-eyed" Kamakshi.

The fifty thousand he gave for the college was the sum he had accumulated and saved by avoiding empty festivals and expenditure on spectacles.

He came forward of his own accord and gave that entire amount to establish a college in Kanchipuram. Nor did he stop with giving: he laboured day and night because he wished to see the college established with his own eyes. He climbed steps up and down and worked as though meeting many people and gaining their cooperation were his unceasing occupation.

A great man rarely seen, gold that could not be found even by searching, has died. This is unexpected and sorrowful news that shakes Kanchipuram.

A human being is born crying. When he dies, it is not he who cries; the deeds he accomplished stand around him.

Kandasamy has died, but his fame is united with his name. It has been engraved in the hearts of the people of Kanchipuram.

His departure is a great loss. Fulfilling his wish and doing what is necessary to establish a college in Kanchipuram would be the respect shown by its people to that departed man of action.

The work he began on the temple of learning must be completed—not so that his soul may attain peace, but for the satisfaction of having completed the undertaking begun by a great man who came forward with concern for the public good and of having established a college.

Kandasamy's unexpected death is an irreparable loss to the college working committee and to his other friends.

We convey our deepest condolences to Kandasamy's wife, who has lost her husband. With grief mingled in our condolences, we also assure the college committee that, as always, our service will be available to complete the work on the temple of learning undertaken by the departed philanthropist and to establish a college for the people of Kanchipuram.

Long live Kandasamy's fame!  
May the work he left behind grow!

(*Dravida Nadu*, 12 March 1950)

## Translator's Notes

The obituary contrasts educational philanthropy with expenditure on temple ritual in the source's own rationalist polemical voice. English preserves that argument as historical source rhetoric. OCR "பணியை மூடித்து" is read contextually as "பணியை முடித்து," complete the work.
