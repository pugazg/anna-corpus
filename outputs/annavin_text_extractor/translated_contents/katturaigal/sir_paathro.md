## English Translation

We are saddened to learn that Sir A. P. Patro passed away at his residence in Royapettah, Madras, on 26 May 1946. He was seventy-one. He was a rare and distinguished leader who laboured for the advancement of non-Brahmins. He was appointed a minister in the Panagal ministry in 1921 and administered the Education and Public Works departments until 1927.

It was during his ministerial tenure that amendments were made to the Madras University Act and the Andhra University Act. He was one of the leaders of the Justice Party. He presided over the Justice Party conference held in Madurai in 1926. In 1931 he was elected all-India leader of the non-Brahmin movement.

He presided over the All-Parties Conference convened in Delhi in 1930 to resolve the communal question. In 1931 he went to the League of Nations in Geneva as India’s representative. He also participated in the Round Table Conference of 1931-32 and in the meeting of the Indian Reform Parliamentary Committee convened in 1933. From 1937 onward he served as a member of the Council of State.

He laboured for the welfare of non-Brahmins generally and, in particular, for oppressed and suppressed people. He was among those who made the world aware of the pitiable condition of non-Brahmins. He carefully studied the grievances of cultivators; the details are evident from his book *Village Economy*. Sir Patro’s departure from our midst is deeply regrettable.
