## English Translation

## 16. We obtained votes saying Dravida Nadu should be independent. Now that the plan is gone, may we remain in the Assembly? They tell us to resign. — continued

Do you remember what these people said then? From the Honourable Kamaraj down to mere purveyors of spicy talk, they said that people had voted for all manner of reasons. They said all Congress's opponents had combined and obtained votes; that the Swatantra Party and the Muslim League had voted for us; and that the votes received by the DMK therefore did not all support separation. You remember that, do you not?

Now these very people say the votes were obtained for separation and that, after abandoning separation, we must therefore leave the Assembly. Is there justice in such talk? It displays only agility of tongue. They speak one way today and another tomorrow, changing their speech from hour to hour. Neither honesty nor fairness is visible in it.

Leave that aside. The manifesto issued by the DMK during the election still exists. Take it out and examine it. Did we ask for votes only for separation? Did our manifesto say that our sole purpose in entering the Assembly was separation? Shall I read it aloud?

The “compact” formed between the autocratic Congress party and wealthy men who grow fat by exploiting the poor will inflict the misery of enslavement upon the people. That compact must be broken; only then will the lives of working people rise and the rights of the people endure. Pledging to labour for this purpose, and having already demonstrated through its work that it possesses the ability to do so, the DMK asks for the goodwill and support of the public so that it may continue pressing the demands and programmes it has advanced until now and may implement many new programmes and methods.

### Socialist Programme

The DMK pledges to labour so that democracy may flourish and socialism may triumph.

It will continue its work of making the world understand that the present political structure serves to enslave Dravida Nadu. At the same time, it has resolved to break the autocracy of Congress, which has failed to carry out even the progressive measures possible within this very structure, and to establish socialism.

Whatever a socialist philosophy in which both private and public sectors coexist may be, Congress keeps declaring, “That is our practical socialism.” The DMK firmly believes that this claim and the party's Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 will not lead the country along the socialist path. It will therefore create wealth through industrial development while eliminating the evil of that wealth accumulating in a few hands.

It will make basic necessities—food, clothing, shelter, education, medical care and rest—available to all people, and adopt a programme that prevents the industries supplying these necessities from becoming hunting grounds for private profit.

Statistics may show that the country's total income is increasing. Viewed at the individual level, however, the average income of ordinary people is falling, while under Congress rule the rich grow richer and the poor poorer. The DMK will labour to reduce these disparities in income.

### Foreign Loans and Capital

Because the uncontrolled entry of foreign loans and capital disrupts the country's economy, the Kazhagam will insist that financial assistance be obtained in moderation and employed in projects capable of yielding appropriate benefit.

### Tax Burden

The proportion of direct taxes imposed on those with high incomes and upon sources capable of bearing taxation is declining, while the proportion of indirect taxes falling upon the general public rises each year. Condemning this, the DMK will strive to reduce the burden borne by the poor and by those conducting small businesses.

### Industrial Development Plans

The South now serves as a commercial market for the industries multiplying in the North. The Kazhagam will work to change this condition by surveying the South's mineral resources and natural advantages and initiating plans to industrialise it.

It will select a suitable port in Tamil Nadu and arrange for it to become a free port.

It will accelerate the Sethusamudram project and the Tuticorin harbour project, and find means to build highways that support them.

It will hasten the Cauvery-basin oil project and endeavour to establish an oil refinery there.

It will accelerate the Neyveli mining project and arrange for it to become the property of the state government.

It will act to establish the Salem steel plant.

It will undertake the establishment of an atomic reactor.

It will initiate a project to obtain water from the rivers of neighbouring states.

It will begin a programme to use solar heat scientifically for industry, as in Israel.

It will undertake a scheme to convert seawater into drinking water by the scientific method recently discovered in America.

It will make arrangements for coastal trade.

It will connect the Buckingham Canal with the Vedaranyam Canal and make the combined waterway navigable by boats.

It will develop fishing through modern methods and increase revenue from it.

It will make the meat industry profitable.

It will establish a bone-manure factory and a chemical-fertiliser factory.

It will guide poultry farming towards operation as an industry.

It will undertake the manufacture of synthetic rubber from coconut fibre.

It will conduct research into cashew and turn its oil and shell into industrial products.

It will establish orchards and export processed fruit abroad.

It will establish paper mills where sugarcane bagasse and bamboo are available.

It will establish vegetable-oil factories.

It will manufacture products from minerals such as magnesite, manganese, gypsum, aluminium and mica.

It will establish facilities for building motor vehicles.

It will start factories producing medicines, chemicals and dye powders.

It will change the proportions of leather exports and increase the quantity exported as finished products.

It will find ways to halt the continual rise in the cost of cultivation.

By applying scientific methods in agriculture, it will attain self-sufficiency in food production. It will also find means to move the country's economy from its predominantly agricultural foundation towards an industrial one.

It will have Madras city recognised as an “A” class city.

To meet losses in cultivation caused by sudden changes in weather and natural calamities, it will endeavour to introduce a government crop-insurance scheme.

It will arrange for government concessions and assistance available to Scheduled Castes to be extended to members of those communities who have changed their religion.

The DMK will arrange for the complete removal of sales tax on foodstuffs and for sales tax to be levied at a single point.

It will find a way to discontinue horse racing.

It will improve the programme that provides employment opportunities for comrades returning from military service and safeguards their welfare.

It will establish factories near salt pans to manufacture products derived from salt.

It will endeavour to establish heavy industries and facilities manufacturing electrical equipment.

It will find means to build railway lines and highways connecting industrial towns.

### Public Sector

It will endeavour to transfer existing high-revenue private industries to the public sector by paying fair compensation through bonds.

Because banks are being used to trap the economic structure under the domination of the wealthy, it will arrange for them to be brought into government ownership.

It will arrange for industrialists with technical expertise in running private-sector enterprises to be commissioned to operate public-sector industries.

It will accelerate the government's programme for taking over bus transport, increase the number of bus routes and arrange for medium-sized buses to connect small villages.

### Labour Welfare

Industries and occupations in the country will be classified; minimum wage rates, holidays, retirement benefits, gratuity and other concessions will be fixed for workers; and means will be found to implement them effectively.

### Handloom Weavers

To relieve the distress of handloom weavers, it will endeavour to give the state government authority to prohibit mills from manufacturing, and imports from supplying, bordered dhotis and saris.

It will seek provision in trade agreements with foreign countries to secure overseas markets for handloom cloth.

It will extend and implement legislation fixing fair wages for all workers.

### Agriculture

Cultivable land classified as wasteland will be distributed to Scheduled Caste cultivators and other landless people: three acres where it can be made wet land and five acres where it can be made dry land.

It will give life to the Land Ceiling Act by amending the ceiling to fifteen acres, invalidating sham sales and arrangements, and removing exemptions.

### Cooperation

It will endeavour to free the cooperative sector from the grip of officials, prevent it from becoming the monopoly of a few, and make it clean and useful.

It will enforce the condition that Scheduled Caste people constitute twenty-five percent of members in cooperative organisations and backward classes another twenty-five percent.

### Rural Welfare

It will prepare a programme of roads for neglected rural areas and use military personnel to complete it within ten years.

Together with that work, it will establish a “labour corps” in every district to repair village wells and tanks, construct small reservoirs and houses, and carry out related works within ten years, providing its members with food, clothing and shelter.

It will arrange at least one medical van for every sub-taluk and thereby expand medical facilities in rural areas.

### Panchayat System

It will genuinely transfer in practice many powers now concentrated in the government to panchayat bodies. To enable them to assume new responsibilities, it will double the government grant made to panchayats, presently calculated in relation to the amount of tax collected.

### Madras City

It will adopt and implement the plans drawn up by experts to provide Madras city with the facilities it needs.

### Municipalities

Underground drainage schemes will be completed in every municipality.

It will arrange for fire stations to be established in every municipality.

### Education

It will make compulsory free education successful, remove the ineffectiveness of the basic-education system, and adopt methods that impart progressive ideas and the industrial knowledge required in the present age.

Measures will be adopted so that the arts may advance and serve as instruments conveying good ideas to society.

It will plan the establishment of another university for Tamil Nadu.

Even if professors capable of imparting advanced scientific and technical knowledge must be brought from abroad, it will guide the rapid advancement of technical education.

It will endeavour to establish a Dravidian research university to conduct research into Dravidian languages, arts, civilisation, literature, history and music, and thereby help demonstrate to the world Dravida's antiquity and distinctive excellence.

It will establish a publicity organisation to spread the way of the Kural throughout the world.

### Minorities

The Kazhagam will labour to remedy the grievances of Muslim and Christian minorities, protect their rights and advance their welfare.

### Condition of the Depressed Classes

The Kazhagam will labour tirelessly to provide special concessions and opportunities to Scheduled Castes, backward classes and hill peoples, enabling them to advance to equality with others.

It will stop huts being suddenly demolished in the name of slum improvement and their inhabitants being driven far outside the town. It will find means to house them within the town itself, close to their places of work.

### Electoral System

The Kazhagam will endeavour, through lawful political means:

- to have elections conducted under proportional representation, a system suited to this country;
- to adopt the referendum for determining public opinion on a particular issue and make its result legally valid under the Constitution; and
- to give voters the power to recall elected representatives who depart from principle or stand opposed to the people's wishes.

### Hindi Domination

To prevent both direct and indirect Hindi domination, and to ensure without any time limit that English continues as the Union's official language after 1965, the Kazhagam will make every effort to amend Part XVII of the Constitution.

### Reform Marriage

It will endeavour to make Tamil reform marriages legally valid.

### Protection of Rights

It will endeavour to alter statutory provisions and government orders introduced in ways that impair the fundamental rights of the people and the political rights of opposition parties.

## 17. Even so, why do members of other parties keep vilifying the DMK?

Every political party explains its programme, announces its method of work and compares its own programme with those of other parties.

But suppose there is a group whose entire day's speech and entire occupation consist of degrading the Kazhagam, calling its leaders deceivers and its comrades sheep, speaking and hurling abuse. What does that mean?

It means they have taken a contract to abuse the Kazhagam.

Can that be called a political party?

They have no voice to condemn:

- the growth of autocracy under Congress rule;
- Congress rule's introduction of intolerable laws;
- its tormenting people with unbearable taxes;
- its indifference to checking rising prices;
- its continual increase of administrative expenditure;
- the growth of disorder, inefficiency, bribery and corruption in administration;
- capitalism's rapid growth in the name of industrial development;
- the waste of thousands of crores of rupees spent on plans;
- the neglect of the South in industrial planning;
- the failure of wealth created through planning to spread through society and improve the condition of the poor;
- the concentration of that wealth in the hands of a few capitalists;
- those capitalists' contributions to Congress election funds;
- Congress's use of those funds to turn democracy into a farce;
- the waste of enormous sums in the name of community-welfare schemes;
- the reduction of community welfare to a mere Congress publicity booth;
- the inadequate attention paid to village welfare;
- the growth of the black market and predatory profit;
- the continuing growth of Hindi domination; and
- the failure to nationalise industries.

They fear that condemning these things will bring down the anger of Congress upon them.

They fear that if they condemn them, periodicals will not give them publicity.

They fear that condemnation will create difficulties for them.

Therefore, lacking the courage to ask why all these injustices occur, some have made it their sole occupation to ask:

“Why does the DMK do this? Why does it do that? Why has the DMK not done this? Why has it not done that?”

What is the reason? What is their purpose?
