## English Translation

Sir C. P. Ramaswami Iyer, Dewan of Travancore!

I hear that you spoke at the Indian Youth Association in Mylapore on October 3. I was astonished to see “The Dewan’s Crushing Reply to Ambedkar!” proclaimed in enormous letters in publications devoted to falsehood. A member of the group that calls itself superior had replied to the independent leader of the oppressed, and with a crushing reply at that! Astonished, I read the full report.

Surrounded by religious initiates and shastris as you proceed in procession, Aryan obsession must naturally have gone to your head. Are you not of a caste deemed holy by birth? Your speech that day does not appear to have followed even a little reflection. In an assembly occupied by Aryans, their attendants, and the ruling group that delights in both, you exalted the pride of your caste and belittled the principled man who criticizes its customs. Excited to the utmost by the resulting empty applause, you poured out words without even recognizing whether what you said was true.

Your own people may have praised you, but friends of other communities who respect you as an intelligent and learned man can only pity you. Do not grow angry because I use harsh words. Anger clouds reason, and surely you know that bitter medicine is good for an illness.

You began by speaking about the glories of Hindu society. It cannot be denied that adherence to the Hindu religion has brought immeasurable benefit to one community and indescribable disgrace to another.

One section of the people spends its days beneath the fierce midday sun, wiping from its brow the sweat that falls to the ground and surviving on half a stomach of gruel. Yet the fruit of that immense labour is enjoyed by idle men who sit in beautiful gardens amid gentle breezes, surrounded by women singing *Vande Mataram* and by eminent men skilled in metre, debating the beauty of the moon, the delight of Kamban’s poetry, and the distinction of the scriptures. Why is this so? Even in these days of severe food scarcity, why do priests receive limitless wealth and food? Why do learned reciters of the Vedas return home with music, gold, and rich fare?

Is it not because your society ordained that Brahmins, born from the Supreme Being’s face, should live by receiving service at their feet from Sudras, born from his feet? What else would you do but praise the society from which you benefit?

You then ask, with sparks flying from your angry eyes, how a person drawing a government salary can speak disparagingly of a community. It is not Ambedkar’s inner purpose to disparage any community. He is the leader of the oppressed. When he devises and explains ways to advance the oppressed classes, is it wrong for him to identify the obstacles and urge the oppressed to acquire self-respect and awareness?

Ambedkar knows even better than you do that he receives a government salary. That is precisely why he proposed measures for the advancement of the people whose welfare is indispensable to good government, the very people whom the Hindu code you exalt declares it sinful even to touch.

You treated that as an offence and, in the manner characteristic of your community, spoke as though reporting him to the government, even though most members of your own society hate that government. But do not your own Puranas tell you that the little snake upon Siva’s head converses amicably with the eagle Garuda upon which Vishnu, who sleeps upon the ocean, rides?

You declare arrogantly that history has always contained societies in which one community occupies a high position, another a low one, and the higher group torments the lower. You cite the earlier histories of South Africa, Australia, America, and other countries in support.

Leaving aside whether your evidence is honest, does a wicked act become good merely because it occurred in other countries? If countless people cause riots in a liquor shop, does drinking thereby become a virtuous habit? Because Aryans drank soma and sang the Vedas, must Dravidians refrain from speaking of their cruelties?

When you refer to the condition of Jews in former times in England, did you consider whether the comparison was just? The people comparable to those Jews in this country are the Aryans. Dravidians labour while Aryans luxuriate. Farsighted Aryans ought to be approaching the Lord and pleading that the people of this country must not conclude that the treatment once inflicted upon such Jews in England was right. Is it fitting for you instead to cite English history?

You mention the rule that the monarch may not be a Roman Catholic. Have all such restrictions transformed the social life of that country? Do the four varnas exist there? Is there a division between Brahmin and Paraiyar? Did you consider whether that country has a doctrine requiring a cow to be given through a Brahmin to the Supreme Being in exchange for a life in heaven?

You spoke to the skies about opening the temples in Travancore. How many oppressed people enter those temples? Do you not reserve senior offices for the oppressed and then hand them back to Aryans on the plea that “no qualified person was available”?

My dear sir, time is turning with fierce speed. This is no age for those who cling with a monkey’s grip. This is the age of valiant people advancing with the principles that birth is common to all living beings, that humanity is one community, and that Dravida Nadu belongs to the Dravidians.

Do not grow angry and say that I insult the Hindu religion. If you dislike the principles of the newly awakened world, stand aside. Do not struggle in vain against the storm of Dravidian self-awareness.

15 October 1944

## Translator's Notes

The letter’s historical caste terminology is retained because its argument directly attacks the hierarchy encoded by those terms. Derogatory social labels are translated only where the source itself invokes them critically; they are not endorsements. Several damaged OCR phrases are documented separately.
