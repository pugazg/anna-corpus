## English Translation

A friend asks: “You write as though giving charity and ceremonial gifts were some ruinous affair. When Brahmins bare their teeth and beg, are not ‘charity’ and ‘gifts’ merely the small coins a compassionate Tamil throws them? Is it right to make so much of this?” He asks because, in the previous issue, I explained that the system of receiving donations was a subtle method of taxation devised by Aryan rule.

Friends who regard giving as a sign of kindness, generosity and pity for poverty would understand how subtle the ancient conspiracy was if they knew what a mass of texts the Aryans accumulated on donations. I am not so cruel as to forbid compassion when we see sunken eyes and realise that people like us have been reduced to skin and sinew. No sensible person would say the strong should not support the weak. My view is that the distinction between weak and strong should not exist at all, and that many have been kept weak precisely so a few can remain strong. That condition must end. Tenderness of heart is an adornment to courage, and I would never forbid aid to the destitute. But money given ostentatiously with drums announcing it to the town is pageantry, not true virtue.

The gifts and charities made to the “earthly gods” did not arise from a heart moved by the poor person's suffering. Religious doctrine said that one who did a good deed would receive divine grace, and its interpreters then defined the good deed as making various donations to Aryans. Ancient Brahmins promised many rewards: help on the road to liberation, a favourable fate after death, the empire of moksha, life in the other world, and a station equal to Indra and Chandra.

One ancient Tamil poet mocked charity performed for such rewards. It contained no virtue, he said; it was mere commerce. If what is done in this world is intended to yield profit in the next, how can it prove the doer's goodness or generous heart? It may prove his calculation. Giving here in order to buy reward above is trade, not virtue. In describing the benefactor Ay as no such merchant, the poet also explained the nature of true virtue.

The Aryan schemes for donation entirely contradict that noble explanation. By spreading obscene, irrational and imaginary falsehoods that corrupt thought, Aryan writers made Tamil people servants through countless stories about donations. Along with their money, the Tamil people's intelligence was lost, leaving ignorance and poverty as their property. That is why I condemned these donations.

If something were presented merely as an ordinary person's assertion, everyone would ask whether it was correct, reasonable and compatible with knowledge. From the beginning, the Aryans took great care to prevent this habit of inquiry from growing. Manu is said to have warned his own people, “Trust neither an educated Sudra nor a bathed horse.” Knowing that anything could be circulated among Tamils if attributed to a divine person, they published texts on donations stamped with the claim that a particular god had uttered them.

Look now at one such Aryan exposition. It was spoken to Garuda and is supposedly no ordinary teaching.

## Vishnu's Instructions to Garuda

1. A Brahmin is entitled to receive and give donations, officiate sacrifices for those qualified, recite the Vedas and teach them to those qualified. Kshatriyas and Vaisyas may give donations, perform sacrifices and recite the Vedas. Sudras, identified here with the Dravidian people, are entitled only to submit and perform the work commanded by the first varna.

2. If a Brahmin accompanying a Sudra's corpse goes to the cremation ground, he incurs impurity for three days and must then bathe in the Kaveri to become pure.

3. If a king confiscates a Brahmin's property and wages war with his fourfold army, that army will perish on the battlefield.

4. Confiscating a Brahmin's property destroys one's lineage.

5. Any possession not placed in the hand of a Brahmin, Vishnu's servant, is not truly the owner's property but wealth held by a miser.

6. By making religious donations, one obtains everything exactly as desired and attains moksha.

7. If sons do not perform rites for a dead man, he wanders day and night crying “Alas!” He is then born as a worm or other creature, later enters the womb of the lowest among humans, and dies again.

8. One seeking the highest reward must donate a cow and other gifts to a Brahmin.

9. On the eleventh and twelfth days after death, a salagrama must be placed in a copper dish with cotton, clothing and other articles and given to a worthy Brahmin.

10. To help the deceased cross the river Vaitarani, one must make a boat of sugarcane, wrap it in silk, place within it a bronze vessel filled with ghee, and donate it to a Brahmin.

11. Whoever gives *puri* to Brahmins during rites for the dead obtains every desired good and avoids birth as a ghost.

12. After death, the spirit consumes through Brahmins what its son has given.

13. Bound and dragged by Yama's servants, repeatedly looking back at home, suffering kicks and crying out, the spirit must walk 247 *kadam* each day, covering 86,000 *kadam* from the human world to Yama's city.

14. If the spirit worshipped the Sravanas, they report only its merits, not its sins, to Yama.

15. So that Yama's messengers may lead the spirit without torment, a parasol, footwear, staff, clothing, ring, water pot, seat, food, ritual substances, sacred thread, copper vessel, rice, salt, cotton, sesame vessel, wheat, chickpeas, field beans, sesame, horse gram, green gram and pigeon peas must be placed in vessels and donated to worthy Brahmins.

16. In whatever birth the giver is later born, he receives there the reward of his charity; the sinner is born as a ghost.

17. One who creates a well, water channel, tank, flower garden, water shed, rest house or temple and later sells it is born as a ghost.

18. If sons perform sraddha and other rites for a year, the deceased mounts a celestial vehicle and reaches the good world.

19. If many kinds of parasols, footwear, rings and clothes are given to Brahmins, along with golden pots filled with milk and ghee, the deceased escapes ghostly birth.

20. At the time of death, it is especially meritorious to donate sesame, iron, salt, cotton, grain, gold, land and a cow.

21. If land is donated for the deceased, the spirit lives in heaven for as many years as the land measures in feet.

22. If one feeds a worthy Brahmin daily and gives him food and water, the dead spirit experiences happiness.

23. One who donates a maiden, land, cow, sesame mountain, elephant and similar things, builds a temple, or builds and donates a house, resides in heaven for thirty-five million years.

24. If annual support is given to a Brahmin, images and pots are made of gold, copper, silver and iron, filled with nine gems, donated and accompanied by rites, the deceased attains a favourable destiny. A Brahmin who travels around the seas receiving donations supposedly produces exceptional reward for the donor.

Is that all? Look at the further explanations of donations.

## Further Great Donations

1. **Hiranyagarbha donation:** Make a lotus-shaped golden vessel seventy-two inches high and one-third as broad, fill it with curd, ghee and milk, place it upon two *marakkal* of sesame on an altar, worship it according to rule and circumambulate it. The donor remains inside the golden “womb” for a prescribed period while a Brahmin preceptor performs conception, quickening, parting-of-the-hair and birth rites. Emerging from the vessel, the donor gives it away together with villages and other property. He resides in Brahma's world for ten billion *kalpas* and rescues his ancestors from hell.

2. **Golden-horse donation:** Make a horse from three to one thousand *palams* of gold, install eight golden vessels, worship according to rule and give it away. The donor is honoured by the gods and reaches Indra's station.

3. **Golden horse-chariot donation:** Make a chariot from three to one thousand *palams* of gold, install a golden image of one's chosen deity, harness four or eight golden horses, adorn and worship it, then donate it. The donor is freed from sin, gains a radiant body and reaches Siva's abode.

4. **Ubhaya-gomukhi donation:** When half a newborn calf remains within its mother's womb and half is outside, decorate the cow with gold, silver and pearls and donate it with a fee to a Vedic priest. The donor receives the reward of giving the earth and satisfies the ancestors.

5. **Golden Kamadhenu donation:** Fashion ten wish-fulfilling vines and images of water birds, swans, Vidyadharas and others from five to one thousand *palams* of gold, worship them and donate them to Vedic priests. The donor reaches Satyaloka.

6. **Golden cow donation:** Fashion a cow and calf from one thousand, five hundred or two hundred and fifty *palams* of gold, worship and donate them. The donor is honoured by the gods and reaches Siva's abode.

7. **Wish-fulfilling-tree donation:** From three to one thousand *palams* of gold make a five-branched kalpaka tree bearing images of the Trinity, birds and fruits. Place a progeny tree in the east, a mandara tree with Lakshmi in the south, and a sandal tree with Surabhi in the north. Perform fire rites and worship, bow and donate them. Honoured by Siddhas, Charanas, Kinnaras and Apsaras, the donor mounts a radiant celestial vehicle and remains in Vishnu's abode for one hundred *kalpas*.

8. **Cotton donation:** Donate a stated large weight of cotton. The donor reaches the higher world.

9. **Mountain-of-ghee donation:** Donate the prescribed number of pots of ghee. The donor reaches Siva's abode.

10. **Black-antelope-skin donation:** During the months of Masi, Adi or Karthigai, or at full moon, an eclipse, Uttarayana Dwadasi or another sacred time, donate a black antelope skin according to rule to a Vedic priest maintaining the sacred fires. The donor attains union with Siva.

11. **Jaggery-cow donation:** Make a cow and calf from four or more *bharams* of jaggery, adorn and worship them and give them away. Similar cows may be made of butter, sesame, grain, sugar, salt, gems or gold, ritually invoked and donated. The donor enjoys every prosperity for a long period in the world of cows and then reaches Vishnu's abode.

12. **Mountain-of-jaggery donation:** Make a mountain from three to ten *bharams* of jaggery and donate it according to rule. The donor receives the presence of Pasupati.

13. **Thousand-cow donation:** Make ten golden cows and a golden tree from three to one thousand *palams* of gold; then decorate and worship one thousand living cows and donate them. Freed from sin, the donor resides on Kailasa with 101 generations of his lineage.

14. **Mountain-of-sugar donation:** Heap two to eight *bharams* of sugar into a mountain and donate it. The donor reaches Siva's abode.

15. **Seven-seas donation:** Make seven span-sized golden pots. Fill them respectively with salt, milk, ghee, jaggery, curd, sugar and pure water; install Brahma with Saraswati, Vishnu, Siva, Surya, Chandra, Lakshmi, Parvati and the appropriate deities; worship and donate them. The donor reaches Vishnu's abode.

16. **Golden-mountain donation:** Fashion and donate a mountain of 250 to one thousand *palams* of gold. The donor reaches Brahma's abode.

17. **Bed donation:** Make a fine wooden bed, adorn it, spread a mat, provide sacred water, sandal paste, flowers, betel and things required by a man and woman, worship the nine planets and donate it. This yields heaven.

18. **Earth donation:** With one hundred to one thousand *palams* of gold, fashion the earth like Jambudvipa, containing many mountains, seas, towns, villages and the nine regions including Bharata, surrounded by an ocean. Worship and donate it. The giver and his lineage live in Vaikuntha for three *kalpas*.

19. **Mountain-of-grain donation:** Form mountains from salt, jaggery, gold, butter, sesame, gems, silver and sugar; place one thousand *marakkal* of paddy as Mount Meru, adorn it with nine gems, worship and donate it. The donor reaches the divine world.

20. **Guardians-of-the-directions donation:** Seat eight Vedic priests, worship Siva among them, fashion eleven celestial vehicles from ten *kalanju* of gold, donate them to the priests and feed many Vedic scholars.

21. **Mountain-of-sesame donation:** Heap three to ten *marakkal* of sesame like a mountain, worship and donate it to attain heaven. Alternatively, erect a ten-span pole, cover it with sesame, make a ritual diagram above it, cover it with cloth and flowers, meditate upon Siva reclining there, worship, and give it with a fee to Brahmins.

22. **Weighing-the-man donation:** Build a pavilion of at least sixteen cubits, purify it, make a seven-foot altar and four fire pits, install ritual pots and worship the Trinity. Plant two seven-cubit cedar or similar posts two cubits deep, adorn them, mount a balance beam and cosmic pans upon chains, and decorate the balance with flags. Invite a guru and eight Veda-knowing officiants, station them in the four directions, perform fire rites, sacrifices and worship, and have everyone fast. Next day the adorned donor worships the balance with his preceptor, sits in one pan and places equal gold in the other. He gives half the material to the preceptor and the remainder to the officiants, then makes other donations with their permission. He gains fame and longevity and reaches Vishnu's abode.

23. **Five-plough donation:** Donate five wooden ploughs, ten oxen with golden horns, golden ploughs weighing from five to one thousand *palams*, and cows with calves, after proper worship. The donor mounts a celestial vehicle and reaches Siva's abode.

24. **Cosmic-egg donation:** According to one's means, fashion from twenty to one thousand *palams* of gold a hundred-inch cosmic egg with two vessels, the elephants and guardians of the eight directions, and images of the Trinity. Adorn it with silk and ornaments, place it upon two *marakkal* of sesame, worship and donate it. Freed from sin, the giver reaches Indra's station.

25. **Great-element-vessel donation:** Make a hundred-inch vessel studded with many gems, fill it with milk, ghee and other substances, install a golden wish-fulfilling tree, worship and donate it. The donor mounts a vehicle radiant as ten million suns and reaches Vaikuntha.

26. **Jewel-cow donation:** According to the Puranas, make a cow from diamond, coral, cat's-eye, hessonite, topaz, emerald, ruby, jaggery and other materials; worship it and make the donations. The donor reaches Vishnu's abode.

27. **Jewel-mountain donation:** Make and donate a mountain from three hundred to one thousand *palams* of gems. The donor is freed from the sin of killing a Brahmin and reaches Vishnu's abode.

28. **Silver-mountain donation:** Make and donate a mountain from 2,500 to 10,000 *palams* of silver. The donor reaches Siva's world.

29. **Salt-mountain donation:** Donate four to sixteen *marakkal* of salt according to rule. The donor reaches the world of Sakti.

30. **Universal-disc donation:** From one thousand to 2,500 *palams* of gold, fashion a sixteen-spoked cosmic disc with seven enclosures, Vishnu bearing conch and discus with eight consorts, and images of his ten incarnations. Worship and donate it. The donor reaches Hari's abode.

31. **Golden-elephant-chariot donation:** Make a flower chariot from five *palams* to one *bharam* of gold, harness four golden and two living elephants, place Lakshmi-Narayana in the centre and images of Brahma, Mahesvara and other gods on either side, worship and donate it. Honoured by Vidyadharas, the donor reaches Siva's abode.

32. Donating cotton removes fear of Yama's messengers.

33. Donating grain pleases Yama and his messengers, who give the spirit what it needs.

34. Donating land grants heavenly pleasure for a period corresponding to the number of feet donated.

35. A donation identified in the damaged OCR as *maradi* lets the spirit ride a horse to Yama's city without suffering from thorns along the road.

36. Donating a parasol provides a shaded road and protection from rain.

37. Donating a lamp illuminates the dark road. Giving lamps for one year after death also admits one's lineage to heaven.

38. Donating clothing causes Yama's messengers to appear in pleasing form.

39. Land, Surabhi cows and gold are especially excellent donations: land is connected with Vishnu, gold is the child of fire, and Surabhi cows are daughters of the sun. Their donor obtains all three worlds.

40. Cotton is a great donation because gods, Brahmins and others use it for sacred thread. Its donor lives in heaven for a time and, becoming beautiful and exalted, reaches Siva's abode.

41. Donations of sesame, cows, grain, gold and land destroy great sins.

42. Sesame and iron donations please Yama.

43. A salt donation removes fear of Yama from the person who makes it.

Anyone who reads and reflects upon these prescriptions will clearly understand the scale of Aryan greed and how extensively Tamil wealth was consumed. To those who invoke religion and ask me whether such conduct was really daylight robbery, I ask: has daylight robbery like this occurred anywhere else?

Published in *Dravida Nadu*; the OCR-rendered date is uncertain.
