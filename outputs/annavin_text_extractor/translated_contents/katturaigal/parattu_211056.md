/6. பாராட்டுகிறோம்

இந்து மதத்தைவிட்டு உளம் வெந்து கூட்டம்
கூட்டமாக வெளியேறுகிற மக்களை, தனது அரவணைப்புக்
குள் அன்புடன் தழுவிக் கொள்ளும் பணியினை, இப்போது
புத்தநெறி ஆற்றி இருக்கிறது.

ஒரே இடத்தில், ஒரே நாளில், ஆடவரும் பெண்டிரும்
சிறார்களுமாகச் சேர்ந்து மூன்று இலட்சம் மக்கள், ஒரு
மதத்தைவிட்டு வேறொரு மதம் புகுந்த சம்பவம், அதிலும்
இந்துமதத்தைவிட்டுப் புத்தமதம் தழுவிய செய்தி, இதுவரை
வரலாற்றில் இடம்பெற்றிருக்கவில்லை. இம்மத மாற்றச்
செய்தியைச் சித்தரித்துள்ள ஒரு நிருபர், “உலகில் வேறு எங்கும்
நடைபெறாத இந்தச் “சம்பவம் நடைபெற்றது” எனக்
குறிப்பிட்டுள்ளார்.

மூன்று லட்சம் மக்கள் கூடியிருந்த காட்சியையும்,
- அநீத இடத்தின் பரப்பையும், அதே நிருபர், “நகருக்கு
வெளியே 70 இலட்சம் சதுர அடி விஸ்தீரணமுள்ள
மைதானம்” என்றும், “மைதானம் முழுதும் ஒரே ஜனசமுத்திர
மாகக் காணப்பட்டது” என்றும் வரைந்துள்ளார்.

இந்துமதத்தைவிட்டு வெளியேறும் திருப்பணி இன்று
நேற்று ஏற்பட்டதன்று. இவ்வெளியேற்றம் தொன்றுதொட்டு
நடைபெற்று வருகிறது.

இருந்தாலும், மூன்று இலட்சம் மக்கள் ஒரே நாளில்
ஓரே இடத்தில் கூடி, இந்துமதத்திலிருந்து நீங்கிப் புத்தமதம்
புகுந்திருப்பது, இந்துமதப் பாதுகாப்பாளர்களுக்கு
பேரதிர்ச்சி தரும் சம்பவமாகும்.

## English Translation

Buddhism has now performed the work of lovingly embracing within its fold the people who, their hearts wounded, leave Hinduism in group after group.

Never before in history have three lakh people, men, women and children together, left one religion and entered another at the same place on the same day, especially leaving Hinduism to embrace Buddhism. A reporter describing this conversion wrote, “This event, which has occurred nowhere else in the world, took place here.”

The same reporter portrayed the gathering of three lakh people and the expanse of the site as “a ground measuring seventy lakh square feet outside the city” and said that “the entire ground appeared as a single ocean of humanity.”

The sacred work of leaving Hinduism did not begin today or yesterday. This departure has continued from ancient times.

Nevertheless, three lakh people gathering at one place on a single day, departing Hinduism and entering Buddhism, must be a profoundly shocking event for Hinduism's protectors.

It would not be a great exaggeration to say that some who changed religion previously did so as victims of desire for gold or possessions, or as slaves to status and authority.

But the conversion of Dr Ambedkar, who entered the Buddhist path today with three lakh people, cannot be placed in that category.

Dr Ambedkar studied the substance of Hinduism deeply. One might say there were no Sanskrit scriptures connected with Hinduism that he had not studied.

He possessed enough legal learning to frame the Constitution of India.

When such a great scholar casts Hinduism aside and adopts the Buddhist path with three lakh people, it cannot be regarded like an ordinary person's rejection of Hinduism.

Upon embracing Buddhism, Dr Ambedkar took this pledge:

“I shall not regard Brahma, Vishnu, Mahesh, Kali, Ganapati or the other Hindu deities as gods. I do not believe in divine incarnation. I do not believe that Buddha is an incarnation of Vishnu. I shall not perform rituals. I reject ceremonies conducted by Brahmins. I shall observe morality. I shall practise compassion. I shall treat all human beings equally. I shall follow Buddhist doctrine.”

That pledge itself clearly expresses the inner substance of Hinduism and the principles of Buddhism.

As usual, news of this conversion failed to stimulate thought among Hinduism's defenders. It produced only the irritation that customarily arises among them.

Congress rulers too have sought peace of mind, as usual, by ridiculing the conversion and abusing the doctor at the centre of it.

They have adopted a style that presents this great historical event to the country as something exceedingly ordinary.

However hard they try to portray it as a small event, they will meet only disappointment.

That is their nature. Who can change it? Will a leopard agree to change its spots?

Will the people of the world decide how much significance to give this conversion by listening to their speeches? Never.

Dr Ambedkar's conversion will not fail to provoke thought throughout the world.

Scholars in many countries will ask themselves why Dr Ambedkar dared to leave Hinduism while taking three lakh people with him.

The Constitution of India, containing a provision that untouchability has been legally abolished in the Indian subcontinent, will not provide the answer.

Nor can the question be answered by the head of the Kanchi Kamakoti Peetha invoking the Niyogi Committee's reasoning and exclaiming that foreigners spend nine crore rupees on religious conversion in the Indian subcontinent, and thus spend such a vast sum to destroy Hinduism.

The people of the world will conclude that, in Nehru's country, which claims to work for world peace, the cruelty of untouchability still persists to such an extent that three lakh people renounced Hinduism in a single day.

Nehru's Panchsheel speeches, the conciliatory coating applied by the Indian Constitution, and the arrows of abuse fired by nationalist newspapers will all be powerless to alter that conclusion.

Congress rulers may feel little obligation to seek out and remove the root cause that continues to drive people out of Hinduism in groups. They may even consider that work no responsibility of theirs.

But the position of the Shankaracharya, head of the Kanchi Kamakoti Peetha, cannot be the same.

The Shankaracharya depends solely on Hinduism for his prestige and income, lives by it and occupies his elevated seat through it.

As always, his present statement seems intended merely to preserve at least the remaining measure of his declining prestige and decreasing income. It does not reveal any wish to purify Hinduism so that others might willingly adopt it, or at least so that those already within it might continue there until death.

The Shankaracharya cries that nine crore rupees are brought from abroad and used to convert Hindus to other religions. We ask him: leaving aside crores, how many lakhs or even thousands has he spent to advance oppressed people? Can he produce the figures?

Only Islam as a religion was imported from Arabia. Not all present-day Muslims migrated here from that land. Those who are Muslims today were, at some time in the past, people within Hinduism.

The proper reason why Muslims, once numbering only in the hundreds, have today grown to nine crore must be discovered within Hinduism itself.

Likewise, those who mock Ambedkar's conversion should consider why and how Christianity was able to spread here.

The situation is such that not only Dr Ambedkar and the three lakh people with him, but many more lakhs, may leave.

A mansion may be a palace made of gold, but when it is inhabited by the deadly contagious germs of untouchability, invisibility, unapproachability, and superiority and inferiority by birth, people like Dr Ambedkar will not agree to live there for long. They will inevitably leave.

21 October 1956
