## English Translation

Tamil title: 4-5-7-10!

They will leap up in fury, threaten to curse us, call upon the government to issue a banning order, or collectively declare, "Otherwise we shall stir opposition throughout the country and overthrow the government itself." Thus speak Brahmin leaders when it is demonstrated that their way of life and culture were entirely different from those of the Dravidians and were such as the Dravidians would despise.

Some say, "Aryan or Dravidian - why this absurd talk?"

Others say it is extremely dangerous to speak of a distinction between Aryans and Dravidians.

Still others say, "At some distant time there may have been a racial distinction between Aryan and Dravidian, but today everything has mixed, dissolved and become one."

Yet others say, "Does Aryan denote a separate race? What dull-wittedness! Does not Aryan simply mean a superior person?"

All of them speak so because they are frightened to see the Aryan-Dravidian question spreading widely among the people and becoming clearly understood.

A generation ago, every Brahmin took pride in calling himself an Aryan. Treating the writings of the German Max Muller as epics, they spoke with delight about the superiority of the Aryan race. They rejoiced in names such as Arya Seva Sangam, Arya Arts Development Association and Aryan Religious Instruction.

And now? Every Brahmin feels hesitation, confusion and fear about calling himself an Aryan. In the meantime, Aryanism, the worth of the Aryan race and its culture have been exposed in broad daylight.

Do you know the Aryans? They entered in search of green pasture while harbouring poisonous thoughts. They babbled one thing after another in crude language and deceived the common people. Drinking many intoxicants in their forest hermitages, they roasted and ate tender calves and deer and passed their days. Even those among them who became kings lived in repulsive ways. The sacrifices and yogic practices attributed to them appear as acts of savagery.

Researchers today can explain all this with proper evidence. Anger boils within the descendants of the Aryans, and so, not knowing what to say, they speak differently from person to person and from one moment to the next.

We know there are "intellectuals" who ask why Dravidians today need to discuss or mock the fact that Aryan Brahmins lived in an extremely degraded manner in those days.

Our work is neither to take delight in stirring a grave nor to dissect a corpse. We present Aryanism and the Aryans' way of life to the Dravidians today not merely to show that it was detestable, and certainly not merely to clap and laugh. We do so to condemn and uproot the cruelty by which Aryans who writhed in such detestable ways have become religious teachers today; the wonder by which they call obscenity divine nectar; and the injustice by which they call a debased way of life the conduct of great souls and render the people mindless.

The sacred stories composed by the Aryans themselves make clear that their way of life was filled with ugliness and injustice.

One coupled with his mother, another married his sister, another slept with his teacher's wife, another enjoyed a sage's wife; one went in a changed form, another entered at midnight, and another became a serpent. Such were the sexual exploits of the Aryan paragons, and these very figures are placed in our shrines and worshipped.

We ask you to consider what degree of mental delusion was required to turn Panchali into an emblem of chastity.

One woman for five men! Even an animal snarls and leaps at another that approaches sniffing around its mate. Yet five men who moved in friendship with an "incarnate god" made the woman their common property. This horror, which even the jungle would not accept, was named a divine story, and minds were corrupted until people folded their hands and worshipped it.

This disgrace, unseen anywhere else in the world, has here been made worthy of the temple.

Which great Dravidian scholar condemns it?

There are virtuous men who speak about morality, and those who speak about chastity so movingly that even stone would melt. Which of them condemned this disgrace?

Who said, "The Tamil people do not need such a 'compassionate goddess'?"

They claim that conducting religious research will destroy morality and rage against our movement, which spreads the light of knowledge. Yet here is one woman for five men, enough to send morality running a thousand leagues away, and no one condemns it. They burn camphor and pray to the woman who was wife to all five.

Is this not sufficient proof of how completely Aryanism can blind the eye of reason?

Why do lecturers who beautifully describe the flavour found in Sangam literature, and researchers who speak of Tamil culture, remain silent?

Ask them privately: "Sirs, is it proper for one woman to be wife to five? Is this the culture of the classical Tamil land?"

They will answer, "Why do you ask so, my boy? The beautiful woman born to the courtesan community ruined her peacock-like appearance when Kovalan left her, and when she learnt he had died she assumed the garb of a mendicant. Is not Tamilakam a treasury of chastity and the dwelling place of culture? One man, one woman: that is the dignity of sweet Tamil!"

Their speech contains learning.

Then ask, "If that is so, O foremost scholar, is it proper for Tamilakam today to accept as a sacred story the obscenity of one woman being wife to five? Is it not foolish to celebrate Panchali as a goddess of chastity?"

They will say, "What doubt is there? Certainly the Tamil way rejects such a degraded act."

Ask, "But our people worship Panchali, do they not?"

"Ignorance," they will reply.

Ask, "Should not learned people such as yourselves, who know everything, come forward and work to remove that ignorance?"

Then they say, "How can that be done? Our people, sunk in ignorance, will become angry and hostile," and depart.

Such is the conduct of the guardians of classical Tamil!

Some among these guardians, to conceal that they have become incapable of fighting such obscenity, use their intellectual skill to manufacture some philosophy or other.

They attribute an inner meaning and a subtle meaning to everything. When? Whenever obscenity is condemned.

Why did Draupadi become wife to five? There is a story. Vijaya reportedly said, "I have brought a maiden," but his mother's ear heard "fruit," and she told all five to divide it among themselves. Could a mother's word be disobeyed? The woman was granted the fortune of entertaining all five, and those powerful men behaved without hurting the feelings of any one among them.

Now hear another explanatory tale.

One story says Panchali met this fate because of Kunti's blunder. Perhaps thinking that made it too ordinary a matter, another story was constructed in which this atrocity occurred through the Supreme Lord's mischief.

A woman named Indrasena performs penance towards Paramasiva.

She does not pray, "O Supreme Lord, grant me the states of salokya, samipya, sarupya and sayujya." She asks, "Give me a husband suited to me."

Indrasena repeats this request five times. Siva appears and says, "We have granted you five."

"Five?" the beautiful woman asks in alarm.

The dancer in the cremation ground reportedly replies, "Yes. Because you asked five times, we give you five."

Do you see Siva's cleverness? What an ingenious god, a tiger at arithmetic!

That Indrasena is Draupadi. The five granted by Hara are the five Pandavas.

Do you see how this shameless liar has made Paramasiva himself a witness?

The author says the woman committed no error and the five men bear no blame: it was Hara's doing.

Those without the courage to launch a moral struggle, declare these Aryan obscenities and remove them from Tamilakam, construct explanatory stories for the obscenities. Those stories are still more repulsive than the original obscenity. They seek a cure for snakebite in a tiger's fang and fall into a cesspit while trying to escape mud.

Once the "burden" is placed upon Siva, who can dare ask, "One woman for five men?" If anyone does ask, they can silence him with, "Do you question Siva himself?"

The Aryan way of life was filled with obscenities such as one woman for five men.

A woman named Madhavi was the banner of King Yayati's lineage. This woman had four husbands, all princes.

The Madhavi of Tamilakam belonged to the courtesan community, yet became an exemplar of chastity. The Aryan Madhavi, however, had four husbands.

25 August 1955.
