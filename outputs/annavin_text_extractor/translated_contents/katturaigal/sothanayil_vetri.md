## English Translation

The Dravidar Kazhagam students and the leader of the Thillai Dravidar Kazhagam, who were bound up in the Annamalai Nagar case, endured many hardships for more than a year, and stood arguing for justice, have been declared innocent. We welcome and commend the comrades who emerged victorious and free from the case, offering them congratulations upon their victory.

The bitterness that arose between Congress and Dravidar Kazhagam students in Annamalai Nagar gradually became a dispute and then reached the stage of open conflict. Jealous of the growth of the Dravidian Students' Association, the Congress students deliberately provoked a quarrel, making dispute and conflict unavoidable. The Dravidian students had no need to seek a quarrel, because they did not believe the Congress students possessed anything worth envying. But they became obliged to confront the fight brought to them. As a result, blows were exchanged and both sides suffered bleeding wounds.

If this disturbance was treated as a crime, the charge should have applied to both sides. Accordingly, students from both groups were implicated and brought before the lower court. The Congress students and the Dravidar Kazhagam students were prosecuted under the same section of law. But the scales of justice tilted to one side. The Congress students were released from the case because those in power belonged to their side. We ask why justice closed one "eye," but there is no answer.

Comrades Mathiazhagan, Govindan, Kannaiyan, Thirunavukkarasu, and Thirugnanam, Dravidar Kazhagam students of Annamalai Nagar, and Comrade Krishnasamy, leader of the Thillai Dravidar Kazhagam, were made to wander for many days because of the case and repeatedly attend court. The students suffered between their studies on one side and financial hardship on the other. The government refused to show mercy.

The lower court found them guilty, fined the students, and sentenced the leader of the Thillai Dravidar Kazhagam to six months' rigorous imprisonment. Resolving to pursue the case to the end for justice, they approached the appellate court. There they received a favourable judgment. The magistrate declared them innocent. Casting aside all the suffering heaped upon them, they emerged free and proud.

Even if they had not received a favourable judgment in the appellate court or High Court, we would not have been surprised, because the law under today's rulers functions along a single track with a single eye.

All these experiences are a testing ground in which members of the Dravidar Kazhagam may be trained. Even while they stood in the dock, the sweet song arose from the chords of their hearts: "The flower grove where the mango cuckoo sings is the prison that thinks it can confine us."

We congratulate those who returned victorious from the trial.

(*Dravida Nadu*, 6-0-49)
