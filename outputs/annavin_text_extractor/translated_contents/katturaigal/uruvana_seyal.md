## English Translation

Congress leaders proclaim that, without any desire for office or title, they stand upon the Gandhian path and believe that “our duty is to perform service and perish.” Yet reports in their own newspapers make it abundantly clear that these leaders have entered the game of obtaining office and, for the past several days, turned the Madras ministry into a hunting ground.

The “honourable men” who stand upon the Gandhian path have divided into several sub-groups within the Congress Legislative Party in Madras. The leaders of those groups are bargaining over the division of ministerial posts. The “sole emperor” of the Tamil Nadu Congress, Comrade Kamaraj, conducts the negotiations. “Go as our messenger and speak for us,” the faction leaders say, repeatedly sending Comrade Kamaraj as their emissary. Comrade Kamaraj has accepted and performs this noble task.

These, we are told, are all people without desire for office. They labour only for the country's welfare!

The “Gandhian devotee” Kamaraj has undertaken the work of bargaining to distribute ministerial posts among factions. Yet he abuses the Dravidar Kazhagam in coarse language, implying that it struggles to obtain office, though the Kazhagam itself remains outside, seeks no post, uses no shortcut to obtain one and proceeds along a direct path.

Let the country judge, from the events now taking place around the ministry, whether it is the Congress or the Dravidar Kazhagam that is hunting for office.

From ministers down to the Kamarajs who create ministers and make them dance, everyone claims that the Dravidar Kazhagam speaks all manner of false and malicious things against the Congress and creates hatred among the people solely to seize office.

The Dravidar Kazhagam possesses neither a hostile desire to snatch away the posts held by Congress members nor a feverish urge to cling to office. But when a situation arises in which office is neither protected nor used properly by those who have seized it, the Dravidar Kazhagam will never hesitate to accept it.

No party whose sole objective is the people's welfare will use office for pomp and display while failing to perform the good deeds it could achieve for the people through that office. The public has begun to understand that the office now obtained by Congress is being used in precisely those two ways.

That awareness is reflected in Dravidar Kazhagam meetings. The presence of many thousands at those meetings, demonstrating their support, is itself an example showing that the Congress in office has neglected public welfare.

This creates terror among Congress comrades who hold office. Even an ordinary public meeting gathers many thousands like a conference. If a party that has not had the opportunity to govern enjoys so much support in the country, Congress comrades fear that their right to rule may perhaps be lost. That is why Congress leaders have resolved to attack the Dravidar Kazhagam in a single breath wherever they go.

They lack the political understanding to recognise that it is contrary to democracy for a party in power, armed with the right to rule, to believe that the public will accept its criticism of another party and remain obedient. Therefore they speak contemptuously of opposition parties.

Do they never imagine that the public may ask: if these rulers possess popular support, why should they fear a party they describe as unsupported and belonging to a minority?

If a tiger fears a cat, must we not conclude that the tiger has reached a condition in which it cannot defeat the cat? Even if the tiger's strength has truly diminished, would not its mere appearance still make the cat flee? But here the cat does not fear even the tiger's form; it is the tiger that fears the cat. The reason is that the tiger has revealed its own loss of strength.

That is the condition now confronting Congress members in office. Congress leaders have realised that their strength, meaning their influence, has declined and that the public has developed dislike for them. To conceal their weakness, they use abuse of the Dravidar Kazhagam as an alternative medicine.

This remedy will cause Congress to lose even the little influence it retains; it will not increase its influence as they imagine. The day on which they understand this is not far away. Yet their obsession with office drives them so fiercely. Who can expect a party whose sole objective is the hunt for office to acquire the character needed to understand and work upon the grievances of ordinary people?

The Congress calls itself one party with one policy, yet many divisions or factions have arisen and grown within it to the point of colliding with one another. What created these divisions?

Was it a defect in party discipline? No.

Was it a difference of policy? Not that either.

Office! Office! Office! That alone is the cause.

The present ministry is gripped by tremendous fear over whether it can survive if Prakasam is included. Each member lies submerged in anxiety over whom to make a minister and which faction to join in order to protect his own post. We do not see them troubled by any desire to perform a concrete deed that would benefit the people.

Like a husband angry with his wife who vents his rage by breaking the pots in the house, Congress comrades try to cool upon the Dravidar Kazhagam the anger they ought to direct at the factions created by their own lust for office.

When Omandur's ministry withdrew and the Rajapalayam ministry arrived, factional conflict accompanied its formation. The echo of that conflict has, for the past several days, taken the form of abusing the Dravidar Kazhagam.

Wherever ministers and makers of ministers now go, and whatever subject they speak about, they first leap at the Dravidar Kazhagam before turning to any other business.

Even if their present obsession with office prevents them from recognising whether this course will bring destruction or advancement, the workings of the world will inevitably teach them in time. Then, in addition to recognising and regretting their error, they will understand that all their accusations against the Dravidar Kazhagam were slanders hastily scattered without knowledge.

Congress newspapers and Congress leaders themselves mock, distort, condemn and advise when they see the conduct of the Congress ministry. The people are not unaware of any of this. Yet in order to make them forget it all, those described as Congress leaders here fabricate false claims against the Dravidar Kazhagam and have begun a campaign of abuse among the public.

Those who perform such “good service” for the country are today engaged in dividing offices among themselves.

In many civilised countries, we see a practice of selecting people who possess the competence, knowledge and ability required to hold ministerial office. Here, however, those who came to rule forget the country, the people and the conditions prevailing in the land. They gather men and then gather posts in proportion to the number of those men. This bizarre practice prevails. Those who blame others apparently fail to see that it is shameful.

It is reported that Comrade T. Prakasam insisted that posts be distributed proportionately according to the number of members in each Assembly faction; that Premier Comrade Kumaraswami Raja did not agree; and that, because no compromise was reached, the attempt to expand the ministry has been temporarily suspended.

After this performance ended, Chief Minister Comrade Kumaraswami Raja expressed a true idea:

> “People in the villages do not worry about how many ministers there are. Only politicians worry about it. The people's concern is that government administration should proceed properly, that there should be good ministers, and that food and other necessities should be available in abundance.”

Had this true idea arisen in the Premier's mind at the beginning, so many embarrassing and ridiculous circumstances would not have arisen.

When reporters asked, “Are you going to expand the ministry?” Premier Comrade Kumaraswami Raja replied:

> “I am not going to expand the ministry now. I have no intention of doing anything hastily. Administrative work is proceeding. I think we should forget all these matters for a while and attend to worthwhile work.”

When the Premier says, “I think we should attend to worthwhile work,” there is room to conclude that he regards all the disputes that have occurred so far over the division of posts as worthless. If that is correct, we have expressed the same view: Congress is entering into worthless activity and must use the authority in its hands to perform worthwhile deeds.

Congress members engaged in many worthless activities such as dividing posts will, we hope, at least from now on perform worthwhile deeds, as Premier Comrade Kumaraswami Raja desires. Even if they regard abusing and hating people with differing opinions as worthwhile activity, let them do so freely.

If Congress comrades who devote attention to dividing offices attempt instead to show concern for distributing well-being among the people, it will be good for the country and good for them. It will also be praised as a worthwhile deed.

8 May 1949

## Translator's Notes

The printed title in Image 1 is unmistakably `உருவான செயல்!`, although the article later repeatedly contrasts `உருப்படியற்ற செயல்` with `உருப்படியான செயல்`. The title is therefore retained as printed and rendered literally as “A Deed Taking Shape!” rather than silently normalised.

`இராசபாளையத்தார் மந்திரிசபை` is the source's shorthand for the ministry led by P. S. Kumaraswami Raja of Rajapalayam. The prose contains substantial OCR damage in scattered words, especially in Image 1 and parts of Image 4; the English follows recoverable sentence-level meaning while the Tamil source remains verbatim.
