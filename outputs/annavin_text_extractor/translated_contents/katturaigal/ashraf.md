## English Translation

A marble palace, a rose garden, and a crystal stream. Swimming and playing within it is a delight to the eyes. This princess is named Ashraf.

She is a princess of Iran. The brother of this beautiful woman is the Shah, King of Iran. Ashraf and the Shah are twins, but Ashraf is two minutes older. She was in a greater hurry than her brother even at birth, and the same tendency continues to this day.

Their father reportedly used to declare proudly, "This one ought to have been my son." And what kind of father was he? A man who seized a minister who dared disobey his word and threw him from an upper floor through a window. Ashraf is that father's beloved daughter.

This princess habitually leaps with passion into Iran's political upheavals.

As an Italian journalist described her, this woman is truly a "walking volcano."

Many may ask why this woman, immersed in amusement, should concern herself with political upheaval.

Her life must remain one of amusement, and politics must be arranged accordingly. That is why this beauty takes an interest in politics.

At other times, she delights in speaking sweetly, strolling through flower gardens, swimming in elegant streams, and giving banquets.

Wine? She is reportedly never weary of celebrating with it. Dancing? It is said that the beauties of Paris could take lessons from her.

If she learns that her life of pleasure may be obstructed in any way, Ashraf becomes a tigress. Once her demand is fulfilled, she becomes a spotted deer.

When King Shah becomes anxious, this sweet woman arouses his anger. Her adventures create conspiracies; her honeyed speech can split military ranks.

Her gaze is poison to political opponents and a feast to friends.

Her first husband did not satisfy her. She found a second husband in Cairo, made him a senior official in Iran, and reduced him to her servant.

When Mossadegh became Iran's unrivalled leader, he put a stop to Ashraf's excesses. She was exiled. She vowed to eliminate Mossadegh and ultimately achieved it.

When Mossadegh gained power, the Shah was forced to flee Iran. While the broken-hearted Shah remained in Italy, this enchantress, then in Paris, met him, encouraged him, gathered assistance for him, and urged him to return home and restore his domination.

The Shah cannot disregard the words of his twin sister.

The Shah's marriage to the woman Soraya too was arranged by Ashraf.

Soraya was a beautiful young woman dancing and singing at a school in Switzerland. Ashraf decided that she would be a suitable queen for the Shah, and Soraya became Queen of Iran.

Now the Shah has become America's dancing puppet. With the support of the army, he boasts that he will govern as he pleases.

What obstacle can now remain to a splendid life for women of luxury such as Ashraf?

They have overthrown the people's leader Mossadegh.

Now it is the Ashrafs who enjoy lives of celebration. But for how long?
