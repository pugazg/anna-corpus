26. அரிகிரடரல் 1

அரிசிநிலை மிகவும் மோசமாக இருக்கிறது.

இந்த ஆண்டு. 10,54,140 டன் கொள்முதல் செய்யப்பட வேண்டும்.
ப்பொழுது 6, 74,829 டன்தான் கொள்முதல் செய்யப்பட்டிருக்கிறது.
ழுது தீ முத ட.௫க்கிறது

இனி, கொள்முதல் செய்வது மிகவும் கடினந்தான். கோதாவரியிலே
வெள்ளம், மலபாரிலே விளைவு மந்தம், தமிழகத்திலேயும் நிலை
மோசந்தான் என்று முகாரி கேட்கிறது.

1952 தொடங்கும் பொழுது, அரிசி கையிருப்பு மிகக் குறைவாகவே
இருக்கும். முதலில் 9,50,000 டன் ௮ரிசி கையிருப்பு இருந்தது. பிறகு
1,00,000 டன்னாகக் குறைந்தது. இன்றுள்ள நிலையில் 50000 டன்
இருப்பதே நிச்சயமில்லை.

இந்நிலையில் ஆறு அவுன்ஸ் அரிசி ரேஷன் தொடர்ந்து கொடுத்து .
வர முயற்சியெடுத்துக் கொள்ளப்பட்டாலும், அரிசி தேவை அதிகரிக்கத்
தான் செய்யும்.

அத்தகைய குழ்நிலையில் இதர தானியங்களைக் கொடுத்துத் தான்
சமாளிக்க வேண்டும்

இப்படி மூகாரி இசைத்திருக்கிறார்கள், சென்னை மாகாணச் சிவில்
சப்ளை அதிகாரிகள்.

அவர்கள் எழுப்பிய அவல ஓலத்தின் எதிரொலிதான் மேல்
காட்டியுள்ளவை.

சென்னை மாகாணத்தின் அரசியலையே புரட்டித்தள்ளி வருகிறது,
அரிசியியல்!

## English Translation

The rice situation is extremely bad.

This year, 1,054,140 tons must be procured. So far, only 674,829 tons have been obtained.

Further procurement will now be exceedingly difficult. Floods in the Godavari, a poor harvest in Malabar, and a bad situation in Tamil Nadu too: such is the mournful raga being heard.

When 1952 begins, rice stocks will be very low. At first, 950,000 tons of rice were in reserve. Later, the quantity fell to 100,000 tons. Under present conditions, it is uncertain whether even 50,000 tons remain.

Even if efforts are made to continue supplying a six-ounce rice ration, the demand for rice will only increase.

In such circumstances, the position must be managed by supplying other grains.

Thus have the civil-supplies officials of Madras Province sung their mournful raga. The statements above are the echo of their cry of distress.

"Rice-ology" is overturning the entire politics of Madras Province.

People who do not understand even the alphabet of this rice-ology have spoken all manner of things and aggravated the crisis today.

Rice-ology is fundamental to every field of knowledge.

It is the field that charts the path for stomach-ology. Only when stomach-ology has been settled can politics, economics, sociology, and everything else be settled.

That is why rice-ology is exceedingly important in politics. The civil-supplies officials have sung their mournful raga about it.

The minister says even more than the officials.

The Honourable Roche Victoria, Food Minister of Madras Province, has spoken in a tearful voice about rice-ology:

> So far as rice is concerned, the food situation is not at all satisfactory.

The minister has displayed the disorder of Tamil Nadu's rice-ology.

He is the Food Minister. He has shown the breakdown of the rice system that ought to proceed under his administration.

Shame and grief alone wave ceremonial fans behind his ministerial seat. What can he do? He is minister of the Food Department, but people call him minister of the Starvation Department.

The statistics sent to the Honourable Munshi, seated at the centre of government, may perhaps bring satisfaction. But when one looks at conditions in many parts of the country, an exceedingly frightening situation is causing suffering. Is the Madras minister saying that, unaware of this, Munshi sometimes sings the pleasant raga Mohanam?

"We are going to inform the central government at once that the people of Madras Province are rice eaters, and ask it urgently to investigate the condition and send more rice," declared Victoria in a voice that, though mixed with pain, carried a heroic note.

The officials who direct the Food Department's operations too supplied sorrowful notes.

Their superior joined them in a song of grief:

> We have more wheat and less rice in stock.

The man now conducting this lamentation is the Premier of the province, the Honourable Kumaraswami Raja.

After singing the refrain of the death song he had raised, he began its subsidiary refrain thus:

> Madras Province has always been a deficit region.

It seems that even before the war, only imported rice saved it.

Nature betrayed us in the meantime.

Thus proceeds the subsidiary refrain, and the final verse ends by demanding unconditional surrender to the empire of wheat.

If the rice-eating crowd will accept wheat, the Premier apparently has no objection even to giving sixteen ounces.

How generous the Premier is when distributing something people do not want!

The course of rice-ology must be watched closely.

The Premier says he will give wheat to rice eaters.

After supplying that wheat, he cannot obtain rice from the central rulers and provide it to the people.

It is only because the central rulers have firmly refused to give rice in exchange for wheat that the Premier has become an emissary of the wheat empire.

The civil-supplies officials beat the drum. The Food Minister played the fiddle in accompaniment. The Premier sang the mournful raga. This concert of rice-ology is what proceeds in the country.

Rice is extremely difficult to obtain.

The Governor of Madras Province, Bhavnagar, has said so.

Speaking about the food situation, he too displayed this difficult condition.

The Governor becomes an accompanist in the concert of rice-ology.

Standing upon the summit of Madras Province's administration, he has declared that the rice situation causes great suffering.

The hardship shown by the Governor, the Premier's mournful raga, the Food Minister's wail, and the civil-supplies officials' difficulty: this is our country's rice-ology.

Those who live here speak in this manner.

The man who is not here, who does not know this country's condition, and who remains in Delhi is the Honourable Munshi.

> The food situation is improving in places such as Madras, Hyderabad, and Bihar.

> In the future, it will not be as dark as it was last year.

People in the province say the condition is not good, yet the Delhi minister speaks in the directly opposite manner.

That may be the Delhi minister's hope, and we shall thank him for it. But the condition is not as he describes. Just as we wished to demonstrate this, the Madras Food Minister has demonstrated it too.

We have continued to say that this confusion in rice-ology exists because our country's rulers have placed their crown and grip in the hands of Munshi, a man who does not know our condition.

Our country's honourables pretend not to notice this.

Many Assembly members who raise their hands to encourage the honourables have no concern about it. Some think, "Why should we invite this trouble? There is an Assembly and a splendid chair. Let us eat well and sleep in it every day."

The party workers who sent these people there try to pierce us with abuse and bury us beneath insults.

We dismiss these things as trivial and continue walking along the path of our ideal.

Munshi and Victoria are a mismatched pair.

If two people without harmony of mind are married, how can the household avoid turmoil and confusion?

That kind of confusion is our country's rice-ology.

Rice-ology is a growing terror.

Those who roam about as blissful beauties of politics will initially regard it with indifference. But they must understand where its conclusion will take us and act accordingly.

Munshi sings Mohanam while Victoria sings Mukhari, both at the same time.

This is the rice-ology of this country. This condition is a stain upon politics. Our desire is that the stain be removed.

As these stains continue to grow, everyone will come to the path of our ideal. That is the political prediction we calculate through the mathematics of rice-ology.

*Dravida Nadu*, 25 August 1951.
