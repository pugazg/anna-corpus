## English Translation

He is fortunate! Truly fortunate! We, who have laboured until we are worn out, sincerely believing that there is no leader other than him, and who have toiled like bullocks at an oil press, did not receive that good fortune. Our friend Achariyar received it!

At the Coimbatore conference I asked what conversation had taken place between the two of them. Many said that I had been wrong to ask. Even after conferring with Achariyar, and without breathing a word about it to any comrade in the movement, he began the work of arranging this marriage in the utmost secrecy.

Somehow the truth emerged. Comrade N. V. Natarajan, his heart boiling and his eyes filled with tears, wrote a letter to Periyar.

The letter was disregarded. Saying that he was leaving Madras for Erode, Periyar went instead to Yercaud and stayed there with Maniammai.

Those here who learned the news trembled and wept. They decided to send a delegation, and the first group set out.

It consisted of Gurusami, editor of *Viduthalai*; Comrade Gurusami of Cuddalore; Thirunavukkarasu of Vellore; Comrade N. V. Natarajan; and Comrade K. K. Neelamegam of Kumbakonam.

They described the bewilderment of the Dravidar Kazhagam members and the ridicule voiced by the public. They pleaded with him. Periyar gave them an explanation and reassurance, not hesitantly but firmly.

He did not speak as one who entertained a doubt that his action might be wrong. He spoke as though asking, “Who are you?”

Another delegation set out: Parthasarathi, editor of *Tamil Nadu*; Kanchi Manimozhiyar, editor of *Porvaal*; Kannabiran, organiser of the Madras Dravidar Kazhagam; and C. V. Rajan, one of the leaders of the anti-Hindi campaign.

To them too Periyar said that he would not change his decision and that, after shouting for four days, the people would subside of their own accord.

Meanwhile, many letters were sent to him. None of this helped to stop the marriage arrangement.

Thinking that the proper course was to express their opinion collectively, the Madras District Dravidar Kazhagam met at Periyar's own residence on 27 June 1949. Its proceedings follow.

After all this, Periyar issued a statement explaining the marriage arrangement that had shocked us, made us sob and provoked the town's ridicule. That explanation clearly demonstrates that the marriage now proposed is not an act that will end with itself; it is advance warning of a future calamity and a great disgrace.

Maniammai is to be not merely Periyar's wife. She is to acquire the qualification to lead the movement.

A new Annie Besant is apparently setting out!

“Periyar! You are entering into an unjust and incompatible marriage, one contrary to principle and fit to be mocked by civilised people. Is this right?” we ask.

In his statement he replies in effect: “Go away! Am I merely marrying? I am entrusting the movement itself to Maniammai. It is precisely in order to entrust it to her that I am marrying.”

It is like boldly telling a distressed woman who cries, “Alas, why are you killing my husband?”: “Do you think I am killing him without cause? I am doing it so that I may possess you as my wife.” Periyar declares that he has resolved somehow to make “her” his lawful “heir” precisely in order to give Maniammai the responsibility and opportunity to manage the movement, its property and its affairs after him.

What is about to take place is not merely an unsuitable marriage. It is also a new coronation.

One wrong is offered as the reason for another. We are not merely expected to bear the disgrace and blame produced by the unsuitable marriage. We must also prepare to work in the future under Maniammai, who is to become our “leader” through this improper method!

“I have been in the movement since the Chengalpattu Self-Respect Conference.”

“I have worked for the movement since the early days of *Kudi Arasu*.”

“I alone remained in my town and faced the hostility of the whole place.”

“Look at this scar; I received it in that disturbance in that town.”

“I conduct propaganda six days a week.”

“I am an expert at writing replies to opposition newspapers.”

There are many in the movement who can make such claims of entitlement. There are those who have worked for a long time.

There are those who have worked skilfully.

There are those who worked after becoming estranged from their families.

There are those who lost property and comfort.

There are those who lost an eye or a hand.

None of them could become the heir. None of them received the opportunity to lead the movement. Maniammai has received it.

By what method? By becoming Periyar's companion.

With what qualification?

Periyar says in his statement: “Maniammai, with whom I have associated for five or six years and in whom I have confidence, and who has conducted herself with genuine attachment to and concern for my welfare and the welfare of the movement.”

That, it seems, is the reason for the unsuitable marriage and, through it, the intolerable arrangement for a new coronation!

He has associated with the lady for five or six years. That association has apparently revealed to him her extraordinary intellectual ability. Periyar has developed enough confidence in her to entrust an immense movement to her.
