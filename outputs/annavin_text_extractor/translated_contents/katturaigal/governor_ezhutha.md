## English Translation

Madras  
Governor's Residence  
*(Special Post)*

Learned Sir,

The distinguished editorial you wrote, overflowing with wisdom and strongly condemning the irresponsible statement by a party member that he intended to conduct an agitation to reduce the Governor's salary, has been brought to the attention of His Excellency the Governor. I wish to inform you that His Excellency has authorised me to convey his pleasure and appreciation.

Having seen the tact, subtlety, and political genius of your editorial, and the emphasis you placed upon responsibility, His Excellency takes pride in having been granted the distinction of serving as Governor of a state in which so talented a person lives.

Your indication that reducing the Governor's salary is impossible under the Constitution, together with your explanation of the relevant section, displays your genius.

His Excellency the Governor is delighted by your knowledge of the Indian Constitution.

His Excellency expects that you will explain to those who engage in activities such as opposing and abolishing Hindi the part of the Indian Constitution that grants Hindi rights and protection, particularly the legal provision under which Hindi is to become the national language within fifteen years, and demonstrate how inappropriate anti-Hindi and Hindi-abolition activities are in these circumstances.

His Excellency commends you for praising his superior qualities, and especially for pointing out that he voluntarily reduced part of his own salary. He greatly hopes that even those who displayed black flags to express their dislike when he arrived to assume office will gain clarity from your excellent editorial and recognise the greatness and sanctity of the Governor's office.

Although your observation that His Excellency voluntarily reduced his salary is indeed a matter of pride, there is concern that, if this point is stressed too often, agitators may demand that His Excellency voluntarily reduce his salary expenditure still further. His Excellency therefore considers it preferable that you not write about it too frequently.

At a time when even Congress Party circles have begun to feel that expenditure on the Governor's salary is a heavy burden, you have courageously written that his salary is neither excessive nor unnecessary and that opposing it is childish. His Excellency conveys his gratitude for this distinguished service, which has secured substantial support for the office itself.

His Excellency expects that you will generally emphasise the necessity for those conducting social, economic, political, and religious agitations to act, like you, with regard for the Indian Constitution and without contradicting it in any way.

His Excellency believes it would be useful to bring to the attention of those who engage in acts such as breaking Pillaiyar idols the constitutional provisions protecting the rights of religious believers.

His Excellency cannot help considering how useful a person possessing such devotion to and training in the Indian Constitution might prove to the government administration.

He firmly believes that an opportunity may soon arise for His Excellency to convey his pleasure and appreciation to you in person.

I am pleased to inform you that His Excellency has instructed me to send you an invitation to the garden party he will give for the committee of legal experts, featuring Miss Vimala's *kurathi* dance.

By order of His Excellency the Governor,

Private Secretary

His Excellency expects that you will frequently publish his speeches and thereby perform a worthy service to the country, enabling the "children" who criticise the great expenditure on his salary to understand how important, commendable, invaluable, and necessary to the nation the Governor's work is.

A. K.  
25 July 1954
