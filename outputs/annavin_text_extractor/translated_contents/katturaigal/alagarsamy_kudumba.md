## A Noble Deed by a Valorous Tamil in Thanjavur

On 29 May 1949, a benefit celebration for presenting a donation to the family of the fearless Alagarsami was conducted with great distinction in Thanjavur, on palace grounds where elephants, horses and infantry had once stood in formation. The generous benefactor who organized the event and presented the donation was the King of Comedy, N. S. Krishnan, a warrior who had not forgotten the dignity of the valorous Tamil.

The King of Comedy had arranged to stage two beautiful and exceptional programmes, *The Great Gandhi Villuppattu* and *The Story of Kindanar*, entertaining the people and giving the resulting proceeds to Alagarsami's family. He asked Comrade C. N. Annadurai to preside over the fundraising celebration. Comrade Annadurai gladly accepted and agreed to discharge the duty.

It was a vast ground enclosed by walls. On one side stood a beautifully thatched shed. Powerful electric lamps cast their light towards it, making the place shine with celestial splendour. Inside the shed was a raised stage, upon which sat the King of Comedy and his associates. Electric lights shone across the immense ground like stars in the sky, and beneath their flood of radiance spread a great multitude. Such was the splendour of the celebration.

Among the many who attended were the revolutionary poet Bharathidasan; C. N. Annadurai; S. Gurusami; S. V. Lingam; Kumbakonam S. K. Sami; Nagai N. P. Kaliappan; T. K. Srinivasan; M. Karunanidhi; R. Nedunchezhiyan; Nanjil Nadu S. K. Sami; T. P. Ponnusami; advocate P. Venugopal; Sir R. S. Sarma; the zamindar of Maruthavanam; A. T. Sultan; Alagarsami's wife and children; movement comrades; officials; leading citizens; and members of the public.

The King of Comedy began with a story about Tenali Raman. Through an elegant introductory address, he made the assembly laugh, then began narrating the history of "Mahatma Gandhi" through villuppattu. Striking the string of a large bow with a small stick held in his hand, he produced sweet music and captivated the people with songs about Gandhi's life.

He then presented *The Story of Kindanar*. Assuming the costume worn by those who conduct religious story entertainments in the country, he performed the tale with song and dance. Time after time the people laughed, plunged into an ocean of delight, and rose again.

Comrade Annadurai, who presided over the celebration, praised in many ways the magnanimity of the King of Comedy, who had come forward at the proper time to help Alagarsami's family in a manner worthy of Tamil dignity. On behalf of the movement, he also thanked him for the assistance.

When N. S. K. spoke, he said the celebration had been conducted specifically to clear the debt of Rs. 6,000 burdening Alagarsami's family. The collection agents had informed him that only Rs. 3,500 remained after expenses. He therefore promised to add Rs. 2,500 from his own pocket and deliver the full Rs. 6,000 to Alagarsami's family within a day or two. Hearing this, the entire audience cheered and acclaimed the King of Comedy's generosity.

For an auction to raise additional money at the celebration, Balu Brothers of Madras had painted and sent a colour portrait of Alagarsami. They were praised for the distinction of donating the picture. Comrade S. Gurusami offered it at auction, and Comrade Duraipandian won it with the highest bid of Rs. 250. That sum too was added to the fund.

Alagarsami's sons, Comrades Ramamurthi and Tulip, were introduced to the public by Comrade S. Gurusami.

The efforts made by the Thanjavur comrades and Comrade T. P. Ponnusami were a principal reason for the celebration's success and deserve the highest praise.

Those assembled raised the cry, "Long live N. S. K., the King of Comedy!" The celebration concluded pleasantly at nine that night.

5 August 1949
