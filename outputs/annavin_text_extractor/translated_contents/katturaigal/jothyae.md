## English Translation

“Disease will depart! Hunger will fly away! Come to Tiruvannamalai.” — Arunachaleswarar

“There is cholera! There is no rice! Do not come!” — The Government

It was a quarrel, and no ordinary quarrel. Nor were those who fought ordinary people like you and me. Their duties were so weighty that they had no time for useless diversions. They could not complete a fixed amount of work and remain idle, nor teach assistants to relieve them while they rested. They bore the whole responsibility themselves. Only those two could supposedly perform their tasks, for which they alone had appeared, and from that day to this they alone have attended to them.

Who were they, what were their duties, and why did they quarrel? Nearly everyone in Tamil Nadu knows the annual flame lit on the summit at Tiruvannamalai on the Karthigai Krithigai day. That event arose from this quarrel. One combatant was Brahma, charged with creation; the other Vishnu, charged with preservation. Meeting unexpectedly at Tiruvannamalai, they did not exchange courtesies about their work. Vishnu declared preservation superior and himself the greater; four-faced Brahma proclaimed the superiority of creation and himself the greater.

Unable to decide who was greater, they quarrelled for a long time. Words became blows. Mountains shattered, the cosmic roof split, sun and moon hid in fear, Adisesha writhed, stars fell, worlds lost their order, rivers including the Ganga dried, seven seas became mud, moving creatures perished, and Mount Meru shook. Creation and preservation stopped, leaving the destroyer without work. Searching for the other two, he found them fighting at Tiruvannamalai, understood that conciliation would fail, and proposed a test that would restore all three functions.

The destroyer appeared there as a column of fire and declared: “Brahma and Vishnu, whoever first discovers the top or bottom of this radiance shall be judged greater.” Vishnu became a boar and burrowed downward; Brahma became a swan and flew upward.

The boar travelled at a thousand *kadam* each half-instant through Atala, Vitala, Sutala, Talatala, Mahatala, Rasatala and Patala, beyond Mahabali's Bhogavati and even the world called Adakesuram, but found no bottom. He returned and admitted failure. Brahma flew for ages without seeing the top and stopped exhausted. Then a screw-pine flower fallen from Siva's matted hair descended past him. Asked where it came from, it said it had left Siva's hair forty thousand divine years earlier.

Stunned, Brahma regretted the contest, then used his creative talent to devise a plan. He asked the flower to testify falsely that he had reached the summit. It agreed. Returning with it, Brahma told Vishnu, who had searched below for a thousand divine years, that he had found the summit and brought the flower as witness.

The fiery mountain exploded. All worlds trembled. Siva emerged and accused Brahma of lying through arrogance, laughing angrily. His laughter shook all universes, dimmed sun and moon, dispersed the seven clouds and caused more distress than the mountain's explosion. He had once burned the three cities with such laughter. What a dangerous Siva! Whether someone errs knowingly or unknowingly, this supposedly gracious being becomes angry and laughs, and both anger and laughter bring ruin. Perhaps this is why Tamil people refuse to accept him as their god. Anger is natural, but it should not produce such horrors. Who, hearing that Siva's anger at Brahma shook the four lands, would not say, “We do not want such a god”?

What power that laugh possessed! Worlds shook, the elephants of the eight directions cried, sun and moon lost light, clouds vanished, stable things became unstable, trees burned, and gods were startled. If a grinding stone flies through the sky, what fate awaits a banyan leaf? These are not Siva's only exploits, but recounting all of them would become another great story, so let us return to the subject.

Siva cursed Brahma: because he falsely claimed to see the summit, he would henceforth have neither temple nor worship on earth. He cursed the screw-pine too: because it joined the liar and gave false evidence, Siva would never touch it and people wearing it would be unable to enjoy its fragrance. When Brahma repented and sought pardon, Siva granted that although he would have no temples or direct worship, worship offered to Brahmins would accrue to him.

Siva then ordered the fiery mountain to shrink. Vishnu and Brahma asked that its unbearable radiance also be removed and the mountain made ordinary. Once this was done, they requested an enduring sign that it had been a mountain of fire. Siva promised that every year on Karthigai Krithigai a light would appear spontaneously at its summit, and those seeing it would live free from hunger and disease. Such are the Puranic “truths” about the Tiruvannamalai flame.

What happens today? Believers say the light appears by Siva's command; we say it does not, and are therefore called atheists. We have seen the flame, but it is not divine radiance arising by itself. It is an artificial light made by people like us. Why does saying so make us atheists? The believers' action contains a thousand times more deceit and falsehood than Brahma's trick and the flower's lie, yet some glare at us more fiercely than the burner of Tripura.

The summit flame does not arise by itself as Siva promised, nor do those who see it live without hunger and disease. Hunger and starvation dance everywhere. This year the government orders visitors to bring enough rice for their own food. Because Tiruvannamalai has cholera, it also orders people not to gather there. Once the deity declared that seeing the flame would drive hunger and disease away; today the government declares that going there will expose people to hunger and disease, and tells them to stay away. If anyone compares these two divine pronouncements and asks why such a Purana should remain, the devotees become venomous serpents.

If we compare the origin of fire and lamplight with nature, who knows what those who worship Siva as fire will say? A lamp is indispensable in human life, dispelling darkness and providing light. It has steadily improved until light can now be obtained instantly. In the earliest uncivilised state, people neither needed nor possessed a distinct lamp and could not understand its benefits. Many objects now used by humanity likewise once did not exist, or people did not know how to use them.

Let us briefly explain how people first obtained light. Saivites call Siva fire itself and say he appeared as fire at Tiruvannamalai to resolve Brahma and Vishnu's contest. Saiva texts repeatedly compare him with fire: “O radiance, O flame, surrounding lamp,” “rich flame of the celestials,” “body of fire,” and “the fire-coloured one.” We must therefore examine fire and light.

Fire is counted among the five material elements: earth, water, fire, air and space, from which living bodies are formed. Yet fire did not originally exist as a lamp giving visible light, but as heat without separately manifested illumination. Only much later, after gaining some civilisation, did human beings discover that heat could produce fire and light. Fire was first obtained in forests through the friction of hard trees such as bamboo. Later, people learnt to strike stones together. Until Western inventions such as matches recently reached this country, our people continued producing fire by rubbing stones and wood.

These facts make false the religious claim that an agency called God created the world and everything in it, and likewise the assertion that Siva is inherently fire or appears as fire. Light did not exist in its usable form from the world's beginning; humans discovered how to produce it through effort and intelligence at a much later stage. To imagine an eternal Siva predating the world as light or fire is therefore wholly false.

Fire, one of the five elements, was initially heat rather than visible illumination; civilised people gave it the form of light through intelligent effort. The statement that a self-existent Siva was naturally fiery is empty pretence. One might, however, accept in another sense that his body radiated feverish heat, for Saiva stories depict Siva as deeply devoted to sensual enjoyment.

One story says that while walking in a flower garden with Uma, a pleasant breeze aroused Siva. Seeking prolonged enjoyment, he became a male elephant and Uma a female elephant; after indulging as he pleased, he fathered Pillaiyar, the elder god. Tirugnanasambandar explains this in the hymn beginning “Pidiyathan uru.” Another story says Siva laboured without rest day and night for a thousand divine years to beget the younger god Murugan. Another says he pursued Vishnu in the form of Mohini, and from the semen that fell before union he created the god Ayyanar. The *Periya Puranam* says he asked for and took the wife of Iyarpagai Nayanar. Another story says he approached King Vallala at Tiruvannamalai seeking a woman for intercourse, and when none was available the king gave his own wife.

Since the great Saiva books praising the faith and Siva contain these stories, the rational world may accept that his body, excessively devoted to sensuality and afflicted by venereal heat, radiated fire, and that this inspired the fiery form. But the thoughtful world will never accept the impure claim that Siva literally possesses a form of divine light.

Consider also the enormous expenditure on today's Tiruvannamalai flame festival. Can anyone with even a little reason fail to ask how the hunger and disease raging through the country are to end? Those who see how many pots of cow's ghee, not even buffalo ghee, and how many yards of new cloth are wasted to display this false light will say that a country embodying hunger and inhabited by disease no longer needs such wasteful festivals, whether conducted in God's name, financed by the rich or supported by rulers. Yet theatrical devotees who never forget his feet will continue their hymn: “Everything is his doing; can we do anything?” But...!
