## English Translation

The celebrated Courtallam Falls of the south presents a captivating spectacle. Such a sight has helped writers like Kalyanasundaranar compose poetry, describing it as Mother Nature's angry turbulence or a flood of desire overflowing with love.

“I saw Courtallam Falls! How shall I describe that sight? What music equals its sound, and what power rivals its force? How far the droplets fall! Those who see it are entranced by its beauty. What shall we call nature? Nature is nature itself. Cherish Mother Nature and rejoice in her majesty!”

If a warrior of the pen had written in this fashion until now, I think he would no longer stop there. He would conclude: “It is at that very Courtallam that our Presidency's Premier is taking his rest. That fact alone can demonstrate Courtallam's exceptional distinction.”

## Everything Is His Doing

When the public was discussing the fall of the Bihar and United Provinces ministries with excitement, I heard that our Premier had become “absorbed” in the spectacle of Courtallam Falls. At once I too wished to visit Courtallam.

The echo of a *Chintamani* song sounds: “Nothing happens as you think; everything is His doing, so do not drink the milk of imagined hope.” We shall see. Perhaps one day I shall visit Courtallam.

But I tell you truly, and I believe you too will agree: we may go to Courtallam at any time, but may we never receive an opportunity like the one Achariar chose.

What an occasion! Congress was holding a session at a cost of seven and a half lakhs. Its Rashtrapati travelled in a chariot drawn by fifty-one bulls. We do not understand why both the Viceroy and Congress developed a love for bulls at the same time. Women national volunteers stood in formation. Yes, Subhas Babu had still not married.

At that very time, the Bihar and United Provinces ministers issued orders to release people languishing in prison. The wicked Viceroy obstructed them, so the ministers resigned and arrived at Haripura. Gandhi blessed them and said Bombay would be next, followed by Madras. Telegrams flew: “Come immediately!”

Would you or I be at Courtallam at such a moment? Is Achariar an ordinary man? Does our Premier normally concern himself with bodily hardship? He once rose at dawn and washed his clothes without even caring that a *Madras Mail* photographer was taking his picture. Would such a man mind the hardship of travelling to Haripura?

But fever prevented him from going. Yes, sir, fever! What fever it was, I do not know. On an earlier occasion when the Working Committee met in Calcutta, he had fever too. If you ask what that fever was and what this one is, how would I know? I can only call the earlier one “Calcutta fever” and this one “Haripura fever.” Am I Dr Rajan?

## Madras's Disgrace

Because Gandhi said the storm would next reach Madras, I expected a crisis here too and thought the ministers would resign. Do they not always keep resignation letters in their pockets?

Even apart from Bihar and the United Provinces, any Madras ministry counting a sense of honour among its principles ought to have resigned and run away already. Why? The Governor has struck the ministry on the head repeatedly. How long can it remain counting the blows while also claiming to preserve its dignity?

Consider the ordinary matter of the summer move to the hills. Even here the ministry performs a somersault and boasts that no earth touched its moustache. It is true that no earth adhered: a moustache must exist before earth can stick to it.

The Governor will go, his employees will go, his retinue will go, and the white officials must go. The official deities called government secretaries will go. Who alone will not go? The ministers. Is this self-respect or courage? It is pure madness, the sacred sport of gods whose thighs tremble.

What heroism lies in saying, “Let everyone whom we cannot prevent from going to the hills go; I alone shall not come”? What difference is there between the gramophone comic song, “No earring to put in my ear, no cloth to tie and wear; I won't come to the festival,” and the ministry's Ooty visit?

With what face will these ministers go to Ooty? They crowed: “We shall stop the expense of summer residence and abolish the official deities' migration to the hills.” If they go now, whom will they see there? What will the Governor's butler think? Will they not encounter the very people whom they boasted they would prevent from going? What greater disgrace could there be?

That is why the ministers did not go to the hills. Had they gone, they could only return as from Tirupati after beholding the deity, heads shaven, sectarian marks painted on their foreheads, crying “Govinda! Govinda!”

If they wished to govern with honour, the ministers could have obstructed the hill migration. If the Governor defied them, they could have thrown their resignation letters in his face and emerged as heroes. Instead, they went to Courtallam to enjoy the cool breeze and reduce the heat of politics. What should that be called? Consult a dictionary and apply whichever term seems appropriate.

## Look at the Debt Relief Bill

Even if we leave that aside, should not the Governor's treatment of the debt bill at least provoke the ministers' honour? What difference exists between what the Governor did and the threat: “Will you leave respectfully, or shall I summon the big man?”

Any person without the nature of a leech or tortoise ought simply to say: “Here is the bill. We enacted it. Accept it if you will; otherwise, here is our resignation.” Instead, are they to burn incense before him? Is this courage?

While so much exists inside the Presidency, another whirlwind blows from outside. Did Congress not formerly say that if one province developed a breach, all provincial ministries would resign? Everyone who had not understood Congress's deceptive drama expected that. What happened instead? The Madras ministry remains stuck to its seat.

## Carrying the Kavadi for the Viceroy

The words granted by Gandhi's inner voice did not come true. New bargaining began. Congress leaders started worshipfully asking the Viceroy, “Will you not grant us a divine audience?” Newspapers began acting as brokers and urging, “Conduct the negotiations quickly.”

Imagine a hawker who makes promises, creates trouble, loses his way, leaves honour at home and cheats those who kneel before him. If we stand in the market and expose his rotten merchandise item by item, people will learn the truth and begin kicking him. The trader will abuse us: “Where did this fellow come from like Saturn?” He may even send hirelings to throw stones. If fear makes us retreat, he will resume shouting and sell his stale goods to the town. Only when his fraud is exposed does his wailing pierce the ear.

Those who see our meetings, clutch their stomachs, shout “Jai!” until the heavens split, and later earn public contempt are in the same condition.

A Self-Respecter lays out the facts: “Do you see the Brahmin conspiracy? Do roughly nineteen of twenty-six government jobs go to them? Is that tolerable or just?” If an extreme nationalist had an answer, he could hold a meeting after ours and refute it. But he does not. His aim is to stop our meeting itself.

The only goods in his possession are British injustice, Gandhi's greatness, Mother India's lament, the burden of taxation and the glory of the yellow ballot box. After our meeting, what else can he display? People who have grown weary of seeing the same goods for fifty years ask him: “Leave all that aside, brother. Why have taxes not fallen? Why impose a new tax? Why does the Brahmin grow fat? Why do election promises fly away on the wind? Why have drinkers not declined in Salem? Why this ruinous Hindi? Why the *Vande Mataram* that antagonizes Muslims?”

What can the nationalist do? The instant he sees us, he wrings his hands and tries to break up our meeting, thinking: “The Self-Respecter will tell the people the facts and reveal that our merchandise is rotten. What shall we do?”

How long can this continue? Their trade will last only until the people understand that this corruption ruins public life. Once the truth is understood, these impostors' dye will certainly wash out. More than half has faded already.

## The Flourishes of the “Tails”

The weekly journals *Tamil Mani* and *Nam Nadu* say they possess unshakable faith in Congress's foundational principles. Yet, unable to tolerate the sight of Congress in Tamil Nadu serving as a Brahmin puppet while intrigue, selfishness and Brahminism run rampant, they occasionally expose its atrocities.

Fearful that Brahmin Congress and its hirelings may abuse it and consign it to a corner for revealing corruption, *Tamil Mani* periodically attacks Self-Respecters and the movement's leader to display its Congress devotion somehow. Self-Respecters do not become angry; they only feel pity, for the journal can survive only by abusing us. When *Tamil Mani* exposes corruption, we reproduce those reports in our papers. Congress manipulators then strike it on the head, and it immediately turns and growls at us.

As for *Nam Nadu*, it condemns injustices committed by Brahmins in Congress's name. Its editors are “scalded cats”: in public life they have experienced the communal consciousness of Congress Brahmins, disregarded true friends' warnings, gone there and suffered.

When *Nam Nadu* began publishing the truth, Brahmin Congress members reportedly followed custom by accusing it of being a Self-Respect journal financed by Comrade E. V. Ramasamy. *Nam Nadu* indignantly denied this. But if the lash-like harassment from Congress Brahmins becomes too great, *Nam Nadu* may suddenly study “market conditions” and leap upon us instead. We shall not be surprised if it does.

We state firmly, however, that *Nam Nadu* is not a Self-Respect journal. There can be no connection between Self-Respect and that paper. Had its editors and associates possessed self-respect, would they work through these methods already tried and proved failures?

## No Reason for Indignation

Still, *Nam Nadu* has no reason to become indignant at the accusation. Why? It is nothing new in Tamil Nadu to say that every account of Congress injustice, whoever states it and whenever, was born in Erode and that the speaker belongs to Comrade E. V. Ramasamy's lineage. Irresponsible people are not alone in saying so; responsible leaders say the same.

What did the Honourable Rajagopalachari say about opposition to Hindi? He did not take the opponents' reasons and explain why they were wrong. He could not. That is not Congress custom. If this is the leaders' conduct, need anything be said about their “tails”?

Thus, seeing *Nam Nadu* complain that some people conduct false propaganda “without a nerve in the tongue, fear in the heart or trembling in the body,” we can only say our prediction has come true and the customary blessing is proceeding.

If *Nam Nadu* imagined that those abusing it possessed nerves in their tongues, fear in their hearts and trembling in their bodies, it has met this disappointment through its own error. What nerve can their tongues have? What fear their hearts? When scalded cats themselves keep circling around them, why should their bodies tremble?

Their intention is not merely to abuse and suppress journals such as *Nam Nadu*, but to set them upon us. Brahminism is the elder brother of the Briton's divide-and-rule policy. We do not know whether *Nam Nadu* will become its prey and spring at us. We ask only that it show at least a little firmness in stating the truth.

*Kudi Arasu*, 27 February 1938

## Translator's Notes

This is a composite political column. The scenic title `குற்றால நீர்வீழ்ச்சி` leads into satire about Premier C. Rajagopalachari's absence from the Haripura Congress session and then several related comments on Congress governance and Tamil journalism.

`ஆச்சாரியார்` refers to C. Rajagopalachari. “Rashtrapati” denotes the Congress president, then Subhas Chandra Bose. The references are to the 1938 Haripura Congress and disputes between provincial Congress ministries and the colonial Governor or Viceroy. `காவடி` is a ritual burden carried in devotion and is used satirically for supplication to the Viceroy. The “yellow ballot box” refers to an election symbol or ballot arrangement of the period.

The heading OCRed as `ஈவாலர்'கள் வாய் வீச்சு` is contextually `வாலர்கள் வாய் வீச்சு`, describing subordinates or “tails” of Congress leaders. `தமிழ்மணி` and `நம்நாடு` are journal titles. `சூடுண்ட பூனை` (“a scalded cat”) describes someone made wary by painful experience.

Several lines are damaged: the *Chintamani* lyric, the comic gramophone lyric, a market-hawker phrase, and punctuation around quoted newspaper titles. Their contextual sense is translated conservatively. The political allegations and statistics are presented as the author's claims, not independently verified conclusions; the corrected Tamil OCR remains unchanged.
