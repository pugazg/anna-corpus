## English Translation

Days of delight that brought the future before our very eyes! Days that displayed to the world the mountain-like stature of the Kazhagam so vividly that one might wonder whether it was dream or memory! Days when Dravidam, which had shone as the cradle of the world's civilizations in an age beyond the reach of memory, raised its head again in pride after many centuries! Auspicious days when the young bulls of the Kazhagam gathered as though oceans had merged and established a public assembly! Days when the cry of liberation rose to make the heavens tremble, leaving our opponents bewildered as though mountains had collided! Heroic days when we heard our younger brothers pledge that they would never forget the ideal, whether in prosperity or decline! Golden days when mothers swelled with pride and said, "Are not these young lions the children we bore?" It was during these four days that the separate Dravidian nation came into being.

Yes, the separate Dravidian nation has come into being!

On the thirteenth, as the Kazhagam flag rose towards the blue sky and the sun reached the middle of the heavens at noon as though approaching to welcome it, a separate nation was born. It is true that today's rulers, descendants of the degraded men who carried stones, have not yet saluted that flag. It is true that Dravida Nadu has not yet obtained membership in the United Nations. But the day when it receives the North's salute and membership in the United Nations is not far away. The Dravidian people's parliament is the state conference. Whether Northerner, degraded ruler, or great power, all must eventually bow before its resolution. No force in the world can prevail against the united decision of a people. That is why we say that the separate Dravidian nation has come into being.

The separate nation has arisen; a government of its own must now be formed. The ship has been built; its engine must now be fitted. The site for the mansion has been bought and its foundation laid; walls must rise and upper storeys must be built. That work too must continue.

*Dravida Nadu*, 22 December [year illegible in OCR].

The publication year in the final OCR line is illegible (`௮]`). It is not silently inferred. The phrase about the rulers as descendants of those who "carried stones" is preserved literally because it is a deliberate historical/political allusion in the source.
