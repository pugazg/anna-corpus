## English Translation

It is indeed welcome that the controversy over bribery in the Madras Corporation is becoming increasingly acute. Everyone who desires honest and sacred public life will support its reaching a crisis, for only when the crisis matures will the truth emerge.

We have already pointed out that both the Local Bodies Subcommittee which investigated corruption and bribery in the Corporation and the Tamil Nadu Congress Working Committee which delivered judgement have acted in a spirit of retaliation.

Comrade Sami Venkatachalam Chettiar's objection is that the inquiry committee was not properly constituted. When comrades Virudhunagar Muthusami, Madurai Sahib and Paganeri Swaminathan were appointed as a committee to investigate local bodies controlled by Congress, *Dinamani* itself condemned the appointment. It said that those appointed had no experience in local administration and that the Tamil Nadu Congress Committee had erred in making them committee members.

The Corporation-corruption controversy was raised by comrade Muthuranga Mudaliar, uncle of comrade Bhaktavatsalam, together with Bhaktavatsalam and the *Dinamani* group. Perhaps feeling that it would look unbecoming now to object to the Congress Local Bodies Inquiry Committee it had initially condemned, once that committee was appointed to investigate the controversy they had raised, *Dinamani* performed a very delicate somersault.

It said that it had indeed once condemned the committee, but had done so believing that it was to investigate local administration generally. Now that it had become known that the committee's purpose was to investigate Congress administration, *Dinamani* said the committee possessed the ability to conduct such an inquiry and that it was proper for it to investigate Corporation corruption.

But even this counterfeit explanation from *Dinamani* does not hold water. The committee was not appointed to investigate local bodies in general. It was appointed specifically to investigate local administrations captured by Congress. If its members went to conduct inquiries in bodies not controlled by Congress, who would take the committee seriously?

Thus *Dinamani*'s position cannot be accepted. Moreover, as one of those punished has pointed out, comrade Muthuranga Mudaliar, who either made the accusation or was closely connected with its author, presided over the inquiry committee that investigated it and then, as president of the Tamil Nadu Congress Working Committee, delivered judgement upon it. That judgement therefore has no validity in law.

Even assuming that proper disciplinary procedure had invited the accused to appear before the inquiry committee and state their objections, they refused because they maintained that the committee itself had not been properly constituted. In those circumstances, it was a grave error for the Tamil Nadu Congress Working Committee simply to accept the inquiry committee's recommendations and deliver judgement. Proper procedure required communicating the inquiry findings to those concerned, hearing their explanation, and only then deciding.

There is more. Once the complaint against Congress members of the Corporation had been conveyed to the Corporation Congress Party, comrade Bhaktavatsalam could have sought a remedy from the Madras District Congress Committee or the Tamil Nadu Congress Committee if a confidential inquiry failed to produce a satisfactory result. He did not do so.

Instead, he wrote provocatively in the newspapers, hurling unfounded accusations at councillors as respectable as himself, and he organised a public meeting and agitated in violation of Congress rules. *Dinamani*, whose editor was a Congress member of the Assembly, stood behind Bhaktavatsalam.

Although the Tamil Nadu Congress Working Committee recognised in its judgement that the conduct of Bhaktavatsalam and *Dinamani* was wrong, it took no disciplinary action and contented itself with expressing "regret." Consequently, the actions of both the inquiry committee and the Tamil Nadu Congress Working Committee are objectionable throughout and bear the character of retaliation.

It appears that the Corporation Congress Party meeting recognised these facts and adopted three resolutions. The report may be seen elsewhere. Their substance was:

1. The accused had not been given an opportunity to reply.
2. A new inquiry committee must be appointed.
3. Party leader comrade Bhaktavatsalam must be asked to substantiate the letters he published in the newspapers accusing certain councillors.

Those resolutions must have been adopted at the party meeting presided over by comrade Sathyamurti, who had been elected leader of the Corporation Congress Party.

In a sub-editorial on Saturday the twentieth, *Dinamani* wrote in a "Hitlerian" tone that it was a tremendous error for comrade Sathyamurti and certain others, after his election as leader, to speak recklessly at the party meeting about the Working Committee's resolution on Corporation corruption; that speaking disparagingly of the resolution or attempting to prevent it from taking effect was a grave danger.

This suggests that strong argument and counterargument over the Tamil Nadu Congress Working Committee's decision had taken place within the Corporation Congress Party.

Furthermore, all those subjected to disciplinary action were non-Brahmins. Although non-Brahmins formed the greater number in the Corporation Congress Party, Brahmin influence was predominant. Fifteen Congress Party members had petitioned the Mayor to appoint a Corporation inquiry committee. Five of them now appear to have withdrawn their names. That itself is evidence of the strength of Brahmin influence within the Corporation Congress Party.

If even a Corporation Congress Party meeting so dominated by Brahmin influence nevertheless adopted the three resolutions described above, one may easily imagine how much resentment and dissatisfaction the Congress councillors felt over the Tamil Nadu Congress Working Committee's decision.

The inquiry and judgement concerning Corporation corruption recall a "Hitlerian court." No one has yet denied the reported complaints that both the public inquiry committee and the Tamil Nadu Congress Committee ignored allegations against many people. Those complaints can be seen in the Congress newspapers of Madras.

Therefore, if corruption in the Madras Corporation is to be fully exposed, an impartial inquiry committee must inevitably be established. Even if, at the request of the Corporation Congress Party, an inquiry committee is appointed under Congress auspices, it does not seem that every fact will emerge.
