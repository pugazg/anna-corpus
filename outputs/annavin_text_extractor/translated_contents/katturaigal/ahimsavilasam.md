## English Translation

Tamil title: “அகிம்சா விலாசம்” திறக்கப்படட்டும்!

Storage pots are rolled away. The front entrance of the house is demolished. The back gate is removed. Everything in the house is declared confiscated. The elderly head of the household trembles and, without intending to, asks the marauders, "Why, sirs, are you doing all this?"

"Where is your son? Where have you hidden him? Tell us! Tell us!" they roar. The old man weeps. Seeing their mother's tears and their grandfather's grief, the children wail aloud.

* * *

Events of this kind have occurred in great numbers at the homes of Communist leaders. Enraged because those living underground had not fallen into their hands, the "arrows" of Congress rule committed such outrages. Ask about them in Thanjavur district and we will receive varied catalogues of suffering: guns thundering in the streets; a man shot while sleeping on his front veranda; people chased, shot and felled; the misery inflicted upon Adi-Dravidian women; and the "nonviolent" entertainment staged throughout the region. These remain engraved in the people's forum as indelible emblems of shame acquired by the Congressmen, scars that will not disappear.

A storm was stirred in the fields by comrades of the Communist Party. As a result, the great body of Adi-Dravidian agricultural labourers leapt up as though sleeping tigers had been roused. In that fury many houses were demolished, damage was caused, and people in various places were forced to endure many improper acts.

When the agricultural people rose in strength, not only the plantation owners but also the "little" mirasidars who acted as their lackeys, and even those owning small portions of land, suffered severely.

We believed this suffering would not bring the achievement of the ideal. We said that the policy and its practical course must change.

At the same time, however, we were distressed by the frenzy of Congress rule, which let loose guns and wicked deeds upon farmers who had risen like valiant tigers and lions provoked to rage. We condemned it and raised voices of protest.

"The trouble caused by the Communists can no longer be endured!"

"Therefore we are enacting a security law."

"Anyone who feeds or shelters those living underground will also be imprisoned."

"We shall imprison people without inquiry!"

When the Congress government arrogantly enacted such laws, there was not a day on which we failed to condemn it, nor a meeting at which we failed to say, "No! No!"

"Do not take the path of repression. Do not commit injustice. You will be destroyed," we warned them. They turned furiously upon those who warned them and presented us too with bullets and baton blows. If we spoke, they threatened us; if we assembled, they drove us away. If we wrote, they showed us prison bars. If we resisted, they shot and crushed us.

"This durbar is the grave you are digging for yourselves. You are sounding the death knell of democracy and nurturing fascism. You will experience the consequences," the people's forum spat at Congress rule.

The consequence today is defeat wherever one looks. All the leading men have had their noses cut. Among Congress leaders sounds the anxious refrain: "Will a Congress ministry be formed? Will we govern again?"

The suffering people had sincerely trusted the yellow ballot box. They realized that the yellow box had severed the marriage pendant: it had governed recklessly, tormented them, disregarded the law, filled the prisons, raged and tortured. They therefore drove away Congress candidates and elected those who stood against Congress.

One of those elected is Manali Kandasami. He was put to trial by the repression of the Congress government. His house remains without a door even today. His father lies bedridden. Yet no one knows where Kandasami himself is, and the government still has not withdrawn the warrant against him.

* * *

It is not Manali alone. For political reasons, countless others remain locked in prison, branded "security prisoners," or subjected to orders for their arrest. Though the "Abode of Nonviolence" now faces expulsion from the seat of power, it has not relaxed its repressive durbar against them. It has not withdrawn the warrants, released those languishing in prison, or removed the label "security prisoners."

Some of these "prisoners" have now been elected members of the Legislative Assembly by people victimized by the disgrace of Congress rule.

But the "Abode of Nonviolence" does not appear to have given this any thought.

Many walk about on parole, many remain in prison, many live underground, and many stand in the prisoners' dock.

"Must this cruelty continue any longer?" the progressive fronts ask with one voice. But those whose noses have been cut have still not relaxed either their rigidity or their severity.

Just as a dying lamp flares brightly, the disciples of Gandhi apparently wish to conduct their durbar until their very last moment.

This course will disgrace them still further and enable the people's forum to place upon them an imperishable garland of contempt. Times are changing and opinions have changed, but the "Abode of Nonviolence" imagines that it alone may remain as it is.

Last week, the Travancore-Cochin government decided to release all the security prisoners against whom it had taken action. Ten of them had been elected to the Legislative Assembly.

The "Abode of Nonviolence" must quickly follow that path. It must release every political prisoner, withdraw the cases, and allow the Communist comrades, who seek to move with a new policy and renewed vigour, to live freely.

On behalf of the growing voice of renewal, we convey this final warning to Congress rule: let the "Abode of Nonviolence" be opened; let the gateway of repression be closed.

Published in *Dravida Nadu*, 3 February 1952.
