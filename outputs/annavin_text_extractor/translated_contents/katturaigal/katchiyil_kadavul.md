காரியம், எந்த முறையில் எந்தக் காலத்தில் இங்குச் சொல்லப்பட்ட
கடவுள் - மதம் ஆகியவற்றின் உதவியைக் கொண்டு நடைபெற்றது

கடவுளின் கடாட்சமும், மதத்தின் மகிமையும், மங்கிப்

## English Translation

We learn that some within our party hold this view: for many centuries our people have been trained to make God and religion the foundation of every sphere of life and conduct every affair accordingly. Therefore, those who wish to work for the people's advancement should neither criticize nor oppose God and religion, and anyone who does so should not remain in the party. We must examine how far this assertion is true.

If they genuinely believe in and are attached to God and religion, should they not first investigate intelligently the truth about these things and how they have helped, or now help, people's lives? Should they not demonstrate with evidence what aspect of life was ever conducted, by what method and at what time, with the assistance of the God and religion spoken of here? Should they not at least show how any connection between God and religion and the people's practical life occurs?

God's grace and religion's glory appear only in writing upon faded pages. Should these advocates not consider whether either brings even one practical benefit to human life?

The doctrine of God and religious teaching reach only one conclusion: God punishes and blesses living beings. If punishment and blessing are divine actions, does such a being fit the definition of God? When all written definitions of divinity are assembled, must we not conclude that no such entity can exist in any fashion?

God is said to be good, to do good to everyone, never even inadvertently contemplate evil, and benefit good and bad, learned and unlearned, seeing and blind alike. Why would such a good God incarnate to protect the virtuous and destroy the wicked?

One answer says that just as all water reaching the sea becomes salty, every life reaching God acquires divinity. Another says God assumes an incarnation and punishes evildoers to prevent them following evil paths and lead them toward good. But why did a good God, love embodied, create beings who commit evil? Would one who desires only goodness create lives capable of wicked deeds?

Where was the God who created the world and its beings before creation? In what state, and doing what? Why would a God said to have made both disease and medicine first inflict disease upon living beings and then produce remedies? He does not stop there: people are told to worship him and make offerings so the illness will be cured. Can such a God exist anywhere? Ought such a being even be permitted to exist?

If a created person cannot know who created him, is that the person's fault or God's? A God creates someone with intelligence incapable of recognizing him, then blames and punishes that person for failing to know and worship him. Is that not an unintelligent act? If God gives knowledge, is he not also responsible for ignorance? A crime committed through that ignorance therefore belongs to God.

If worldly wrongdoing receives fitting punishment in this world, why must another world called hell exist? If a person punishes a crime caused by divine action, does he not violate God's order? If this world is the place for both pleasure and pain, what sense is there in separate upper and lower worlds for their experience?

If minor pleasure and pain are experienced here and great bliss and suffering elsewhere, should this world not likewise contain separate realms for its two lesser experiences? Since people enjoying pleasure and enduring pain coexist here, would not one other world suffice for both greater experiences?

If this world belongs to minor pleasure and another to supreme bliss, why was Sundarar, one of the sixty-three saints, cast from the flood of eternal bliss into this world's mud for the sake of one small pleasure? Who or what produced that desire in him there? Religious texts call supreme bliss imperishable and sensual pleasure perishable. If one immersed in eternal bliss can be sent into a perishable world, what do those descriptions mean?

Can pleasure and pain be divided into great and small? What seems greater or lesser to different people at different times remains pleasure or pain to the one experiencing it. One can recognize pleasure as pleasure and suffering as suffering, but how measure magnitude between them? The distress of one person lacking sugar for milk and another lacking salt for stale gruel is equally real relative to each condition.

Therefore, are claims of lesser and supreme pleasures, separate worlds for experiencing them, and a God ruling over them anything but false speech?

Can God assign a world of pleasure to the good and suffering to the wicked? If that is his nature, is he good or evil? Why would a God who desires universal goodness create wicked people, evil acts and the tools required for them? If drinking liquor is harmful, can the creator of liquor be considered wise? Is it wrong to call the God who created a burglar's crowbar a consummate thief? If God is good, why does he hold trident, spear, sword, discus and other weapons?

If something called God exists, whose fault is our inability to know it? If the God who made us did not identify himself, or identified himself in a manner we could not understand, who can deny that the fault is his? When a child cannot recognize its father, whose fault is it?

Suppose God is unlike other beings and cannot be known unless revealed. Who reveals him: God or someone else? If God does, no one has seen in action, apart from written claims, either such a being or proof that it reveals itself. If another person reveals God, that person must belong to humanity like us. Why then can we, who do not differ from that person, neither know nor see God?

Is it God's nature to give some people the capacity to know him and deny it to others? What then becomes of texts saying God exists identically in every life? If God is present in all without preference or aversion, is it not his fault to reveal himself to one and remain hidden from others?

All religious teachers said to have seen God were human. Why should what appeared to their eyes remain invisible to others of the same humanity? Why did God create people unable or unqualified to see him, then punish them as wicked for that inability? After saying human birth was divinely granted so one might see and worship God and attain supreme life, can anything more contrary to reason be imagined than a God who makes himself impossible to see?

Does anyone among us who claims to have seen God show him to us? No. We receive the easy answer: “You have not yet acquired the maturity to see God.” Who creates that maturity? If we do, why need a guide to reveal God? If God does, why must we undertake the effort? If not an atom moves without him, why should we move?

Can an all-pervading God, leaving no room even for movement, move himself or move us? Once it is decided there is no place without God, is it intelligent to command us to “see” or “know” him? One can seek something absent here but present elsewhere. A pearl-oyster absent from land exists in the sea and may naturally be sought there. But who can seek something eternally and identically filling land, sea and everything? Where, how and why?

If observer, revealer and revealed object are declared one, can they also exist as three separate entities? Why should they?

We are now told an “inner voice” exists and visits only certain people to speak. Whether it differs from the God of religious texts need not occupy us; our purpose is to understand its character. Comrade Gandhi at Wardha says this inner voice frequently comes and speaks to him. He has not said in what form, at what time or in what language, and we have not begged him to do so.

We make only one charge: is the inner voice's residence near Wardha but far from Erode? Why does it speak to Gandhi of Wardha but not Ramasamy of Erode? If Ramasamy is wicked, correcting him is surely its work. If it cannot correct him, why did God or the inner voice create such a person?

How can something unable to correct wrongdoing be accepted as omnipotent God or as an inner voice teaching “This is good, that is bad; do this, do not do that”? Otherwise must defenders of religion and God say that Gandhi repeatedly goes astray and therefore receives correction, while Ramasamy never strays and consequently receives no visit?

If every human effort succeeds solely through divine grace, why do some succeed and others fail? The answer given is that people undertake unnecessary or impossible tasks and therefore receive no grace. But who placed within them the thought of attempting those tasks? Was it not the God present in every atom?

If God bears no responsibility for such thoughts, can there be any connection between God and humanity? If one event occurs by grace and another without God's knowledge, what supports the scriptural claim that God stands as knowledge within knowledge and alone performs and causes everything?

Could people perform an act unknown to, or believed unknown to, a good God who is their intelligence's intelligence and prevents error? If so, why rage at the rationalist who calls both that claim and other religious assertions false?

If Dravidian people possess a God and Vedas or Agamas explaining that God, should he not know at least one Dravidian language, especially Tamil, the ancestral language among them? Should not those scriptures be in a Dravidian language and their authors Dravidians?

Some may claim Dravidians learned Sanskrit and wrote their scriptures in it. Such speech cannot withstand investigation. Many religions, gods and explanatory texts exist in the world, visibly created by each country's people in their own languages. Christian teaching first appeared in Latin, Islamic teaching in Arabic and Buddhist teaching in Pali. Who can deny this?

Yet self-forgetting people shamelessly call Saivism and Vaishnavism Dravidian religions while saying their truths were written not in Dravidian but Sanskrit. Their defence resembles someone who falls down and insists no dirt touched his moustache.

Nor does Sanskrit contain one distinct book, comparable to the Bible or Quran, that can be identified as the definitive account of Saivism and Siva or Vaishnavism and Vishnu. Hundreds of Vedas, Agamas, Upanishads, epics, Puranas, *Tevaram*, *Tiruvacakam*, *Prabandham* and other works stand as authorities, explaining without clarity the gods and qualities of those faiths. Further books generated by competition over Saivite or Vaishnavite superiority have appeared from then until today.

Unable to find any instrument for deciding which faith is greater or which to follow, our people nevertheless conduct religious disputes and spend immense sums upon them.

I ask defenders of Hinduism and its gods to consider one point. If Hinduism and its deities alone possess primacy and distinction, how can this land's forty crores contain nine crores of Muslims and Allah; more than a crore of Buddhists and Buddha; a crore of Christians and Jesus; over twelve lakhs of Jains and the Arhat; hundreds of thousands of Sikhs, Jews and Parsis with their gods; people who conclude there is no God; and people unconcerned with God—all divided by irreconcilable doctrines, customs, food and dress?

If creating this division is God's nature, must we not conclude humanity has no need for such a God?

For a long time our Dravidian people followed religion and God without investigation and arranged their lives along mistaken paths. The Dravidar Kazhagam therefore works to explain the truth of these matters and secure genuine Dravidian liberation. Some who do not understand this raise wild cries that religion is being inserted into politics and God criticized.

I have shown above that God and religion are so constructed that their advocates cannot demonstrate what they have done or now do for humanity, or substantiate any claimed benefit with evidence under their own doctrines. I remain ready to provide clarification in whatever manner is desired to those not persuaded here.

It is not easy, however, to make truth understood by those who attempt foolish argument outside reason. The sunlight of time will certainly explain it to them. Then they will understand why religion and God entered politics, why divinity had to be explained and why religion was criticized. The hope that they may at least reform afterward prompted this article.

1 October 1944.

## Translator's Notes

The first and final scans confirm the title `கட்சியில், கடவுள் - மதம்` and the date `1-10-1944`. The supplied ten-page sequence is complete.

The article's arguments about divine omnipotence, punishment, revelation, heaven and hell are translated as a chain of rhetorical questions; apparent contradictions have not been harmonized. `அந்தராத்மா` is rendered “inner voice” in the Gandhi context, while its literal philosophical sense is closer to “inner self.”

Historical population figures and claims about the original languages of religions are preserved as the author's 1944 assertions rather than silently updated. `அருகன்` in the Jain list is rendered “the Arhat.” The source contains a few stray page-number/OCR marks, but none changes the recoverable argument, and the canonical Tamil block is retained verbatim.
