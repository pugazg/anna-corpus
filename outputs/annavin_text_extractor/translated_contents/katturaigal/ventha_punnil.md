## English Translation

Yes, one must not thrust a spear into a raw wound. Such a method will only increase bitterness, not reduce it. It will also destroy the relationship that already existed.

One must not hold the mistaken belief that power has the capacity only to confer status. Power immediately creates certain special responsibilities as well. Only by understanding this clearly and using acquired authority for work that benefits the people can one hope to retain it for even a few days.

After gaining the people's goodwill and support and ascending the seat of government, if rulers become unsteady at the sight of their power, cease respecting the people, and seek ways to rule the province regardless of them, the very people who gladly placed them in authority will one day express in action the disappointment and resentment they have suffered. Power will pass into other hands. A government that does not respect the people cannot endure. Whoever comes to govern the country must regard this general rule as a foremost duty.

It appears that the Congress ministers have forgotten it. Their forgetfulness is plainly visible in several of the measures they take. Perhaps this disease of forgetfulness has gripped them because they trust that they possess sufficient strength in propaganda and armed force.

It would produce far greater good if they showed poor workers even a small measure of the care and affection they display towards capitalists and moneybags. They cannot already have forgotten the strike conducted by the workers of the Madras B. & C. Mills from 11 March to 19 June. Fourteen thousand workers did not strike for amusement, nor with the malicious intention of reducing production. They entered the strike struggle because they needed to rid their lives of the disease of poverty.

With enticing words and threats, the government ordered them to abandon the strike. It promised that their grievances would be considered. Improper efforts were also made to disrupt the workers' unity. Somehow, the workers gave up the strike and returned to work.

Against their wishes, compulsory arbitration was used to hear and decide their grievances. The benefit the workers obtained from this must be called zero. Justice Lakshmana Rao, appointed as arbitrator, remained as though deaf. He delivered the single judgment "No" upon every demand the workers made. The government accepted this decision and ordered the workers to obey it for another year. Perhaps the rulers accepted it and issued the order with the lofty expectation that the workers would conclude, "Our own people govern us; that alone is enough to satisfy our hunger."

Should they display their authority by striking at the stomachs of the very people who gave them power, instead of properly using it against the white capitalist who earned tens of millions?

This mistaken course by the government will not increase production. Nor would it be reasonable to expect enthusiasm for production among the workers. The cost of living rises from day to day, but workers' incomes have not risen to the same degree. In this situation, it is undesirable for the government to disregard their demands instead of satisfying them.

Whatever grievances the B. & C. Mills workers had before the strike, and whatever needs caused them to strike, remain exactly as they were. In their wounded hearts, the government's order will cause the pain of a spear thrust into a raw wound. Before their bitterness assumes a dangerous character, it would be beneficial for the government to reverse its order and fulfil their just demands. Will this reach the ears of those in power?

(*Dravida Nadu*, 30 November 1948)

## Translator's Notes

The title completes the Tamil idiom “வெந்த புண்ணில் வேல் பாய்ச்சுதல்,” meaning to inflict fresh pain upon an existing injury. The OCR prints “வேலைப்” in the opening sentence; English follows the clear idiom “வேலைப்/வேல்” as spear. The mill name is retained as the source's B. & C. Mills.
