## English Translation

"The writer's first enemy is fear. Only when fear is banished can a writer stand with head held high."

Thus spoke the revolutionary poet Bharathidasan on Sunday, 29 May 1949, while presiding over and addressing the first annual conference of the Tiruchirappalli District Tamil Writers' Association at Devar Hall in Tiruchirappalli.

The event began at 9:30 in the morning. Writers and members of the public interested in the arts were seen gathering inside the hall.

The president of the association asked Comrade C. P. Chitrarasu to open the conference, and the secretary seconded the request. Comrade C. P. Chitrarasu explained that the pen is mightier than the sword, set out the duties that writers must undertake, declared the conference open, and took his seat amid loud applause.

The association invited the revolutionary poet Bharathidasan to preside over and conduct the conference. After taking the chair, the revolutionary poet asked the King of Comedy, N. S. Krishnan, to unveil a portrait of Raja Sandow.

The King of Comedy rose. As joy blossomed among the people, laughter poured forth, and applause resounded, he came and stood before the microphone with the distinctive bodily movement natural to him. Meanwhile, the flood of people pressing against the entrance broke through the barrier, surged inside, and spread throughout the hall.

Comrade N. S. K. began to speak, and silence prevailed. He described at length, clearly, and with abundant humour Raja Sandow's life; the sense of duty visible in him from his youth; the severe hardships he endured in the early years of cinema; the many ways in which his service helped Tamil films rise and shine; and the great concern he showed in bringing young actors from his homeland forward. He then unveiled the large and beautifully coloured portrait of Raja Sandow. The people welcomed it with thunderous applause.

This was the first portrait unveiled for display in Devar Hall, Tiruchirappalli. The hall has become a suitable venue for theatrical performances, and portraits of celebrated actors such as Raja Sandow adorning it with their beauty are something to be welcomed.

Next, Comrade Kamalaveni and Comrades R. Nedunchezhiyan, Ki. Aa. Pe. Viswanatham, S. Gurusamy, and C. N. Annadurai delivered addresses.

In accordance with the association's wish, the revolutionary poet conferred upon Comrade N. S. Krishnan the title "Revolutionary King of Wit." N. S. Krishnan, Revolutionary King of Wit and King of Comedy, responded suitably and offered his thanks.

In his concluding address, the revolutionary poet explained with perfect clarity the qualities a writer must possess.
