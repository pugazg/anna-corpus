## English Translation

We have previously written three editorials under the heading “Tamil Nadu for Tamils,” and at the end of yesterday's editorial stated that the Aryan-Dravidian relationship in religion would be discussed next.

Accordingly, in today's editorial we begin to consider what Aryan and Dravidian religions are and what their foundations are. In doing so, we rely chiefly upon matters directly experienced at present, rather than make textual research our foundation. Let us leave that research to scholars. If evidence or research contradicts present observation and thought, may what we write at least help experience change accordingly. It is with this intention that we intervene.

The chief reason is our belief that corruption in the religious sphere enabled Aryan domination to grow and remain established in this country, and that it also caused the Dravidian decline. Dravidians may at least accept that God has neither form nor equal. Yet most Dravidians today will recognize their contrary practice as mistaken, and will readily understand that this mistaken doctrine itself made them slaves to Aryans in every sphere.

What has been imposed upon Dravidians, and accepted by most of them, is the so-called Hindu religion of the Aryans. Likewise, the gods of Dravidians are Hindu gods, and the customs followed by Dravidians in society are those of Hindu religion.

## Hindu Religion

To speak frankly and openly, no religion called Hinduism ever existed, nor is there a way to say that one exists. How, then, did the term “Hindu religion” come into circulation? What now passes as Hinduism consists of contrivances created to secure Aryan superiority, keep other peoples in darkness so that Aryans may rule them advantageously, and use Dravidians whenever convenient under the name of religion.

Within Aryan tradition itself, the expression “Hindu religion” cannot be shown to have existed. It is absent both from ancient Aryan sources and from the religious authorities they created down to comparatively recent times.

Nor is there any fixed account of what Hinduism is or what its doctrines are. There is no rule by which to say who is a Hindu and who is not. There is no authority for declaring that an Indian Muslim, Christian, Parsi, Sikh, Brahmo Samajist, or rationalist without religion or god is not a Hindu.

In practice, only one test is now applied: whatever speaks or exists against Aryan superiority and welfare is deemed non-Hindu. Hinduism itself has no definite doctrine or programme. Even a person who says there is no God, that the Vedas, sastras and puranas are false, may be called a great Vedantin or ascetic so long as he does not dispute the foundations supporting Aryan supremacy.

## Conclusions of Research

Many researchers have concluded and demonstrated that no such religion as Hinduism exists. Numerous research books written by Europeans and eminent scholars of this country say so.

Writers acquainted with several languages have asserted that the word “Hindu” has carried meanings such as thief, scoundrel, troublemaker, betrayer of trust, slave, coolie and barbarian, and that English dictionaries can be cited in support. More generally, dictionaries and encyclopedic works are said to describe Hinduism as Aryan doctrine, a priestly religion that elevates Brahmins; a Hindu as one who follows Brahmins; the term as referring to peoples north of the Vindhyas; and also as indicating an ancient, uncivilized condition.

However those research claims may stand, we do not enter into them here. Our question is whether the Dravidian people belong to a “Hindu” religion containing birth-based caste distinctions, many gods and image worship.

We wish to examine how the majority who practise worship actually conduct it, and thereby determine whether Dravidians possess a Dravidian religion, worship a god of Dravidian philosophy and have corresponding foundational texts.

Today those called Vaishnavites and Saivites mostly worship the trinity Brahma, Vishnu and Siva with forms, characteristics, wives, children and domestic lives imagined according to Aryan tradition. When controversy arises, they defend themselves as Gandhi does when he says, “My varna-ashrama is different,” “My Rama is different,” and “My Hindu religion is different,” while in practice acting to maintain Manu's varna-ashrama, the Rama of the *Ramayana*, and a Hindu religion serving Aryan superiority.

In the same way, Dravidians worship the Aryan creations Brahma, Vishnu and Siva, jointly or separately. More than Hindu religion itself, it is these Aryan Hindu gods that have enslaved Dravidians and bound them so that they cannot escape.

Because religious corruption caused Aryan domination and the Dravidian fall, we wish the people to understand the relationship between Aryan and Dravidian religion.

## Dravidian Religion

It may be difficult to establish beyond dispute what religion the earliest Dravidians possessed. Yet there is no indication that they held doctrines of previous and future births, superior and inferior castes resulting from birth, separate worlds corresponding to caste, idols, many gods, or people originating from those gods' faces and feet. It is therefore certain that the religions, gods and texts associated with such doctrines are not compatible with Dravidian tradition.

From this certainty, one may think the views of the great Prophet Muhammad concerning God more appropriate to Tamil conceptions of God and society. Muhammad said that God has no form, no name and no equal, and established the compulsory rule that no partner may be attributed to God. He condemned anyone who invented form, partner or similar attributes as contrary to the doctrine of God and as not belonging to Islam.

Vaishnavites in Dravida Nadu may orally say, “We do not worship an Aryan god; we worship Tirumal, a Dravidian god.” Saivites may say the same of Siva. Others may say, “We worship neither Siva nor Tirumal, but nature embodied as Murugan, representing natural beauty, youth and strength.” Still others may say they worship no quality or symbol, but the qualityless and formless purity called *kandazhi*. People within each religion may offer many explanations that we cannot understand and that even some of them do not understand.

But among the people of Dravida Nadu, Aryan Hindu gods possess a stronger practical presence than abstract Hindu religion.

## Our Scholars

Most of our scholars are learned in gods and religions. Scholars trained in general knowledge, natural understanding and the powers of nature are exceedingly rare. We must ask them not to take offence when told that Saivite and Vaishnavite scholars gave strength to Aryan gods and religion and thereby made Dravidians slaves to Aryans. Our own scholars created, spread and continue to propagate the Dravidian-language works concerning Aryan gods.

Ninety-five out of every hundred Dravidian people have been kept without education or literacy. Some scholars argue that religion and God can be made intelligible to them only by writing and preaching religious tales, stories of saints and gods, and by imagining wives, children and concubines for deities, adorning them with food and jewels, and staging marriages, festivals, worship and ablutions.

Can anyone call this the statement of an intelligent, unmixed Tamil son of Dravidian blood?

Muslims possess both a religion and a God, and perhaps ninety in a hundred Muslims too are without literacy. Do they create religious and divine feeling by these same means? Does refraining from them deprive Muslims of awareness of God, religion or humanity? The claim reveals only these scholars' own ignorance.

Did Shakespeare, Shelley, Bernard Shaw, Wells and Wilson live, or become famous throughout the world, merely by writing paraphrases, interpretations and new commentaries on puranas? We ask our scholars to consider it.

We speak this way because our scholars played a major part in making Dravidians slaves to Aryans. We recognize that the responsibility and power to recover them now also lie with those scholars. We fear that if this side does not yield, we shall suffer the necessity of fighting these scholars just as we fight Aryans and their weapons.

We therefore ask scholars to define clearly what the religion of Dravidians is, what their God is and what the doctrines of each are. We ask them to produce only arts and literature suited to those definitions. We gladly disclose that many Dravidian scholars have spoken in support of our view.

Our present desire is nothing more than complete separation from Aryans, their religion, gods and doctrines. We ask scholars once again to believe this.

Just as British assistance is required for our country to separate politically from Aryans, Dravidians themselves must act to separate from them in religion, concepts of God and social life.

Dravidian scholars and ordinary people should consider how Christian religious feeling has enabled Christians to attain high standing in the world's eyes, how Islamic religious feeling once enabled Muslims to attain greatness before the world, and how Japanese religious feeling raised Japan's standing.

Had these peoples followed every comma and full stop written by their religions, they too would have remained in a state more degraded than that of Dravidians. Instead, each community treated as its religion the determination that its country and society should live honourably and prosperously, neither enslaved to others nor inferior in any sphere. They sacrificed some of the rest and worked toward that purpose. Consequently, they live as separate, self-respecting and free societies.

If Christian scholars and priests in Western countries, where all lived as Christians, had confined themselves to writing commentaries and new interpretations of the Bible and insisted that life follow it literally, not one Christian would have teeth in his mouth or cloth around his waist today. Their religion tells them to turn the other cheek when struck and surrender even their upper garment.

Yet people of that religion now govern their own and foreign countries. They are guides and teachers in world civilization and marvellous invention. In daily Western life they devise systems designed to avoid distress, false delicacy and resentment, and live freely. They plan every stage of life and conduct it with the regularity of a clock.

Likewise, Muslims have moved beyond spending their effort interpreting the Quran and determining the precise length of a beard and the correct form of hair, cap and shirt. Concerned instead with how to live as human beings, Turkey, Afghanistan, Persia, Egypt and other countries live independently, country by country, without subjection to another religious society.

In India too, an “Islam without a beard” stands as leader, enabling Muslims to impose conditions for a free and self-respecting life without needing to describe themselves merely as a minority, and leaving others astonished.

Japan likewise has a country smaller than Dravida Nadu and a population smaller than the Dravidian population. Yet it exists as an independent state and one of the world's great powers, defies surrounding countries three or four times larger in area and population, and advances in every sphere.

Published in *Viduthalai*, 24 November 1939.
