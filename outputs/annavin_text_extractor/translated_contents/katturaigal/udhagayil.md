## English Translation

Members of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam are eager to learn about the legislative assembly, so full of enthusiasm for public work, that is to sit in Ooty.

News has arrived that the leader of the government party has gone with the opposition leader to inspect the place directly. Their briskness shows how urgent and essential both the government and opposition parties consider a journey to Ooty. They inspected Aranmore Palace and other buildings, as well as several places, and offered many suggestions about what should be altered here and corrected there.

It is indeed the duty of a responsible government to provide every facility members need when the legislature meets. Facilities must also be provided for the officials accompanying the government.

Thus, if democracy is to remain tasteful and attractive, some expenditure is unavoidable, and the ruling party will not hesitate over it. It is true that when dealing with “unimportant” programmes such as slum clearance and housing, those responsible for government proceed with great hesitation, thrift, and the speed of a tortoise. But this is a matter of “pleasing the masters”! Here they will devote all their attention, generously accept every proposal, and make the required alterations. The government's enthusiasm and speed over this matter alone reveal how eagerly they wish to go to Ooty.

Only the DMK opposes it. That is why I did not join the “inspection tour.”

Friends in Ooty wrote asking when I would arrive, pointing to newspaper reports that the government-party leader was travelling there with the leaders of other parties.

But I did not receive the rare opportunity to accompany him. The reason is that I do not support the proposal to hold the assembly in Ooty.

Ooty is not a town I dislike. I know its natural beauty and captivating surroundings, and I have enjoyed them many times. I also have many friends there.

Yet my opposition to going to Ooty is no private fantasy. I firmly believe this transfer is unnecessary and extravagant. Congress leaders once made lakhs of people believe that it was a decision taken by the British to display their “superiority.” The British could not endure the cruelty of summer. They said that suffering in Madras's hot wind would damage body and brain and that they therefore sought a cooler place.

Those same Congress leaders then travelled from town to town, thundering heroically against this “wrongdoing,” “wastefulness,” and “arrogance.”

I remember S. Satyamurti's voice roaring something like this:

> Look at these foreigners, working people! While you labour until sweat falls from your foreheads onto the earth, they plunder you in a thousand ways and travel to Ooty. They will not die of heat merely by staying in Madras. If summer is so cruel to them, why should they remain in this country? Why not return to their land, where they delight even in seeing the sun? Go, white men, go! This country does not suit you. Its very summer kills you. Go to your land of snow and mud!

They taught us this fundamental truth: convening the assembly in Ooty because Madras summer is severe is a kind of political conceit. The people will not, cannot and must not tolerate it. While all our people endure this heat and remain here, why should a few flee to a mountaintop in fear of summer?

At least the British had a reason for going to Ooty: they came from a cold country. But spending a vast sum today merely to imitate them and satisfy the petty desire of a few is unjust and meaningless.

I firmly believe this entire “journey” is nothing more than the trivial longing of a few. It provides no real benefit.

Yet they feel embarrassed to tell the country's masters this truth. They cannot simply say that summer is cruel and they want to climb the hill for coolness.

They are experts at diverting every issue. Is that not their greatest “asset”? They gave a different reason:

> People, we too are going to Ooty, but do not imagine it is like the British going there. They went to display their superiority. We go with an important objective. What objective, you ask? We bow our heads and tell you: our presence will develop Ooty and its surroundings. We go so that the people of that region may gain convenience and prosperity, not so that we may enjoy ourselves.

That is what those who came to rule the country said.

When this proposal appeared before the estimates committee and later before the assembly, I strongly opposed it on the DMK's behalf. I warned that it would earn public resentment and tried to have it abandoned.

At one point I even believed they had dropped it.

The government-party leader announced his noble decision in the legislature: the government would take no decision to go to Ooty unless every party agreed.

I was therefore surprised when the question arose again. My surprise became astonishment when I heard that even the Communist Party had agreed to the journey. When asked for my view, I flatly refused consent. But all the others together decided to proceed despite my refusal.

They later announced in the legislature that the Ooty journey would take place regardless of the DMK's opposition.

No one in the chamber cheered the announcement. There was silence instead. Because the finance minister had declared categorically that there would be no journey unless every party agreed, perhaps they had assumed the legislature would not go to Ooty. Or perhaps they grieved at a government that forgot its word.

To say Ooty will develop because the legislature goes there is mere deception.

There are a thousand ways to give Ooty prosperity. A few people going and staying there for a short period is certainly not one of them.

Newspaper reports show what sort of development is contemplated. The inspection party visited Aranmore Palace and the Connemara and Dasaprakash hotels and learned how many people each could house and what facilities they needed. That is the development Ooty is to receive!

Ooty would genuinely grow if our government concentrated on increasing the opportunities and facilities for visitors from India and abroad.

Because the cost of living in Ooty is high, legislators ask for their allowance to be doubled while there. It would not surprise me if they wanted it tripled.

After the Diwan leaves, all our members, except Sardar Vedarathinam, will put on suits and flannel clothes and present a fine sight. The members and their companions will behold and delight in Ooty's lovely plains and beautiful scenes. That alone will happen. Ooty will not advance. The Ooty session will give members pleasure; it will not help Ooty.

The town will not rise because legislators assemble there briefly. A raw-film factory could be established there; more tourists could be attracted; many other paths to growth could be pursued.

A friend who is a legislative member offered me another argument. He said that instead of always meeting in Madras, the assembly could meet in different parts of the country and establish contact with the people.

If that argument is accepted, why not meet in Thanjavur? There is a suitable palace. Alter the palace where the Cholas, Pandyas and later the Serfoji kings ruled and enjoyed power, and meet there.

If not there, Madurai has Tirumalai Nayak Palace. Historic Madurai! Sitting there even once might awaken a sense of heritage in their hearts. Or let the gentle sound of the Courtallam waterfalls serve as medicine while the assembly meets there for several days.

No, their purpose is not to travel across the land and connect with its people. Their only thought is: “The British held court there; why should we not?” The DMK refuses to assist that harmful idea.

By first declaring there would be no Ooty session unless every party consented and then reversing that decision, the government party has clarified one fact. So long as the people remain silent mouths accepting everything without asking why, rulers will remain arrogant. This obstinacy proves it.

My chief regret is that the other parties, particularly the Communist Party, have authorised this extravagant scheme.

The decision to convene the legislature in Ooty was not an accidental mistake but a planned act. What these people themselves described under British rule as “the arrogance of authority,” they now conduct under another name. The luxury and courtly display they denounced in the foreigner's time are preserved and cultivated by them today.

An editorial in the nationalist daily *Hindustan Standard* said:

> In earlier days Congress legislators voted against expenditure on governors and viceroys, red carpets, bodyguards, brass bands, and many other luxuries. It shocks us today to hear, “We did not despise all those things as luxury; we opposed them only because the luxuries were available exclusively to the British.” It does no credit to Gandhi's heirs to preserve in Gandhi's name what they once condemned as extravagance.

Nationalist or otherwise, today's rulers listen to nothing. They first make a decision and only afterward ask those before them, “What can be done?” When opposition is voiced, they do not even turn to look at it. They have now climbed to such a height.

I am therefore not surprised by their course. I find consolation only in the fact that acts like this reveal their position.

But why the Communist Party agreed to this scheme remains a riddle I cannot understand. The people must give the verdict.

**4 January 1959**

## Translator's Notes

`Udhagai` is the Tamil name for Ootacamund, now commonly Ooty or Udhagamandalam. The article concerns a proposal to move a sitting of the Madras legislature from Madras to Ooty during summer. `DMK` expands to Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam.

The OCR renders several proper names imperfectly. `அரண்மூர் அரண்மனை` is interpreted from context as Aranmore Palace, and the hotel phrase appears to refer to Connemara and Dasaprakash; these readings should be checked against a clearer print source before being treated as definitive. S. Satyamurti, Sardar Vedarathinam, Serfoji and Tirumalai Nayak retain familiar historical forms.

The quoted *Hindustan Standard* passage is translated from the Tamil reproduction in the source and has not been checked against the original English editorial. Political characterisations are retained as the author's argument rather than independently verified claims.
