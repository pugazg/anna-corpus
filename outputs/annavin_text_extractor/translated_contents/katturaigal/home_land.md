## English Translation

The Dravidian movement, which had been subjected to a blackout, has today gained a measure of influence among the public because, recognising that we would receive no support from other journals, we ourselves have continued to run at least a few movement publications.

Tamils gained understanding through Tamil journals.

Should not the people of Andhra, Kerala, and Karnataka now understand the reasons for a Dravidian federation and offer their support?

Should not the northerners learn of the contact our movement demanding Dravida Nadu maintains with the public, the agitations it conducts, and the influence it has gained?

Should not the nations and great assemblies of the world learn that Pandit Nehru, who travels everywhere proclaiming rights and preaching virtue, has turned the sacred Dravidian land into a forest of slavery in the name of false nationalism?

Will *The Hindu*, *The Mail*, *The Times*, and the rest carry this news to the world?

They not only conduct a blackout; the other journals largely manufacture false and malicious reports and distorted accounts that disparage and vilify our organisation.

We ourselves must find a way for other countries and governments to learn of our struggle for rights.

Many friends who recognised the urgent need for an English weekly that would explain our principles have urged me for days and years to undertake it.

I understand the circumstances and the need for an English weekly. Even so, fear grips me.

For an English weekly to flourish with good support, it must be nourished for a long time without expecting returns.

The expense and difficulty will be great. I even fear that anxiety and weariness may assail us.

The cost required for an English weekly is high in every respect. When I ask whether supporters will be found in sufficient numbers to meet it, I genuinely tremble.

Unless comrades studying in colleges, student friends in high schools, and employees in various offices show enthusiasm and gather support, giving attractive form to an English weekly, not a daily, will itself be difficult.

From typesetting to the addition of colour, an English journal must bear heavy expenditure.

This English weekly must enter and establish relations with every publishing house that issues journals in the many languages of the Indian subcontinent.

For at least a year, we must undertake this without considering it wasteful expenditure.

By bringing the journal to the attention of editors, political leaders, and professors of history abroad, an explanation of the principles of Dravida Nadu must spread there.

Places unable to know the true situation because of the glare of manufactured fame must be enlightened.

For this purpose, at least a thousand copies must be distributed without regard for income.

The English weekly must necessarily bear many difficulties of this kind, especially in its opening days.

If friends maintain their enthusiasm and support it, we can spread the light of knowledge with greater speed and vigour than we expect.

The peerless history of delightful Dravidam has been hidden!

The conspiracy that turned an undivided society into a sewer has not been explained to the world.

The anguish over the harm the Indian empire has inflicted upon the South has been concealed.

An English weekly is certainly required to change this condition.

Trusting that friends will surely gather support, I have overcome the fear and hesitation that persisted for a long time and decided to publish an English weekly.

I made one or two attempts before. Fear and the thought of a new burden prevented the conception from taking form.

This time, I do not wish to abandon the effort.

I have resolved to publish the English weekly in January and have begun the work.

Arrangements have been made to publish it from the *Dravida Nadu* press in Kanchipuram.

Nearly six months ago, I informed the authorities.

It appears they needed all these months to investigate it minutely. On 3 November 1956, I received notice that permission would be granted to publish the English weekly.

The journal will appear in demy format with eight pages and cost two annas.

Many friends throughout Tamil Nadu possess the ability to provide explanatory articles about our movement. We shall enjoy the fruits of their intellect.

The journal will also offer an opportunity to learn about liberation movements and intellectual movements flourishing abroad.

The English journal will serve as a link and foster comradeship.

I am ready to publish it in January, but on one condition. Friends, if my motivation is not to weaken, my enthusiasm not to diminish, and my eagerness not to falter, every one of you must immediately become a subscriber to this weekly.

When the first issue appears, it must have at least one thousand subscribers.

Without securing even this degree of support and foundation, I fear that attempting to run the journal will become a performance lasting only a few days and end in heartbreak.

Friends, you know me well. I have complete confidence in you.

That is why I speak to you without even the slightest reserve.

Please come forward and give me the strength needed to publish the English weekly in January.

We need one thousand subscribers. The annual subscription is seven rupees.

The English journal will connect the Dravidian movement with the whole world.

Friends who recognise the excellence of this purpose, I greatly trust that you will do what is needed today.

In January, as a feast for your thought,

*Home Land*

is to appear!

Your support is the only path to a safe delivery.

Affectionately,

Annadurai

*Dravida Nadu, 4 November 1956*

The first scan's item number is damaged, but the title Home Land is clear. The OCR dates `3-17-56` and `4-11-26` are resolved contextually as 3 November 1956 and 4 November 1956. The pregnancy and safe-delivery metaphor in the final appeal is retained.
