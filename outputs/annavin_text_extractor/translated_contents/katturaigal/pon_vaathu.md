## English Translation

The goose lays golden eggs, and its owner was overjoyed!

Every day, he received as much gold as one egg. His heart swelled at the sight of it.

"What a marvellous goose! It gives me golden eggs! The man who possesses it is truly fortunate," he never tired of telling himself.

As his gold increased day after day, his greed too assumed gigantic proportions.

Taking merely one egg a day began to seem intolerably slow.

"Are not all the eggs inside the goose's belly? If I take every egg from its belly in a single day, I shall have all the golden eggs at once!" he thought.

He sharpened his knife and cut the goose's throat.

The rash fellow was disappointed. There was only the one egg it was to lay that day. He took it, and the goose fell dead.

Afterwards he sobbed and wept, but the goose was dead, and dead it remained.

*Ananda Vikatan*, which is said to circulate through the country dispensing humour, published a cartoon of this kind.

Through that cartoon, *Vikatan* tries to explain the harm caused by the Madras government's new tax proposals.

It shows that if a government imagines that, because people pay taxes, it can impose tax upon tax and collect everything in one stroke, it will be disappointed like the owner of the goose that laid golden eggs.

A good lesson, although it is a picture drawn for humour!

The Madras government introduces new taxes. It levies taxes but does not relieve the suffering of the people who pay them. This belief has spread among the public.

Moreover, the tax imposed when the people did not suffer such poverty and deprivation was smaller; today it is greater. The taxation figures teach us this lesson.

That is not all. Before coming to office, the Congress rulers now imposing taxes declared that taxation itself was wrong; that the British were plundering the country through taxes; and that under their rule people would see how many taxes were abolished and how many reduced. "Wait and see," they said.

After ascending the seat of power with such speeches, they increased the existing taxes. Year after year they continue introducing new ones.

Compare the taxes we paid when these masters of tax-free government had not yet come to power with the money now squeezed from us under the "complete self-rule" administered by these sons of Bharat.

Think upon it, and you will shed tears. Should anger fill you, you may even wonder whether blood ought to be made to flow!

These statistics will open our eyes.

In just ten years, the revenue we pay the state has risen from a hollow into a mountain.

Let us consider only the figures for our province:

Tax in 1938-39: Rs. 16,13,45,000

Tax in 1949-50: Rs. 55,75,32,000

Rate of increase: 3.46 times.

Within ten years, we pay three and a half times as much as before.

Those virtuous men who came to govern us never cease proclaiming that the people's welfare is their very life and prosperity!

To put it more plainly: the moment the sons of Bharat set foot upon the seats of power, taxes rose and life declined.

These very men, who have enlarged the tax burden beyond endurance, shout themselves hoarse that theirs is a people's government!

If a man paid Rs. 3-6-1 in tax in 1939, he pays Rs. 10-5-7 in 1950.

As though the ever-growing tax burden were insufficient, the Madras government is still searching for further means of raising taxes.

At this sight, the hasty man who cut open the golden goose comes to mind.

The golden egg is the tax paid by the people. Just as the goose gives one egg a day, people can pay only a limited amount under specified heads. If there is a rush to take everything at once, the Congress rulers whose political life is nearing its end will suffer the anguish of the man who lost his goose.

We are not saying that it is wrong for those who administer a country to collect taxes from its people.

Our question is whether taxes should be poured on without restraint merely because people consent to pay them.

To keep the government functioning, the people give it money in many forms. They pay it as taxes and contribute it in other forms as well.

Some taxes are paid directly, while others are collected indirectly.

We must recognize that, just as provincial revenue includes sums received under the name of tax, collections are also made through other departments.

Government revenue in 1938-39 was Rs. 69.5 crore. In 1949-50 it was Rs. 137.4 crore.

Revenue has risen today. One needs no mirror to see a wound in one's palm: taxes have been increased, and new taxes introduced.

They are going to kill the goose for the sake of its golden eggs!

Although the public may be geese, they are not lifeless beings. Once the seeds of revolution are scattered in their minds, they will cease to be geese and become life-taking lions.

The rulers must understand this truth.

Anyone who carefully followed this week's debates in the Madras Legislative Assembly could have heard the dreadful cry of the man who greedily cut open the golden goose.

New taxes, under a Congress government that itself is soon to go up in smoke! This can only remind us of the story of the golden goose.

The rulers must not act rashly like that impulsive man. If they keep imposing new taxes, they must bear the consequences.

This is the golden charter we present to them at this moment. We offer it after pointing to the inscription upon it: taxes are necessary, but they must understand that taxation has its proper method and discipline.
