## English Translation

Good people said that this amendment bill was absolutely necessary.

Ambedkar passionately desired the passage of that bill granting rights to women.

Many Members of Parliament insisted that the bill must somehow be passed.

Through their conferences, Indian women gave it their support.

Every progressive newspaper welcomed the bill and wrote in its support.

Many described it as a political reform that would remove some of the defects within Hinduism.

The Dravidian Women's Progressive Association held a conference in Madras and supported it.

At several meetings of the Congress legislative party, many leaders insisted that the bill should be passed.

Pandit Nehru proclaimed many times: "If the people do not support this bill, it means they do not support me. If members of the legislature oppose it, it means they oppose me."

In the legislature he passionately sounded the conch: "If this bill is not passed, I shall resign my office."

But orthodoxy has won the wreath of victory. It has prevented the bill from becoming law.

The Sanatanists' bogey of fasting, the disorderly demonstration they conducted before the legislature, and the clamour raised through reactionaries have succeeded.

Great leaders stood united, but religious madness forced their effort to its knees.

Orthodox fanaticism, declaring that religious reform must not occur and supported by idols of wealth, has defeated the bill.

Pandit Nehru did not panic!

The old order laughs!

Reform did not succeed!

Orthodox fanaticism rejoices!

Those who introduced the bill were great leaders. Yet orthodoxy forced them to kneel. The people supported the bill's passage, but religious fanaticism brought it down!

(*Dravida Nadu*, 30-9-51)
