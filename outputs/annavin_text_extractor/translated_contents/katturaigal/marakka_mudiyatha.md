## English Translation

He did not possess a heart that harmed those around him. Political gambling was an art unknown to him. He touched neither intrigue nor conspiracy. What he thought—what he felt in his heart—he stated bluntly and directly, without regard for anyone's approval or dislike. There was no concealment in his actions either.

Office bowed before him. Titles hid themselves. Money fled and disappeared. Nothing could erase the thoughts that arose in his heart or the deeds he performed. He lived as a great man filled with integrity, firmness, truth, and industry.

Did he ever defy the rulers' orders to realise his dream? Did he practise civil disobedience? Did he enter prison? Did he face a condition like de Gaulle or de Valera, having to mount the gallows and take various measures to escape with his life? Not one of these drew his attention or occupied his thought.

Did he ever alter his clothing or ornaments in any way to win the affection of the people of his community? Not in the slightest. Until his death, he retained his customary dress and appearance. Did he gain stature among his people by invoking the sacred name of God at every turn? Even his opponents would not claim so.

The distinction of creating a separate country for his people in the Indian subcontinent belongs to him alone. Was this achieved by the strength of the sword, the power of the atomic bomb, or spiritual force? Only impartial historians must decide what the cause was.

This idea arose in his mind one day during his lifetime. Seven years ago, he announced it to the world. Within a short time, he created a great Islamic realm. No comparable event had until then found a place in world history.

His political life began as that of a nationalist. His heart was so immersed in nationalism that he refused even to participate in the Khilafat Movement created in support of Turkey. Nationalism dwelt so deeply in the temple of his heart that Sarojini Ammaiyar herself garlanded him with praise as the "ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity."

Later, through the consequences of someone's actions, he became a first-rank communal leader. No one would say that he alone bore full responsibility for this change. His surroundings caused him to change.

No one in the world had ever faced opposition to the extent he did. It is no exaggeration to say that the opposition of others hastened the fulfilment of his goal. Every word of abuse thrown at him turned his heart into steel.

He met his end as a victorious warrior last week. There was no country or person of goodwill in the world that failed to express sorrow at his passing. During his lifetime, people who disliked and fiercely condemned his policy, those who raged against him, and those who delighted in drawing caricatures of him all forgot their former hatred when they heard that he had died and made their condolences and profound grief known to the world.

Yet the editor of *Dinasari* alone remained, as always, a volcano. Wise people say that there must be no mockery at the grave. Even this worthy counsel proved false in the case of *Dinasari*. We pity its condition.

The lawyer Jinnah ultimately became Governor-General of the country he had envisioned and died on 11 September 1948. At a time when his ability and knowledge were needed not only by Pakistan but also by the government of the Indian Union, his departure was an irreparable loss to both states.

Today there is no harmony of mind between the two governments. Allowing this condition to continue will bring harm upon the citizens of both. It must change, and changing it is worthy work that must be undertaken immediately. It would be excellent if those engaged in governing both states would henceforth direct their attention to this task.

Death is natural for everyone who is born. Neither office, title, nor money can prevent it. Jinnah too has gone that way. As one person offering condolences said, after the Prophet, Jinnah created an "unforgettable event" unmatched anywhere, by anyone, at any time. Let us too pay our final respects to him.

(*Dravida Nadu*, 19 September 1948)
