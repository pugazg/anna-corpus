## English Translation

“Hey, King! Money is needed for the sacrificial rite I am going to conduct. I have appointed you as its guardian. Complete at once everything required for the sacrificial rite!” the rishi would say. The king would fall at the feet of the bearer of the water pot like a tall palm cut off at its base, bow, and declare with fearful devotion: “I bear your command upon my head. Lord who grants salvation! Sacred embodiment of God! Guru Maharaj! Here I stand ready.” The treasury would melt away, and smoke from the sacrificial fire would rise in curling coils.

“Great men” made kings dance to their tune.

Wearers of jewelled crowns lay kneeling before bearers of rosaries.

To become a king, one needed the blessing of the holy man; for kingship to endure, one needed his support.

A ruler might have performed magnificent deeds worthy of public celebration. He might have become a great warrior and conquered many realms, attacked and demolished forts, and flown his banner of victory in all eight directions. Yet if he hesitated to bear the “command” of Aryan gurus upon his head, his crown would not endure and his hold would fail. The king would be “made a sinner.” The guru's curse would touch him, and he would lose his fitness to rule.

There was such an age.

It was the age of Harishchandras who sold their wives and went to guard the cremation ground.

It was an age when the rosary wielded such power.

Not only here, but everywhere.

In Europe today the Pope preaches qualities such as love, grace, peace, tranquillity, and morality, advising his multitudes of devotees not to follow the path of sin. But “in those days,” the Pope's word itself was law. Kings bowed at his feet and fear possessed them. No king dared disobey his command. If one did, he would be driven from the throne and the Pope would excommunicate him. Other rulers would obtain the Pope's blessing and set out to “destroy” the outcast who had incurred his curse. Such an age once existed.

One king who incurred the Pope's anger later realised his fault and condition. Seeking expiation and the Pope's gracious regard, he remained pleading at the entrance to the papal palace for three days and three nights, bareheaded, barefoot, and dressed as a humble man. A palace? Sacrilege, sacrilege! A temple!

There were days when religion exercised that degree of domination over politics.

There was an age when people advanced and accepted the doctrine that political power existed solely to protect religion and execute the commands of religious leaders.

The sufferings and degradations it produced are beyond the capacity of books to contain.

Scoundrels and frauds grew fat; falsehood and error abounded.

Rulers became servants, and religious leaders pulled their strings.

It took several centuries to cast off this cruelty.

The doctrine arose that politics and religion were separate and that religious domination of politics was improper. Plans had to be made and many wars fought. A compromise emerged: kings would not interfere in the work of religious leaders, and religious leaders would not intrude upon the king's authority.

“This world belongs to these kings; the empire of the next world belongs to us,” religious leaders pretended to say with satisfaction after realising that their domination could no longer continue.

Even so, whenever an opportunity arises, religious leaders attempt to gain political power.

Although they know that democracy leaves no path for this, they try to insert their domination into politics on the principle that any gain achieved while the attempt succeeds is profit. In most places they are defeated.

A few days ago, the Burmese Prime Minister defeated religious authoritarians of this kind and taught them a fitting lesson. He declared firmly: “Religion is separate and politics is separate. It is improper for religious leaders to control politics. I will not permit it.”

The eyes of Burma's great Buddhist monks flashed fire. The Burmese government, attentive to the condition of the country, the public mind, and the signs of the times, devised an educational plan. Claiming that it contradicted their command, the Buddhist monks donned the garb of battle.

Burma is a country that embraced Buddha's path of love.

Buddhism is a noble moral path that removed domination and established the way of love.

To discover this path, Buddha cast aside royal luxury as worthless, spent his life seeking truth, and taught the way to human redemption.

Many sword-bearing kings came forward to worship at his feet because they had been conquered by love.

Those who rose in anger were captivated by his gentle smile and became his disciples.

The Lord showed the same love to a lamb as to an emperor.

He did not enact laws to impose Buddhism.

He spoke sweetly; he did not issue commands.

It was not by proclaiming miracles or displaying magic that he made rulers kneel.

They found truth in his speech and holiness in the path he showed, and therefore submitted.

In Burma, some who today stand as guardians of the religion operating in his name behaved in a manner that brought disgrace upon his sacred name.

Burma contains not only Buddhists but also a large Muslim population.

Just as Buddhists regard their religion as sacred, Muslims regard Islam as sacred.

Burma is a country, not a Buddhist monastery. It is a country in which Buddhists, Muslims, and Christians live.

To say that Burma must remain under Buddhist domination is to deny the dignity of democracy itself.

It is largely because of this fear that the Karens, most of whom are Christian, have been carrying on an agitation for a separate state.

Likewise, Muslims fear that the country may fall under Buddhist domination and that their religion and culture may thereby be endangered. They therefore demand a separate Islamic state.

As though to prove these fears true, the Buddhist monks began to attack the Burmese government.

As here, people in Burma said that religious teaching was needed in the schools.

Taking account of public attitudes, the political situation, and democratic principles, the Burmese Prime Minister arranged for both Buddhist and Islamic religious instruction in schools. No reasonable person would object to this. But the Buddhist monks donned the garb of battle.

They began issuing commands that Burmese schools must teach Buddhism alone and must not teach Islam.

“The government is common to everyone; it does not exist only for Buddhists. Muslims too are citizens of Burma, and they cannot be ignored,” replied the Burmese Prime Minister.

The monks would not relent. They arose in heroic fury and poured forth condemnation. They began to speak and abuse him: “Religion is being destroyed. Buddhism is perishing. The Prime Minister has become an enemy of religion. He has become a sinner who says Islam and Buddhism are one.”

The Burmese Prime Minister decided that he wanted none of this trouble and ordered that there should be no religious instruction at all in schools. This struck the Buddhist leaders like a thunderbolt. They therefore attempted to create opposition to the Burmese government. The Prime Minister showed that he was not a man to fear intimidation.

He stated categorically:

“Religion is separate and politics is separate. I will not permit religious leaders to intervene in political affairs and exercise domination. Do not deceive yourselves into believing that stubbornness will make me submit. If necessary I shall resign as Prime Minister; I do not even need the leadership of the party. But while remaining Prime Minister, I will not allow ‘rosaries’ to issue commands in politics.”

The Buddhist monks had imagined that a great upheaval would erupt, that the Prime Minister would run to them in fear and surrender. They were disappointed. The Burmese Prime Minister has made the dignity of democracy victorious. We commend him.

This incident demonstrates that even in this age of such intellectual clarity, religious leaders who believe they can control governments wait for an opportunity beneath the guise of holy men.

It also shows how much suffering a plan for religious teaching in schools can produce.

There are Kulluka Bhattars here who dream of turning politics into religion's puppet and seeing priestly domination once again.

The Burmese Prime Minister has taught such people a fitting lesson.

But here...!
