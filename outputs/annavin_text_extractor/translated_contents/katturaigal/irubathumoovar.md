## English Translation

I garlanded him! I worshipped at his feet! A heart like milk! Speech like honey! He played with me like a little child! But when he opposed the ruinous imperialism that had seized and tormented the country, he roared like a lion! When he sang, “Fear not! The Mother has forty crore faces and is renowned throughout the four quarters,” old men became youths! Idlers were driven away! Lovers of office were removed! Those who aided the foreigner fled with feet at their backs!

Alas, how many times must they have trumpeted in this fashion, with pride, deep faith and delight!

“Our own people have come to rule! The companions of the poor have ascended the seat! The sceptre has reached worthy hands! Now every mean quality will scatter and flee!” How many joyful songs those comrades must have sung.

How many times, brows knitted, must they have argued with comrades of the opposing party!

The faith they held has fallen away; a bullet has entered their hearts!

Yes, they were the ones who performed *arati*, welcomed with blessings and stood asking for boons!

They asked for life; what they received was death! They sought their friends; their friends sought their guns!

They went where they had been told they would find love, and found destruction. They approached those from whom they expected rights, claiming kinship, and from them came only the deadly force that drinks life. Sarojini Devi said, “They asked for bread and received a stone!” She must now write a new poem, in tears: they asked for life and received death!

“This is not a party quarrel, nor a struggle to capture office! It is work to sustain Indian society,” said people in pure clothing and in language that made hearts throb. How could those disposed to believe that everything white was milk refrain from supporting them?

Moreover, they displayed the scars of sacrifice. They beat their breasts and declared that labouring for the motherland's freedom was their religion. The enthusiasm they aroused was no ordinary thing. The heroism they displayed could enchant anyone. There was no command they failed to issue, no god they failed to summon as witness! It is no wonder that pure-hearted people, hearing them speak, came forward to praise them.

And what did those worshippers behold? Bullets piercing their chests!

Everyone knew that General Dyer would shoot, for killing by gunfire was his profession. He was a foreigner, crazed with domination. He fired until his ammunition was exhausted. People lamented, but they were not stunned.

Today they do not merely lament; they stand stunned!

How could they have expected that those whom they welcomed by lighting camphor and performing all sixteen ritual honours would shoot them?

When one sees an anthill, one may fear that a snake lives there. Why should that fear arise on entering a flower garden? Why should the sight of fruit make one think of deadly poison? Likewise, why would anyone think of guns on seeing “saviours of the nation”? They could not have imagined they would be shot; they thought they would prosper. They toiled for well-being and sang blessings for many years. Yet, pitiably, they were shot.

Blood gushed from the hearts in which longing and affection, ardour and faith, had surged.

This “incident” did not occur after they had wearied of waiting, wearied of supplication, after their eyes had grown dim and their minds despondent. Had it happened then, even the measure of its horror might have seemed less.

Their faith had not weakened! Desire had not dried up at the root! Their attachment to the nation had not broken! Even as they were saying, “Friends! Lovers of the country!”, the bullet struck and life flew away.

Not even one year has been completed since the Congress came to power! Even the hands that performed *arati* have not yet grown tired. Yet there have already been two ministries!

Let that pass; it is the comic chapter.

What we described above is the chapter of agony: the pitiable story of people who stood asking for a boon and lost their lives. After the tale of seeking refuge came the tale of the execution rock!

How many believed that only when Congress came to rule would hardship end, anxiety disappear, poverty leave and life bloom! Here, relying on the minister's own statement, we have described the “reward” received by twenty-three of them. There are countless others languishing in prison, beaten with batons or struck by tear-gas shells, and still others standing before the courts. The reward these twenty-three received under Congress rule was death!

In the Legislative Assembly, a member asked a minister, “Sir! Since Congress members became ministers, how many people have died in police firing?” Is that not stranger than asking a doctor, “How many people have so far died as victims of your operations?”

In the kingdom of those who came to give life, under the rule of those who came to grant boons, under the rule of people committed to love and nonviolence, rain ought to fall three times a month, cow and tiger ought to drink at the same watering place, and people ought to live happily. How, then, could gunfire be heard? Is this the rule of foreign masters? This is the good government that we ourselves established with incense, lamps and ritual offerings! What work could a gun have here?

Was this the minister's answer?

No! No! The minister even knew the mortuary count!

“Since the Congress ministry was formed, twenty-three people have died in police firing,” Minister Dr Subbarayan replied.

Remember: the ministry has not yet completed even one year.

Yet already twenty-three!

Noble people who laboured for the elevation of those who stopped the heartbeats of these twenty-three, shed at least one drop of tears for your dead friends.

If a single body fell here to a British bullet, they cried that we must struggle until we ourselves became corpses. Yes, platform oratory, the speech of intellectuals, the speech of the privileged!

Twenty-three bodies before one year has passed: a sign of patriotic love!

We lie helpless, without even the means to condemn it. We have lost even the capacity to think!

Those bodies themselves would laugh if they saw our condition!

How deeply those pitiable twenty-three must have believed when they welcomed and blessed this government! How their hearts must have swelled, poor souls! At the moment of death they could not have thought very much; they were people felled by bullets. They would have had only a second, a few seconds, to think. Some may even have cried aloud:

They asked for life!

They received death!

6 April 1947
