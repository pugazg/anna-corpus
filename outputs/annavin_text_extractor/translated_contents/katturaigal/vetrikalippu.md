## English Translation

## A Flood of People: The Sight of Madras Assembled!

Compulsory Hindi abolished; a victory celebration at Pethunaickenpet resounded with the trumpet call of young bulls!

Tree branches, compound walls, temple towers, upper storeys of buildings, rooftops, lamp posts, and vehicle shelters were crowded everywhere. The streets of Pethunaickenpet illustrated the saying that a crowd was so dense one could not scatter even sesame seeds upon the ground. People wherever one looked; masses in every direction.

People gathered like a flood, making one wonder whether the sight was dream or reality. As they moved this way and that, it seemed as though the nearby sea itself had shifted and entered the city.

"It was from this place that our volunteers in the righteous struggle set out every day. We have assembled at the same place to celebrate the day compulsory Hindi was abolished," said Comrade K. Govindasami, secretary of the Madras Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, referring to the Kasi Viswanathar temple where the celebration took place.

Students, teachers, city notables, movement volunteers, and women volunteers gathered in delight at the abolition of compulsory Hindi. Pethunaickenpet had never seen such a meeting. The crowd filled and overflowed every street, leaving no room to move, and made one wonder whether all Madras had entered Pethunaickenpet.

Comrade C.T.T. Arasu, who had served as the first captain during the anti-Hindi righteous struggle, presided over the celebration.

As a sign of affection, members of the Madras Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam garlanded C.N.A., the *Sarvadhikari* of the struggle; Arasu, who had served as captain; and the men and women who participated in it.

"We have won. This victory is a sign of the victories we shall obtain in the future," declared Comrade K.A. Mathiazhagan, president of the Students' Kazhagam, with pride.

Comrade Alamelu Appadurai, a woman volunteer in the struggle, proclaimed firmly that mothers would not hesitate to participate not only in the language struggle but in any struggle conducted for the victory of the ideal.

"The rulers mocked us, but today we play upon the summit of victory. This is the victory of our policy, a victory announcing that our policy will always prevail," said Comrade Rama Arangannal.

After Comrade T. Shanmugam of Tiruvottiyur condemned the rulers' conduct, Comrade K. Anbazhagan, Tamil professor at Pachaiyappa's College in Madras, explained how badly the Congress rulers had damaged education.

"They asked, 'What can these people accomplish?' Today, shouting, 'Look, we can!' we have attained complete victory. Boastfulness has fallen; righteousness has endured," he proclaimed enthusiastically.

DMK General Secretary Comrade C.N. Annadurai then began: "Compulsory Hindi has been abolished, and the people are immersed in a sea of delight. Seeing their happiness doubles our joy. We were the people who declared that compulsory Hindi must not be imposed and entered the righteous struggle. We bore the hardships. That is why we rejoice today upon hearing that compulsory Hindi has been abolished."

## A Rare Opportunity, Very Soon

Continuing his speech, C.N.A. said:

"Whether it was an election stratagem, whether our opposition caused it, or even whether neither was responsible and it resulted from the rulers' own wisdom, there must be some reason why the government has now ordered that Hindi is not compulsory.

"Nothing happens without cause. Whatever the cause, we were the people who said compulsory Hindi must not be imposed, and therefore we rejoice.

"We said ten years ago that Hindi must not be imposed. Achariyar looked at us arrogantly. Thalamuthu and Natarajan became corpses for opposing Hindi. Shouting, 'From which direction will Hindi come, and how large an army will it bring?' thousands of us went to prison. At last, Hindi fell.

"Though Avinashilingam knew that history, he introduced Hindi, and Madhava Menon insisted upon it. They regarded our opposition as contemptible. Though they knew that scholars, poets, and the people of the country opposed it, they mocked us.

"But today, just as enlightenment came to the Buddha beneath the Bodhi tree, it has dawned upon Madhava Menon. He now says that the government must act according to the wishes of learned thinkers, the public, and officials. After all these days, the government now says precisely what we of the Dravidian movement said then."

## If the Train Leaves the Track, We Have the Means!

"Some may suspect that the government's new order making Hindi optional contains some stratagem. I can assure them of one thing.

"The train has reached the station, though it arrived late. It has arrived without leaving the rails, and to that extent we are happy. But if it leaves the station again, takes the wrong path, and comes off the rails, we possess the strength to stop it.

"Through our righteous struggle, we have proved that we possess that strength. No one need therefore entertain any doubt. If these rulers enter a harmful path that injures the people and the country, we can stop them."

## Our Sacrifice Was Not for Five Acres!

The DMK General Secretary continued:

"They mocked us then, yet we did not grow weary. Bring before your mind's eye the volunteers setting out every day from this very place, the police standing in formation, and the people straining to overcome them. You will understand the hardships and losses we bore.

"We began by saying, 'Fear not, victory is ours!' Today, just as we said, we have won.

"Our mothers and comrades entered this righteous struggle for victory. They endured baton blows. Disregarding rain and sun, they stood picketing at school entrances. Even when police dragged them away in lorries, they did not lose heart. Even when police abandoned a pregnant mother without support at Savukkukadu, their resolve did not weaken.

"Those from our movement who endured all these hardships were small shopkeepers, mothers responsible for households, and young men. Why did they face such losses and join the righteous struggle? Was it from a desire to receive five acres of land? No. They did it from a duty to remove the danger threatening their language.

"To every man and woman who willingly entered the righteous struggle as soon as our appeal reached them, we offer from our hearts our affection, respect, praise, and congratulations."

## We Shall Carry the Tar Pot!

He referred to measures taken by the rulers to deprive us of freedom of writing. Then, condemning the neglect of Tamil at railway stations, he said:

"The names of towns are written wrongly at railway stations. No Tamil heart can see that practice without throbbing. The rulers have deliberately given Hindi the top position and placed the mother tongue below it.

"We have pointed this out and condemned it many times, but the practice has not changed. At this moment of rejoicing, we therefore tell the rulers with affection that the boards must be changed and the names written in Tamil at the top.

"When Hindi was made compulsory, we said with affection, 'We do not want it.' They did not listen. They allowed the trouble to grow and have now yielded. Likewise, we now appeal with affection: 'Do not degrade the mother tongue. Do not wound Tamil hearts.'

"We expect the government, unlike before, to accept our request now and make the necessary arrangements.

"If the government says, 'No, it cannot be done; there is no time,' I wish to tell it humbly: please grant us permission. We shall set out with a tar pot in one hand and a brush in the other, and we ourselves shall change and rewrite the names!"

He concluded with these words.

*Dravida Nadu*, 30 July 1950.
