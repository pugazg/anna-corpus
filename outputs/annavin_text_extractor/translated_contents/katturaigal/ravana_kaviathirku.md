3. இராவண காவியத்தூக்குத் தடை!

ஆசிரியர் குழந்தை அவர்களால் ஆக்கப்பெற்ற
“இராவண காவியம்” என்ற இலக்கிய நூலுக்குச் சென்னை
சர்க்கார் தடை விதித்துள்ளது. இராமாயணம் பாடிய
வால்மீகியும், கம்பரும் அக்காலத்து ஆசிரியர்கள். இராவண
காவியம் பாடிய குழந்தை என்பவர் இக்காலத்து ஆசிரியர்.
வால்மீகிக்கும் கம்பருக்கும் அக்காலத்தில் இராமன்
நல்லவனாகக் காணப்பட்டான். குழந்தைக்கு இராவணன்
இக்காலத்தில் நல்லவனாகக் காணப்படுகின்றான். இராவணன்
நல்லவன் என்ற கருத்தை ஆசிரியர் குழந்தை அவர்களுக்கு
ஊட்டியது. இராமனை நல்லவனாகக் கூறும் இராமாயணம்தான்
என்பதை எவரும் மறுக்க முடியாது. ஆனால், இராமன்
நல்லவனாகக் கூறிய வால்மீகி, எந்த ஆதாரத்தின்மீது இராமன்
நல்லவன் என்ற கருத்தை வெளியிட்டார் என்பது
தெரியவில்லை. இராம-இராவணப் போர் நிகழுவதற்கு
முன்னமேயே வால்மீகி இராமாயணம் பாடிவிட்டார் என்று
சொல்லப்படுகின்றது. எனவே, வால்மீகி பாடிய
இராமாயணத்துக்கு வால்மீகியேதான் ஆதாரம். இராமாயண
ஆசிரியரான வால்மீகி, இராம-இராவணப் போர் நிகழுவதற்கு

முன்பே, இராமனை நல்லவனாகக் கருதும் உரிமையைப்

பெற்றிருந்தார் என்றால், இராம-இராவணப் போர் நிகழ்ந்ததாகக்

ஆராய்ந்த இராவண காவிய ஆசிரியர், இராவணனை

நல்லவனாகக் கருதும் உரிமையை என் பெறக்கூடாது?

ஒருவனை, அறியாமுன் - அவனோடு பழ்கா மூன் -
அவனுடைய நடவடிக்கைகளை அறியாமுன் அவனை
நல்லவன் என்று கூறும் உரிமை வால்மீகிக்கு இருந்தது.
ஆனால், ஒருவனை, அவன் இப்படிப்பட்டவன் -
அவனுடைய நடவடிக்கைகள் இப்படிப்பட்டவை என்பதை
நன்கு அறிந்தபின் அவனை நல்லவன் என்று கூறும் உரிமை
ஆசிரியர் குழந்தைக்கு இல்லை. இது எந்த வகையில்
நியாயம் என்பது எமக்குப் புரியவில்லை. இராமபிரானை
நல்லவர் என்று கூறும் உரிமை சிலருக்கு இருக்கும்போது,
இராவணேஸ்வரனை நல்லவர் என்று கூறும் உரிமை சிலருக்கு
ஏன் இருக்கக்கூடாது?

அயோத்தி மன்னனான. இராமனை வால்மீகி
நல்லவனாகக். கூறியதால், இன்று இராமன் கடவுளாகக்
கருதப்படுகின்றான். இலங்கை மன்னனான இராவணனை
வால்மீகி கெட்டவனாகக் கூறியதால், இன்று இராவணன்
இராட்சதனாகக் கருதப்படுகின்றான். இருவரும் நாட்டை
ஆண்ட மன்னர்கள்தான். இவர்களில் ஒருவர் கடவுள்! மற்றவன்
இராட்சதன்! சண்டை போட்டவர்களில் வென்றவன் நல்லவன்
மட்டுமல்ல, கடவுளுமாகின்றான். தோற்றவன் கெட்டவன்
மட்டுமல்ல, இராட்சதனுமாகின்றான். இவை எந்தவகையில்
நியாயமென்று ஆசிரியர் குழந்தை அவர்கள் தம்முடைய

நூலான இராவண காவியத்தின் வாயிலாகக் கேட்கிறார்.
இப்படிக் கேட்பதைத் தவறென்றும் சட்ட விரோதமென்றும்
சென்னை சர்க்கார் கருதி அந்நூலுக்குத் தடை விதித்துள்ளனர்.

மக்களின் உரிமைகளைப் பறித்த எகாதிபத்திய
சர்க்காரை விரட்டிய 'ஜனநாயக' சர்க்காரே இன்று மக்களின்
உரிமைகளைப் பறிக்கும் பணியைத் தங்களுடைய முதல்
வேலையாகக் கொண்டுள்ளது. இது உண்மையிலேயே வருந்தக்
கூடியதும் வெட்கப்படக்கூடியதுமான ஒருமுறை தவறிய

செயலாகும்.

இராவண காவியம் என்ற அறிவியல் இலக்கிய
நூலுக்குத் தடை விதித்த சென்னைச் சர்க்காரின் இந்தப்
போக்கைக் கண்டித்துத் தமிழ் எழுத்தாளர் மாநாட்டுத் தலைவர்
தோழர் நாரணதுரைக்கண்ணன் அவர்கள் 15-6-48-ல் வெளி
வந்த 'பிரகடண்ட விகடனி'ல் எழுதியிருக்கிறார். தோழர்
நாரணதுரைக்கண்ணன் அவர்கள் வெறும் எழுத்தாளர்
மாநாட்டுத் தலைவர் மட்டுமல்ல, எழுத்தாளர் நலனில் பெரிதும்
அக்கரை கொண்டவர். எழுத்தாளர்கள் தங்கள் எழுத்து
வாயிலாக நாட்டுக்குச் செய்யக்கூடிய சேவைகள்
இன்னின்னவென்பதை நன்கு உணர்ந்தவர். அதனாலேயே
அவர் எழுத்தாளர் மாநாட்டின் தலைவராகத் தேர்ந்தெடுக்கப்
பட்டிருக்கிறார்.

எழுத்தாளர்களில் பலர் இத்தகைய நடுநிலைமையைக்
கொள்வதில்லை. தோழர் நாரணதுரைக்கண்ணன் அவர்கள்
எழுத்தாளர்களுக்கு ஒரு விழிப்பையும், உண்மையை
உரைப்பதில் தயக்கம் காட்டக்கூடாதென்பதையும் இராவண

## English Translation

The Madras Government has banned the literary work *Ravana Kaviyam*, composed by Pulavar Kuzhanthai. Valmiki and Kambar, who sang the *Ramayana*, were authors of their age. Kuzhanthai, who sang *Ravana Kaviyam*, is an author of this age.

To Valmiki and Kambar, Rama appeared good in their time. To Kuzhanthai, Ravana appears good in this time. Nobody can deny that it was the *Ramayana*, which calls Rama good, that planted in Kuzhanthai the idea that Ravana was good.

Yet we do not know upon what evidence Valmiki expressed his opinion that Rama was good. We are told that Valmiki completed the *Ramayana* even before the war between Rama and Ravana occurred. Valmiki himself is therefore the authority for Valmiki's *Ramayana*.

If Valmiki possessed the right to regard Rama as good even before the supposed Rama-Ravana war, why should the author of *Ravana Kaviyam*, who examined the *Ramayana's* account of that war with the aid of reason, not possess the right to regard Ravana as good?

Valmiki had the right to call a person good before knowing him, associating with him or learning his conduct. But Kuzhanthai has no right to call a person good after learning clearly what kind of man he was and what his actions were. We cannot understand how this is just. When some possess the right to call Lord Rama good, why should others not possess the right to call Ravana good?

Because Valmiki called Rama, king of Ayodhya, good, Rama is regarded as God today. Because Valmiki called Ravana, king of Lanka, evil, Ravana is regarded as a demon. Both were kings who ruled countries. One is a god and the other a demon. Of two who fought, the victor becomes not merely good but divine; the defeated becomes not merely bad but demonic.

Through *Ravana Kaviyam*, Kuzhanthai asks how this can be just. The Madras Government has regarded the very asking of that question as wrong and unlawful and has banned the book.

The “democratic” government that drove out an imperial government for depriving people of their rights has now made depriving those rights its own first task. This is truly a deplorable, shameful and improper act.

Comrade Naran. Duraikannan, president of the Tamil Writers' Conference, condemned the Madras Government's ban on this rational literary work in the issue of *Prasanda Vikatan* dated 15 June 1948. Duraikannan is not merely president of a writers' conference; he cares deeply about writers' welfare and understands the services they can render the country through their writing. That is why he was chosen to preside over the conference.

Many writers do not adopt such an impartial position. By condemning the ban on *Ravana Kaviyam*, Duraikannan commendably awakened writers and demonstrated that they must not hesitate to speak the truth. If other writers and editors in our country followed this method, the government would not dare plunge blindly into such unnecessary actions.

Duraikannan wrote his condemnation only after reading Kuzhanthai's *Ravana Kaviyam* carefully. We reproduce his statement here in the hope that the government will heed it and correct its mistake:

> The Madras Government has confiscated an excellent epic worthy of serving as an ornament to Tamil literature. The work is called *Ravana Kaviyam* and was written by Kuzhanthai. Its central idea is that Ravana was superior to Rama.
>
> An author possesses as much right to speak highly of Ravana as the authors of the *Ramayana* possessed to speak highly of Rama. Some call the *Ramayana* an imaginative tale, while others call it history. We do not now wish to enter that debate. But we regard the Madras Government's ban as an obstruction placed upon the growth of Tamil literature.
>
> Eminent Western scholars have written many different works of research on the birth and life of Christ, praised as the Son of God. They have even argued that no person called Christ was ever born, or that, if such a person was born, he could not have arisen from the womb of the Virgin Mary. Likewise, scientific scholars have published important research arguing that no god exists who protects this world.
>
> Such books have not been banned in European countries governed by people who believe in Christ and his religion. The reason is that knowledge must not be prohibited and that freedom of speech and writing, fundamental human rights, must exist.
>
> By following this course, Europeans have granted people the right to direct thought into many fields, decide what is right or wrong, and allow literature of many kinds to flourish. It is evident that this has earned European peoples a distinctive standing in the world.
>
> By banning *Ravana Kaviyam*, the Madras Government has itself created influence and distinction for the book. Even people who have not read it will now wish to read it secretly. In place of one *Ravana Kaviyam*, many more may arise in new forms.
>
> When the former Madras Government banned Bharati's songs, the people began reading them with greater interest and desire. Similarly, the British rulers of India believed that preventing Marxist books from entering from abroad would stop Communism spreading in India. Nothing happened as they expected. Instead, we see socialist doctrine spreading through the country like wildfire, while they also failed to stop the Marxist books.
>
> The Madras Government imposed this ban without remembering past experience. We cannot refrain from strongly condemning its action. It is urgently necessary that the government recognize its mistake and withdraw the order immediately. If it refuses, its intention will not be fulfilled; instead, its attempt will end like the tale of setting out to make an image of Pillaiyar and producing a monkey.

Thus wrote Duraikannan.

If any prohibitory order concerning the *Ramayana* were justified, it should have been imposed not on *Ravana Kaviyam* but on the *Kamba Ramayanam*. Anyone comparing Kambar's work with Valmiki's knows that Kambar, while translating Valmiki, inserted many events absent from the original and concealed many that it contained. Should not the government ban the work written by adding what was absent and hiding what was present?

Even if the government had never previously considered banning the *Kamba Ramayanam*, once it developed the urgency to ban *Ravana Kaviyam*, it should at least have read Kambar's epic once. It should have read not only that book but its source, Valmiki's *Ramayana*. It would then understand why the author of *Ravana Kaviyam* describes Ravana as good and Rama as bad. It could see that the seeds enabling Kuzhanthai to portray Ravana favourably lie buried here and there in both epics.

More questions follow. Why did Kambar turn Rama, whom Valmiki had not made a god, into God? Had we suffered such a famine of gods that Kambar needed to provide a king as one? Before Kambar made Rama divine, did we possess no god? If we already had one, why did Kambar need to make another person into God? Why did he choose not a sage or ascetic of the kind appearing in religious texts, but a ruler of a country? At the same time, why did he turn Ravana, another ruler like Rama, into a demon whom people would hate and despise?

What virtues in Rama caused him to be made divine? What vices in Ravana caused him to be made demonic? The government should have compared these and related questions with its reasons for banning *Ravana Kaviyam*.

First, it should have tried to understand what caused a work called *Ravana Kaviyam* to arise. When one book, the *Ramayana*, already tells us about Rama and Ravana, why did a new *Ravana Kaviyam* become necessary? Why does it lower Rama and elevate Ravana? When one author has the right to write Rama as good, why should another not have the right to write Ravana as good?

The *Kamba Ramayanam*, containing many newly inserted and incongruous incidents absent from Valmiki, circulates throughout the country. Why alone should *Ravana Kaviyam*, a work of inquiry that adorns knowledge, not be allowed to exist?

Before banning it, the government should have reflected that the *Kamba Ramayanam* contains many events capable of creating differences of opinion. That is precisely why books such as *Ravana Kaviyam*, expressing ideas contrary to Kambar's, arise. Without the *Kamba Ramayanam*, there would have been no *Ravana Kaviyam*; Kambar's epic produced Kuzhanthai's.

Had the government possessed the ability to think this through, it might have formed the sound intention of first banning the *Kamba Ramayanam*. Instead, like blaming the arrow while leaving the archer alone, it bans *Ravana Kaviyam* but not the work that served as its cause. This cannot be just in any sense. If the government has not considered this until now, it should begin doing so.

Some lovers of epic poetry may argue: “Whatever the narrative of the *Kamba Ramayanam*, and however contrary to reason it may be, contemplation of its poetic beauty inspires a desire to cherish and cultivate it, not destroy it.” The government may have refrained from banning it because it accepts that view. We do not say that this is wrong. Let people savour the epic sweetness of the *Kamba Ramayanam* like sugar candy.

But likewise, even setting aside the ideas in *Ravana Kaviyam*, why not allow people to enjoy its poetic artistry? Why ban it? If people acknowledge Kambar's faults while enjoying his poetry, they may acknowledge any faults in *Ravana Kaviyam*, if such faults exist, while enjoying its art. Can anyone say that *Ravana Kaviyam* is inferior to the *Kamba Ramayanam* in poetic quality, grammar or literary character?

The president of the Writers' Conference, Comrade Naran. Duraikannan himself, calls it “an excellent epic worthy of serving as an ornament to Tamil literature.” Nobody can therefore say that it lacks poetic merit or that people should not read it for that merit.

If the *Kamba Ramayanam* is fit to exist in this country, *Ravana Kaviyam*, a literary work superior in many respects, may exist here too. Kuzhanthai did not turn Ravana into God as Kambar turned Rama into God. With appropriate evidence, he refuted what Valmiki, Kambar and others said about Ravana being evil and explained that Rama was the bad man and Ravana the good one.

If the government regards this as a crime capable of causing danger, we must conclude that it cannot identify the criminal. Who is guilty: Kambar and Valmiki, or Comrade Kuzhanthai? Let the public deliver the judgment.

*Dravida Nadu*, 20 June 1948

## Translator's Notes

`இராவண காவியம்` (*Ravana Kaviyam*) is the Tamil epic by Pulavar Kuzhanthai. The article concerns the Madras Government's 1948 ban and argues both for freedom of literary expression and for a Dravidian reinterpretation of the *Ramayana*. The claims about Valmiki's chronology, Kambar's alterations, European scholarship and the relative literary merits of the works are translated as arguments made by the article and quoted editorial, not independently verified judgments.

The OCR gives the journal title as `பிரகடண்ட விகடன்`; contextual identification supports *Prasanda Vikatan*. The quoted writer's name appears as `நாரணதுரைக்கண்ணன்`, commonly rendered Naran. Duraikannan. `பிள்ளையார் பிடிக்கக் குரங்காய் முடிந்தது` is a proverb for an undertaking that produces a grotesquely unintended result.

`அறிவியல் இலக்கிய நூல்` here means a rational, analytical or knowledge-oriented literary work, not a work of natural science. `இராவணேஸ்வரன்` is an honorific form for Ravana used rhetorically. Catalogue number `3.` is not part of the title. Scattered punctuation and word-division damage has not been silently changed in the preserved Tamil source.
