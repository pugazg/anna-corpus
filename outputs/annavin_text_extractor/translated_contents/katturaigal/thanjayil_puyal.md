## English Translation

> “We have completed what we promised!” - M. Karunanidhi  
> “This is the great assistance provided by drama!” - C. N. A.

At six o'clock on Tuesday evening, 28 April 1953, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam's cyclone relief fund presentation and completion celebration was held at Tilakar Grounds in Tanjore.

At the opening, Comrade K. R. Sellamuthu sang the Dravidian anthem. Tanjore comrades A. V. Pathi and K. Pattu respectively proposed and seconded that K. K. Neelamegam, secretary of the Tanjore District DMK, preside. In his introductory remarks, the chairman explained the fund presentation. Comrade N. S. Ilango then spoke, followed by Comrade A. Ponnambalanar.

Next, Comrade M. Karunanidhi, secretary of the Cyclone Relief Fund Committee, spoke as follows:

“Beloved chairman, Anna, elders and comrades!

“Today's meeting, convened on behalf of the Tanjore City DMK, has been called to inform the people that the fund committee appointed with me as secretary has completed the task entrusted to it. That task arose from the resolution passed by the General Council at Tanjore to provide relief funds to people affected by the cyclone.

“At the public meeting held on the evening of the General Council session in Tanjore, when we mentioned the resolution to collect and contribute Rs 25,000, we worried whether we would be able to provide it. It remained our one thought both while sleeping and after waking.

“But at the hour when we needed funds, our god, ‘powder,’ came to our aid. Through plays, admission-charging public meetings and small sums collected and sent by comrades and supporters of the organisation, we have raised Rs 27,500.

“From this amount, we have given an advance of Rs 5,000 to our comrade Thyagarajan of Podaturpet. Instead of placing cash directly in the hands of those affected, we have asked the Podaturpet Cooperative Handloom Society to prepare clothing, dhotis and saris for distribution. We have asked that our black-and-red emblem be woven into their borders.

“We made this arrangement because it would simultaneously aid those affected by the cyclone and help relieve the suffering of handloom workers afflicted by unemployment.

“The first play in Madras, in which Anna, K. R. Ramasamy and Sivaji Ganesan acted, yielded a surplus of Rs 7,000. Though Anna's health was poor when that play was staged, he remembered the responsibility he had accepted and participated. We followed it with many plays and fundraising meetings.

“In addition, comrades devoted to our movement raised and sent money from important towns throughout Tamil Nadu and from places abroad such as Bihar, Orissa, Indo-China and Ceylon. Workers in Madras hotels and people selling ribbons in the streets also collected and contributed money.

“At this moment I thank everyone who helped and cooperated in raising the fund. By the second week of May, we intend to convene a great meeting in Madras and present the fund to the Governor of Madras in the form of clothing.

“While a cyclone blew here, a gentle breeze was blowing for Nehru, Prime Minister of the Indian subcontinent, in Travancore. While people here suffered from the cyclone, Nehru, who came to Travancore, no, who paid a ceremonial visit there, returned to Delhi without even turning to look in this direction.

“The northerners have neither attachment to nor concern for us. We beg here, and the central government refuses to turn and look. Why this wretched condition? Because our life is trapped in the hands of a government without sympathy or affection for us.

“The government there is a puppet government, a hollow government. Achariyar, who rules us here, invokes God for everything. Comrade Palaniappan, the Tanjore District Collector, has taught Achariyar an apt lesson. Instead of placing all the gods in temples, he has followed the very propaganda we have long conducted and turned the gods into museum exhibits. Even that is not surprising. He made Achariyar himself open the museum!

“In his book, the Italian political thinker Machiavelli said: ‘Turn the minds of suffering people towards God; place the burden for everything upon God.’ Many dictators used that method. The last dictator, Achariyar, uses the same one.

“Achariyar's marvellous strategy is to invoke God on one side and, on the other, invite members of opposition parties to cooperate with him in opposing the central government. But he does not display opposition to the North from our standpoint.”

He again thanked everyone who had helped raise the cyclone relief fund and concluded.

DMK General Secretary Annadurai then spoke:

“Beloved chairman and comrades,

“As resolved at the DMK General Council meeting in Tanjore, we decided to collect Rs 25,000 and provide relief to people affected by the cyclone in Tanjore and Tiruchirappalli districts. We formed a committee with our friend Karunanidhi as secretary. We have convened this meeting to give the people the happy news that more than the amount we fixed has been collected.

“Our friend Karunanidhi has told you of our decision to provide the money in the form of clothing. When we made this decision at the General Council and announced it at the public meeting, our friends in the opposition wondered whether these people could accomplish it. Now that we have raised the fund and announce it, they will mock us by asking, ‘What is so remarkable about this?’ Whatever they may say, I declare that we have raised the fund.

“I convey my thanks to the comrades of the movement and our actor friends who helped raise and provide it.

“Before our friend Karunanidhi has recovered either from the delight or the exhaustion of raising this fund, I am going to send him to the field of struggle. I am confident that, just as he raised this fund successfully, he will conduct that struggle properly and bring us victory.

“The reason is that the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has never undertaken a task and left it unfinished or disorderly. The Erode school in which we were trained gave us this maturity. Though it may now have more teachers and fewer students, it was quite sound when we received our training there.

“Once we finish raising a fund, our opposition friends say, ‘What is so remarkable about it?’ They are the very people who asked, before we began, ‘Can this be done?’ We need not concern ourselves with that.

“The friends who spoke before me referred to our government. I know that Tanjore district is filled with intelligent people. I ask the general public at this meeting, our Congress Socialist friends and the friends who have withdrawn to the side: let them place a hand upon their hearts and say what remarkable deed Achariyar accomplished during the year since he came to the seat of government in this province.

“What deed that no one else could perform has he done? What has he achieved that can be called superior to the accomplishments of his predecessors as heads of government, Kumaraswami Raja and Omandur Ramasamy Reddiar?

“I shall now show you comrades who achieved many things within a single year. What deed more remarkable than theirs has Achariyar performed?

“In Egypt, at the western edge of Asia, Minister Naguib, within a year of assuming power, expelled the king who lived in luxury without concern for the people. He removed corruption from the political parties there and prevented party members from plundering money from the people. He enacted a law that no one might hold more than two hundred acres.

“Next there is the venerable elder Mossadegh. He prevented England and the capitalist imperial power America from sharing and plundering the oil wells in Persian soil. He defied all the opposition of those empires. Not only did he defy them, he went to America itself and said in a trembling voice, ‘I cannot tolerate the people of my country writhing in hunger.’

“They asked whether oil beneath the earth could be brought out without the kindness of their experts. He brought it out and showed them. They asked whether it could be sent abroad. He sent it. They asked whether it could be sold without their goodwill. He sold it and showed them.

“What supremely marvellous deed has Achariyar achieved like those of Naguib and Mossadegh?

“He speaks about God in connection with everything. Who needs God? What need has arisen to preach about God to the poor people of the country? When a stone strikes their foot, they cry ‘Muruga!’ When they enter their home and hit their head upon the doorframe, they call ‘O God!’ What meaning can there be in speaking about God to poor people who already invoke God for everything?

“Religious preaching is needed among the rich, the landlords and estate holders, and those who plunder in the black market. Why preach it to the poor?

“There is a Latin proverb: *Vox populi, vox Dei*. In English it means, ‘The voice of the people is the voice of God.’ That is its meaning.

“Did the people's voice send Achariyar to the seat of government? In the last election the people delivered a verdict that the Congress Party to which Achariyar belongs should not ascend to power. Did Achariyar respect that verdict and act accordingly? Why, then, does he speak about God?”

After speaking thus, he thanked those who had helped raise the fund and concluded.

Tanjore City DMK secretary Comrade Pattu then proposed a vote of thanks, and the meeting ended pleasantly.

*(Report, Dravida Nadu, 3 May 1953)*
