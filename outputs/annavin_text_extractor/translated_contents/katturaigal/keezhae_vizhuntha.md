## English Translation

A fruit gave the world sublime discoveries.

A tiny apple fell from a tree. It gave rise to a magnificent thought.

"Why did it fall?" The man who saw the fruit fall asked his own mind.

Had he asked others, they would have replied, "Why would a fruit fall? It must have fallen because it was ripe!" He had grown tired of hearing that answer from many people. So he questioned his own mind: "Why must the fallen fruit descend directly towards the earth?"

Had anyone asked another person this question, it would have been mocked. But he questioned his own thought. He threw a small stone into the sky and calculated the speed at which it fell. He dropped an object from a high place and counted the seconds it took to reach the earth. As a result, the scientific world obtained a remarkable truth. Newton became a man of history by explaining that the earth possesses a force of attraction. This happened there, in England.

One fruit gave the world a sublime truth.

Here is another fruit. A preacher of the Bharata describes it in a flood of devotion. During the Pandavas' forest exile, Draupadi is said to have asked for a fruit, and Arjuna plucked it for her. It belonged to a sage. Fearing danger if he discovered this, Dharmar and his brothers supposedly prayed to Chakrapani to return it to its place. The dark, cloud-hued Lord arrived and said that, if each of them spoke the truth within their heart, the fruit would return to the tree.

As each person spoke the truth, the fallen fruit reportedly rose and stopped at a point above. Draupadi concealed the truth, and immediately the fruit descended towards the earth. Krishna smiled and said, "You have lied, Draupadi!" She then told the truth: "I am wife to five men. Though these five are mine, in one corner of my heart my eyes rest upon Karna too." The puranic storyteller describes it dripping with devotional sentiment:

> Though the five, like the five senses,
> are my husbands,
> my great heart melts,
> O Lord,
> for yet another man
> to become my great husband.
> Is there any woman born upon this earth
> who can be trusted
> to remain without another man?
> Thus spoke she,
> like the wife of Vasishta.

"Yet another man to become my great husband!" Did you notice the poetry? Her heart supposedly melts! The puranic expositor too describes it with a melting heart. The instant this truth was heard, the fruit reportedly flew back and attached itself to the tree.

There, one fruit served to reveal a scientific truth.

Here, one fruit serves to display puranic glory.

No one has built a temple to Newton and worshipped him. But here there is no shortage of festivals for Vyasa, Lord Krishna, and the "chaste" Draupadi. Both stories concern fallen fruit. What can one do? There, knowledge marches forward. Here, sanctified mythology attempts to parade.
