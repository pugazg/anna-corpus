## English Translation

"Say whatever fault or criticism you wish about Congress rule, sir. But there is one matter you must sincerely accept. You simply cannot deny it," began Kannusamy of the khadi shop, with a grand preface.

"Why such a strong preface? If you say something that can be accepted, very well. What do you take me for, a blind fool or a savage? Tell me," I replied.

"Congress rule has implemented prohibition, that magnificent plan praised throughout the Presidency, the Mahatma's rare, great, heartfelt scheme..." my friend began to describe.

"The plan is magnificent, and the courageous sacrifice undertaken by our Provincial Government to implement it is proper," I said, slightly reducing his labour.

Delighted, my friend patted me and said, "You have always been somewhat bound by fairness. You do not conceal the truth. That is why I am fond of you," without considering what I might say next.

I gave him a couple of seconds' rest and then offered my own preface: "One must always acknowledge the truth. Is that not the honest course, the course beneficial to the country?"

"What objection could there be? That is righteousness itself," Kannusamy said in support.

"Kannusamy, you and I, along with all discerning people in the country, speak with appreciation in accepting that prohibition is necessary and that the Congress ministry implemented it. What debate is there in that? Naturally I must acknowledge the truth," I said.

"Yes. That is the noblest quality," my friend graciously replied.

"Friend, you too must possess that good quality. You and I and many others praise the ministers, but the prohibition scheme has not succeeded. Illicit liquor flows like a canal wherever one looks," I said. Kannusamy's delight began to melt.

"More than that, friend, your Congress friends themselves sell the illicit liquor. A Gandhi cap upon the head and illicit liquor beneath the arm. This is what happens everywhere prohibition exists. The smell of arrack has not died. Do you know that?" I asked.

He nodded faintly. I again allowed him a few seconds of rest.

"Friend, you know Kaleswara Rao, do you not?"

"Of course! A veteran patriot. After Prakasam Garu, he is the lion of Andhra," he said.

"Did you read in the newspaper how that lion roared a few days ago in the sacred city of Bezwada? Forgive me, I forgot: Vijayawada."

Kannusamy's face wilted slightly. "What would he have said? 'Madras is ours,' surely?"

"No, not that. It concerned prohibition."

My friend asked what he had said, and I began:

"Friend, prohibition, the scheme for which you and I just praised the ministers, is in force in the sacred city of Vijayawada. But men wearing Gandhi caps reportedly sell illicit liquor there in soda bottles under the name 'power soda' at one rupee a bottle."

"A lie! A fabricated story!" he said angrily.

"Here, look at the *Mail* of September 2," I said, handing him the issue.

He looked at it with alarm-filled eyes.

"Kannusamy, I shall give you a brief translation. Tell me whether it is correct," I said, and read:

> Prohibition is in force in Vijayawada, formerly Bezwada, but everyone knows that liquor is abundantly available for purchase.
>
> Men wearing Gandhi caps sell the liquor.
>
> Two kinds of "soda" are sold here. One is ordinary soda at one anna a bottle. The other is "power soda" at one rupee a bottle.
>
> Everyone knows what this "power soda" contains.
>
> If one Congressman arranges for the police to arrest a seller of power soda, another Congressman approaches senior officials and works to rescue the dealer. He will not sleep until the man is released.
>
> When this is the condition, how can the country be reformed?

I handed him the paper containing the translation. Drops appeared in Kannusamy's eyes. He tried to say something, but his throat was dry.

I called to the shop boy: "Hey! Go and buy a soda. An ordinary soda, mind you, not the power soda mentioned by Kaleswara Rao."

Kannusamy said, "I shall come tomorrow. I have urgent work," and left.

(*Dravida Nadu*, 3-10-48)
