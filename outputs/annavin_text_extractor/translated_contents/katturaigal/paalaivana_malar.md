37. பாலைவன மலர்!

பாழ்பட்ட மனம் பாலைவனத்துக்குச் சமமாகும் பாலைவனத்திலே,
பயிரேது, மலர் ஏது! பாழ்பட்ட மனத்திலே, சிந்தனைக்கு இடம் ஏது, உரிமை
உணர்ச்சி ஏது! எழ, முடியாது!

பாரததேவி ஆசிரியரின் கண்களிலே பாலைவன மலடிரான்று
தெரிந்தது! - தூத்துக்குடி மாநாட்டின்போது. சேலிக்குரியது என்ற
எண்ணத்திலே அவர் அவ்விதம் எழுதினார். நான் அவருடைய கேலி
கேட்டுக் கோபமடையவில்லை. ஏனெனில், அவர் கேலிக்குரியது என்று
எந்தக் காட்சியைக் கருதினாரோ, அந்தக் காட்சியும் அதை ஒட்டியுள்ள
கருத்தும், உண்மையாகவே இதுவரை பாழ்பட்டுக் கிடந்த திராவிட
உள்ளத்தில், புதுமலர் பூத்திருக்கிறது என்ற உண்மையைக் காட்டுவதாகவே
நமக்குத் தோன்றுகிறது.

பாலைவனத்து மலர்! - கேலிச் சொல் என்று எண்ணுகிறார் தேவி
ஆசிரியர் - நாம், திராவிடத்தின் நிலை என்று அதைக் கொள்கிறோம் -
களிக்கிறோம் - ஆசிரியரைச் சிந்திக்கும்படி கேட்டுக் கொள்கிறோம்.
பாலைவனத்திலேயே மலர் பூததிடத் தொடங்கிவிட்டதே, முழு மறுமலர்ச்சி
ஏற்பட்டுவிட்டால், எப்படி இருக்கும் என்பதைச்சற்று எண்ணிப் பாருங்கள்!
திராவிடம், பூந்தோட்டமாக மாறும் காலம் பிறக்கிறது என்பதன்
அறிகுறிதான், பாலைவனத்திலே மலர் பூத்திருக்கும் காட்சி.

ஆறு, பத்து, இருபது தலையுடையான், ஆமை, வராக முகமுடை
யான், அந்தப்புரத்திலே அறுபதனாயிரம் பேருடையான், அண்ணன் தம்பி
மகனுடையான், கூத்தி, குமாரி, குறும்புடையான் என்று கூறித்
கடவுளையே, மறைமுகமாக நிந்தித்து வந்தனர் ஓர் கூட்டத்தினர்.

இந்தப் பிரம்ம சொருபத்தைப் பாருங்கள். இவர் பால்பழம் பத்து
நாட்களுக்கொரு முறைதான் சாப்பிடுவார், பக்கத்திலே பாம்பு வந்தாலும்

## English Translation

A ruined mind resembles a desert. What crop or flower can exist in a desert? What place can there be for thought or consciousness of rights in a ruined mind? It cannot rise.

During the Tuticorin conference, the editor of *Bharata Devi* saw what looked to him like a desert flower. He wrote of it that way, thinking it an object of ridicule. I did not grow angry at his mockery, because the scene and idea he considered laughable appear to us as proof that a new flower has bloomed in the Dravidian heart that until now lay barren.

“A flower of the desert!” The *Devi* editor considers it a phrase of mockery. We take it as a description of Dravidia's condition, rejoice, and ask the editor to think. If a flower has already begun to bloom in the desert, consider how things will appear when a full renaissance arrives. The flower blooming in the desert is a sign that the time approaches when Dravidia will become a garden.

One group had long insulted even God indirectly by telling stories of beings with six, ten or twenty heads, tortoise or boar faces, sixty thousand women in their inner quarters, wives related as brothers' daughters, mistresses, maidens and mischievous exploits.

Another dominant group displayed men with matted hair and proclaimed: “Look at this embodiment of Brahman. He takes milk and fruit only once in ten days. Even if a snake comes beside him, he will not rise from the lotus posture. His eyes have been blessed with the vision of the Supreme Being. The goddess of the four-faced one's tongue dances upon his own. Through the special radiance of his gaze, Kamadhenu and the wish-granting tree will arrive in your backyard.” By such claims they reduced heroes to useless men.

When warlike peoples entered like a storm upon horses from Tartary, wearing glittering armour and carrying shining shields, what could the group clutching dried grass do? Empires fell, kingdoms collapsed and warriors' blood mixed with the soil. Built fortresses became dust, dug moats filled up, kings lost their crowns and foreigners established governments.

What did the chanters of the Vedas do then? What could the flames rising from sacrificial pits do to the invaders? People born in the most ordinary households attacked the enemies advancing upon their homeland and bathed in the fire of sacrifice. They made tents in forests, forts in mountains and camps in caves, and fought bitterly, whirling flashing swords.

Yet those very heroes and sacrificers were called people born at the Supreme Being's feet. That degrading claim was presented as profound philosophy until they themselves were made to believe the absurdity.

You worship a powerless group as recipients of God's grace and speak of the “glory” within them. What glory did you see? Inability to act is one glory, deception another, and fabrication of tales another. We transformed forests into cities, yet that contains no glory. We launched ships upon the sea and mastered the waves, yet that contains no glory. Without losing courage when friends became corpses, we passed through the valley of fame, attacked enemies and won, yet that contains no glory. They mesmerise common people by displaying a green palm leaf, and that supposedly contains glory, great and magnificent glory!

There was a time when the hero who dared ask such questions was abused as a reviler of the Vedas, a low man, enemy of scripture and custom, destroyer of eternal religion and a man destined to receive the curse of the virtuous.

Some open-mouthed fools still wish the days when bow-bearing warriors bowed before grass-bearers to continue.

Their representatives gathered and shouted in Madras a few days ago under the name “Progressive Brahmins.”

What religious and caste fanaticism took the life of the noble Gandhi? What curse of caste allowed Dutch, French, Portuguese and British people to settle here and raise their flags? What caste division shattered society and kindled jealousy in people's minds? Against what curse of caste did Vivekananda, Ramalinga, the discerning Vemana, Veeresalingam, Raja Ram Mohan Roy and others speak, struggle and die?

What caste cruelty turned villages into halls of torment and cities into schools of deceit? What curse of caste denied even those who built a temple and dug its tank the right to enter the temple or bathe in that tank? What caste frenzy cultivated animal feeling among people and created barbarism in the country?

Brahmins declared that this very caste order must remain and that it was God's command.

What answer did the intellectuals who prescribe medicine for the disease of communalism give these babblers? How did they permit such ignorant speech?

When a small-minded wearer of the sacred thread said caste must remain, should not those who beat their chests claiming to preserve democracy's great principle have challenged him? Did they?

They said caste could not and should not be abolished because it was divine justice. This was not merely irrational, arrogant and treacherous to Gandhi's ideas; was it not also speech betraying the constitutional plan? Why did the fire-breathers remain silent? What were the sacred mouths of those clouds of condemnation doing?

Gandhi's son goes to South Africa, lashes Lord Smuts for speaking in terms of black and white, and is imprisoned. Editors here write condemnation as though General Smuts stands at their own threshold: “Arrogant man! Man bereft of reason!”

Yet before leaders who rule beneath a flag bearing Ashoka's emblem, Ashoka who laboured to remove divisions based on caste and religion in the capital, a group poured out filtered poison that would not ordinarily emerge even from the mouths of the most orthodox ritualists. Was there a question? Was there anyone to ask? It proceeded grandly!

Letters appear daily in *The Hindu* advising the rulers that the Dravidar Kazhagam creates hatred and spreads communalism, that it must be dissolved, its newspapers prohibited and its members imprisoned.

The rulers will know, and Tuticorin will have made clear to the government, how enormous a crowd would have to be imprisoned once imprisonment begins. They will not listen to these extremists. That is not why I raise this.

These “upright” people incite the government to prohibit the Dravidar Kazhagam, which peacefully campaigns to remove legitimate grievances and social degradation. Yet they themselves spew poison and perversely insist that the caste order condemned by every discerning person must remain. What would the position have been without resistance?

One and a half lakh people travelled from one end of Tamil Nadu to the other in fiercely hot weather, at a time when travel difficulties had increased. Whole families assembled for three days. Does that not clearly show how extensively the campaign has spread and borne fruit? Knowing this, what does it mean when some still speak of perpetuating caste?

The grass-bearers possess a foolish belief: caste abolition is only the Dravidar Kazhagam's programme, while others do not hold it. Therefore, if they denounce the Kazhagam as an atheist party, set the rulers against it and suppress it, Dravidians outside the organisation will follow the path the caste defenders show. Everyone devoted to national welfare, civilisation, sound knowledge, justice and integrity must state firmly that this belief is wrong.

The world must be told that the country has awakened and that the order in which people carrying reeds as weapons moved about in our ancestors' age cannot continue today.

Honourable people within Congress must declare openly that they and the Dravidar Kazhagam have no difference over abolishing caste. The Chief Minister, born among labouring people and now endowed with the quality of governing, is called a conciliator and a devotee of Gandhi; he must speak.

The education minister who has learned the *Kural* must say: “Do not imagine, fools, that you can reintroduce antiquity and sustain caste when it has become like a scorpion without its sting, a tiger without teeth and an eagle without sight. Do not wound intelligent minds with such speech. Do not betray the great Gandhi.”

Kamaraj must say Tamil Nadu will not accept caste. Everyone conscious of duty must say it. If a united opinion is expressed even once, the palm-leaf bearers will themselves understand that their time is over; afterwards they will become perfectly meek.

Only after thirty years of campaigning did wearers of the sacred thread begin addressing others respectfully as “Mudaliarval, Naidugaru, Pillaival, Konarval, Nayakarval, Chettiarval.” Did not our grandfathers and fathers hear only speech of the kind, “Hey Munusami! Hey Ethiraj! Come here, Kanda! Where is that Masi? Call Sonachalam”? Only in our own days has our condition risen enough for us to be treated civilly.

When a few parasites imagine they can destroy this progress, should indignation arise only among Kazhagam members? Should it not arise among others too? Should they not declare publicly and firmly, “Not only the Dravidar Kazhagam; we too say caste must not exist. We shall abolish this caste order before attending to any other work”?

That is what they asked at Tuticorin. They did not ask for seats in the Legislature or a share of office, nor say they would come for street fights at election time. They said: let caste disappear and society be reformed; let division go and fellowship bloom; let the country prosper and deceit perish.

The Tuticorin conference was a message.

To *Bharata Devi*, Periyar's speech at the Tuticorin conference was reportedly like a desert flower. Its editor did not perceive the whole truth buried in that mocking phrase. If he did, he would be astonished.

A flower has begun to bloom even in the desert! In a country that lay without thought, people have begun to think. The full significance of this can be seen page after page in the historical records of many nations.

At Tuticorin they declared: “We have obtained the wealth of thought. We shall continue giving it to the country.”

*Dravida Nadu*, 23 May 1948
