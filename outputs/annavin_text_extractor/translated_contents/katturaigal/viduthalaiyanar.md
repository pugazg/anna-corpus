சிறைச்சாலையிலிருந்து, சென்ற வாரம், பெரியார்

அவர் சென்றவிடங்களிலும், போட்டியிட்டுக்கொண்டு
வரவேற்பும் வாழ்த்துமளித்திருக்கின்றனர். மன்பதை நிரம்பிய
ஒரு தலைவர், காங்கிரஸ் நன்றிகாட்டவில்லையென்றாலும்,
காமராசர் ஆட்சி வழங்கியது சிறையென்றாலும், தள்ளாத
வயதிலும் தளர்ச்சியில்லா ஊக்கமும். உறுதியும்
கொண்டுள்ளது காண எவருக்கும் மகிழ்ச்சியே ஏற்படும்.

பெரியாரையும் அவரது படையையும் சிறைக்கனுப்பு
வதன் மூலம், அவர் கொண்டுள்ள கொள்கையைச் சாய்த்து
விடலாம் என்று ஆட்சியாளர் கண்ட கனவு பலிக்கவில்லை.
சாதி ஒழியவேண்டும் என்கிற பெரியாரின் கோட்பாட்டிற்
காக, இலட்சக்கணக்கான மக்கள் உள்ளனர், இந்தக்
கோட்பாடு, வெற்றி பெறவேண்டும் என்பதிலும், கருத்து
வேற்றுமை இருக்க முடியாது.

“நான் எதற்காக, இந்த கிளர்ச்சியைத் துவக்கனேனோ,
அது இன்னும் நிறைவேறவில்லை. ஆகவே, அடுத்தது என்ன?
என்பதற்குத் தயாராக வேண்டும் - தமிழ்நாடு இல்லாத
இந்தியாவின் படத்தைக் கொளுத்துவதுதான், அடுத்த
கிளர்ச்சியாயிருக்கலாம் - அதற்குத் தயாராக இருங்கள்"
என்று தன்னைப் பின்பற்றுபவர்களுக்குப் பெரியார்

## English Translation

Tamil Nadu rejoiced on hearing that Periyar had been released from prison last week; every Dravidian heart was filled with delight. In Madras and wherever he travelled, people competed to welcome and congratulate him. Though the Congress showed no gratitude to a leader devoted to humanity, and though Kamaraj's government gave him prison, anyone must feel glad to see that even at an advanced age he possesses undiminished enthusiasm and resolve.

The rulers' dream that they could defeat Periyar's principles by imprisoning him and his force has not come true. Lakhs of people support Periyar's doctrine that caste must be abolished, and there can be no difference of opinion over the need for that doctrine to succeed.

Immediately after his release, Periyar informed his followers:

> The purpose for which I began this agitation has not yet been fulfilled. We must therefore prepare for what comes next. The next agitation may be to burn a map of India from which Tamil Nadu has been removed. Be ready for it.

We see, and welcome, a change in Periyar's language and tone concerning the struggle to abolish caste. Before he went to prison, his slogan had been, "Brahmins must be expelled." Though we respected and esteemed him, we clearly expressed our disagreement with such a slogan. As usual, we received abuse and denunciation as our reward.

Our struggle is not against individuals. It is to change an institutional order that has continued for a very long time. We received anger as the reward for saying this. Nevertheless, we are now pleased to see a change in Periyar's voice and language, and we trust that this change will produce good results.

The curse of caste cannot be abolished by burning agraharams or tormenting Brahmins. A clear plan and discernment are needed. The path to caste abolition must be found through these, rather than through what fire and sword can accomplish.

Periyar has said that, as a symbol of caste abolition, an agitation should burn the map of India. We would not call even this the correct path.

A change is also visible in Periyar's language and tone concerning Kamaraj's government. His former enthusiasm is no longer as evident. Reports say that he has also criticized Kamaraj's administration.

Even if Periyar says that Delhi incites and Madras pours out repression, it appears that he too realizes Kamaraj and his colleagues should have shown some seriousness and enthusiasm in abolishing caste.

People around Kamaraj now say he has acquired the courage even to criticize and reject Periyar. Periyar, for his part, seems to believe that his force is stronger now than before; that taking further action against Congress will increase that strength; and that it does not matter even if he goes so far as to condemn Kamaraj.

Kamaraj thinks the Dravidar Kazhagam is necessary only because there must be opposition to the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam.

His apparent intention is: let the DK and DMK clash, while we strengthen our own hand.

The DMK understands this clearly. That is why it remains patient, however much the Dravidar Kazhagam abuses or denounces it. The DMK does not wish to become prey to the Congress strategy.

However much Periyar abuses the DMK or calls its members "drops of tears", he is nevertheless compelled to condemn Delhi's domination. That is the natural result of his campaign. If so, the benefit and advantage accrue to the DMK, which opposes Delhi's imperial domination.

Whether Catholics or Protestants within Christianity, the work each performs in competition with the other ultimately benefits Christianity. The DMK thinks in the same way. If Periyar's affection for Kamaraj weakens and the caste-abolition question reaches a further stage, Periyar's attack will fall upon Delhi's domination. The DMK will welcome that.

In these circumstances the cage has opened, and Periyar has again begun to proceed among the Tamil people. He sets out to conduct his sacred struggle with his customary blend of speed and discernment. Tamil Nadu welcomes him with the cry, "Come, come!"

*Dravida Nadu*, 29 June 1958.
