## English Translation

Everyone knows that the Kambar Conference held at Gokhale Hall, Madras, on 16 April 1944 ended in confusion.

What is worse and more regrettable is that those who conducted the conference abandoned its president, Mahamahopadhyaya Pandithamani Kathiresan Chettiar.

Pandithamani came outside as soon as the conference ended. But no one was there to take him away. The chairman of the reception committee was nowhere to be seen. Nor was the secretary. Nor was the patron of the arts.

As he stood alone and unattended in the street, a gentleman unconnected with the organisation of the conference saw him, summoned a taxi, seated him in it, and sent him on his way. A Brahmin with a tuft was with Pandithamani.

Is it proper or seemly to invite a president from another town and then disappear after leaving him in the middle of the street? As though that were not enough, they did not even come to see Pandithamani off at the railway station.

Was it not the duty and courtesy of the principal organisers, the secretary, the chairman of the reception committee, and the important committee members to see him off? It is condemnable that the leading figures failed to extend even this courtesy and remained indifferent, as in the proverb about an unwanted feast meant for someone else.

Poor Chettiar! It is not known why he came to Madras trusting such people. Self-Respecters have learned of this and are extremely angry. Self-Respecters may condemn matters directly, but otherwise they conduct themselves with dignity and courtesy.

Learning that the conference organisers humiliated Pandithamani in this manner, Self-Respecters are angry and even wonder whether the Kambar Conference organisers deliberately invited him as part of a plot to humiliate him. Whatever the differences of opinion, Self-Respecters condemn the humiliation of a great Tamil scholar in this way.

(*Dravida Nadu*, 30-4-44)
