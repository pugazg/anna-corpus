## English Translation

Omandur Reddy asks whether Dravidam can exist independently without a navy. Kamaraj asks whether destroying India's "unity" would produce terrible consequences. Patel asks what the separation of Dravida Nadu means. Northerners say that talk of a North-South distinction is highly dangerous. Members of the Tamil Arasu Kazhagam ask what relationship Dravidam could establish with Andhra and Kerala, which are going to separate from it. Tamils of Nanjil Nadu say, "Release us from Kerala's grip." Tamils living in Ceylon cry that the suffering inflicted by Sinhalese ministers is no small matter.

What is the person who ought to examine these matters, find remedies and a mature course, consult the leaders concerned with each issue, and act, doing with his position, resources, and ability? He remains engaged in such "statesmanlike" tasks as: "Arrange a meeting in your town; consult such-and-such people and obtain signatures; if a Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is formed, immediately issue a denial saying that it is invalid."

I am not foolish enough to imagine that I possess ability equal to his. He has probably visited every important town ten times for propaganda, while there are more towns I have never visited. It would be unreasonable for me to possess his range of contacts and friends. Even so, within my limits, I entrust many tasks to my friends and ask them to perform them, with confidence.

To this day, Periyar has not acquired that confidence in anything or anyone. Without even worrying that his listeners may be hurt, he says, "I think I must do everything myself; there is no one who can be trusted." What suspicion! What fear! Why this condition?

Even amid war clouds over Kashmir, unrest in Tibet, war in Burma, hostility with Pakistan, and crises of this kind, Nehru did not think, "It will take a month to return. What will happen to the country without me? Whom can I trust with such immense responsibility?" He possesses confidence that Patel will look after his work and Mathai will attend to it. Our Periyar possesses not even a drop of that confidence.

One incident comes to mind. The Dravidian Students' Training School was to be held at Mavur, and its announcement appeared in *Viduthalai* once or twice. At that time, the Udumalai case arose. Immediately a statement appeared saying that, because Periyar might be imprisoned, the students' training school had been postponed. What an attitude: no one can be trusted with it! What an expansive outlook! Nehru did not think like this; only our Periyar does.

Because his thought is continually filled with distrust, suspicion, and fear, he can easily think and speak of even people like me as "traitors," people who had never before even raised their voices in his presence.

After a certain degree of experience and age, there should be neither the thought that one must personally supervise every task nor the thought that no one else should perform any task. The practice of preparing many others, having them carry out work, supervising them, and taking pleasure in it must grow. If it does not, the heart will inevitably become a checkpoint of suspicion. It is as a result of this that he reviles all of us. I ask you to examine and understand this.

Unlike him, let us live by trusting one another. Let us act upon the fundamental truth that everyone possesses the ability to carry out some task. Let us honour and develop the principle that the collective strength of many is more effective than the power of one individual.

Let him continue to relish the belief that work proceeds properly only when all authority rests in a single place and a single hand. Among us, every person has responsibility and a share in the work. Thought has work to do, and we believe decisions must be reached through consultation. That is, we shall continue to work with complete faith in democracy.

We must refuse to be distracted by petty quarrels and problems. With the aim of performing the good work the country needs, let us laugh when suspected, endure when reviled, bear suffering when it comes, and take the blows when struck, continuing to work peacefully but eagerly. I invite you to come.

There are many thousands of comrades accustomed to work in our camp. Slander will not destroy us. Abusive words cannot wreck our endeavour. We do not want the impurity of mind that forgets yesterday's affection because of today's problem, not even a trace of it.

The chariot of victory cannot be driven by harnessing the horse of hatred. Let us harness the steeds of enthusiasm, bridle them with goodwill and tolerance, and drive the chariot called the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam along the path of civility. I invite you because I know your enthusiasm and ability well.

16-10-1949
