## English Translation

Waves gather and curl, only to disappear with a great roar. A flower that buds in the morning, opens its petals, and spreads fragrance withers and falls by evening. The moon that gives beauty, the tree that casts shade, and all that captivates memory do not endure forever.

Time is an ocean, and the years of human life resemble its waves. The wave rolls away, the flower withers, evening disappears, and the human being who is born also dies. The sound of the wave, the fragrance of the flower, and all the events of life meet their final moment and vanish.

The events of life keep changing like a whirling wind. The jasmine that danced upon the vine yesterday lies upon the ground today, fallen, withered, and unattended. A wave that raised a mighty sound subsides within the next instant. A human life that shone with the light of fame also disappears through the turning of time.

The human being who is born dies. Birth and death are eternal events in this world, like the sea. Yet the departure of some people crushes our hearts and makes us shed tears. Though we know the speed of time's current, the thought that those who lived and rejoiced beside us have died and can no longer be seen unsettles the heart, makes the mind falter, and turns memory into tears.

The scattering of stars does not disturb the human heart or command its concern. But if the moon itself should break and fall, the human mind must surely be shaken. When we think that the lovely moon which dispelled darkness has entered death's great mouth and will never return, life itself trembles and the flood of emotion overwhelms consciousness.

If we hear that one who stood with us until yesterday, welcomed us with a pleasant face and the words "Come, come", and delighted us with wit has today become a corpse, the heart cannot help but recoil.

When news reached us that Ramasami, Principal of Salem College, had departed for the world of death, we stood stunned and sobbed; we trembled as though we had lost our own selves. We wondered in distress whether it was dream or reality. With trembling voices we asked our friends, and their only answer was tears. We opened the newspapers; they gave us the cruel words "He has passed away", like a spear driven into a burning wound.

The departed scholar was a jewel of humanity and a moon that dispelled the darkness of Dravidian society. For twenty-three years he served as Principal of Salem College. He was a sculptor shaping the world of the future and freely distributed the wealth of thought.

Ramasami was a treasure of the Tamil world and a guardian endowed with mastery of pure Tamil. He made labouring for Tamil and the Tamil world his duty. He was no mere grasshopper, but a generous spirit who cut through the clouds and kissed the arc of the sky. He flew through the firmament of knowledge, gathered its discoveries, and offered them to the world of students.

The many services he performed were all infused with a desire to turn the public sphere into a garden of knowledge. The distinction of making Salem College a first-grade college and enabling it to excel in many respects belongs to Ramasami. The people of Salem reportedly stood stunned and refused to believe the news of Principal Ramasami's death. When the students learned that their leader was gone, they grieved like a sky that had lost its moon.

Ramasami was a singular jewel in human society. Through his selfless service, however, he captivated every heart and obtained a place within them all.

He was a noble man who devoted his whole life to the advancement of the diminished Dravidian community. Kanakamma Ramasami, his wife who now stands grief-stricken after losing her husband, is a radiant lamp of the Self-Respect Movement. Together, the departed gentleman and his companion rendered many services to the rationalist world. As a result, the rulers' hostile gaze and the fury of orthodoxy sometimes rushed upon him. Yet he did not waver; in his eagerness to perform his duty, they became mere dust.

On the night of 19 August 1950, a sudden pain in the chest took this Ramasami, filled with such courage and valour, disturbed all our hearts, and mingled his final breath with the air. It made the fragrant flower fall and concealed a moon that had been giving light to a society filled with darkness.

We cannot even bring ourselves to think that Ramasami has died. At the age of fifty-three, his gentle smile and cheerful face continue to dance within our thoughts. Our hearts are shaken by the question, "Has he truly passed away?" We have lost a good friend; Tamil Nadu has lost a scholar; the student world has lost a father of thought. Dravidian society has become like a sky bereft of its moon.

On behalf of the Dravidian people, we express our condolences to Comrade Kanakamma, who has lost her companion, and to their family. We offer consolation to the grieving student world. Long live Ramasami's fame; may the greatness of his service continue to grow.

*Dravida Nadu*, 27 August 1950.

## Translator's Notes

The title's missing vowel is restored from the obituary's explicit closing description of Ramasami as the student world's `சிந்தனைத் தந்தை`, “father of thought.” The body is translated in full and the canonical Tamil OCR remains unchanged.
