## English Translation

The following are some of the views expressed by the D.M.K. General Secretary while speaking at the great reception held in Kanchipuram on 7 December 1953:

"It is customary and natural for people outside prison, who have no experience of prison life, to welcome those released from it. But today the situation is different. Those who were released before us are welcoming us as we emerge today. We rejoice at receiving this rare opportunity.

"Here is comrade Annamalai, who delivered the welcome address. Only a few months ago, he lay in Madras prison. Like him, numerous comrades of the Kazhagam from this town went to prison and returned. They have gathered here and are welcoming us.

"In Madras prison there were five of us; in Tiruchirappalli, not only Karunanidhi; across every part of the country, more than five thousand young men of the Kazhagam have lain in prison, and some are still languishing there. Were the hardships endured by all those comrades insignificant?

"When officials and others concerned deal with us, they might at least restrain themselves somewhat at the thought, 'One day these men too may become ministers.' But can we suppose that such caution was shown towards every one of our Kazhagam heroes?

"However deeply troubles pierced them, our comrades disregarded them and entered prison for a principle. Those who govern must reflect upon their enthusiasm, the principle that produced it, and the Kazhagam that upholds that principle.

"The Raja of Ramanathapuram is now a minister. It is said that he spoke here and asked, 'Let them go to North India and write their names there in Tamil!' Perhaps he will report with distress to Achariyar what he said here. He may have spoken so for that purpose, or it may reveal his own hostility towards North India.

"Perhaps, having no other means to express that hostility, he used us to satisfy his desire by thinking: 'These people possess great integrity. They have the ability to complete what they undertake. They possess sound organisation and strength. None of this exists where we are.' Whatever his reason for saying it, I tell him and those associated with him: we shall certainly go to North India and write in Tamil.

"But not upon a railway board, as the Raja of Ramanathapuram expects. When the constitution of the Republic of Dravida Nadu is written, and Nehru on one side and we on the other place our signatures upon the agreement, we shall write there in Tamil.

"A prison is like an excellent forge. In a forge, iron is heated until red and beaten into spears and swords. Likewise, those marked by the scars of sacrifice in prison will one day govern the country. I say this to those who unleash repression today.

"Here, they imprison us. At the same time, Ashoka Mehta of the Socialist Party is in prison; Baladandayutham of the Communist Party is in prison. If every progressive party is sent to prison in this way, when members of those parties return, they will return as the rulers of the country. I wish to point this out to Achariyar.

"Achariyar knows that the future will be entrusted to us. He understands well that we shall obtain Dravida Nadu. That is why he is preparing us to accept that responsibility.

"I wish to remind him of one thing. During the last great world war, Hitler's forces rained bombs upon the city of London, pouring them down from aircraft. Churchill then said, 'London can take it.' Churchill said that London would endure it. I wish to repeat the same words to Achariyar: whatever may happen, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam can take it.

"I do not say this from the arrogance that I am as great a man as Churchill. He is a very great man; I am a very ordinary one. Yet I have received from the people of this country many times more affection than he enjoyed in the public forum of his country. It is a rare blessing.

"People in other political parties address their leaders with honorifics such as 'Sriyut,' 'Sriman,' 'The Honourable,' or 'Mahatma.' But everyone calls me, a servant of the people of this country, 'Anna'—elder brother. I have received such affection that all call me Anna. In what other party does this family affection, this relationship of elder and younger brother, exist?"

(*Dravida Nadu*, news item; publication date printed in damaged OCR as 6-12-௦2)

## Translator's Notes

The source says the reception occurred on 7 December 1953, while its final publication date is damaged and appears inconsistent. English retains the conflict without inventing a date. The corrupted Latin-script quotation is restored as Churchill's “London can take it” from the surrounding explanation. Political predictions and reported remarks remain attributed to the speech.
