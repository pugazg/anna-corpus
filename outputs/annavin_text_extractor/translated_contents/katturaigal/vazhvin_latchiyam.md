## English Translation

"I adopt this as the aim of my life."

He says it, and our ears are delighted; our hearts rejoice!

He is a minister. When he says that he adopts something as his life's aim, happiness wells up within us.

Even while he was in Geneva to attend the conference of the International Labour Organization, he travelled to West Germany and returned after meeting experts concerning this matter.

Yes, the Honourable Sitarama Reddy, Revenue Minister of the Madras government, is that deeply concerned. It is something to welcome and praise.

We know that lignite was discovered in South Arcot district and that the government undertook arrangements for a mine.

The Honourable Minister has explained the mine's distinction.

This lignite mine will excel not only in South India but throughout India. It will increase not merely Madras's wealth but India's entire stock of wealth.

The coal mentioned by the minister is of such importance!

He has made establishing this mine the objective of his life. We are very pleased. Let him proceed generously and complete it.

Without becoming lost in deliberations over whether to appoint an American expert or summon a German specialist, the government should decide quickly and arrange for the experts to arrive.

A coal mine in South Arcot will bring both wealth and distinction to the South!

The minister says that a mine in Dravida will benefit not only the South but also give life to the North.

True. Only if prosperity increases in Dravida can the North too live in flourishing abundance.

If we wither, they will have no choice but to sing a song of poverty.

Why? The South is a fertile land. Vast quantities of goods travel from here to the North. If those goods do not reach them, their lives cannot blossom.

The Honourable Sitarama says that he makes establishing a coal mine in Dravida the aim of his life, the purpose of his birth.

He himself also points out that the northerners too will profit from it.

Yes, coal is found in South Arcot. Iron is found in Salem. The aid of the Kaveri too will be available, since Mettur is nearby. Transport facilities are also present.

These are the most fundamental requirements for industries to arise. Every one of them lies arranged according to need and convenience.

If Dravida becomes filled with industrial prosperity, wealth will increase enormously.

If only the Honourable Minister's dream becomes reality, a new condition will flower luxuriantly in Dravida.

He has made establishing the coal mine his life's objective. We are delighted by his resolve. We thrill to hear this song of an ideal and offer our praise. But the work does not end with establishing a coal mine in Dravida!

Lignite is not the only thing found here. There are many, many more resources.

Dravida is a golden land overflowing with wealth, and we possess the honour of having it as our homeland.

Here are perennial rivers, golden-lotus tanks that astonish the beholder, lakes that store delight, and countless ponds that radiate joy. Our Dravida Nadu is a land abundant in water.

Its fertile fields yield harvests that add prosperity to life.

We give rice to others and therefore walk with our heads held high.

Dig the soil and there is gold; gold lies beneath the hills. Where else in the world can one find such wealth?

Has anyone found pearls beneath the sea anywhere else? We have them here.

We obtain mica here. We gladly supply manganese to the world.

Enter the forest and the fragrance of sandalwood, the beauty of teak, the strength of sal, and the charm of ebony immerse us in delight.

The resin obtained from the forest is useful for many kinds of work.

Every day we see the Nilgiris' tea and coffee and Kodaikanal's fruit.

Salem's iron will even open a new route for mining.

Yes, this is merely a sample from the list of resources available; it is by no means the end!

Dravida Nadu, which supplies all these resources, still remains in the northerner's grip.

Sitarama says that establishing a coal mine in the South is the aim of his life because he is a minister.

Otherwise, he would not stop with a coal mine. He would have gone further!

Until a short time ago, no one knew that lignite could be found in South Arcot.

Now they have discovered it, and therefore they devote attention to it!

The minister makes establishing the mine his life's aim.

Dravida's prosperity, power, and history are not merely unexcavated coal; they are gold. Their brilliance has not caught these people's eyes.

We are engaged in the work of making that light visible to them too. We have made that work the aim of our lives.

The moment coal was found, they began working briskly to establish a mine. They roam about seeking experts.

Just as Rama searched for and found the Sita he had lost, the Honourable Sitarama has not yet found the lost Dravida!

We know that if Dravida's greatness becomes visible to him, he will understand what we think.

When we reflect that Dravida, filled with so many such resources, is trapped and decaying in the northerner's grip, grief torments us and sorrow pursues us.

We work with the separation of Dravida from the North as the objective of our lives.

The Honourable Sitarama considers establishing a coal mine to be his life's aim because coal alone is visible to his eyes!

*Dravida Nadu*, 29 July 1951.
