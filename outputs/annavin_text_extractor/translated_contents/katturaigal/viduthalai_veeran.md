## English Translation

Dr Jagan, who drew the world's attention to himself a few months ago, takes his place in the roll of freedom fighters.

Dr Jagan visited the world's major capitals, including Madras, explained the history of the liberation uprising being conducted in his country, and gathered support.

A man who had been a minister governing the country was suddenly driven out by British imperialism.

British Guiana lies at the northern edge of South America. It covers 53,000 square miles.

Its population is 437,019.

East Indians form the majority.

Africans are the next largest group.

In earlier days, people were brought from many different places to clear the forests in these regions and develop their wealth.

After changing hands many times, Guiana is now under the British.

Another region adjoining it is called Dutch Guiana.

It is under Dutch control.

Dr Jagan is leading an uprising for the liberation of British Guiana.

Although much of it is still forest, the sugarcane grown in British Guiana yields enormous profits.

It also possesses mineral resources such as bauxite, gold, diamonds, and manganese.

The British exploit this natural wealth to the extent that it brings them profit, not for the welfare of the people.

Therefore, public-spirited people, especially the young, began a liberation movement.

Dr Jagan leads this uprising.

Dr Jagan studied in America for seven years.

It was there that he gained an understanding of political conditions and the resolve that the fetters binding his homeland must be broken.

The People's Progressive Party was founded in 1950.

A man named Burnham was its chairman.

Burnham was African.

Dr Jagan's wife, Janet Jagan, was the party's secretary.

With a threefold programme of national liberation, national development, and socialism, the party worked and won influence among the people.

In the general election, Dr Jagan's party won eighteen of the twenty-four constituencies.

Three parties contested against the Progressive Party.

The leaders of British imperialism and the priests set out to defeat the Progressive Party in the election.

The people, however, gave the Progressive Party overwhelming support.

"They will demolish the churches! Church property will be plundered! Religion is in danger!" cried the priests.

The victorious Dr Jagan became Chief Minister.

He began working to fulfil the promises he had made to the people during the election. British imperialism raged! His government had been in office for four months, and British imperialism could endure it no longer.

Claiming that Dr Jagan was a Communist who had seized power and was bringing ruin upon the country, it dismissed the ministry in October 1953.

Imperialism declared that Dr Jagan was a revolutionary who intended to unleash bloodshed in the country and summoned new troops and warships to its aid. Dr Jagan called upon freedom-loving people of pure heart to support him.

What does British imperialism do to those who conduct a liberation struggle? It throws them into prison!

But the people's faith in Dr Jagan will only grow through the measures British imperialism has taken. How could it diminish?

Dr Jagan has drawn the attention of the world.

Contrary to British imperialism's accusations, no bloodshed has occurred in Guiana.

The People's Progressive Party has instilled the dignity of nonviolent resistance among the people with such effectiveness.

The episode in which Dr Jagan was made Chief Minister and later thrown into prison is a fascinating stage in Guiana's liberation struggle...
