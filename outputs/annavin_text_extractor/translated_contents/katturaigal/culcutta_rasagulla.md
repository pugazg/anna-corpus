## English Translation

Here is Calcutta rasagulla! It is ready! Genuine Calcutta produce! Those who taste it will never forget it.

You ask where the rasagulla is? Very well, continue reading and decide whether what I say is right.

The All India Congress Committee met in Calcutta.

For the committee members who assembled, mere escape with their heads became an act of divine mercy. Instead of the customary greetings, Bengali Congress members reportedly welcomed their leaders with cries of “Shame! Shame! Traitor, go back! We do not want you!”

It had previously been customary to escort a leader's motorcar to his lodging. This time, the patriotic crowd reportedly threw stones at the car.

Everywhere, leaders usually receive garland upon garland. But no garlands fell upon those who came to this committee. Stones and earth rained down; a shoe was even thrown at one leader. Until now, hundreds of young people gathered to drink Pandit Jawaharlal's socialist *payasam*. Now Bengali youth reportedly surrounded, abused and distressed him.

When a leader ascended the dais, it had been normal to offer congratulations and chant his names. This time, when Rajan Babu took the seat formerly occupied by Subhas Bose, anger erupted with cries of “Shame! Get down! Go away!”, like the statues rebuking King Bhoja when he tried to ascend Vikramaditya's throne.

Conference volunteers had normally prevented the public from pressing against leaders and cleared a path for them. In Bengal, however, a volunteer who went to guide Rajan Babu reportedly seized him by the throat, shook him and tore his shirt.

After conferences ended, one leader had usually praised and congratulated another's ability. In Calcutta, one leader instead abused another. Conference volunteers had customarily been praised as public servants; in Calcutta, they were denounced as hooligans and hirelings.

Calcutta did not move from its place, nor did the Bay of Bengal rise in fury. Yet all these terrible changes occurred in Bengal.

What a Bengal it was: Bengal that nourished nationalism for all India; Bengal that gave birth to male and female nationalist heroes ready even for the gallows; Bengal that supplied warriors unafraid to enter the British lion's den; Bengal prepared for every repression, including transportation for life, imprisonment in the Andamans and detention without trial; Bengal that gave birth to Subhas Babu. It was in that Bengal that all these “honours” were performed.

Were they inflicted upon communalists or office-hunters? No, no! All this boundless trouble befell the jewels of patriotism, Gandhi's own group.

Why this change? *Ananda Vikatan* says, “The times have gone bad. No person and no thing can be trusted. The sun burns in the rainy season, the breeze blows in winter, and heavy rain destroys the country during the fierce summer.” I ask it to add to that ornate list: Bengal rages and shoes are thrown at leaders.

Why have the times gone bad? Why can no one be trusted? Why have earth, stones, shoes, abuse and ridicule replaced greetings in Bengal? Is it the play of fate? Or, Vikatan, as you might say, the divine will? Why this transformation? Who caused it? Think for a moment.

How could the times remain good, Vikatan? When expected things do not occur, why should unexpected things not happen?

Your class went to drive out the British but stroked their feet; went to shatter the reforms but began repairing them; went to abolish communalism but performed communal acts; went to reduce taxes but imposed new ones; went to develop Tamil but forced Hindi upon the people so that Tamil would decline; promised to abolish repression but imprisoned 1,001 people. A minority band of plotters overthrew Subhas Babu, elected by a great majority. Gandhi, not even a four-anna member, rejected the Subhas Bose chosen by the country. When such undesirable, repellent and impossible things occur, how could the times not go bad?

When all this happens in the name of the nation, self-rule and Gandhi, must I not say, as you do, that no one and nothing can any longer be trusted?

Honestly considered, it is true that no one can be trusted. Gandhi constantly speaks of truth and nonviolence, but after seeing his conduct toward Subhas Babu, can radicals or believers in democracy trust him?

Rajaji, who wrote *The Path Shown by Krishna*, now teaches Tamils the road to prison forts. Can he be trusted?

No, Vikatan, three times no!

What can be trusted? Looking at the display under Congress domination and the new taxes imposed, what can those who expected wondrous benefits from Congress rule, and are now deceived, believe any longer?

We cannot even believe that rulers elected by a great majority will be loved by the public. Before our provincial ministers became “Honourables,” were they not cherished like the public's favourite children? Today they see black flags and hear cries of condemnation. We can no longer believe that becoming a minister increases the people's affection.

We cannot trust even the ordinary proposition that a person elected leader by a large majority of votes will be allowed to remain leader. Subhas was elected by a great majority, yet the leadership was taken from him.

What can one trust, sir? Whom can one trust? Such have the times become: a time when people do not trust one another, when nothing can be believed. In this era of the yellow ballot box, people indeed find nothing trustworthy.

Once, twenty-one months ago, you made the people believe even things they should not have believed. The strength of propaganda and the press made it possible. If people now reflect upon what they believed then, they will laugh at themselves.

Yes!

They believed the yellow box alone was the auspicious box.

They believed you were heroes who would seize the British lion by the mane and shake it.

They believed every other party was a party of traitors.

They believed comrade Sathyamurthi when he declared before a vast gathering at Manamadurai, “I shall summon the Ganges from the sky.”

They believed every beat of your small drum proclaiming, “Good times are coming! Good times have arrived!”

When Congress ministers took office, they believed you when you said, “We have seen things never seen before.”

Having believed all this again and again, the public now looks at what is happening and stands truly bewildered. They are confused about what and whom to trust.

Hereafter, they will not believe anyone who says honesty, justice, fidelity to one's word and democracy exist in Congress or Gandhism.

Fraud, intrigue, the hunt for leadership positions and startling actions behind the curtain flourish within Congress. People will therefore believe firmly that liberation cannot come to the country through it.

That is why I say everyone should taste this “Calcutta rasagulla.”

*Kudi Arasu*, 14 May 1939
