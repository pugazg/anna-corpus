## English Translation

The life of a hero has ended. The elder brother of Subhas Bose, who demonstrated through action that he would labour "by any means" for an ideal, has died. A voice that continually cried "rights, rights" has fallen silent. A commander who built a camp of resistance against growing fascism has departed. Sarat Chandra Bose, the lion of Bengal, has passed away.

Public life contains locusts, birds of opportunity, and selfish pleasure-seekers who regard their own advancement as paramount. Sarat Chandra was a great hero. He plunged with his family into the fire of sacrifice. He lived with his chest held high, declaring, "Office is not important; the welfare of the people is my goal." Praised by the people of Bengal as "gold, gold," he was a selfless sacrificer, a champion of ideals, and an architect of a new world. His departure is a great loss to the growing progressive forces.

Having remained in the Congress, suffered hardship and loss, and borne the scars of the freedom struggle as badges, Sarat Bose served as a minister of India and offered his service to the people's forum. Then he left, proclaiming that the Congress Party had stepped onto a path of misery.

Had he wished, he could still have lived comfortably today as one of the "leaders" and as a minister. Instead, declaring the Congress a den of deceit, he issued a public statement that it had forgotten the lives of the people and that his conscience would not permit him to remain attached to it, and he walked out.

He formed another party. He proclaimed that both fascism and capitalism must be abolished. He was mobilising the people for that great task. The pot broke just as he tried to churn the butter. As he stepped forward to pluck the fruit, a black cobra struck. Death tore him away from us and from the people of the country.

India has lost one of its true leaders. A future filling with darkness has lost a moon that could dispel it. We have lost a good man who aided the song of our ideal. The country has lost one of the commanders of the anti-fascist struggle.

We have always held the highest regard for that great hero who fought for people's rights with socialist principles. We trusted him as one of the leaders from outside our province who would offer his cooperation in the work of opposing both fascism and antiquated ways. In our mental list, we counted him among the forces whose support would rally behind the separation of Dravidam.

Today he is gone. This country has lost a good man. Chandra has departed bearing the burden of praise from those who, until yesterday, wandered about reviling, opposing, and antagonising him.

The life he lived and demonstrated is a path of ideals, a primer for the emerging world. We share the tears of the progressive forces shaken and grieved by his death. Long live his fame! May his ideal grow!

(*Dravida Nadu*, 26-250)
