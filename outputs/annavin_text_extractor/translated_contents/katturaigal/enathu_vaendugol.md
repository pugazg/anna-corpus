## English Translation

Comrades,

Periyar's marriage took place on 9 July 1949.

From the moment this arrangement became known, many hundreds of letters were sent to him condemning it and explaining that this unsuitable marriage conflicted with the movement's principles and would bring disgrace upon the movement itself. They also stated that if Periyar did not change this course, it would be impossible to accept his leadership and continue working, and that the writers would therefore stand apart.

Many local branches have likewise adopted resolutions to this effect.

At a meeting of the Executive Committee held at the home of its chairman, comrade Vedasalam, in Tennur, Tiruchirappalli, when it was not yet known that the marriage had already taken place, a resolution was adopted stating that the marriage should not occur and asking Periyar to abandon it in the movement's interests.

Thirty-two committee members were present. Only one person stood alone and said that the resolution was unnecessary.

The marriage has now taken place. Thousands of Kazhagam comrades have been brought into a condition of bewilderment, confusion, and agitation over what must be done next.

Plans and strategies of many kinds are circulating.

Many rumours, accusations, and attempts to divide people into camps are multiplying.

Various conjectures circulate concerning the party leadership and future action.

Alongside all this, an accusation is being made that the entire affair is a conspiracy by "me" to seize the leadership. Using that allegation as its basis, one side is also busy predicting my future. In the same manner, some publications that revile and condemn Periyar are circulating.

In keeping with the notion that rising anger and turmoil bring "profit" to certain people, the work of fanning the flames proceeds vigorously on both sides.

In this situation, what we chiefly require is clarity, calm, and firmness of mind.

My appeal is that no one should engage in any act that would destroy these qualities.

I ask you not to help make the situation deteriorate further.

Until the very end, we made our efforts without deviating in the slightest from the goodwill behind our position: this marriage should not take place; it should be stopped; and we should continue performing Kazhagam work under Periyar's leadership as always.

The marriage has now taken place. We must decide what to do next without haste or anger and without becoming prey to those who provoke, fan the flames, manufacture quarrels, stir up whatever they find, or make us the laughing-stock of the street.

This marriage conflicts with the movement's principles and could ruin the movement. Although thousands upon thousands of Kazhagam members pleaded repeatedly that it should not take place, the act was obstinately carried through. Many people and many branches have already declared: "We cannot work under the leadership of the person who did this. So long as the Kazhagam remains under his leadership, we shall stand apart from Kazhagam work." I am eager to learn how many more branches and comrades hold the same view.

Holding public meetings, publishing notices of condemnation, or issuing rebuttals will not clarify this situation.

I therefore ask each person to think carefully about the circumstances, reach an individual decision, and communicate that decision.

Once evidence is available that a definite number of Kazhagam comrades hold this view, those who share it can gather, confer, and decide how we should proceed thereafter.

Until then, the work that must take place is simply to ascertain this opinion, calmly and without anger.

It is noteworthy that the view, "If the leader does not abandon this marriage, we cannot work in the Kazhagam under his leadership," has already been expressed. Yet more responses are needed. Only after receiving them can friends who hold that view gather, confer, and formulate a plan.

Meanwhile, news has arrived that Periyar may face government repression in the Udumalai case. If that occurs, it would be honourable for us to gather and confer only after his release.

It is easy to arouse hostility and invent wrongful suspicions, but it is not honest. Waiting patiently, thinking deeply, and acting will prove beneficial in every way.

This issue is not a game conducted on the basis of personal rivalry or hatred. It concerns people whose hearts ache because the bonds and affection that had grown over many years did not even induce him to respect this one appeal, and who have now been cast into the dilemma of how and in what capacity they can continue working without violating their principles.

That is why I ask you to think calmly.

If some imagine that "I myself have acted in haste," comrades should consider how much harm sudden decisions can cause in this matter.

We have stated our purpose clearly, and many support it. Let us learn more about the number and extent of those supporters; then let us gather, confer, and arrive at a sound decision. That is the proper course. That is my appeal.

Yours affectionately,

C. N. Annadurai

*Dravida Nadu*, 17 July 1949.

## Translator's Notes

The source's organization-specific `கழகம்` is rendered as Kazhagam. `தூபமிடுதல்`, literally offering incense, is used politically for feeding or fanning agitation. The reference to the Udumalai case is retained without speculative expansion. Annadurai's closing `அன்பன்` is rendered as “Yours affectionately.”
