## English Translation

## Periyar's Elder Brother, E. V. Krishnasamy

I cannot even bring myself to think. My heart burns; every memory falters; every thought settles upon "life in Erode." Longing and grief turn into waterfalls in my eyes.

He has passed away! The beloved elder, a mountain of virtue, has gone into the tomb! We can no longer see that face upon which serenity played. Alas, we can no longer hear those affectionate words with which he lovingly called us!

His final breath mingled with the air at 4:30 on Saturday evening, 4 February 1950. He has left this worldly life. He has died!

I cannot even contemplate it; the letters falter! The heart becomes a surging sea!

The South has lost a generous physician.

Periyar has lost his beloved brother.

Kannammal has lost her elder brother.

Madam Aranganayaki has lost her life companion!

Sampath, Selvan, Miranda, and Chella, members of our own family, have lost their father!

We have lost a good man. We have lost a cool breeze and a cascade of water!

Everything rushes before my eyes. It all seems like a dream!

"There is nothing wrong with my health. My comrades are staging a play, and that too in this very town! What if I go and see it? The illness will only grow worse, will it not? Let it! Must I lie inside the house in fear of that? Impossible! Take me with you!"

Even in his frail condition, he cried out in this way when the movement's fund-raising play was held in Erode last month. That treasury of affection has vanished! Leaving us all, that serene face has been laid quietly in the tomb.

A desire to go somehow and see rationalist propaganda wherever and in whatever form it was conducted always possessed him. He would travel from Erode to Madras to see a film! He did not go to the cinema merely for amusement or pleasure. If he learned that a film presented ideas that fostered intellectual growth, he would set out immediately, not alone, but with his family.

"I have decided that I must write and send this without fail. Siddha medicine is falling into decay. I will keep sending articles. They must be published!"

Not even a month has passed since the hands that wrote such a letter, issuing a loving command with a sense of duty, could still be seen. Within that time they have become senseless and dead!

My thoughts become confused the more I think of him! When I remember the affection and support he showed, my heart becomes a volcano!

Whenever turmoil like the summer heat swept through the movement, he was a grove that gave coolness! When we were attacked by the spears of harsh words, his loving speech was the medicine that healed the wounds they caused!

He has passed away; he has departed from us all! He has followed the path of nature!

E. V. Krishnasamy occupied a throne in the hearts of the people of Erode. He filled the thoughts of Self-Respect and Dravidian comrades. He suffered the rulers' repression. He served as editor of rationalist journals; he was a guardian of Siddha medicine; and he shone as a benefactor who cured the illnesses of those without means!

We have lost him! A mountain of virtue has been removed from among us!

Neither tears nor sorrow will return the great man we have lost. He laboured through his life for others and engraved his name upon the hearts of the people of this country. After giving us his dear younger brothers for the advancement of the cause, he disappeared! He has taken an enduring place in our memory.

We too can only sob with the grieving family.

Yet neither longing nor sorrow will return the benefactor we have lost!

Let us walk on the path he showed, the path of love. Let us fulfil his wishes!
