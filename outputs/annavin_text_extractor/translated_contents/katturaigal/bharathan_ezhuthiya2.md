சுவாமி, எங்கே தங்கள் பேச்சு மூச்சேகாணோம். காங்கிரசில்
ஏதேனும் நெருக்கடி ஏற்பட்டால், நான் அதில் சேர்ந்து
பணிற்றுவேன் என்று ' கூறி வெளிவந்தீரே. கல்கத்தா சென்று
பேரரசையும் கண்டீரே. திரும்பி வந்த காங்கிரசில் பிளவு ஒன்றும்
வராது என்றீரே. நீர் சொன்னபடி பிளவு வராது என்று நான் கூட
நம்பினேனே. கடைசியில் என்ன இப்படி வந்துவிட்டது. நெருக்கடி,
கல்லடி, மண்ணடி, செருப்படி, எனப் பல அடிகள் நடந்து
விட்டனவே. ஏன் நீர் மயிலாப்பூர் மாளிகையிலேயே இருக்கிறீர்.

காங்கிரஸ் கட்சியிலே ஜனநாயகம் வேண்டுமென்றீர்.
ஜனநாயகம் குதிரைக் கொம்புதான் இங்கு என்று கல்கத்தா காட்டி

புரட்சிக்கொடி உயர்த்தப்பட்டு விட்டது. ஆனால் மயிலாப்பூர் வீரர்,
சுயராஜ்ய கட்சியின் தீரர் நீர் மட்டும் மெளனமாக இருக்கும்
விந்தைதான் என்ன? ்

நீர் “தொட்டது துலங்காது'! என்று யாரோ சொன்னதாக
ஞாபகம். அப்பனே, நீர் காலடி எடுத்துவைத்ததும் காங்கிரசே

கூறப்படுகிறது. அது உண்மையா தெரிவியும்.

## English Translation

## To the Mylapore Statesman, S. Srinivasa Iyengar

Sir, why do we hear neither speech nor breath from you? You emerged saying that if any crisis arose in Congress, you would join it and work. You went to Calcutta and saw the great men too. On returning, you said there would be no split in Congress. I too believed your assurance that no split would come. How, then, has it ended like this? Crisis, stones, earth, and sandals: so many blows have already been exchanged. Why do you remain inside your Mylapore mansion?

You said the Congress party needed democracy. Calcutta has shown that democracy here is as rare as a horse's horn. Bose's drum of revolution is heard. The flag of revolution has been raised in Punjab too. Yet what marvel is this, that you alone, hero of Mylapore and champion of the Swaraj Party, remain silent?

I remember someone saying, "Nothing he touches will prosper!" My dear man, the moment you set foot inside, Congress itself split in two. It is said that you are working behind the curtain. Is that true? Please tell us.

Yours,

Bharathan

## To Sir T. Vijayaraghavachariar

*Bahut achchha, bada tamasha hai!* Well, well, Acharya Swami, in this advanced age you have arrived to sing an accompaniment to the Honourable Acharya's Hindi. Did you reason that no one but one Acharya could support another? Or that the word of one Acharya was insufficient and there ought to be two?

It seems you said that you have been enamoured of the lady Hindi for twenty years. While distributing prizes to students at the Dakshina Bharat Hindi Prachar Sabha, you reportedly said, "If I already thought twenty years ago that Hindi ought to be introduced, after twenty years that thought must surely have grown." I read it in the newspaper.

I do not know what reward the Honourable Acharya will give you for this sacred utterance, issued at so precious a moment. For my part, I intend to give you an excellent and necessary gift: hair oil that darkens grey hair.

Though advanced in years and adorned with grey hair, you apparently declared, "Even if black flags come, I shall not change my policy." This speech alone displays your swagger. Swagger combined with grey hair would look comical. That is why I wish to present you with hair-darkening oil. Apply it, come out smartly like a bridegroom, and try speaking in support of Hindi. See how many people roar with laughter on hearing your argument. Countless Congress leaders have already repeated these things until ears have soured from hearing them. Even a youngster engaged in the anti-Hindi struggle can cut down these hollow arguments. What difference does it make whether you unleash them in a torrent or remain silent?

If you speak because you believe your life will otherwise go unfulfilled unless you render even this assistance to the Honourable Acharya, and if you wish to grind flour that has already been ground, at least apply a vigorous dose of the oil I shall send you before you come. Shall I send it, Acharyar? Tell me.

Thus,

Bharathan, who wishes to present you with a gift

## To the Honourable Minister Varkey

Sir! You are a minister! The Minister of Education!

Remember that; do not forget it. Judging from the speech you gave at the Madras Educational Conference, it genuinely appears that you forgot you had become a minister and spoke as though you were still a schoolmaster. Good heavens, sir, why were you so angry? Fortunately, there was no cane there. Had there been one, you seemed ready to seize it and begin giving out a couple of strokes.

You forgot that you had become a minister. You also forgot that those seated at the educational conference were teachers. You imagined that you were the master and those seated before you were schoolchildren frightened of the cane and the coloured pencil used for marks. It seems you berated them: "Hum! You must behave properly, understand? If there is any grievance, you must tell me directly. Do you understand? You must not write about it in the newspaper and make it public. You must not try to frighten me. If you do anything of that sort, I am a very dangerous fellow. Beware!"

What would those teachers have thought? Even if you did not reflect that day, think about it today:

"What misery is this? We invited this man to open the conference, and he forgets himself and threatens us! We invited him thinking that, since he too had been a teacher like us, he would understand our difficulties and conduct himself accordingly. Instead, the man threatens us. Until last year he was just like us, wearing a shirt and headcloth and carrying a coloured pencil. Acharya thought it would be good to have a Christian and pulled him into politics. He became a minister. At once he speaks as though he has forgotten that he too was once a teacher. Ah! It seems the fellow became a different sort of man as soon as he became a minister. It is like the snake around Siva's neck asking Garuda, 'Are you well?' This is all the work of that green velvet cushion, the ministerial seat."

Is this not how they would have spoken about you? Why speak in this manner, sir? Do not. Merely because you have become a minister today, you must not forget that you were once a teacher. Nor should you speak as a schoolmaster threatening his pupil.

Thus,

Bharathan, who was not your pupil when you were a teacher

*Viduthalai, 13 May 1939*
