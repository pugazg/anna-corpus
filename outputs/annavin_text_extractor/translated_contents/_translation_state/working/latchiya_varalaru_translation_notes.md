# History of an Ideal — working translator notes

These notes are separate from the English draft and corrected Tamil. Incorporate relevant notes into the final bilingual document only after all six parts are complete.

- Part 1 Images 2–4: the opening statement is attributed at its end to Leader Periyar. Its claims about self-rule and the departure of British domination are translated as the statement's claims; they are not editorial corrections to the printed 29-6-1947 date. Do not change either source wording or date to resolve the apparent chronological tension.
- `பிரச்சார இயக்கம்` is rendered “movement for spreading/propagating its ideas,” with ordinary English variation. The contrast is between an ideological/social movement and a party seeking electoral office, not between honest and deceptive communication.
- `தெளிவுபடுத்தி ஆய்விட்டது` is the unusual printed form already preserved in OCR recovery. “Already made this clear” conveys the contextual function without silently repairing the Tamil.
- `தாழ்த்தப்பட்டவர்கள், ஷெடியூல் லீக்` is retained as “the Depressed Classes, the Scheduled League,” following the printed list. Do not silently substitute a different organization's official name.
- The opening quotation on Image 2 and renewed quotation on Image 4 have no corresponding closing quotation marks before the attribution in the corrected source. The working English reflects this typographical irregularity; the final notes should explain it rather than invent an omitted passage.
- `ஒரு மூச்சுப் பார்த்தாக` is rendered “put that strength to the test,” preserving the call for a concerted effort. `சூத்திரர்` is “Shudras,” the caste designation repudiated by the speaker.
- `அதல சுதல பாதாளம்` names mythological lower worlds. “Cosmic falsehoods” preserves the polemical force of `அண்டம் புளுகு`; the translation does not present the author's judgment as editorial narration outside the work.

- Part 1 Image 5: `மனத்தை` is printed, rather than the expected `மானத்தை`; “their spirit” follows the retained word without silently changing it to “honour.” The claims about ancient Dravidam are translated as the author's rhetorical contrasts, not independently established historical findings.
- Part 1 Images 5–6: “Dravidam” is retained as the geographical/cultural name. “Seth” retains the merchant honorific; “Bania,” “Marwari,” “Gujarati” and “Multani” retain the source's community/regional labels and polemical generalizations. “Asher” transliterates the printed `ஆஷர்`; no unverified corporate identification is substituted.
- Part 1 Image 6: “The bell that rings every day” preserves the wording and possible newspaper-name wordplay in `தினம் ஒலிக்கும் மணி`; Goenka's name is retained without adding a publication name absent from this sentence. `கட்டியம் கூற` is rendered “singing the praises,” conveying the heraldic/deferential image.
- Image 6 ends with `நஞ்சை`, joined to `புஞ்சையையும்` on Image 7. English continues “irrigated fields here and” / “dry fields...” at the scan boundary; do not duplicate the paired land categories.

- Part 1 Images 7–8: the chronology follows the corrected source's separated column blocks and retains their nonchronological order. All dates, including 20-8-1944 and 1-7-1947, are translated as printed; they are not silently harmonized with other historical chronologies or the opening date.
- `பரணி பாடினோம்` is “sang our battle song,” retaining the martial literary image rather than merely saying “announced.” “Dravida Nadu” is used for the named separation programme in the chronology; “a Dravidian country” renders the accompanying prose demand.
- Image 8 prints `சொன்னோம்` above both opposed statements. Both are retained as “We Said”; do not silently change the second to “They Said.” The attribution `ஜவஹர்` is retained as “Jawahar.”
- The Pakistan/graveyard phrase preserves the force of `பாகிஸ்தான் ... கபர்ஸ்தான்`. The connecting printed `இன்` is irregular; “or” renders the clear choice in context and should be identified in the final notes as an interpretive connection, not a Tamil correction. The malformed `ஆபசமானது` and `பார்க்கு மனவு` in the opposing quotation are also left unchanged in Tamil; the English conveys the contextual sense “dangerous” and “worthy of consideration.”
- “Pakistan has been obtained” is the source's own retrospective claim; it is not rewritten to resolve the chronology's relation to the opening 29 June 1947 date.

- Part 1 Image 9: `தெ. இ. ந. உ. சங்கம்` is expanded by its Tamil components as “South Indian Welfare Rights Association”; the source uses initials, not an English institutional name. Retain that distinction in final notes. The Salem date is “the 27th” as printed; do not harmonize it with the chronology's 20-8-1944.
- `விவேக சிந்தாமணி` remains the work title *Viveka Chintamani*. The contrast is between scholarly exposition or political manoeuvring and the liberation struggle; no added description of that book's contents is needed.
- Part 1 Image 10: the solitary asterisk following the first paragraph is retained; no corresponding footnote is supplied on this scan. `சாவு ஓலை` becomes “summons of death,” preserving the written-message image. `மனப்பால் குடித்து` becomes “indulge the fancy,” not a literal claim about milk.
- The Tamil's historical achievements and generalizations about communities remain the author's rhetoric. `பொன்னாடை` is “golden cloth”; `கடாரம்` remains “Kadaram” without silently identifying a modern territory. The title question continues from Image 10's soap into Image 11's combs and mirrors.
