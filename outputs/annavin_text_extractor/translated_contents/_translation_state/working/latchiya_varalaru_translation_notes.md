# History of an Ideal — working translator notes

These notes are separate from the English draft and corrected Tamil. Incorporate relevant notes into the final bilingual document only after all six parts are complete.

- Part 1 Images 2–4: the opening statement is attributed at its end to Leader Periyar. Its claims about self-rule and the departure of British domination are translated as the statement's claims; they are not editorial corrections to the printed 29-6-1947 date. Do not change either source wording or date to resolve the apparent chronological tension.
- `பிரச்சார இயக்கம்` is rendered “movement for spreading/propagating its ideas,” with ordinary English variation. The contrast is between an ideological/social movement and a party seeking electoral office, not between honest and deceptive communication.
- `தெளிவுபடுத்தி ஆய்விட்டது` is the unusual printed form already preserved in OCR recovery. “Already made this clear” conveys the contextual function without silently repairing the Tamil.
- `தாழ்த்தப்பட்டவர்கள், ஷெடியூல் லீக்` is retained as “the Depressed Classes, the Scheduled League,” following the printed list. Do not silently substitute a different organization's official name.
- The opening quotation on Image 2 and renewed quotation on Image 4 have no corresponding closing quotation marks before the attribution in the corrected source. The working English reflects this typographical irregularity; the final notes should explain it rather than invent an omitted passage.
- `ஒரு மூச்சுப் பார்த்தாக` is rendered “put that strength to the test,” preserving the call for a concerted effort. `சூத்திரர்` is “Shudras,” the caste designation repudiated by the speaker.
- `அதல சுதல பாதாளம்` names mythological lower worlds. “Cosmic falsehoods” preserves the polemical force of `அண்டம் புளுகு`; the translation does not present the author's judgment as editorial narration outside the work.

- Part 1 Image 5: `மனத்தை` is printed, rather than the expected `மானத்தை`; “their spirit” follows the retained word without silently changing it to “honour.” The claims about ancient Dravidam are translated as the author's rhetorical contrasts, not independently established historical findings.
- Part 1 Images 5–6: “Dravidam” is retained as the geographical/cultural name. “Seth” retains the merchant honorific; “Bania,” “Marwari,” “Gujarati” and “Multani” retain the source's community/regional labels and polemical generalizations. “Asher” transliterates the printed `ஆஷர்`; no unverified corporate identification is substituted.
- Part 1 Image 6: “The bell that rings every day” preserves the wording and possible newspaper-name wordplay in `தினம் ஒலிக்கும் மணி`; Goenka's name is retained without adding a publication name absent from this sentence. `கட்டியம் கூற` is rendered “singing the praises,” conveying the heraldic/deferential image.
- Image 6 ends with `நஞ்சை`, joined to `புஞ்சையையும்` on Image 7. English continues “irrigated fields here and” / “dry fields...” at the scan boundary; do not duplicate the paired land categories.

- Part 1 Images 7–8: the chronology follows the corrected source's separated column blocks and retains their nonchronological order. All dates, including 20-8-1944 and 1-7-1947, are translated as printed; they are not silently harmonized with other historical chronologies or the opening date.
- `பரணி பாடினோம்` is “sang our battle song,” retaining the martial literary image rather than merely saying “announced.” “Dravida Nadu” is used for the named separation programme in the chronology; “a Dravidian country” renders the accompanying prose demand.
- Image 8 prints `சொன்னோம்` above both opposed statements. Both are retained as “We Said”; do not silently change the second to “They Said.” The attribution `ஜவஹர்` is retained as “Jawahar.”
- The Pakistan/graveyard phrase preserves the force of `பாகிஸ்தான் ... கபர்ஸ்தான்`. The connecting printed `இன்` is irregular; “or” renders the clear choice in context and should be identified in the final notes as an interpretive connection, not a Tamil correction. The malformed `ஆபசமானது` and `பார்க்கு மனவு` in the opposing quotation are also left unchanged in Tamil; the English conveys the contextual sense “dangerous” and “worthy of consideration.”
- “Pakistan has been obtained” is the source's own retrospective claim; it is not rewritten to resolve the chronology's relation to the opening 29 June 1947 date.

- Part 1 Image 9: `தெ. இ. ந. உ. சங்கம்` is expanded by its Tamil components as “South Indian Welfare Rights Association”; the source uses initials, not an English institutional name. Retain that distinction in final notes. The Salem date is “the 27th” as printed; do not harmonize it with the chronology's 20-8-1944.
- `விவேக சிந்தாமணி` remains the work title *Viveka Chintamani*. The contrast is between scholarly exposition or political manoeuvring and the liberation struggle; no added description of that book's contents is needed.
- Part 1 Image 10: the solitary asterisk following the first paragraph is retained; no corresponding footnote is supplied on this scan. `சாவு ஓலை` becomes “summons of death,” preserving the written-message image. `மனப்பால் குடித்து` becomes “indulge the fancy,” not a literal claim about milk.
- The Tamil's historical achievements and generalizations about communities remain the author's rhetoric. `பொன்னாடை` is “golden cloth”; `கடாரம்` remains “Kadaram” without silently identifying a modern territory. The title question continues from Image 10's soap into Image 11's combs and mirrors.

- Part 1 Image 11: the Shelley quotation is translated from the Tamil wording, not silently replaced with an English edition's wording. The comparison “Britain's Bharathidasan” belongs to the author. `தளபதி பாண்டியன்` is retained as “Commander Pandian,” without expanding the personal name beyond the source.
- Part 2 Image 1: a fresh visual check confirms the anomalous printed `வரு நிரம்பிய உடலும்`. “A body full of vigour” is a contextual interpretation, possibly pointing to intended `வலு`; it is uncertain and is not a correction to the Tamil. The final notes must explicitly retain this uncertainty. `வைரம் பாய்ந்த உள்ளம்` becomes “a heart of adamant,” preserving hardness/resolve rather than inventing literal diamonds.
- `தாலமுத்து நடராஜன்கள்` is rendered as the pluralized names “Thalamuthus and Natarajans,” retaining the invocation of exemplary people without adding biographical claims. The source's military language—commanders, camp, fighters and battle—is retained as movement rhetoric.
- The Part 1/Part 2 boundary falls inside a sentence: “It is to” / “accomplish this...” Image 1 of Part 2 itself ends “not to us,” to be completed from the next scan.

- Part 2 Images 2–3: the numbered reasons are translated as the author's political argument. Figures (32 European countries, 56 pre-British countries and 574 princely states), the designation “Dutch India,” and the threefold classification of peoples are retained as printed rather than corrected by reference to a modern historical account.
- `இனம்` in this argument is rendered “people/peoples,” and `இன இயல்புகள்` as “collective characteristics.” The author defines these through religion, language, art, outlook, shared belonging and history; the translation does not add a biological definition. “According to its peoples” renders `இனவாரியாக` consistently in these reasons.
- `சுதேச இந்தியா` is rendered “princely India” in the list of jurisdictions, following the immediately subsequent discussion of princely states. `இரவல் பொருள்` is “borrowed resources”; no specific debt arrangement is inferred.
- Reason 2 crosses Images 2–3. Reason 7 ends on Image 3; continue the original numbering at 8 on Image 4.

- Part 2 Images 4–5: reasons 8–15 complete the numbered argument. Statements about Turkey, the ancient/medieval extent of India, British coercion and Pakistan are retained as the author's claims, without silently updating or harmonizing them.
- `பூதேவர்கள்` is “gods on earth,” retaining the polemical description of Brahmin social status. `வர்க்கத்துக்கொரு` in reason 13 is “for each group,” following the surrounding argument about peoples rather than imposing a separate modern class-theory meaning.
- `வலுத்தவனுக்கு வாழ்க்கைப்படும் வனிதை` is translated with its gendered marital metaphor intact. It is the author's simile for subjection to the strongest, not an editorial description added by the translator.
- Reason 15's throat-crushing sentence crosses Images 4–5. The corrected Tamil's unusual `அழித்து நெரித்துக்` is rendered “crushed ... and strangled”; the Tamil remains untouched. The final `இன அரசு` is “a state for their people,” consistent with this section's rendering of `இனம்`.

- Part 2 Image 6: `போர்வாள்` is treated as the section heading “The Battle Sword.” “The following week” retains the relative timing without supplying an event date absent from the passage. The bare `2000` remains “two thousand,” without adding an unprinted currency.
- `தங்கக்கட்டிகள்` affectionately describes the mothers' babies as treasures; `ஏணைகள்` are suspended cloth cradles. `மிட்டா மிராசுகள்` is rendered collectively as “great landed proprietors”; `ஜெமீன்` retains the zamindar context. The anomalous printed `திருஇடம்` is rendered “land” in context, without rewriting the Tamil to திராவிடம்.
- Part 2 Image 7: `தீண்டினால் திருநீலகண்டம்` invokes a religious oath/taboo against touching; “not to be touched—by Tiruneelakantam!” retains the allusion with this explanation. `காவடி தூக்குவோர்` is rendered “bearing the burdens,” retaining its service/burden image in the sarcastic political context.
- Quoted songs are translated from the supplied Tamil; no remembered or externally sourced continuation is added. The violent sword imagery remains a quotation within this historical text. `பெரியோரே` is “the great elder,” following the printed form. `கடும் பத்தியம்` conveys severe restrictions or discipline; it is not limited here to dietary rules.
- The grove/prison contrast is retained as presented. The final sentence crosses Images 7–8: “Inadequate railway facilities,” followed by the weather difficulties and the assembled crowd.

- Part 2 Image 8: the long sequence of “after” clauses is retained as a rhetorical accumulation. `ஞாயிறு` is “the sun,” preserving the metaphor rather than replacing it with a person's name. The unusual `சுனை விழுங்கிகள்` in the burrow-creature insult is not securely interpretable; “creatures wriggling in their holes” translates the clear surrounding image while leaving the precise epithet unresolved. This lexical uncertainty must remain explicit in the final notes, with Tamil unchanged.
- `ஜல்லடம்` is rendered “fighting trunks” in the mock-heroic context. `சர். சண்முகனார்` remains “Sir Shanmuganar,” and the description as a Silappathikaram commentator is the source's own. No biographical expansion is added.
- Part 2 Image 9: `கோடி உள்ளங்கள்` becomes “ten million hearts,” preserving the rhetorical quantity. Atana is retained as the musical name. The yellow-to-black lyric is translated literally without supplying an unprinted explanation of party colours. `கனங்கள்` is rendered “‘Honourables’” with the ironic quotation marks intact.
- `தாட்சணியம்` is “consideration for others,” in the adhesive metaphor continuing onto Image 10. The inability to chew or swallow is retained as an idiom for Periyar's predicament. The text's quoted ‘Salem’ denotes the earlier-described party transformation; no extra event date is inserted.

- Part 2 Image 10: `தேர்தல் காளான்` is kept as “electoral mushroom,” the author's dismissive image of a rapidly assembled electoral formation. `பில்லைச் சேவகர்கள்` is rendered “liveried servants,” preserving the badge/retainer implication rather than an unmarked modern “assistants.”
- The later reference to people watching and enjoying `போர்வாள்` treats it as a performance title, *The Battle Sword*. The earlier section heading uses the same English title. No playwright, production date or additional stage detail is inferred.
- The Panagal quotation crosses the Part 2/Part 3 boundary and is kept continuous. The quotations attributed to Achariar are translated as reported in the source, not presented as independently authenticated transcripts. `வேதியர்` is “Brahmin” here, following its polemical personal reference.
- Part 3 Image 1: the printed name `நடராசன்` is transliterated “Natarasan,” while the preceding part's `நடராஜன்` was “Natarajan”; the source variation is not silently normalized. `புரட்சிக் கவிஞர்` is “the revolutionary poet,” with no personal name added. The final accumulating conditional sentence continues onto Image 2.

- Part 3 Image 2: `திருநாமம்` is the sacred forehead mark; “‘Brahmin pollution’” preserves the deliberate inversion in `பார்ப்பனத் தீட்டு`. “Gandhi's relative by marriage” follows `சம்பந்தியார்` without adding an unprinted personal name.
- The military comparisons, including the attribution to Wavell and the claim about seizing Pearl Harbor, are translated as the author's rhetoric, not silently corrected against military history. `மேற்கு அரசாங்கத்திலும் ... கிழக்கு அரசாங்கத்திலும்` is rendered “in the Western governments ... Eastern governments,” retaining the unusual printed governmental wording rather than substituting “fronts.”
- Part 3 Image 3: `ஏகபோக மிராசு பாத்யதை` becomes “exclusive proprietary right,” preserving the landed-property metaphor. `சமதர்மம்` is “socialism” in the jab at paper-only adherents; this does not add a party affiliation.
- The source explicitly explains the black shirt as mourning for social degradation. Its reference to fascism is an anticipated accusation by opponents, and is translated in that attributed form. The final sentence continues “social” / “degradation” across Images 3–4.

- Part 3 Images 4–5: the temple-entry debate distinguishes equal access as a right from endorsement of temple religion. The translation preserves both the radical objections voiced by delegates and the explanation on which the resolution was ultimately adopted.
- `ஆதித்திராவிடர்` remains “Adi Dravidians”; `பழங்குடி மக்கள்` is “original inhabitants” in this passage, without substituting a modern administrative category. `சனாதனம்` and `வைதீகம்` are rendered contextually as religious orthodoxy and ritualism; the hostile fortress, thieves' den and trap images remain attributed to the quoted speakers.
- T. Shanmugam, Neelamegam and Arjunan retain their printed names and specified conference roles. No external biographical identification is added. The signboard passage crosses Images 4–5; the sentence about Vedachalam's efforts crosses Images 5–6.

- Part 3 Image 6: Periyar becoming forty-five after arriving at sixty-seven is an explicit image of rejuvenation through the conference, not an age correction. The sugarcane-joint comparison is preserved as a metaphor for difficulties encountered within an otherwise rewarding endeavour.
- The final battle-sword paragraph targets figurative cactus, poisonous vines, thorny caste divisions and antiquated custom. These metaphors are retained; the translation does not turn them into added instructions about physical attacks.
- Part 3 Image 7: musical `சுருதி` is “underlying pitch,” conveying the organizing reference to which the other programmes are aligned. `சில்லரைகள்` is “small change,” preserving the dismissive image of concessions short of the ideal.
