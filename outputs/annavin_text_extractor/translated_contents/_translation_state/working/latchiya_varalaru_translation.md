History of an Ideal

“I have been saying for ten years that the Dravidar Kazhagam is not a party as it was when it bore the name Justice Party, but a movement for spreading its ideas. That is why I have been shaping the Kazhagam into an organization for propagating those ideas, so that people will regard it as a movement rather than as a party. For it is the Self-Respect Movement, which we began in 1925 and have been conducting since, that has taken over the Justice Party and is carrying on the movement's campaign; it is not in the hands of a separate body of party politicians. There may perhaps be some small alteration in the Self-Respect Movement's programmes, but in the Dravidar Kazhagam there is no change whatsoever in that movement's foundations or its principal policies. The Annadurai resolution at the Salem Conference already made this clear. Thus, the point is that the people for whom the Justice Party was founded and conducted—the Dravidian people—have accepted the Self-Respect Movement in its entirety and have largely become adherents of Self-Respect, while those who were not adherents have gradually slipped away. We may therefore say that, under the name Dravidar Kazhagam, the Self-Respect Movement is chiefly carrying on its own work of spreading the movement's ideas. That is why I repeatedly say that the Dravidar Kazhagam is a movement for propagating ideas, and not a party organization like Congress, the Communists, the Socialists, the Hindu Mahasabha, the Varnashrama Swarajya Sangam, the Brahmin Protection Association, the Liberal Association, the Muslim League, the Christian Association, the Depressed Classes, the Scheduled League, and others. To explain: this Kazhagam is an organization that, in people's intellectual life,

### Image 3: 003-1380f72045.png

social life, religion and beliefs about God, works to remove the follies, obstacles to progress and deficiencies that have persisted for so long; to bring all people together as one society; and, especially, to eradicate the social degradation, religious superstition and divisions of caste that afflict us, the Dravidian people, and to free us from Aryan oppression. These are the things it urges upon the people.

The Dravidar Kazhagam has neither an imaginary doctrine that we must obtain self-rule nor the mistaken notion that we must stand for election, secure a majority and capture ‘political power.’ For what prevails in the country today is itself called self-rule. The domination of the foreign white man—the British—has gone. In the Dravidian country, Dravidian people themselves are the first minister and the second minister; indeed, the cabinet has a Dravidian majority, with ten of its thirteen ministers said to be Dravidians. In these circumstances, what further meaning can there be in obtaining self-rule? Who is to obtain it? Capture political power? Who is to capture it from whom? How can political power hereafter pass into the hands of people who are not Dravidians? Thus, to speak of obtaining self-rule or capturing political power is henceforth meaningless talk, and to pursue these things is meaningless action.

In this province, we ourselves are now going to constitute more than ninety out of every hundred voters. Most ministers and officials are going to be Dravidians. There is no reason to doubt it. But the objection that may be raised is this: will that bring us a separate Dravidian country? Will the Dravidar Kazhagam's policies and programmes be put into practice? Let us suppose that this will not happen easily. But if we—the members of the Dravidar Kazhagam—enter the legislature, capture political power or become ministers, will a Dravidian country thereby come into being? Will the Kazhagam's policies and programmes

be carried out through political power? Considering the present circumstances, our view is that these are not things that can easily be achieved.

**Victory Is in Sight**

“Our work is to remove our degradation and the obstacles to our progress, to free ourselves from Aryanism and superstition, and to become rationalists and a society with self-respect. In doing such work, dealing with ordinary people is like dealing with fire. We must win their trust. We must reform them. This requires effective propagation of our ideas. It requires cooperation. We must unite. We have to bring together the people who lie scattered and shattered, gather everyone's strength into one, and put that strength to the test. ‘We shall no longer live as Shudras’ is the declaration of our ideal, our very breath.

Victory is visible before our eyes; it is no mirage. I am certain that, if we give it thought and care, we shall assuredly attain it.

Leader: Periyar

**Written in Tears**

**Dravidian Kings**

They did not, like Dharmaraja, lose their country at gambling.

They did not, like Harishchandra, give their country away to a sage.

**Dravidian Poets**

They did not write cosmic falsehoods about Atala, Sutala and Patala.

They did not compose tales claiming that God assumed every imaginable form.

They did not say that rolling at someone's feet in worship would win divine favour.

They said: one community, one God.

**The Dravidians**

They did not surrender their spirit merely to stay alive.

They did not live as mean-spirited people without resolve.

They did not take deceit and intrigue as their weapons.

They gave even their lives for a life of freedom.

**Dravidam**

It had not been enslaved under foreign rule.

It had known neither famine, disease nor anguish.

It had not stood by and tolerated the suffering of working people.

It rose through labour and won its rightful place in the world.

**Dravidam: Today**

The Dravidian kings have disappeared; the people have forgotten their character.

Valour has withered! Worthless men have gained dominion.

Dravidian art and languages command no respect.

Dravidian industry lies dormant. The Dravidian sobs.

Dravidian wealth has become locked up in temples, flagstaffs, drumming and fanfare, useless activities, and foreign lands.

The Dravidian suffers as a labourer on tea, sugarcane and rubber plantations.

In every nook and corner of the Dravidian country there are Marwari shops, Gujarati warehouses, Multani establishments and Seths' companies.

All the great businesses are in the hands of northerners.

In the factories, there are no longer British masters—there are Banias!

### Image 6: 006-51593aaf54.png

Dalmia has a whole town near Tiruchirappalli.

In the Tiruppur–Coimbatore region, all the mill kings are reduced to singing the praises of the Asher Seths.

In Madras there is Sowcarpet; there is the Bharat Bank.

The bell that rings every day belongs to a northerner, Goenka.

Today the great buildings in Madras belong to northerners!

In a Dravidam reduced to this condition, everything from clothing to nails comes from the North. To say that it comes from there is to say that enormous sums of money go from here to the North.

Dravidam has lost its prosperity; it has poured even the little wealth that remains into the North and is wasting away with the disease of poverty. For it to raise its head, for its people to enjoy a full life, for those who went abroad as labourers to return, and for the standard of living to rise, there is no programme other than ours: a Dravidian country for the Dravidians!

We ask everyone outside the Dravidar Kazhagam to view this question, which has economics at its foundation, with eyes full of love for the country, rather than through a merely partisan lens.

Look at the Marwari who has wound coloured cloth around his head in coil upon coil and sits beside heaps of goods, examining his interest accounts and stroking his belly; and look at the Tamil who pulls a heavily laden cart in place of an ox! Look at the Seths riding in procession in motorcars whose polish has not faded; and look at the Tamil, in torn clothes, with a parched head and eyes that have lost their light, waiting for a ship ticket to go abroad as a labourer. Look at the lush green irrigated fields here and

### Image 7: 007-b1e0b94677.png

dry fields, and at the same time look at the Tamils emaciated by hunger, without even the strength to walk. Forget us and look at these sights. Forget even that we are proposing a programme; think for yourselves and propose one. Is it just that a country with an ancient greatness, a country rich in resources of many kinds, should today be joined to the North and suffer like this? Is it a crime to free it from that union, to stand independently with a government of its own, to increase the country's wealth and ensure that this wealth serves its own people? What programme can there be for this other than a Dravidian country for the Dravidians alone? If there is another, tell us!

Standing on a ship's deck, unable to find the means of supporting himself in his prosperous motherland, the Tamil says in anguish: “Here I am, going to Malaya, a country I have never seen. I am going to work in the forests. I am leaving my mother. Poverty is driving me from my motherland. I am going abroad as a labourer.” The ideal written with the tears he sheds is this: a Dravidian country for the Dravidians alone.

**Dravidar Kazhagam**

At the conference held in Salem on the 27th, the new name Dravidar Kazhagam for the South Indian Welfare Rights Association, and the programme of relinquishing titles and offices to form ranks of workers who would restore the country and end its afflictions, won the support of many thousands of ardent, energetic Dravidian champions. We do not regard that successful conference as merely a ceremony or a victory for one group; we regard it as an important, noteworthy stage in a movement's development. It adopted radical programmes—not for the satisfaction of having done so! We are not concerned about those who draw up programmes and then stand aloof. Such people have no concern for the programmes either. But the gathering of brave people who assembled there that day, ready to bear hardship and loss, wanted nothing less than a struggle for liberation! Those thousands upon thousands of fearless comrades have no interest in expounding the *Viveka Chintamani* or drawing up a schedule of political manoeuvres. They are not people who conduct their public lives by waiting for a favourable opportunity to acquire a title or office. “Must a great people with an illustrious past be ruined? Must a country that has its place in the pages of world history become a haunt of worthless men? Can we bear the name of men of courage without fighting to change this condition?” With this fire blazing within them, those champions wanted their rights! Yes, they wanted the right proclaimed in “Dravida Nadu for the Dravidians alone.” The light in their eyes, the sound they raised that day, the sight of them slapping their shoulders, standing with chests held high and forming their ranks—these were signs of a people's awakening, the trumpet call of an army of liberation, the cry of those who understood the root of the matter,

### Image 10: 010-c44b2197b8.png

not a fashionable gathering that offered congratulations to a gracious government, bowed before the wealthy, and carried messages to lords and ladies. The country must understand this; we too must impress it upon our minds. *

Here was a people disregarded by the ruling group, mocked by rival parties, insulted by Aryans, tormented by northerners; a people who had lost their land and honour, been robbed of their wealth, called delusion to their aid, and, finding no way forward, lain on their knees before the arrogant. That day, it was a gathering of champions who took a great vow: “We must obtain liberation. I shall work for it! I shall give my life! I shall not be beguiled by anything that comes along under the name of pleasure. I shall fight! Either the garland of victory or the summons of death!”

It was not a gathering of pleasure-seekers who meet as a matter of routine, speak charmingly, display verbal bravery without bending or stooping, indulge the fancy that they have won, and then go off to a land of pleasure surrounded by women! It was a gathering of people who knew the nature of poverty! People who knew what hunger and starvation were!! Working people—but not fools who bear the feet of Brahminism. Not clowns who imagine that they can become crowned rulers without first defeating that Brahminism in religion and society. Noble people, worn and transformed by their labour for the community, gathered that day! They revealed their determination in a way that even the country's rulers would feel and understand. That day they wiped away the slur that this was a party lying about, yawning only for titles and offices. The grime upon the jewel was wiped away; its light had begun to emerge. Why a title? Why an office? Must the European and the Brahmin decide whether to confer a title upon the Tamil who once ruled the world? It was the Tamil who crossed the seas! The Tamil who engraved the tiger on the Himalayas! The Tamil who took Kadaram! The Tamil who sold golden cloth to Rome! The Tamil who savoured literature! The Tamil is a warrior whom every country admires; the Tamil strides like a proud bull! Even when faced with hardship, the Tamil smiles! Is a title for him to come from the masters who came to sell soap,

### Image 11: 011-c0e7e50301.png

combs and mirrors, and later began to rule? Why? What use is the title Rao Bahadur to someone who has not moved a little finger to remove the degrading title ‘Shudra’ that the Aryan imposed on us long ago? There are many who would take up these titles and offices only if they happened to fall at their feet! There are a few who talk of nothing else by day, dream of them at night, and think of them at every moment!! Their number is very small! As the English poet Shelley, whom we may call Britain's Bharathidasan, said: “They are few; we are very many!!” The many assembled and told them: “Either change your ways, or, if you cannot, make room for us to lead a separate life.” That is the meaning of the resolution to give up titles and offices! Commander Pandian supported this resolution and said that, above all, systematic organizational work within the party was necessary to make it practicable. “Yes! We shall do it!” the supporters replied.

As far as Tamil Nadu is concerned, the commanders have agreed to this constructive work. The responsibility for providing the necessary encouragement rests with comrades who feel the cause deeply. The young possess the ‘power’ to put it into practice. Go everywhere, from the smallest village to the largest city; tell people the news; demonstrate how many comrades you can enrol in the Dravidar Kazhagam. The leaders must be astonished at the ranks you assemble. We need ranks that will make Aryanism cry out and the world take notice! We need them quickly!! There is much work to do. The drum of liberation has sounded. The struggle for a government of our people—the final struggle—must be fought. Set out today to establish and strengthen the Dravidian camp!!

The day when Andhra and Kerala witness this momentum is not far off. The means to bring it about will certainly be devised.

The point is that our people's present condition must change, and Dravida Nadu must belong to the Dravidians alone. It is to

**Dravida Nadu for the Dravidians Alone**

This is not the slogan of a party; it is the song of a people's heart! A fundamental truth. A truth accepted everywhere, by peoples of every kind. Those who think they must oppose this idea merely because we put it forward, and those who dismiss it, are doing harm not to us

### Image 2: 002-e3f3af3635.png

but to the country; they are betraying their own people.

Even tiny countries, countries without natural resources, countries that must live on borrowed resources, have obtained independent sovereignty. Here, however, a country that already possessed a rich literature two thousand years ago is today bound to another country and held captive. We call upon those brave people who consider it a great task to break, within their own lifetimes, the shackles fastened upon their motherland. We say: make “Dravida Nadu for the Dravidians alone” known to people in every nook and corner—join the ranks.

To explain to everyone the blameless character of our policy, the necessity of our programme and the greatness of our ideal, the Dravidar Kazhagam has arranged to celebrate this coming 1 July as Dravidian Separation Day. We have not drawn up the programme “Dravida Nadu for the Dravidians” without meaning: there are sound, irrefutable reasons for it.

Whoever they may be—those who condemn, ridicule, dismiss or disregard it—we ask them to sit down calmly, consider the reasons we offer for “Dravida Nadu for the Dravidians alone,” and then reach a conclusion. We have stated these reasons many times. Now, as we celebrate a day devoted to this purpose, we state those same reasons to the people of the country once again.

1. India is a continent. It must therefore be divided into several countries. The European continent contains thirty-two separate countries. No one says that all Europe must be under one umbrella. Nor is there any necessity for India to remain under a single umbrella.

2. Before British rule was established, India was a continent containing fifty-six countries, each with its own government. It was only to make the conduct of their rule

### Image 3: 003-1dfaa2436e.png

more convenient that the British treated India as a single country and made others regard it that way too.

3. Religion, language, art, outlook, a sense of belonging to one people, and historical kinship—these are the characteristics of a people. Viewed in this way, India contains many distinct peoples. They may be grouped into three broad divisions: Dravidians, Muslims and Aryans. Of these three peoples, the Dravidians and Muslims do not differ greatly in their collective characteristics. Between the characteristics of the Aryans and those of the other two there is no compatibility whatever; there is considerable antagonism. Because these distinct collective characteristics exist, only a division of India according to its peoples will give each its own territory and government. Otherwise, the other peoples will have to live in subjection to whichever people is most skilled in cunning, deceit and intrigue, and most disposed to crush others for its own advantage.

4. Continuing to call India a single country allows Aryan domination to grow. Aryan rule has reduced the interests of the other peoples to dust.

5. Binding together, by intrigue, peoples with conflicting characteristics has only increased unrest, distress and trouble. Therefore, if future troubles are not to multiply and turn India into a wilderness of blood, India must now be divided amicably according to its peoples.

6. Dividing a country according to its peoples is neither new nor unheard of. Already there are many Indias: British India, princely India, French India and Dutch India. It is not wrong to ask, in the same way, for three separate regions—Muslim India, Aryan India and Dravidian India.

7. There are 574 princely states alone. Each has its own government and its own system. Similarly, it is an undeniable right for three great regions, each with its own form of government, to seek the means of developing their respective collective characteristics.

### Image 4: 004-219e0fede6.png

8. Just as Turkey, freed from the bonds of the Ottoman Empire, became foremost among the great powers, each region will shine with its own distinction if India is divided according to its peoples.

9. Separate regions would make it possible for each to develop military strength suited to its own characteristics.

10. Even in the time of emperors such as Ashoka, Kanishka, Harsha, Samudragupta and Akbar, India was not a single country. Even then, there was a separate country called Dravida Nadu.

11. If separate regions are formed, each can develop its economy according to the resources available there, and the cruelty of one region exploiting others can be ended.

12. Only when each people has its own territory and government can it enjoy equal rights and equal standing with the others.

13. It was by declaring India a single country that the Aryans made the land from the Himalayas to Cape Kumari their hunting ground. Thus arose a situation in which they could be officials in government, teachers in education, preceptors in religion, gods on earth in society, and people who enjoyed economic ease without labour, while the other peoples languished as servants and workers, worn beyond recognition by their toil. The best way to remove this cruelty is to set apart a region for each group!

14. One people has no trust in another. The mere suggestion that one people should live under the government of another has come to arouse fear. Terrible revolution is the child born of fear and distrust. We say that separation must take place now precisely to prevent this terrible revolution.

15. There is no evidence that India's remaining undivided has so far increased its military, economic or intellectual strength, or created a new society called the Indian people. The reason one people has not

### Image 5: 005-90cc3c5c45.png

crushed another's throat and strangled it to death is simply that British guns held all the peoples down at the same time. Therefore, once the British grip is gone, India will become a battlefield. The wars that frequently occur in Europe provide an example. Thus, to prevent such suffering, the way to avert danger is to divide the territory according to its peoples.

If India is divided according to its peoples, there is consequently a way for each people's characteristics to stand independently and with their own distinction; for Aryan domination to be curbed; for economic exploitation to end; for the distress of intellectual repression to disappear; for future quarrels to be avoided; and for tranquillity and peace to prevail. That is why those whose good intention is that India should not become a battlefield of peoples say that India must be divided according to its peoples. Those who deny this must have forgotten history, or must think that the public does not know it. Only those with the evil intention that one people's character—that is, Aryan domination—should trample, batter and destroy the others, and those with the ruinous notion of making India like a woman married off to the strongest man while somehow securing a livelihood for themselves, will oppose this scheme of separation.

We set out these and similar reasons, with evidence showing how Aryan domination grew, the cruelty by which it destroyed other characteristics, and the necessity of curbing Aryanism from now on. We explained why the arguments of those opposing this programme were untenable. We said that we must perform our duty without being deterred by our opponents' attempts to overthrow reasons with stories, suppress evidence with mere prattle, knock down history with bluster, and destroy determination by growling threats.

The Muslims understood these truths, worked according to their leader's programme, and achieved victory. In the North, Pakistan, a state for their people, has come into being.

### Image 6: 006-2c9488667e.png

The ideal for which they fought and won was ours before they made it their slogan. Before they adopted the Pakistan resolution at Lahore, we had resolved: Dravida Nadu for the Dravidians alone. Throughout the country we campaigned to explain this ideal... and we continue to do so.

There are more reasons, and stronger ones, for demanding a separate Dravidian government than those advanced for the necessity of Pakistan. Yet we see no sign that the great Dravidian people have understood this even now.

**The Battle Sword**

At Tiruchirappalli! We assembled there the following week. There were no figures of royal pomp there! No great landed proprietors, no zamindars in gold-laced caps! In the conference pavilion, cloth cradles had been tied up for the mothers' little treasures to sleep in. In Congress, they put up with the crooked antics of a king seated in state because he had given two thousand; here, we saw mothers kiss their crying children and keep them from making a sound, and we felt pride and joy. There, those who call themselves champions of patriotism raised a mighty noise in pursuit of votes; here, we undertook the good work of finding a healing herb for a people lying stricken. There, they assembled to find a way for kings to rule; here, they found a way for knowledge to grow. There, seekers of office gathered; here, champions gathered who pledged to bear any hardship or loss to wipe away their people's degradation. There, it was a festival of people dressed in white; here, it was an assembly of young people who vowed: “Henceforth we shall wear black to signify the degradation of this sorrow-stricken land, and begin the struggle for liberation.” There, the harvest; here, the ploughing.

### Image 7: 007-9bf80ef852.png

There, bargaining; here, bravery. There, Congress; here, the Dravidar Kazhagam. In wealthy Bombay, there was the Taj Hotel, the Royal Restaurant, and so many delightful lodgings! So many conveniences! All the pleasures the rich require! Here, a shed in the open! Two miles between the shed and the town! The conference organizers provided no meals. There was not enough lodging. The trouble caused by the rain would not end. Thunder, lightning and the officialdom's heavy hand were all abundant! There were no maharajas or crowned queens at the conference. No fashionable men and no wax dolls to rule over them! Just one motorcar ran here and there—and even that was hired!

Did they lay out a path to office? No! They said: “Give up the office you hold, or stand aside!” Were there preparations for elections? No! They were not to be touched—by Tiruneelakantam! Were there former ministers? None were to be seen! Great landed proprietors? No! Were there people bearing the burdens of the ‘most gracious’ government? No! Who else was there? A flood of Tamils! What course did they devise together? They declared: “We have learned that the country we inhabit is ours; we have also learned that the right to it is ours alone.” They sang: “Take up the killing sword, that the deeds of the most cruel may cease!” They asked: “Aryan rule in the sacred Dravidian land? And a Brahmin drum to proclaim it?” They condemned the government's readiness to accommodate religious orthodoxy. They chose the great elder as the leader fit for this mighty force and cried out for him to show them the field of struggle. They pledged themselves ready for every severe restriction; they assumed the bearing of combatants and departed. In their own villages and hamlets they will say: “We have inscribed our names in the Dravidians' army of liberation! We do not know when the call will come. When it comes, we shall not remain idle. It may be a flowering grove where the cuckoo calls, or a prison that seeks to trap us. We do not know what it will be! But we have pledged ourselves ready for anything.” Tiruchirappalli has displayed its ranks of champions! Inadequate railway facilities,
