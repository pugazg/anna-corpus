Once matters have reached this stage, why should comrades in the South think of retaining a connection with an all-India party? Why should they desire it? What meaning, benefit, or necessity does it possess? I ask you to think and reach a sound conclusion.

Every Congress friend accepts that Congress's work is nearly complete. Congress existed to defeat imperialism, did it not? Very well. Once imperialism, deprived of its fundamental strength, says that it is packing its bags, has not Congress's basic task succeeded? Did you not join Congress to abolish imperialism? Imperialism itself is to disappear in 1948. What service remains to be performed from within Congress?

You will say: "Is obtaining self-rule enough? Must it not be put in order? Must the country not be reformed? Must we not prevent reactionaries from entering into power? Have we no work until the weakened country regains its splendour? Congress still has work: it must enrich the country it has recovered."

That is a reasonable argument, a necessary statement, and an undeniable reason. I do not reject it. These things must indeed be done. Only then will we obtain good government and the good life produced by it. I reject none of that argument. But I ask one question, friend.

Why is an all-India party required to reform the country, create prosperity, avoid entanglement with troublemakers, and perform all these other good works? Is it not proper to have a party for the land in which you live, with a programme suited to it? To perform the necessary tasks you rightly desire,

### Image 2

in what way is association with the All-India Congress essential? Think and answer. Do not answer for my sake, nor hesitate to speak the truth merely because I ask. For the sake of your own country, say what your heart tells you. Why remain bound to an all-India party that has abandoned the all-India ideal, when your purpose is to perform the good work of reforming your own land? What necessity, benefit, or meaning does it possess?

#### Why the Ganges When We Have the Kaveri?

What possible connection can exist between the tasks that must now be performed here and the North? Although our own people were born in this land and possess the right to live here, our failure to provide opportunities has left many lakhs of them in the degrading condition of labouring until their bones melt in distant and unseen lands such as Malaya, Ceylon, Zanzibar, and Mauritius. We must bring them back and give them a life.

To do this, our country must be enriched. Are we to enrich it with water from the Ganges? We have the Kaveri, Vaigai, and South Pennar. Beautiful fields lie on both banks. We have forests and the useful wealth within them. What do we lack, and for what resource must we yearn and look toward the North? Why should the work to be done on the Kaveri's banks require a connection with the banks of the Ganges?

Do not be angry, friend; think. A thousand afflictions exist here. Why do we need a northern connection or association with an all-India party to resolve them? If ours were a land without resources, means, capacity for thought, ability to act, or an ancient history, it would be proper to seek assistance and connections. But ours is not in that degraded condition.

Everything is here, including people capable of doing anything. Two thousand years ago this country produced literature of a kind no other people had discovered. In ancient times it established commerce with the Yavanas and connections with China. What can we say we lack today that would require a connection with the North?

### Image 3

The League of Nations was once established to preserve peace in the world, and many countries joined it. Mussolini and Hitler arose and shattered the League. War broke out. Today not only is the war gone; the ineffectual League is gone too.

The All-India Congress existed to bring all India under one umbrella. Today Congress itself has realized that an all-India government is impossible and has altered its practice accordingly. Under these circumstances, why should the South retain an all-India connection?

Good work must be done to reform the country; only then will the benefit of self-rule become visible. But no northern connection is required to perform that work. The work we must do must be suited to our country's condition, culture, needs, and facilities. An all-India party is unnecessary for it. Why, then, retain one?

Do not be displeased that I ask questions enough to bewilder you, friend. You may feel moved to ask: "If merely recognizing this would lead them to dissolve Congress, why have they not done so? Do you think they understand nothing?" That too is a reasonable question.

The North still requires Congress. It has a need and work for it. Congress is required to perform two important tasks, though they embrace each other so closely that they can scarcely be separated. One is to prevent Muslim domination. The other is to protect the flourishing capitalist order from collapse. They have planned to use Congress in the North for both purposes.

That is why members of the Congress Socialist Party such as Jayaprakash, Aruna Asaf Ali, and Achyut Patwardhan openly say, "Within Congress..."

### Image 4

"the Dalmias and Bajajs exercise domination." Northern capitalists have already seized the propaganda machinery so that North Indian Congress may continue to serve this purpose.

Under these conditions, why should we remain connected to North Indian Congress?

Why should we purchase trouble at a price?

Even a hand that held honey must be washed after the honey has been eaten; otherwise tiny ants will bite it. Dalmia, Bajaj, and the others are no tiny ants. If capitalism stings, our already weakened country cannot endure it.

That is why I say that Dravidians in Congress can no longer claim that a northern connection and an all-India party remain necessary. They have no meaning, necessity, or benefit.

#### A New Problem Requires a New Method

"Does that mean no party is needed at all?" you ask. A party is needed. From today onward, the country has an abundance of good work to perform. Thousands upon thousands of social and economic problems require resolution.

We must remove the cruelty of caste running rampant among Tamils who once knew no caste; stop poverty from dancing its savage dance among Tamils who once knew no poverty; awaken sleeping nature; set idle wealth into motion; enable the languishing worker to live; and bring home our kinsmen who suffer in many directions.

Friend, as one reflects, countless tasks appear before the mind's eye, each quarrelling, "I come first! No, I do!" We must welcome science. We must create a path to life for comrades who, because life on earth has been wounded, stand raising their hands toward heaven. Society must be melted down and cast anew. A party is needed for this work.

### Image 5

As these tasks are undertaken, parties will form along reactionary, radical, and moderate lines. Dravidian comrades in Congress, come forward to organize a party on that basis. When you do so, first examine whether the Dravidar Kazhagam possesses a policy for such work. If it does, join it. Why merely join, friends? Take possession of it! If you believe it does not, form another party. I find no fault with that.

But remove the unwanted, unprofitable, unnecessary, and trouble-producing connection with the All-India Congress, and with it the northern connection obtained through that Congress.

British imperialism will leave in 1948. Bring about a condition in which, on that same auspicious day, the South too resolves to shine in its separate sovereignty, devises a plan for it, and comes forward to perform the good work needed for the country's progress, severing the northern and all-India party connection because it recognizes that neither is required for this task.

"I am packing my bags! I shall leave in June 1948. Enough of this pointless trouble!"

White imperialism has announced this publicly, before all the world, in the Parliament that rules it. Prime Minister Attlee's declaration has announced that the imperialism which seized and tormented India for the past two centuries is ending.

Joy! Boundless joy! Tamil! Comrade!

The burdens loaded upon Tamils have bent their lives. Aryanism, mythology, caste, northern capitalism, and many others weigh upon them. Carrying all these burdens has left Tamil life bowed.

White imperialism is leaving India. Freedom is being obtained.

Will Aryanism release the Tamil? Will freedom be obtained?

### Image 6

Are there no heroes who will undertake the effort to obtain freedom?

It is said that when just the uppermost of the many loads carried by a camel is removed, the animal imagines its burden has been reduced, rises from where it lay, and begins to walk. Poor creature! It does not realize that many more loads remain on its back.

Tamil! They say that in 1948 one burden - white imperialism - will be cast down.

Is that enough? Unless the other burdens strangling our lives are also cast down, there is no life. Come forward to labour for life and freedom. Victory is ours; fear not!

Lord Mountbatten, arriving as the last Viceroy, is said to be bringing two plans. One sets out how power should be transferred if Congress and the League reach a settlement. The other sets out how the British should act if the two parties fail to come to terms until the very end.

An Invitation to That Lawrence!

Unless this second plan is rendered unnecessary, no path will open for the country's progress. The country's condition is deteriorating to the point where both parties consult omens about which side British strength will join. Would Britain act without calculating which side offers it profit?

Think now and it will become clear: if the measures taken in the days of the present Lawrences are examined closely, they prepare the way for the old policy of Stringer Lawrence, who entered a civil conflict and acquired the right to rule the country.

This must be prevented, and it can be prevented. But under the present northern conditions, there is no way for clarity to arise.

### Image 7

The League may believe that Britain must support it in view of its connections with Muslim countries across the world. Congress may likewise think Britain will support it in view of its growing commercial and economic strength and alliances. Whichever party's plan Britain supports, what will be the result? The return of the British grip.

What if Britain supports neither party and says, "Resolve your domestic quarrels yourselves"? The condition of China: disintegration.

#### Why Should Dravidam Be Worn Away?

Why should Dravidam be worn away through a connection that has no relevance or necessity for it and can only bring harm? Dravidian comrades in Congress must examine this with the greatest seriousness. If the country's future is to be prosperous, we must clarify our course now.

#### Curzon - Carson!

"Down with Curzon! Down with Carson!

"Down with the demons of partition!

"We will not let Mother Bengal be cut apart! We will not let the foreigner's plan succeed!

"We will not let Mother Ireland be cut apart! We will not let the unjust Carson triumph!"

Thus cried those driven by nationalism, once upon a time.

Curzon and Carson were both caught in the fiery vortex raised in nationalism's name.

### Image 8

Curzon was Viceroy of the Indian subcontinent. For administrative convenience, he devised a plan to turn Bengal into two provinces. Angered by it, the Bengali movement, the Vande Mataram movement, the Swadeshi movement, and the Congress movement successively acquired the power of popular resistance.

Carson devised a plan to establish one portion of Ireland, the region called Ulster, as a separate state.

In Ireland, the "patriots" condemned Carson. In India, patriots condemned Curzon.

Curzon's plan did not succeed then. Carson's plan - the partition of Ireland and the creation of a separate Ulster government - succeeded at that very time.

Curzon has now taken his revenge. Congressmen today seek to implement the same plan to partition Bengal that they once opposed and defeated.

Just as Carson demanded and obtained Ulster, Pakistan is demanded here. It has been refused, yet continues to take shape.

Both Curzon and Carson have gained influence here today, many years after their deaths.

The plan spoken by the grandson into the ear of the Bengali Babu today is, "Divide Bengal in two." Even Curzon's stone image must laugh mockingly at this condition.

The English entered among a fragmented empire, declining powers, and princely states tangled in complications, and thereby acquired the opportunity to rule the country. Although military force won victories for them in many regions, statecraft was the principal reason they remained here.

The British were not somehow the world's foremost invincible heroes, nor were the peoples living across the Indian subcontinent cowards. The sidelong glance of a maiden...

### Image 9

may have felled men in the flavour of epic poetry, but many battlefields here have also marvelled at the sweep of the sword in the hands of our own people. The English did not subdue the powerless. We did not lose our land to warriors; we lost it to tacticians who turned our futile quarrels to their advantage.

At that time the Europeans did not possess the modern instruments of war they have today. They carried rifles like those now used to shoot cranes. Men boasting descent from the Ikshvaku line, the Solar dynasty, Ranabhadrakali, and Viravaku; the Palas, Senas, Kadambas, Rashtrakutas, Rajputs, Marathas, Mughals, and Sikhs - bearers of so many titles - proved unable to withstand men carrying bird-shooting rifles.

What could be done? Gunpowder yields nothing when water has been poured over it. Courage had been dimmed through association with the worthless. That is why the English, who first pitched tents, later attained the position of sitting enthroned.

They had to reorganize the many scattered governments: join them, cut them apart, melt them down, and cast them anew. At first they remained in the port regions of Madras, Bombay, and Calcutta; later they established governments in all those places.

When forming provinces, they paid no attention to ethnicity, language, or culture. Why would they possess such concern? Their sole object was an orderly administration suited to governance, and especially to military operations. They converted many regions into "administrative units," changing those arrangements from time to time according to emerging difficulties and circumstances. That work is still unfinished.

We have witnessed many changes within our own country, matters worth recalling.

Older Congressmen will remember the occasion when, viewing India as one nation,

### Image 10

they personified the country as a goddess and published her image. An old copy may still be found somewhere. In it, Burma appears as the loose end of the goddess's sari. What of today?

The loose end is gone. Burma was separated and made an independent state. The cry "Burma for the Burmese" was not heard for even ten full years before the Burmese obtained their separate sovereignty.

Even those who had drawn Burma as the end of the goddess's sari and sung the praises of that image saw it become an independent state and finally said, "Very well, it is their wish." At first they angrily asked, "Can Burma be separated?" Then they declared indignantly, "We shall not permit Burma to be separated." Later they asked sorrowfully, "Must Burma really separate?" Still later they stood in tears and said, "Burma has separated after all." Thus the pitch of their voice changed step by step.

As soon as what had happened became an accomplished fact, the pitch began changing again: "Though separated, our desire is that Burma should prosper"; "What if it has separated? We shall not forget it"; "Separation has not destroyed our affection"; "Burma is our companion, friend, and relative"; "Who else can possess the concern we have for Burma's joys and sorrows?"

Today the situation has changed so far, and friendship become so firmly established, that newspapers write with delight of the Aung San-Nehru embrace.

Burma was once treated simply as a part of India. There was a time when even imagining that Burma might become a separate country was denounced as the work of a traitor.

Separated Burma can teach us a lesson if we study its history thoughtfully.

Burma, which remained joined to India until 1937, became an independent country. No disaster appears to have occurred. Merchant princes may have lost part of their hunting ground, but the people suffered no loss. Nor did separation prevent friendship and relationship from arising.
