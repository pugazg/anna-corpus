# Without Mercy or Favour...!
Tamil title: தயவு தாட்சண்யமின்றி...!

### Image 1

An economic crisis has arisen in the country.

Everyone must share in the hardship.

Every wasteful expenditure must be prevented.

Economy must be practised in every way.

The Central and Provincial Governments must reduce their expenditure without mercy or favour.

People must use goods as sparingly as possible.

More than thirty lakh rupees will be spent on the Jaipur Congress Conference.

Considering the high prices at which goods are now sold, it cannot be said that an expenditure of thirty lakhs is excessive.

A silver chariot has been arranged to take the president of the Jaipur Congress Conference in procession.

Two special trains from Madras, three from Calcutta, and special trains from several other places are travelling to the Jaipur Conference.

Experts in their respective arts have been invited from many regions to present dances, songs, dramatic performances, and kummis in the conference pavilion.

### Image 2

The statements quoted at the beginning are some of the important passages in the resolutions sent by the All India Congress Committee to the Jaipur Conference.

The statements in the next group describe some of the arrangements made by that same committee for the Jaipur Congress Conference.

The very committee that sent the All India Congress Conference a resolution declaring that an economic crisis had arisen in the country has arranged to conduct the conference at a cost of thirty to fifty lakh rupees.

Those who resolved that everyone must share in the hardship have themselves arranged a silver chariot in which to take their president in procession.

Those who passed a resolution that every wasteful expense must be prevented have arranged dances, songs, dramatic performances, and kummis in the conference pavilion.

Those who say economy must be practised in every way are travelling to the conference in several special trains arranged exclusively for them at enormous cost.

Friends should compare the mountain-and-valley difference between the words "economic crisis," "sharing hardship," "wasteful expenditure," and "economy," on the one hand, and "thirty lakhs in conference expenses," "silver chariot," "special trains," and "dance, song, drama, and kummi," on the other. We write this so that everyone in general, and Congress comrades in particular, may consider whether even a mustard seed's worth of agreement exists between word and deed, and whether such people can remedy the hardships, losses, grievances, and agitation of the country's people.

### Image 3

The Congress Working Committee has recognized that an economic crisis has arisen in the country, offers intelligent counsel that expenditure must be reduced accordingly, and has grown powerful enough to issue commands to the Government. It ought to undertake the great task of securing the freedom it demanded and safeguarding the freedom obtained, so that there is no longer any need to hold a conference to drive out the white man. We ask it to consider whether it is proper, necessary, or useful to conduct a conference costing many lakhs.

Freedom has been obtained. The leaders of the freedom struggle have assumed responsibility for administering that freedom. In these circumstances, why must the Congress organization, founded on the single basis of attaining freedom and accustomed to holding conferences to inspire in the people a feeling for freedom and enthusiasm to win it, hold a conference now? Congress formerly held many conferences on the basis of driving out foreign English rule; now it holds a conference on the basis of driving out poverty.

The foundation of Congress has now changed. Its present purpose is not to drive anyone out, but to unite everyone in this country. That is the foundation of today's Congress. Has the present Congress conference been convened on that basis? Or has it at least been convened on the basis that, although we struggled for freedom and obtained it, several parties in the country refuse to share in that freedom and live united with the Indian Union, and therefore the conference must learn their grievances and seek appropriate remedies?

### Image 4

There is no room even to claim that the conference has been convened in that manner.

Was it not after hurriedly imposing prohibitory orders under Section 144 on meetings connected with the Dravidar Kazhagam's anti-Hindi picketing that the Madras ministers and Congress leaders engaged special trains and departed for the Jaipur Conference?

Can we avoid the thought that the Jaipur Conference is going to be used to formulate plans for further increasing the power by which prohibitions and punitive action are imposed on Dravidar Kazhagam members in Kumbakonam, Tiruvarur, Nannilam, Kovilpatti, Tirukkoyilur, and other places?

How can we refrain from telling the public that today's system of free government proceeds by embracing such repressive methods throughout the country, and that Congress has convened a conference for this very purpose, wasting lakhs of public money?

We thought that the conference at Jaipur would formulate plans to reduce wasteful expenditure, as resolved by the Congress Working Committee. Yet the very conference they convene wastes many lakhs!

And in this splendid state of affairs:

An economic crisis has arisen in the country, they say!

Everyone must share in the hardship, they say!

Wasteful expenditure must be prevented, they say!

Economy is necessary, they say!

### Image 6

What fine preaching!

The Congress Working Committee instructs the Government by resolution that "the Central and Provincial Governments must reduce their expenditure without mercy or favour," while spending lakhs on the conference it convenes! Let us set aside for a moment whether this is just or proper.

In a country described as the summit of destitution and the dwelling place of poverty, the Governor-General's monthly salary is twenty-one thousand rupees, besides many thousands more for various additional expenses!

Ambassadors abroad receive twelve thousand, eight thousand, or six thousand rupees, together with two, three, or nine cars for each of them!

The Prime Minister of the Indian Union receives three thousand rupees, besides allowances!

Provincial ministers receive one thousand five hundred rupees, besides allowances!

Members of the Legislative Assembly receive one hundred and fifty rupees, besides allowances!

Did the Congress Working Committee ask why? The committee that commands the Central and Provincial Governments to reduce expenditure without mercy or favour has, for some reason, shown its own mercy and favour without restraint in this matter. That alone is difficult to understand. Had its resolution on reducing government expenditure included even one reference to reducing the expenses listed above, one might believe that it had truly understood the country's economic crisis and was working to change that condition.

### Image 7

But the Congress Working Committee has not undertaken that work. In other words, it has no good intention of relieving the real economic crisis. Recently the Madras ministers appealed that the salaries and allowances received by ministers and ambassadors were insufficient and should be increased. They said that, because they must frequently conduct feasts and ceremonial functions, the money they receive is inadequate, and asked for another four hundred rupees.

The Congress Working Committee says that the Central and Provincial Governments must reduce expenditure without mercy or favour. Provincial ministers, however, say that their salary is insufficient because they must hold banquets, and therefore ask the Government to increase it without regard to mercy or favour. What a course the system of free government is following!

There is another amusement in this. The committee that resolves to reduce expenditure includes ministers who say their present salaries are insufficient and demand still more. While participating in the Working Committee, they become conscious of the country's economic crisis and of the need to reduce expenditure. Later, after finishing the committee's business and returning to occupy the ministerial seat, they become conscious of their "personal economic crisis": that they lack enough money for the banquets they hold and that the Government must pay them still more.

The country's general economic crisis, which appears before their eyes in the Working Committee, disappears when they sit in the ministerial seat amid banquets for all who come and go.

### Image 8

Their own personal economic crisis alone then appears before their eyes, confuses their judgement, and even makes them open their mouths and ask, "Our salary is insufficient; give us more!"

It is people endowed with this kind of political wisdom and this "broad" outlook toward understanding the country's condition and conducting affairs accordingly who say that economy must be practised in every way and that expenditure must be reduced without mercy or favour. What fine mercy and favour they display!

19 December 1949
