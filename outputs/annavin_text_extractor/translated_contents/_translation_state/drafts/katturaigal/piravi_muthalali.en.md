# Fight the Capitalists by Birth!
Tamil title: பிறவி முதலாளிகளுடன் போரிடுக!

### Image 1

If Aryanism is destroyed, socialism will be born.
Congress carries only the gentleman's kavadi.
The May Day celebration and upper-caste arrogance.

The May Day celebration is the battle drum of the working people's struggle for rights, a sacred day born to end the worker's suffering. Comrades who celebrated it: capitalism in Dravida Nadu is not made of money alone. Here there are capitalists by birth. Only by escaping their grip can you establish an egalitarian order.

Comrades who understand the greatness of May Day, destroy the arrogance of the upper castes!

May Day, celebrated by working people throughout the world, was observed in many places this year.

Can the celebration yield its full benefit?

Karl Marx's ideas and analyses, Lenin's plans for the path of revolution, and the ideals of class struggle would have been discussed and explained during May Day. Methods to remove workers' grievances and conduct strikes would have been taught.

### Image 2

“Inquilab Zindabad!”

The slogan “Long live the revolution!” would have arisen. We welcome all this and the still greater feelings of enthusiasm. But if people celebrate, read the history of the heroic Lenin, return home, and merely contemplate the splendours of the Soviet land, May Day will become another festival like the Mariamman festival rather than yield a real, tangible, enduring result.

What must be done for the movement to succeed?

Awakening and emotion are necessary, but they alone are insufficient. For a labour movement, or any other movement, to succeed, comrades who delight in hearing eloquent talk about the beauties of class struggle must concentrate on these questions: What is their own condition? What forces surround them? What action is needed to turn thoughts into deeds, fulfil intentions, and make dreams come true? What must they learn? Otherwise May Day will remain merely another holy day, and the paths given by great leaders to create fundamental change in human society will turn to dust.

Therefore we ask comrades, at least after the May Day celebration, to bring certain matters into their thought and examine them.

We call the Justice Party a movement of the working people and believe its service will be service to labour. For when compared with the Aryan class, the Dravidian class, the non-Brahmins who constitute ninety-seven out of every hundred, appears to us as a class of working people and labourers. We do not say there are no lords, wealthy men, mill owners, or landed proprietors among Dravidians.

### Image 3

We insist that most Dravidians are working people. Dravidians must toil and live only through labour, while we see the entire Aryan class living without toil, pain, or bodily exertion.

The modern poet asks:

“The little streams, the embanked fields, the fine canals storing river water, the shoulders whose abundant labour orders them, ploughs them, gathers the grain, and yields ancient bounty: whose shoulders are they?

The hand that split stone and mountain, mined minerals, and made every tool: whose hand is it?

The breath held under water to retrieve golden heaps, sea pearls, and clusters of gems: whose breath is it?”

With a deep sigh and tear-filled eyes, we answer: Dravidian shoulders and Dravidian breath, without doubt.

But the parched head, darkened eyes, blackened face, dry lips, empty stomach, and weary body belong to the Dravidian. A leaky hut, broken vessels, a wife beating her breast, children rolling in the dirt, a sobbing mother, struggling father, a sister robbed of peace, a brother become a corpse, poverty, and disease are the Dravidian's ornaments. Why? Why does the class that labours, the people who mine gold and dive holding their breath for pearls, live this life?

Our appeal

To explain this and seek liberation, May Day once a year is not enough; it is needed every month. Working people, we humbly ask you to take as your first May Day thought the fact that you are Dravidians.

### Image 4

We know that Sharmas and Banerjees are your leaders. We know they will say, according to Marx's philosophy, that a worker has neither race nor class; Indian or Eskimo, all are one; there must be no Brahmin/non-Brahmin division among workers; otherwise you betray Lenin and overthrow socialism; workers of the world, unite!

Knowing this, we still say: remember that you are Dravidians. Why?

Because we firmly believe that without this thought the liberation of the working people in Dravida Nadu will not come easily.

To gain the consciousness that workers must obtain freedom and rights, we must first understand who we are, why we became trapped, what bond trapped us, and what trapped us and continues to hold us.

We say that if people gain the feeling and strength to remove the domination of Aryans who are capitalists by birth, and remove that domination, it will afterward be very easy to remove the domination of the group whose members become capitalists by acquiring wealth and workers by losing it.

If the high and low status taught as inherent in birth is abolished, and distinctions taught solely on account of birth are removed, distinctions created by money will fly away like cotton.

Our belief

Once social equality is established, equality in the economic sphere will not seem difficult and will be readily achieved. That is our belief.

### Image 5

The capitalist-worker distinction concerns wealth; it can torment the body and bend one's life. But the Aryan-Dravidian distinction can destroy life itself and ruin both present and future existence. Those who examine it will see this. The capitalist and worker emerge as two classes when mills and factories arise here and there, whereas we see the Aryan-Dravidian class distinction present from birth itself.

A worker has a path to becoming a capitalist; many know people who have done so. Likewise, we have seen capitalists become workers. But a Dravidian can never become an Aryan. Even if educated, wealthy, and holding high office, he is not accepted as high caste. Ritual observances do not grant him a place seated as an Aryan among Aryans. The Aryans, those called high caste, will speak of him only as a red lotus that appeared in mud.

The Aryan-Dravidian distinction must be resolved urgently.

It is older and more permanent than the capitalist-worker distinction, is said to possess the sanction of Vedas, Puranas, epics, and shastras, and permits no change. Therefore the Aryan-Dravidian distinction must be resolved first and most urgently. We say Dravidians need a May Day demanding that resolution. We ask workers to consider this.

Because of the capitalist-worker distinction, the labourer cannot enjoy the full fruit of labour: the silk weaver wears rags, the builder of mansions suffers in a mud house, the grower of fine red rice eats coarse millet, and the person whose forehead sweat falls upon the land looks at today's pot of gruel and speaks of yesterday's hunger.

### Image 6

Likewise, because of the Aryan-Dravidian distinction, we labour while they live; we build temples while they arrive as priests; we remain illiterate while they become scholars of dharma shastra; we pull the punkah while they occupy numerous high offices; we remain Sudras while they are priests. Our labour does not benefit us. Our country is not in our hands. Even our consciousness is not our own.

The Justice Party therefore works with the determination that liberation from this capitalism by birth must be won. Workers themselves will receive its greatest benefit.

Political spectacle

This is our position. After May Day, we ask comrades also to consider what benefit Congress, which embraced, kissed, and caressed labour, actually gave workers. There is scarcely a greater political spectacle than the way workers trusted and supported Congress and danced to its words.

Workers believed freedom and deliverance would come through Congress. Congress conducted special propaganda to create that belief.

When labour's awakening began, Pandit Jawaharlal started speaking socialism and became a teacher of the Soviet path. Comrades cried enthusiastically, “Pandit Jawaharlal! Jai!” We ask: where is that lion of socialism today? Congress patted workers, obtained their votes, took power, and governed for twenty-seven months. Why did Jawaharlal not try to introduce even a little of his socialism during that period? Why were working hours not reduced? Did the Justice Party obstruct him? Did the Muslim League prevent him? Did Ambedkar say no?

### Image 7

Congress's conduct

If Congress had possessed genuine concern for the labour movement, could it not have done much good in twenty-seven months?

Set aside its failure to do good. Did it at least refrain from betrayal and harm?

Congress openly supported the capitalists. A Government report itself states that ninety-nine out of every hundred strikes during Congress rule failed. To suppress labour unrest, Congress governments used everything from batons to guns. Many labour leaders were imprisoned. Workers were beaten in Coimbatore, Madurai, Choolai, and Pasumalai. During the Madras labour agitation, many comrades were imprisoned, Choolai Mill was closed, and thousands suffered. Police fired upon workers in Dharavi, Sirala, and Sithavalasa, and some workers died.

In Bombay, a law concerning labour strikes was enacted largely to protect capitalists. Workers strongly opposed and condemned it. During that protest the police carried out a baton charge. Lord Patel and Congress minister Munshi rode in a car and watched that “baton-charge durbar.” This was Congress's gift to workers while it ruled.

The strike and the Congressmen

An enormous strike swept Bombay like a terrible storm. More than a lakh people participated. What happened in the end? The strike was abandoned. Why? Labour leaders declared openly at a public meeting that one principal cause of its defeat was the failure of prominent Bombay Congressmen to support the strikers.

### Image 8

Thus, while deceiving workers and obtaining their labour during its rule and afterward, Congress stood with the capitalists and did not do even a drop of good for workers. It beat them until their backs broke.

We therefore ask comrades who celebrated May Day to consider: What is their problem? Who are their true comrades? Who are their enemies?

We wish this year's May Day to help open the mind's eye of comrades.

6 May 1945

---NOTES---
“Capitalists by birth” renders பிறவி முதலாளிகள், Anna's central contrast between inherited caste privilege and economic capital that may be gained or lost.

அபேதவாதம் literally denotes non-difference or equality. It is rendered “socialism” where the historical political context points to socialist doctrine, and “equality” where the argument concerns social or economic equality.

The poem beginning சிற்றாறும் is by Bharathidasan, called “the modern poet” in the source. Its compressed imagery is translated closely while preserving the questions about whose labour created material wealth.

கனவான் is rendered “gentleman” in the opening slogan; காவடி retains the image of bearing another's burden. பங்கா refers to a manually pulled fan. “Baton-charge durbar” preserves Anna's sarcastic phrase தடியடி தர்பார்.

Several place names in the account of labour repression are retained according to the printed Tamil. Their precise modern spellings should be checked against contemporary labour-history records before quotation as independent historical evidence.
