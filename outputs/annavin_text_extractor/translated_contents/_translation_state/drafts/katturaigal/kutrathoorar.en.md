# The People of Crime-town
Tamil title: குற்றத்தூரார்

### Image 1

58. The People of Crime-town

Untouchability has been abolished under the Constitution. Harijans may enjoy rights like Brahmins. Yet in some villages people continue to torment them.

The law speaks! The Government asks! Even so, the people refuse!

They brush the law aside and disregard those who rule. They cannot be restrained.

### Image 2

It has been made illegal to cast people aside as untouchable. Yet in some places that evil custom does not appear to have been abandoned.

Let them enjoy rights like Brahmins. It would be enough if they could at least live like human beings!

Even now, commands such as these prevail in some places: do not enter the tank; do not come into the street; do not enter the temple; do not wear your dhoti hanging down; walk carrying your sandals in your hand; and, for women, do not wear a blouse.

The law does not speak bravely to them; it dances and collapses. The very Government that made the law does not gird itself for action; it sings a song and rests.

The person who pointed this out was none other than Congress leader Tandon. He said that people live without respecting the law.

The law calls something a crime, but people commit it without shame. They commit that crime eagerly, arrogantly, and angrily.

The law says there must be no untouchability, yet they carry that evil through with intensity.

Why are there people who commit crimes? They know the law exists and know that violating it can take them before a court, bring punishment, prison, and suffering. Yet they act. What is the reason?

### Image 3

Tandon described the condition, but he did not show its cause. He could not.

The law calls it a crime. Yet people commit it calmly and cheerfully, without fear or anxiety, with the inward satisfaction of having performed some great good deed. Why?

The number of offenders is increasing! Come, let us reduce it!

Let us become death to crime and Yama to cruelty! Arise!

Let us bring crimes near death, remove cruelties, abolish errors, destroy wrongs, and uphold the law!

Burn away old fear! Cut down ancient hostility! Cooperate!

No hatred; the responsibility is great!

Everyone must help remove crime. Otherwise crime will grow without the slightest hesitation.

### Image 4

People, vigilance committees exist to obtain your cooperation. Abandon irritation, stop abuse, and cooperate with us!

Run to us at the sight of even crime's shadow. Tell us at once!

Seize the cruel people who commit crime! Catch them, and then come and tell us!

Your hatred, and the desire for crime that has grown within you, have crippled our duty.

This is the Police Commissioner's loving appeal to the public.

To reduce and detect crime, Devasahayam seeks the people's help. Speaking to a vigilance committee, he appealed to the public in this manner.

Tandon says that although the law exists, people do not respect it.

The Police Commissioner, although the law exists and although there are courts, prisons, and police to enforce it, appeals to the public.

Crimes continue to grow. They are not being reduced.

### Image 5

Do offenders not know that their acts are illegal? Do they not know the police will investigate? If they did not know, they would not hide.

They know, yet follow that evil path. Why?

He steals, kills, gambles, drinks, lies, engages in prostitution, and violates many more of society's laws and rules. Why?

He commits the five great sins without fear. He does not fear that people will denounce him as a sinner or condemn him as wicked. He does not even tremble at being placed in the dock as a criminal.

What causes people to follow this evil path? Some may say poverty. Yet people living lives of pleasure in homes untouched by distress commit such acts too.

Crime may be caused not only by having nothing but also by having too much. Yet why does crime grow?

The reservoir called crime has two springs: one is poverty, the other prosperity.

### Image 6

These two alone cannot be the causes. Are there others? Certainly. What are they?

When people commit crimes, the thought that their acts are wicked must pierce their hearts like a spear and strike their heads like a hammer.

That thought is shaped by the books given to them, the social arrangements in which they live, and all the gods they worship.

These should have taught them not to commit those crimes and that doing so is wrong. They did not. Therefore the blame for multiplying crime belongs to these things.

Just as the offender is placed in the dock, these influences that foster the thought of offending must also be placed there.

From childhood, blind ideas and narrow-minded practices have been lodged in their hearts through books, performances, songs, and the counsel of elders. These do not prevent them from committing crimes.

### Image 7

Will a thief be afraid if, from childhood, he has thought that Krishna stole butter and later stole women? Will he not think, “When the Lord himself did so, why should an ordinary servant like me not do it?”

Will one who has heard in books and songs of Narada receiving praise through lies be afraid to lie?

There is hardly a god here without two wives. As though Parvati were insufficient, Ganga sits in the matted locks. As though Lakshmi were insufficient, what he enjoyed through incarnation after incarnation cannot be recounted. They preserve the scene beneath the punnai tree as worthy of worship. Will this cultivate morality or increase prostitution?

Gambling is a crime, yet gambling itself has been made into an epic and the Bharata is recited. What does Dharmar stake in the game? His wife Draupadi herself! Stranger still, only one-fifth of her belongs to him. He gambles even with property that is not his. This is an epic. Why would people not commit crimes?

After reading that the gods and the Trinity drank sura and soma, what can prohibition accomplish?

### Image 8

If murder is defended by saying the Lord destroys the wicked and protects the good, will it not occur in the country too?

That is why the daily newspapers contain abundant reports about people committing such crimes.

The books, epic and Puranic ideas, customs, and old arrangements that fill their minds do not sow the thought that crimes must not be committed.

Antiquity has seized their hearts. New ideas cannot enter. When they do, a war occurs between old and new. In that battle of the mind, crime triumphs.

The law dies before them, and the Government collapses before them.

Because the scheme of untouchability has been planted indelibly in their hearts as pleasing to God and inherited through divine command, they commit many of the five great sins.

To oppose one law, they break nine laws.

### Image 9

To uphold untouchability, lovers of antiquity commit the very acts they themselves taught were sins.

They burned cheris, looted, raped women, and hired labourers and ruffians to inflict many cruelties.

How many laws were shattered! The cause of all this is the antiquity that grips them like iron.

The Government enacted a law; Sanatana tradition struck it down.

If these causes are understood, crimes can be reduced and cruelties restrained.

To reduce crime, they propose going to the prisons, calling the prisoners together, and preaching to them.

They propose chanting “Jai Jai Sitaram,” singing “Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna,” and crying “Mahadeva, Mahadeva!”

They would abolish crime through preaching. It cannot be done.

### Image 10

The Prison Reform Committee has offered a proposal. In prison there are arrangements for Muslim and Christian preaching; a similar arrangement should be made for Hindus. Responsibility for this preaching might be entrusted to an institution such as the Ramakrishna Math, they say.

Preaching will not remove wickedness. Crime will diminish and disappear only through revolutionary changes.

The circumstances, ideas, way of life, social structure, and economic system of those who drink from the reservoir called crime are its steps. Those steps must be demolished and the reservoir filled in.

Rather than merely punishing a crime after seeing it committed, we must make it our method and rule to discover why crime arises and what makes it grow. Only then will that reservoir be closed.

A society that commits no crime will emerge.

Only then can we see a flower garden without cactus or fungus.

A condition will arise where only the breeze wanders and there is not even a trace of the harsh wind.

### Image 11

We live in Crime-town while trying to discover crimes.

Everything written in a book must not be accepted. It must be examined.

Even if it is a divine book, everything stated in it must not be taken as truth.

Tandon preaches this. Had this method prevailed for ages, this ruined condition would not have arisen. Crime would not have grown beyond the Government's control even after laws were enacted.

The condition in which police assistance must seek the people's assistance would not have arisen.

To reduce crime, the criminal ideas massed in people's minds must be removed.

Then there will be no need to cry, “Do not commit crime.” People will know only when they are told what crime itself is.

Crimes pile up like a mountain, yet they cannot be restrained.

### Image 12

No one, whatever field they belong to, hesitates to commit crime.

The hand that holds the scales and the hand that takes up the pen both steal.

The mouth that speaks law and the mouth that reads scripture both lie.

Even those who sing, dance, and rejoice in beautiful mansions engage in obscene sport.

There is no sin they have not committed and no wickedness they have not spoken.

The reason is that no thought vividly portraying the cruelty of those sinful deeds has grown in their hearts.

Our ancient arrangements do not merely induce people to commit crimes; they also provide remedies after the crimes are committed.

Therefore he commits crimes boldly.

He will torment the poor, but his sin will be absolved if he performs a milk ablution in the temple.

He will charge extortionate interest, but if he makes a silver bull he will be praised as a devotee.

One ablution turns a sinner into a virtuous man.

### Image 13

For greater sins, they have prescribed pilgrimage and immersion in holy waters.

Otherwise, would three people have been sacrificed in a dispute over whether a sacrifice might be offered to God? This killing took place in Siru Nadar Kudiyiruppu village. Such was the fate of devotees who went to offer a sacrifice to God.

Did the devotees not know murder was a sinful act? Why did they do it?

To understand why Crime-town exists, one must understand the foundation. Only then will the people of Crime-town decrease.

Even the leader of the party that runs the Government says that, despite making a law, the Government has been unable to establish the abolition of untouchability.

The people of Crime-town have grown so numerous. Action must be taken in a manner capable of reducing them. Tandon must understand this.

*Dravida Nadu*, 24 June 1951

---NOTES---
The coined title குற்றத்தூரார் joins குற்றம் (crime), ஊர் (town/place), and the honorific plural suffix ஆர். “The People of Crime-town” retains both the place metaphor and its reference to offenders.

The source uses the historical term அரிஜனங்கள் (“Harijans”). It is retained to preserve the period argument and is not presented as current preferred usage.

பஞ்சமா பாதகங்கள் is rendered “the five great sins,” its conventional religious sense. துஷ்ட நிக்கிரக சிஷ்டபரிபாலனம் is translated as “destroying the wicked and protecting the good.”

சேரி is retained as “cheri,” referring here to a segregated settlement inhabited by oppressed-caste communities. The source's criticism of epic and Puranic narratives is translated as Anna's argument, without endorsing or softening it.
