# Those Who Do Not Know How to Govern!
Tamil title: தெரியாதவர்கள்!

### Image 1

5. Those Who Do Not Know!

The anklets rang out, “jan, jan.” A song burst forth in the dance-master's “weighty” voice. She danced, circling round and round!

But shouts and commotion arose among the spectators.

“Run, run, run inside,” cried one voice.

“Is that dancing, madam? Look at you dance!” came another taunt.

“Is this what you call dance?” came an angry shout from one corner.

She danced, but the people detested it. They said she was not dancing but merely “jumping.”

Yet she bore the title *Beauty of Dance*! The Beauty of Dance's performance was unbearable to watch.

Ignoring the people's protests, she danced on. The anger of those who had come to watch crossed all bounds!

That was all it took....

Stones flew; chairs broke; benches were smashed to splinters; lamps shattered to pieces; coloured lights exploded with loud bangs!

### Image 2

The dance-master slipped away; the mridangam player fled in terror; no one knew where the accompanying orchestra had gone.

Only Annam, the Beauty of Dance, was trapped there alone.

“When you do not know how to dance, should you have come out onstage?” an angry man asked.

“The fault is not mine. The hall in which I danced is crooked,” said the woman who did not know how to dance!

Everyone burst out laughing.

* * *

Though she did not know how to dance, she knew how to talk. That was why she shamelessly called the hall crooked, without the slightest thought that her listeners would laugh!

She was the Beauty of Dance. Though she could not bewitch people through dance or beauty, she tried to charm them with words!

But when the fine gentlemen who came to govern the country speak this way, we do not laugh. We grow furious!

She spoke amid entertainment. These men perform their “verbal cleverness” in a house of death!!

Those who came to govern show excessive concern for their ancient glory. Whatever the subject, they sing the same refrain: “Ah, do you know who we are? The incomparable heroes who drove out the foreigners.”

Hunger! Starvation! A food crisis. Man eats cactus! The “Honourables” say, “Eat grass; eat oilcake!”

They cannot provide rice, but they certainly produce more anger!

### Image 3

Those who advised fasting as a cure for hunger and told human beings to live like animals still move among the people!

The Congress rulers arrived and announced that the people's first feast would be “hunger.” They have progressively reduced the food ration and have now declared it to be nine ounces in our Dravidian land!

Our ministers went to Delhi seeking the boon of greater food assistance. Here is the “great assistance” they brought us: a reduction of 25 percent!

Twelve has become nine, and yet they remain in office!

Such is the condition. But the “trumpet of self-praise” they blow pierces our ears and wounds our hearts!

There was no seasonal rain.

Nature wrought havoc.

The yield was poor.

The expected quantity was not obtained.

Each offers a different reason! If nature had caused no calamity, the seasonal rain had fallen, the crops had flourished, and the expected yield had been obtained, who would have needed these men? The skill and intelligence of the “Honourables” ought to serve in times of crisis. Why would the people seek the aid of these “heroes” when all is well?

Natural calamities are not subtle calculations that people can understand only when these men explain them. They are truths known to everyone.

### Image 4

The rulers need not state them. They are known to Varadan standing beside his field and Kandan gazing longingly at the sky. Those men already voice this daily lament. If the rulers too raise the same helpless cry, it has no meaning. We did not send them there for that!

A child is crying. “Why are you crying?” asks its mother. “There is a thorn in my foot, Amma,” says the child. What will the mother do? She will look for a needle to remove the thorn. Suppose instead she delivers a sermon: “Do you know why the child is crying? While walking down the street, a thorn lying somewhere pierced its foot. Unable to bear the pain, it is crying. What can anyone do about it? Did I scatter the thorn in the street? It was lying there by itself.” What would we think of her? Yet that is precisely how our rulers speak. Pitiful!

What should they have done when signs first showed that natural calamities might occur? They should have imported food from abroad and stored it so that no shortage would arise. They did not. Worse, they had boasted that they would not import food from foreign countries.

Though they knew a flood was coming, they built no dam; now they merely speak in mournful tones!

Watching their conduct, we can only commend the experiential truth in the proverb: before disaster arrives, wisdom departs.

The Honourable Munshi, who set out proclaiming, “I shall set the food situation right,” offered a “marvellous” plan: “Plant trees, good people!”

### Image 5

The great change he has now achieved in the food situation is to turn twelve into nine. Long live his “ability”!

While people writhed in hunger, the Honourable gentleman told them to celebrate *Van Mahotsav*, as though pouring oil upon a blazing fire. Only the people's anger grew; the “trees” they planted withered away!

He failed to do what was necessary. He squandered public money on an unnecessary and disastrous scheme.

By declaring that food must not be imported from abroad, he worsened both the situation and the crisis. Nehru made the food situation terrifying by talking of self-sufficiency. Echoing him, Munshi has now taken the disastrous path of turning twelve into nine.

We do not know what these men imagine the people to be. Are they balloons that remain inflated only as long as air is blown into them, eating whatever quantity is handed out and then wandering about? Do the foodstuffs they consume not need quantity, quality, and preparation suited to their labour? If the rulers may reduce rations as they please and provide whatever food happens to be available, why should these men parade as rulers?

* * *

We are prepared to accept food wherever it is available.

Nehru says so today. It was he who earlier declared that the self-sufficiency programme would succeed in 1951 and there would thereafter be no need to import food!

We said even then that the crisis could not be resolved unless foreign food was purchased. They mocked us! Today they have been forced to say that they will accept it, not merely from this or that country, but from any corner of the world!

### Image 6

Our sorrow doubles when we calculate the losses this has caused!

We are starving. That is not all. Countries that were once ready of their own accord to supply us now say they have no food when asked. “We have it and can provide it, but the price will be a little higher,” they say with affected reluctance. Of course they will! After first telling them we did not want it and now going out of our way to ask for it, what else would they say?

The loss grows: loss of food and loss of money. Will they not mock, in the world market, the governing incompetence of rulers who stand holding a beggar's bowl? Beyond all these, therefore, there is a loss of honour!!!

They try to escape by placing the blame for their mistake upon nature.

If the woman who cannot dance says the hall is crooked, those who cannot govern say there has been a natural calamity. What difference is there between the two?

As far as Dravidian land is concerned, we argued that this posture of standing before Delhi with folded hands must end. We insisted upon obtaining the right to import food directly from foreign countries. They kept silent.

They cannot even buy coarse rice from Burma! Today, when the ration is nine ounces, they plead for the “grace” of at least ten ounces. They submit a “petition for mercy,” asking the Indian government to raise its assistance to one million tons of foodgrain.

### Image 7

When we speak, they dismiss us. Afterwards, they suffer!

She is ashamed to admit that she does not know how to dance. But would the public not create a commotion? If she says the hall is crooked, no person, however “crooked” his mind, will accept it!

To play at governing a land without authority brings ruin to the country and offers only suffering to its people. That is why we say that we can live well only when Dravida Nadu separates. They increasingly understand the meaning of this call, and the food crisis prepares the path appropriate to it!

* * *

We say that the food crisis is caused not by natural calamities but by the calamities of their rule. They reply that our only occupation is to find fault with Congress rule.

Let us step aside. Here is an economist, Professor D. R. Gadgil, Director of the Gokhale Institute of Politics and Economics. Let them listen closely to what he says. He does not belong to our camp. He says:

“India has now reached a condition in which it must stand on the world stage holding out a bowl and begging others for food, while they mock it.”

The professor paints a picture of the country's present condition and himself explains its causes.

“The government, having itself brought about this dreadful situation, now places the blame upon natural calamities, deficient rainfall, and the like!”

This is the conjuring trick of politicians.

### Image 8

The Government of India has not acted consistently in the matter of rationing.

It did not maintain an adequate reserve of food.

It was wrong to reduce food imports.

The claims of self-sufficiency and foreign-exchange convenience are absurd.

The government failed to observe the situation.

Thus the professor, an expert in economics, levels charge after charge against the government!

He says the government's error caused the food crisis. That is three-quarters of the truth. To put it briefly and clearly, one may say with certainty that this chaos exists because power fell into the hands of people who do not know how to govern!

Without saying it directly, he calls them people who do not know how to govern. He lists what competent rulers ought to have done and says that today's rulers did none of it.

The present food crisis did not suddenly arise in a single day. Even before natural calamities such as the Assam earthquake occurred, there were signs that famine was coming. The government should have paid attention. It did not. It should have assessed the situation, increased imports, and expanded its stocks. It did not. Surpluses from surplus provinces could never fully satisfy the needs of deficit provinces. It should have understood this and acted. It did not. A government willing and able to import luxury goods and marble for buildings handled food supplies wrongly. It should have corrected itself. It did not.

### Image 9

Why did it not? Because it did not know how; because it did not know the art of governing!

In the villages there is a saying: “The man who does not know how to reap carries fifty-eight sickles at his waist.” In the hands of those who do not know how to govern lie rule, government, justice, administration, money... everything!

The Beauty of Dance blamed the hall. Congress rule places the blame upon nature.

As though she had discovered a truth unknown to every person gathered there, she said the hall was crooked. As though she alone had learned it, unwilling to admit the actual truth that she did not know how to dance, she sought a lame excuse. Congress rulers likewise seek a “deceptive” explanation.

She did not know how to dance. These people do not know how to govern!

(*Dravida Nadu*, 28 January 1951)

---NOTES---
The title is deliberately elliptical in Tamil: “Those Who Do Not Know!” The concluding contrast supplies the full political sense, “Those Who Do Not Know How to Govern.”

`கனம்` was a period form of address for an “Honourable” minister. The translation preserves Anna's sarcastic quotation marks rather than treating it as sincere praise.

The source's `வன மகோற்சவம்` refers to *Van Mahotsav*, the tree-planting festival associated with K. M. Munshi. The name is transliterated because the article criticizes that specific programme.

The paired idioms `பிகுவு` and `பிட்சா பாத்திரம்` convey affected reluctance and a mendicant's begging bowl respectively. They are rendered contextually without softening the source's ridicule.
