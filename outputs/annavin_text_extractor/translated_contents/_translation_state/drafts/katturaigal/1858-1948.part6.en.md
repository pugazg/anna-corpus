# 1858-1948
Tamil title: 1858-1948

## Part 6

### Image 1

To think, "We must never separate; if we separate, we must become enemies," is neither wisdom nor a practical truth of politics. Once the desire to separate has arisen, compelling people to remain united is the greater danger. It is a path that cultivates hatred, a vessel of poison that such a country, through our obstinacy, leaves behind for later generations.

Burma's total population is 17 million. Burma, with fewer than two crore people, is an independent country. Since when? Since 1937.

Burma was one of the territories the English joined and cut apart after entering the Indian landmass. British power gradually grew in Burma. In 1826 Burma was joined to India and brought under the Delhi government. For 110 years, from 1826 to 1937, Burma was ruled by the "Government of India." No one here considered this unnecessary or unjust. People rejoiced that Burma too was part of Bharatavarsha.

Even that is not surprising. No one even in Burma thought, "We have lost our separate government and been joined to India." In 1937 Burma separated. Today they have become Burmese once again. To use the old picture, the loose end of the goddess's sari is gone; Aung San possesses that piece of cloth. Pandit Nehru is not angered by the sight. The two are excellent friends.

Just as Burma was a province of the Indian government, it also held a place in the All-India Congress. Just as every province now has a Congress committee, Burma had a Provincial Congress Committee. Congress members in other regions greeted leaders arriving from Burma with the same enthusiastic joy with which they greet the Frontier Gandhi today. Buddhist monks in Burma worked actively in the Congress movement,

### Image 2

went to prison, and became prominent leaders known as Burmese patriots. Senior Congressmen will remember. Such was the intimacy created in Congress's name.

The Burma Congress and its parent body, the All-India Congress, agitated on the position that Burma must not separate, Bharatavarsha must not be partitioned, the proposal was a British conspiracy, and they must not fall victim to it. Later, step by step, Burma's union with India came to be described as a conspiracy that consumed Burma's wealth. The very people in Burma who had supported Congress began to say, "Burma belongs to the Burmese; this alone is true nationalism," and supported separation.

Burma separated. The All-India Congress does not now demand that it be reunited with India. It possesses no such intention.

Even a survey of several provinces shows that this joining and cutting is neither new nor something that should make us tremble at the very mention of it. Under the new plan, are not the Frontier Province, Punjab, and Sindh together to form Section B? What does the Frontier Gandhi say? "What! Shall the Frontier be placed under Punjab's domination? What injustice! My brave Pathans will never desire it."

But do the Pathans truly desire what the Frontier Gandhi claims? There is no sign of it. The available evidence points in the opposite direction. Even nationalist newspapers report that groups of Pathans are "invading" Punjab. Why? To assist the Muslims there.

The Frontier Gandhi is alarmed at the prospect of Punjab and the Frontier being joined, and nationalist comrades here are alarmed with him. But consider a little history. Through the British method of "joining and cutting," Punjab and the Frontier remained one province until 1901. Curzon separated the Frontier into its own province for military convenience.

### Image 3

Nor is that all. History in many northern regions resembles railway carriages left separately on a line and coupled at different times to different engines. Whichever direction the attached engine travels, the carriages follow.

When Mahmud of Ghazni conquered northern India, he separated the conquered territory from India and ruled it from Ghazni, his capital. The territory then included what are now the Frontier and Punjab. Until then it had been joined to the Indian landmass; it was cut away and joined to the kingdom of Ghazni. That joining was later cut.

Muhammad of Ghor ruled the same territory first from Lahore and later from Delhi, rather than from Ghazni. At that time it was again joined to India.

Under British domination, the same region remained a single province joined to India from 1849 to 1901.

Until a few years ago Sindh was joined to Bombay. During that period several attempts were made to join Sindh with Punjab. The two regions are neighbours, and people of the same ethnicity formed, and still form, the majority in both. A river itself provides a natural connection between Punjab and Sindh.

Why should regions with such convenient links, including the Frontier and Punjab areas that remained one province for so long, be denied a place under Pakistan's flag?

The British later altered many of the unions they had created for administrative convenience and established new arrangements for that same reason. It was they who joined Burma to India.

### Image 4

That unnecessary act later brought danger, not to the British but to our own people. The Burmese knife struck not the white man, but the white man's Chettiars. In 1937 the British cut apart the same Burma they had once joined. Between those acts Burmese blood and Tamil blood were shed.

A general truth emerges from these varied events. The coercive method of forcing regions that cannot unite to remain together kindles not friendship but hatred. Good counsel cannot extinguish that hatred. When hatred matures, the animal sleeping within man emerges and man becomes enslaved to animal feeling. There in Noakhali, Bihar, Punjab, and the Frontier, the human animal spills fresh blood. The foreigner watches. What will he think?

If illness causes a body to swell, is that bodily growth? If a blood clot protrudes from the body, would only a fool say, "We have acquired a new lump of flesh that others do not possess; therefore it is a gain"? In the same way, the systems of one India, central government, and provinces constructed by imperialism for its own comfortable existence are neither a gain nor a source of strength for us.

Even if regions so ruined that they cannot unite are our ancestral property, we must let them go. We must protect and enrich what we possess: the place that remains united and gives confidence that unity can be maintained, thereby giving its people a life. We urgently require this political wisdom now.

The propaganda conducted until now both against and in support of partition can only have aroused hatred.

### Image 5

Platforms resounded with tales of each people's heroic prowess, the glory of crowned ancestors, victories over enemies, forts built, and flags flown. The ember became a fire. That is why ordinary people in the North must now suffer beyond endurance.

Had Pakistan been treated as Burma was - opposed to some extent and then accepted - today's disorder would not have occurred. A Jinnah-Gandhi friendship could have arisen like the Aung San-Nehru embrace. Even now, there is no need to abandon hope on the ground that the situation has been completely ruined.

Whether knowingly or unknowingly, amid all this turmoil the fundamental question is being resolved. The issue is no longer whether Pakistan should be granted. It has become a question of size: whether all Punjab and all Bengal should lie within Pakistan.

It is proposed that one part of Punjab must be cut away...

### Image 6

for the sake of the Sikhs. What proportion of India's total population do you think those Sikhs constitute? One in a hundred. A separate political right is deemed necessary for the welfare of this minority.

The differences between Muslim and Sikh need not even be as great as those between Hindu and Muslim. Neither Muslims nor Sikhs practise image worship. Neither possesses caste or clan divisions. History, however, records wars fought between them.

To protect the welfare and rights of Sikhs, who form one person in a hundred, it is proposed that the part of Punjab where they form the majority be separated and joined to a Hindu-inhabited region. If this appears necessary, what was wrong when Muslims, who form more than thirty per cent of the total population, asked that their own government should operate in the places where they are the majority? Why was partition opposed until hatred between the two peoples rose so high?

While coming forward with such concern to protect the rights of one person in a hundred, why must the more than four crore Dravidian people alone be joined to the North like orphaned children admitted to an institution? They governed themselves until the British rifle arrived; they differ in language and culture and possess ancient greatness and literary distinction.

Why should Dravidam be given in adoption to northern commerce? Our voice may lack strength, but does it lack justice? Is there no place here for fairness? On what ground do Dravidian comrades today believe they must remain in Congress and retain an all-India connection? Is Dravidam a directionless child with no one to seek it? Must it perform menial work behind whoever calls it? Dravidian comrades in Congress must consider these questions.

How easily Congress has declared that one part of Punjab must be separated. Why do Dravidian Congress comrades lack that courage?

Let us now examine the subtle purpose contained in the demand to divide Punjab.

Sikhs believe that division will enable them to avoid Muslim rule. Hindus believe that division will reduce the territory under Muslim rule while enlarging the Hindu region.

### Image 7

Another problem lies hidden within. It will emerge later: what relationship should exist between Hindus and Sikhs in the divided Punjab?

If Punjab remains one undivided province, Muslims will receive the first honour, but Sikhs will hold second place. In both numbers and historical importance, the Sikhs rank next to Muslims in Punjab.

If Punjab is divided and its detached part joined to the United Provinces or to Section A, Sikh influence will be smaller. Sikh leaders may take some time to understand this, but they will understand it.

It is said that Congress's demand to divide Punjab is intended to make the League grieve: "Has this fate not arisen because we demanded India's partition?" Congress supposedly hopes the League will then abandon its demand for Pakistan. League leaders who understand political strategy will instead use this situation to their own advantage.

Congressmen once cried in alarm, "The Leaguers demand that the country be cut apart!" and made the world feel disgust toward the League. Today that disgust is being removed, with Congress itself leading the work. It has entered upon this course without concern for the consequences.

Will the world now say that Muslims alone demand partition? They at least seek to divide a great subcontinent, though this was denounced as a great sin. Congressmen now cut apart even a single province.

Dividing the provinces as Congress demands is not impossible. Although the League would suffer some loss,

### Image 8

one cannot say that League leaders skilled in statecraft will reject it outright.

The question has passed beyond whether Pakistan will or will not exist. It has reached the stage of asking whether Pakistan will be this large or that large.

Even if its size is somewhat reduced, Leaguers will henceforth work with the conviction that their ideal of political rights has triumphed.

By losing one portion of Punjab, the League gains the opportunity to govern the greater part of Punjab exclusively, while also reducing its minority problem. In one respect, this is a gain.

To show how politically astute people have acted on such occasions, we offer a historical example.

The League's plan is that one part of India should become Pakistan. Congress's present plan is that one part of Pakistan should be detached and Pakistan's size reduced.

Similarly, a movement arose to turn one part of Ireland into a separate state called Ulster. Its leader was Sir Edward Carson.

Ireland itself is a small country. Should a region comprising roughly one quarter of it be separated and made an independent state? "No Ulster!" opponents declared against Carson's plan.

Opposition did not extinguish the partition movement.

They then stirred opposition within Ulster itself. Ulster comprised nine counties, three of which resisted inclusion in the Ulster polity.

This became a perplexing problem for Carson.

### Image 9

To obtain Ulster, he had to lose three counties. If he resolved that not one of the three could be lost, the establishment of an independent Ulster would be jeopardized. What was to be done?

Sir Edward Carson wavered between ideal and practicality. Then he reached a courageous decision: he consented to lose the three counties that refused to join Ulster. Ulster was obtained. Even today, it is the dream of Ulster that disturbs de Valera's sleep.

Janab Jinnah today faces a problem like the one that confronted Sir Carson. Even his opponents recognize Jinnah's political judgement. We can therefore understand, to some extent, what may occur.

Whether Pakistan is reduced in size or obtained in the full measure demanded, the idea of one India has disintegrated.

Under these circumstances, we ask: why should we, why should Dravidam, retain a connection with the North?
