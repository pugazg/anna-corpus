In other words, Muslims have dared to abandon the places where they were born, raised, and lived in order to live among Muslims, while Hindus have done so in order to live among Hindus. Two racial circles are taking shape.

Congress, which had long declared, "We shall never consent to this ruinous plan to partition the country. Nowhere has anyone heard of such a method of demanding a separate state. Who would agree to it? Shall Mother India be cut apart? Shall a cow be slaughtered? Shall a child be dismembered?" - speaking with anger and grief - passed a resolution in its Working Committee last week. What resolution? Something unbelievable! No one could have expected such a resolution from Congress:

"The province of Punjab must be divided into two parts."

So says the Congress Working Committee. The Indian landmass is a subcontinent extending more than a thousand miles from east to west and from north to south. To govern all of it under a single umbrella is neither right nor proper, and would not be efficient. India should therefore not be described as one government. In each territory, government should be constituted by whichever people form the overall majority there.

Viewed in that manner, Muslims form the majority across the territory comprising the North-West Frontier Province, Punjab, Sindh, Baluchistan, Bengal, and Assam. Janab Jinnah therefore demanded that this become "Pakistan." His Pakistan consists of two separate territories, one in the west and one in the east. A corridor would be required between them if they were to live as one. The "Dravidian Nation" we demand faces no such difficulty. Nature itself has provided the Vindhyas as its boundary.

### Image 2

When a national state was demanded in this fashion, Congress cursed those who sought it. Yet now Congress says that not merely the vast landmass, but even Punjab, a single province within it, must be divided in two. This shows that even one province may be broken into two if necessity arises, and that Congress has now accepted this principle.

If Congress considers it necessary to divide Punjab for convenience, consciousness of rights, or protection, does it not follow that a plan to create divisions in India so that national states may arise for similar or other reasons is eminently appropriate?

There was a time when Congress spoke of India with intense devotion. When the British devised a plan to divide Bengal, Congress fought against the partition. Gone are the days when it advised, "Let Janab Jinnah rule the whole country if need be, but do not partition it." Those who opposed partition now demand that a province be split into two. What a marvellous transformation of mind!

It is said that Hindu and Sikh people can live safely only if Punjab is split into one region where Hindus and Sikhs are the majority and another where Muslims are the majority. If Punjab remains one province, majority rule will produce a Muslim government. Sikhs and Hindus cannot live under it, they say; hardship will result. The Working Committee therefore demands that the region in which Sikhs and Hindus form the majority be separated. It cites the communal riots in Punjab as the reason.

Janab Jinnah must be smiling to himself,

Congress proposes to separate these districts from Punjab and join them to the United Provinces. It says that the Hindus in these districts cannot be left under the League ministry that would be formed in Punjab.

Congress has therefore accepted that Punjab will have a League ministry. Earlier it said something different: "The League has no influence in Punjab; only a non-League ministry can be formed there." It is now clear that Congress itself has recognized that government in Punjab will fall to the League. It consequently declares that regions with large Hindu and Sikh populations cannot be left under League rule.

But Muslims will remain in several provinces governed by Hindus. Congress refuses to consider what answer should be given if they too demand separate regions within their provinces. Nor is that all. Although non-Muslims form the majority in the territory Congress wishes to detach from Punjab, Muslims are also present there in significant numbers. Muslims in such great numbers are expected to submit to Hindu rule, and are said to consent to it. Under the present arrangement, Janab Jinnah himself must live not in Pakistan but in Bombay. Even so, Congress advances to the point of breaking every Muslim province into pieces on the ground that non-Muslims must not live under Muslim rule.

#### Why Should We Be Drawn into the North's Troubles?

Thus plans are now being prepared to break the provinces into pieces in Punjab, Sindh, and Bengal. The new plan of the very men who once asked, in voices mingling fury and lament, "Partition India?" no longer merely rejects Pakistan; it seeks to mutilate and diminish it.

Whether this will lead to the conclusion that the rule of one people is utterly intolerable to another can be considered later. The lesson already obtained is that there is no longer any place for the fantasy of one government. And is that all? The northern situation is being drawn into this poisonous vortex of partition within partition, and miniature provinces within provinces.

### Image 6

The South, which has no need whatever to be caught in this vortex, is forced into it merely because it has been placed in Section A. Is this good? Is it necessary for us? Must we become embroiled in northern quarrels and lose our prosperity?

Why should we not say: "Resolve your territorial disputes by whatever method you choose. Dravidam is naturally separate, capable of living independently, and need not become a breeding ground for disputes in which lives are at stake. Do not place it in Section A and entangle us in your quarrels. We wish to live separately and shall constitute ourselves as an independent state"?

Why should our Congress comrades not prevent the South from being caught and ruined in the dreadful vortex arising in the North? This is no mere question and no rebuttal from an opposition party. It is the appeal addressed to Dravidian comrades in Congress by every Dravidian youth who has lost sleep in anxiety over the future.

Friends! Resolve today that, for the country to recover and for us to live, Dravidam must become an independent state. We want no bond with Section A.

The condition, history, and mentality of the northern people have become such that quarrels and coercion flourish. The League began by saying that the country must not be ruled by one people and that it required a state of its own. Today, in the North, Congress presses a plan to divide province after province.

The League does not participate in the Constituent Assembly. Britain says the Assembly's decision cannot be implemented without the League. Assam refuses to participate in the meetings of Sections B and C. Congress leaders in the Frontier and the Sikhs of Punjab also refuse. The sectional meetings therefore do not convene; and even if they meet and devise plans, Britain says those plans cannot be imposed on regions that did not participate.

This difficulty grows and matures,

### Image 7

until the leaders themselves are bewildered and see no path forward. Choosing precisely that moment, the British say: "We shall remain only sixteen more months and then leave. Reach some decision before then; otherwise we shall establish whatever arrangement seems expedient to us and depart."

This may be called intimidation, or even mockery. When every proposed arrangement has already intensified mutual bitterness and hostility, it can be maintained only through force. There is no other way. And the brief but terrifying name for that is civil war. Is the country's present degradation not enough? What does civil war mean? Is it not the condition of China, now being ruined?

Is this what all that "sacrifice" is to be used for? If such a condition arises, what can happen except that the country once again becomes the hunting ground of great powers? Instead of withdrawing from this trouble, why entangle Dravidam, which possesses every facility, right, and necessity for independence? Our already diminished land will lose all form and disintegrate if we are forcibly bound to the North. Why should our Congress friends not recognize this in advance?

#### Krishna-Karim Is Not Enough!

We have shown the condition toward which they are dragging the North, already turned into a land of disorder. Walking through the fields and narrow paths of Noakhali, Gandhi added two new lines to the familiar hymn "Raghupati Raghava Raja Ram, Patita Pavana Sita Ram":

"Ishwar Allah Tero Nam,
Sabko Sanmati De Bhagavan."

The meaning is: "O God! Ishwar and Allah are both Your names. Grant all of us sound understanding and guide us along the right path." There is also another song:

### Image 8

"Bhaj man pyare, Ram-Rahim;
Bhaj man pyare, Krishna-Karim."

Ram-Rahim! Krishna-Karim!

Gandhi thus presents them joined together. Is this a new method? No. Teachers of reconciliation have emphasized the same point for a very long time. No one has denied that although people worship under many different names, God is one. Nor has anyone failed to draw attention to it in times of crisis.

But such teachings of reconciliation have never, in any age, dispelled the suspicions arising between two peoples in the political, economic, and social spheres. The dispute did not begin over whether the name is Rama or Rahim. Are not the real questions Congress or League, imperial rule or two governments? The adhesive spell "Krishna-Karim" is not enough to resolve them.

Gandhi gave courage to Hindus, showed a path toward Hindu-Muslim unity, and asked Hindus not to leave Bengal. Now he has gone to Bihar to console Muslims and ask them not to leave Bihar, while advising Bihar's Hindus to behave in a manner that gives Muslims courage and confidence. Punjab too offers Gandhi work.

Would it be responsible and loving to keep assigning him forever the task of applying medicine to wounds? Why does such a condition continue to grow there? The northern environment is a major cause. We ask you to consider calmly why our land should be joined to that region and ruined. An explanation and a path will appear only when thought is allowed to move beyond party bonds.

We make an appeal to our Dravidian comrades in Congress. Friends! You said: "India is one nation. Its rule must therefore be constituted as one government. It is wrong to speak of North and South within it. We require all-India politics, and so we must remain in the All-India Congress."

### Image 9

Your argument may have been valid until yesterday. What is the position today? Many parties, including Congress, have abandoned the principle of one government. If the League's plan succeeds, the Congress system of government will apply only to the regions excluding the Frontier, Punjab, Sindh, Bengal, and Assam.

Even under the new Congress plan intended to weaken the League's proposal, the Frontier and Assam will stand separately, one part of Punjab separately, one part of Sindh separately, and one part of Bengal separately. Can this caricature be called "All India"? Is this Bharatavarsha?

Whichever of these two plans succeeds, the land that results will not be the ideal territory you envisioned when joining Congress. It will not extend from the Himalayas in the north to Kanyakumari in the south. The boundary of that ideal land has changed.

"Upon the banks of the Indus,
With a maiden of the noble Chera land,
Singing melodies in beautiful Telugu,
We shall launch our boats and play."

There is no path by which this poet's dream can become reality, whether events follow the League plan or the Congress plan, because there is no longer an all-India political scheme. Congress has agreed that the country may be divided into Sections A, B, and C and governments formed accordingly.

All of Congress's present efforts are not directed toward abolishing Sections A, B, and C, but toward determining how much the two Muslim sections, B and C, can be reduced. Whether Congress will reduce them, or remain obstinate and then yield at the last moment as it has on many other questions, we do not know. Whatever happens, it is now established that there is no whole India, no single India.

Once this is clear, Congress friend, why should we still retain a connection with the All-India Congress? It is a somewhat uncomfortable question,

### Image 10

but as the days pass it will inevitably stir thought.

Once it has become clear that an all-India political scheme cannot be created, what reason can comrades in the South give for claiming that association with the All-India Congress remains necessary? Think about it without anger.

Do not ask, "Are you playing Punnagavarali and calling upon everyone to join the Dravidar Kazhagam immediately?" Friends, remain a separate party if you wish; we find no fault in that. We ask only this: after the all-India political scheme has collapsed, why do you remain connected with the All-India Congress? What benefit does it bring?

Perhaps it brings no benefit. You might console yourselves by saying that affection has not released its hold. But the connection not only lacks benefit; it causes harm. Why retain such a relationship? Is there any reason? There may have been one before, friend. But today, after all-India politics has ceased to exist and after the country has become A, B, and C? Consider it.

Do not imagine that a net is being cast. I merely draw your attention to the subtle point stated by Periyar, the leader of the Dravidar Kazhagam, on many platforms: "If the connection with the North is severed, my comrades and I are prepared to join the Congress of the South."

Do not therefore regard this as an attempt to recruit people into a party. I say it because the unnecessary and profitless party division among us must end.

What is now taking place in the North is the Congress working to reduce the Muslim Sections B and C, and the League working to preserve B and C according to its plan. Neither side is working to prevent the country from being divided into the three Sections A, B, and C. That effort has already been attempted and abandoned. What is now taking place there...
