மாணவ மாணிக்கங்களின் இந்தி எதிர்ப்பு மாணவர்
மாநாட்டில் முதலமைச்சர் டாக்டர் அறிஞர் அண்ணா
அவர்கள் ஆற்றிய உரை :

இந்தி எதிர்ப்பு மாணவர் மாநாடு இங்கு நடைபெறுகிறது.
இதற்கு ஏராளமாக மக்களும் - குறிப்பாகத் தாய்மார்களும்
வந்திருக்கிறார்கள். மாணவர்களின் மனநிலை எப்படியிருக்கிறது
என்று அறியத்தான் அவர்கள் இங்கு வருகை தந்திருக்கிறார்கள்.

இந்த மாநாட்டில் மாணவர்கள் கூடிப் பல்வேறு
பிரச்சினைகள் பற்றி ஆராய்ந்து பல தீர்மானங்களைப்
போட்டிருக்கிறார்கள்.

இந்த மாணவர்களுக்கும் எனக்குமுள்ள தொடர்பும்,
தோழமையும், பற்றும் பாசமும் எப்படிப்பட்டது என்பதை
நிரூபிக்கும் வகையில் - அவர்கள் நிறைவேற்றிய தீர்மானங்களை
எங்களிடம் ஒப்படைத்து, உணர்ச்சிவயப்பட்டு நாங்கள்
ஒருவேளை தீர்மானம் போட்டிருக்கலாம், “அதைத் திருத்தித்
தாருங்கள்” என்று ஒப்படைத்திருக்கிறார்கள். அந்த அளவுக்கு
என்மீது அளவற்ற நம்பிக்கை கொண்ட மாணவர்களுக்கு என்
நன்றி.

நானும் மாணவனாக இருந்தவன், அவர்களும் என்
நிலைக்கு உயர வேண்டியவர்கள்.

மாணவர்கள் என்ன நினைப்பார்கள் என்பதை என்னால்
உணர முடியும்.

## English Translation

The address delivered by Chief Minister Dr. Arignar Anna at the Anti-Hindi Students’ Conference of the “student gems,” held at Kodaikanal on 3 June 1967:

An Anti-Hindi Students’ Conference is being held here. Large numbers of people, mothers in particular, have come. They have come to learn what the students’ state of mind is.

At this conference, students have assembled, examined various issues, and passed several resolutions.

In a manner demonstrating the relationship, comradeship, attachment, and affection between these students and me, they have handed their resolutions to us and said, “We may perhaps have passed them under the influence of emotion; please revise them.” I thank the students who place such boundless trust in me.

I too was once a student, while they are people who must rise to the position I now occupy.

I can understand what students think.

But it will take students another ten years to understand what those who bear responsibilities like mine think. I greatly respect the feeling they have expressed.

That feeling is like iron heated red-hot in a forge.

Only when iron is heated red-hot can it be fashioned into a spear or sword.

In the resolution they have passed today, I see iron heated red-hot and throwing off sparks.

If you observe a forge carefully, after beating red-hot iron and shaping it into a sword or spear, they immerse it in water and cool it. Only then can it be taken in hand.

The students’ resolutions are red-hot iron.

The public shape them into swords and spears.

My task is to immerse them in water and cool them.

I respect the students’ emotion, and at the same time I cool it.

Only then do we obtain the sickle that reaps the harvest, the axe that splits wood, and the implements that serve a cart.

In one resolution, the students said that unless Hindi was removed from the pedestal of official language and all fourteen languages were made official languages, they would conduct an armed revolution on 25 January 1968.

That is iron forged while red-hot. By revising it, I cool it.

Moreover, we do not agree with violence. We have never supported it, and we shall not support it in the future.

Nor is violence something undertaken in this manner after being planned in advance. This resolution is therefore inappropriate.

If students say, “We shall wage an armed struggle in 1969,” and our journalist friends print it in the newspapers, the students will not even be left free until that year.

I have therefore replaced “violence” with “non-violent struggle.”

The students must give the corrected resolution to the newspapers and the people.

The feeling that Tamil is being neglected filled the students with courage.

They desired change. The DMK government has been formed as the written expression of that desire.

The students said that if any harm befell that government, they would rise with a roar and protect it.

If the students of Tamil Nadu encounter any impediment or hardship because of their linguistic feeling, the Tamil Nadu government will certainly protect them.

It was because of that conviction that, immediately after assuming office, the DMK released the thousands who had been prosecuted, sentenced to rigorous imprisonment, and languishing in prison during the Anti-Hindi agitation. It withdrew the cases. This brought relief to two thousand families.

We thank Law Minister Madhavan, whose legal knowledge assisted this good action.

Another resolution passed at this conference calls for a good position to be given to Ilakkuvanar, who lost his employment during the Anti-Hindi agitation.

## Ilakkuvanar Brings Honour to a College

Ilakkuvanar is a renowned and eminent professor. Whichever college employs him gains honour from his presence. It was Ilakkuvanar who translated the *Tolkappiyam* into English and enabled the world to know its greatness.

He lost his job during the Anti-Hindi agitation. I must admit that I feel some regret that you have passed a resolution asking that he be given a good position.

Would we fail to do even this? Do you think that even this must be told to us before we act?

To tell the truth, we decided two months ago that Ilakkuvanar should be given a good position.

Nevertheless, passing such a resolution may demonstrate your cleverness.

The students may celebrate their claim by saying, “We passed a resolution at Kodaikanal, and Ilakkuvanar received a good position.”

Whatever else may be said, am I not your elder brother?

I too am not surrendering my claim. We had already made our decision about it.

Although the Anti-Hindi movement has operated since 1937 under several names, including the Self-Respect Movement and the Tamil Protection Association, we made the world realise that Tamils have continuously opposed Hindi.

At that time, students did not assemble in this manner and support us. Indeed, we did not even imagine that students would one day support us like this.

Many students then thought that one could become a nationalist only by studying Hindi.

## Does Studying Hindi Make One a Nationalist?

That was because Hindi was then proclaimed the sole national language. It was assumed that only one who studied that national language was a nationalist.

Gradually we opposed that idea and struggled to establish that Hindi alone was not the national language and that other languages possessed the same status. After that struggle, the other languages too received the status of national languages.

Those who carried on the opposition movement from 1937 onward were the reason for such a change. They would certainly feel proud on seeing the anti-Hindi feeling now shown by students. I too feel that same pride.

During that first Anti-Hindi struggle, Periyar declared me its first “dictator,” and I was imprisoned during Rajagopalachari’s administration. After the DMK won a great victory in the recent election, I mentioned this when I met Rajagopalachari.

I said to him in jest, “It was you who first sent me to prison; now it is you who have sent us into office.”

Because the Anti-Hindi movement grew in this way, even Nehru, who had once spoken of Hindi alone as the national language, later called Hindi the official language. A short while before his death, he argued that Hindi was a common language. Hindi was lowered from national language to official language and then to the status of common language.

The Anti-Hindi movement and its struggles were the cause.

Among those Anti-Hindi struggles, we witnessed an uprising by students who made sacrifices we may recall again and again with pride.

I convey my respectful salutations to the student community for fighting without regard for the losses, hardships, and suffering it endured.

## The Country Knows They Fell to Bullets

The country knows that while students were protesting, they were hunted like wild beasts and shot so that they collapsed in the street; that conditions reached a point at which one might ask whether the law existed or had died; that the agitation rose beyond even this; and that, on seeing it, linguistic zealots relaxed their obstinacy.

During that struggle, Union Minister Satyanarayan Sinha was visiting Chennai. From the home of a Member of Parliament, he telephoned me and said that he wished to meet me. I replied, “Very well, come.”

But in a trembling voice he said, “I am wearing a Gandhi cap. If I come outside and they decide that I am a northerner, it may end dangerously. It would therefore be better if you came here.” Accordingly, I went and met him.

He said, “I did not know there was opposition to Hindi on this scale. I shall tell the Prime Minister about it.” I believe he did so. Likewise, when I spoke in Delhi about opposition to Hindi, Indira Gandhi asked, “Is there this much opposition?”

Thus, they have reached the conclusion that Hindi can no longer be imposed. Not only that, they are gradually relaxing the idea that English can be removed altogether.

It is now said that a resolution will come before Parliament stating that English should continue for as long as others desire it. I can comment only after seeing the nature of that legislation. When I meet you again, I shall give you my view of it.

The feeling shown by the students here has proved that opposition to Hindi has not faded.

## A Good Future

The concern students show for the language gives us firm confidence that Tamil has a good future. The agitation conducted by the students has not gone to waste. The benefit and fruit of that agitation has come to the DMK government.

I am pleased that the students said they would stand beside that government if others caused it difficulty.

When members of other parties denounce the DMK government, you are here to protect it.

Merely performing this police duty of protection is not enough. Students must also serve as emissaries who explain this government’s plans to the people. You must use your time, thought, means, and opportunities for this purpose.

Apart from this, I have not yet formulated a proper plan for the manner in which students should be engaged.

I shall announce it when it is ready. At that time, I shall expect your complete cooperation in full.

## Translator's Notes

The source is a seven-page published address delivered at an Anti-Hindi Students’ Conference in Kodaikanal on 3 June 1967. All Tamil OCR pages remain verbatim.

The title uses the linked metaphor “மாணவமணிகளின் மாணிக்கத் தொண்டு,” describing students as gems and their service as gem-like or precious. “The Precious Service of Student Gems” preserves both parts without forcing a literal English repetition.

The source first dates the proposed armed revolution to 25 January 1968 and shortly afterward refers to students saying they would fight in 1969. Both dates are translated as printed and are not silently harmonised.

“அறப்போர்” is rendered as “non-violent struggle” in the immediate contrast with “பலாத்காரம்,” violence. In other contexts the term may also mean a righteous or moral struggle.

The title “முதல் சர்வாதிகாரி” belongs to the historical organisation of the agitation and is therefore retained in quotation marks as “first dictator”; it does not describe governmental dictatorship in this passage.

Political, linguistic, legal, biographical, numerical, and historical assertions are translated in the speaker’s voice and have not been independently verified.
