## English Translation

The presidential address delivered by C. N. Annadurai, M.A., upon receiving Rs 7,011 as a DMK election-fund contribution from Dravidian women at the Madras beach on 9 January 1957:

“A Legislative Assembly is not greater than the hearts of the people. The office of a member of the Assembly is certainly not more honourable than the exalted place the movement's members have won in the people's hearts.

“A comrade who contributed to the election fund here said that, along with giving a few rupee notes to the DMK fund, they would give us their votes as well. People give both notes and votes to a movement. Can anyone point to any other party, not only anywhere in India but anywhere in the world, to which people give as they give to our movement?

“But the money possessed by Congress, as I have often said, is like butter held in the hand of a person with leprosy. Their money may even carry the fragrance of rosewater. The money we possess, given by pavement vendors, barbers, and washerfolk, carries the smell of sweat.

“I wish to remind Congress that history tells us it is money touched by sweat that has defeated money scented with rosewater.

“If Congress has the courage, let it try distributing money for votes a week before the election. Honourable people will not accept that money. Even those who do accept it may hold a meeting like this on the beach the very next day and hand it over to me, saying, ‘I give you this money that Congress paid for my vote.’ Just wait and see whether that happens.

“To the Congress members who imagine that votes can be bought with money, I say this: we are not fools. We may have no money; we may be poor, but we are not fools. Remember that our comrades will photograph you giving money. Whether or not those photographs can be used to have the Congress election declared invalid, Congress must bear in mind that we shall publish them in many newspapers and expose to the town and the world the manner in which Congress obtains votes.

“Today, the mothers of the Dravidian people have honoured the movement by contributing to it. The mothers who possess the greatness and generosity of having given us to ourselves now also give us funds and assist our work. This is not merely a matter of pride; it merits our profound gratitude.

“At election-fund events of this kind throughout the country, we see the appearance of a great festival. That spectacle of the people's awakening gives us delight and confidence. If the people continue to display the eagerness, enthusiasm, and awakening they show now, one day we shall certainly celebrate a victory festival on this very beach.

“The mothers gave one thousand rupees toward the election fund, while many others contributed according to the extent of their efforts. Contributions are presented in this manner on only two kinds of occasion: one event takes place in the home and the other in the country. At weddings, the relatives and friends of the bride and groom record their *moi*, their ceremonial gifts. That takes place in the home.

“What takes place in the country is the contribution being presented here. When people realise that their country is in danger, they assist people like us in overcoming that danger.

“In former times, when the country faced danger, each home would send one warrior. In this age, because one warrior from every home is not enough, the people also give funds to help those warriors participate in the work of liberation.

“Barbers contributed funds here. Hair is cut to remove an unattractive appearance from the face. With the funds they have given, we shall remove the ugliness present in the government.

“Washerfolk contributed funds. Clothes are washed to remove the dirt upon them. With the funds they have given, we shall remove the dirt that has settled upon the politics of Tamil Nadu.

“Transport workers, pavement vendors, and cooperative-society workers have also contributed. They have given in a manner that demonstrates their character, dignity, and courageous spirit.

“I regard this as an advance announcement of the victory we are going to attain. On behalf of the movement, I thank them all.

“I have travelled throughout the country. I find no one asking why we are standing for election. There are only those who pity us and say, ‘They are contesting the election, poor things, but they have no money.’

“There are a few people who did not want us to contest. Some among them now want us to stand, but those few are embittered people whose hearts have been scorched by seeing our growth. Let them say whatever they like about us. At this auspicious moment, let us forget them.

“Our comrades and supporters still have much to accomplish, but very little time remains.

“Comrades and people must not forget one thing: winning a victory does not necessarily require fighting for a very long period or using enormous and terrifying weapons in that struggle.

“We see a lean but technically skilled wrestler defeat another regarded as a great mountain of flesh. Congress may indeed be a huge mountain of flesh, but that does not mean the movement must fear that enormous mountain of money.

“If Congress members think they can win this election with money, they must realise that we shall defeat that effort.

“Next, we hear that Congress plans to bring Pandit Nehru here and gain publicity. Should they not be ashamed to do such a thing?

“If every member of our movement is an ordinary person, why must they bring so eminent a man as the Pandit here? Whom are the members of our movement going to parade before the people while asking for votes? It is we who moved among the people yesterday and today, and we shall ask for their votes by speaking tomorrow exactly as we speak today.

“Very well, when the Pandit comes, which of the conditions here will he conceal from the people's eyes? Which can he conceal?

“Will the Pandit hide from the people the poverty here, the torment of unemployment, and the suffering of those in the slums? Will he claim that the government's repressive conduct and authoritarian zeal do not exist? Will he deny the cruelty of prices rising like poison? What will he say does not exist? What new achievements will he show as the work of Congress? Let Congress reflect upon this once before inviting the Pandit.”

## Translator's Notes

The event title “மகளிர் நிதியளிப்பு” refers to women presenting an election-fund contribution. The source states a total of Rs 7,011 and later says the mothers contributed one thousand rupees; these figures are retained as distinct statements without attempting to reconcile their scopes.

The comparison “குஷ்டரோகியின் கையிலிருக்கும் வெண்ணெய்” uses stigmatizing historical language about a person with leprosy. It is translated faithfully as source rhetoric, not endorsed or silently softened.

“பன்னீர்” here means fragrant rosewater, contrasted with the smell of workers' sweat.

“மொய்” is a customary ceremonial gift, often recorded at a Tamil wedding with an expectation of social reciprocity. The Tamil term is retained and briefly glossed.

“பயில்வான்” is rendered as wrestler in the source's contrast between bulk and technical skill.

Political, electoral, financial, and historical assertions are translated in the speaker's voice and have not been independently verified.
