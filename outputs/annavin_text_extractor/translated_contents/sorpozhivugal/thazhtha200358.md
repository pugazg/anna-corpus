## English Translation

Speech 16, 20 February 1958

**C. N. Annadurai:** Mr Speaker, before approving the allocation for the Harijan Welfare Department, a cut motion has been moved so that we may express views and suggestions about its administration. I rise to support it.

Congress members say criticism from the opposition may discourage the Honourable Minister. I do not think so. If our purpose were only to encourage him, we could cry, “Long live Kakkan! Long live the Honourable Kakkan! May the Harijan people live for ever under his supervision!” But our task is to examine whether work begins with good intentions, whether those intentions are properly fulfilled, and whether the money spent actually serves its stated purpose.

Political arguments over which party serves these communities can become heated. Yet no party in Tamil Nadu can survive by ignoring the oppressed. Every party therefore takes pride in claiming what it has done for them. We want the goodwill and political awareness that command such attention to grow further.

One member said the Harijan people ought to show Congress gratitude for Government benefits. The issue is not who owes gratitude to whom.

For generations, people with upper-caste attitudes in every party have oppressed a community responsible for the country's labour, property and protection of life. Every opportunity to remove that oppression must be treated as atonement for our former wrongs, not a favour performed to earn thanks.

Nowhere else, the speech argues, is an entire section considered low through caste in this manner. Members may think of African Americans in the United States. There is a fundamental difference. A century earlier they had been bought as slaves, yet by 1958 they possessed formal political freedom and shared in America's national, political and economic development. When uncivilised conduct occurred, other Americans came forward to condemn it.

Compare that change with the condition of communities treated here as permanent servants from the ages of the Puranas and epics through the periods of the Nayanmars and Alvars. Once in twenty-five years only their name changes; their condition has seen no comparable transformation. Comparing the two problems can provide comfort or escape, but cannot disclose our truth.

Adi Dravidars are described as socially backward while also counted as Hindus. If the present Government, earlier Governments, or even British imperial administration provided some benefits, these should be regarded as reform and atonement, not acts performed to receive gratitude.

When I spoke on this department last year, I said this was a national problem beyond parties and that partisan hostility should not intrude. Human beings have faults, and sometimes one party gains spirit by rubbing against another. I do not object to ordinary opposition. But Congress must not boast that no other party could perform this work more efficiently.

Congress is the first ministry to possess full powers. The public pays the taxes it asks. With plentiful revenue, authority and unrivalled influence, has it fully discharged its duty to Adi Dravidars? Even members who begin by praising the Government and complain that the opposition mentions only defects end by identifying defects themselves. They hesitate to call them defects, perhaps fearing party leaders will ask what becomes of discipline if they speak like the opposition.

When we call them defects, Congress members call them “conditions.” What are these conditions? Villages lack proper roads and wells. Discrimination persists in cremation grounds. Student scholarships are inadequate and do not reach recipients on time.

Party discipline prevents them saying “defects,” so they modestly call them “conditions.” Anger at the opposition is useless. The conditions exist, and no party will obstruct their removal. In this House of more than two hundred members, no one openly supports orthodoxy, ritual caste hierarchy, or caste itself. Everyone competes to advocate a casteless society and recognises Adi Dravidars as the backbone of the country.

A member reported obstruction of beneficial work in one village. I am genuinely sorry. I offer him two suggestions: strengthen his own influence enough to remove the obstacle, and when reliable information exists, give it to responsible authorities before raising it in the House. This is a problem beyond parties. Merely saying someone blocks reform is not enough; the caste mentality that permits obstruction must be removed.

The Ministry's report says that three welfare workers and three assistants have been appointed in each district. What did they do? What efforts did they make to remove deficiencies within Adi Dravidar society? Where is that information? What propaganda did they conduct against untouchability?

I do not belong to only one district; for good or ill I constantly meet the people. Sixty or so persons have apparently been appointed on good salaries to eradicate untouchability, yet I have never encountered them. You may say my not seeing them is unimportant. But if even I have not seen them, where would ordinary people have met them?

The report says leaflets were printed to explain the cruelty of untouchability. I have not seen many, but one remains fixed in my mind because it illustrates Government haste and waste.

A poster explaining the punishment for saying that another person's touch pollutes depicted a Brahmin figure. Because some people allegedly objected, the posters were withheld from distribution. The report says this avoided public anger. To test whether the public truly objected, the DMK printed five thousand copies at its own expense and sent them to villages. The OCR damages the following account of local reactions, but the argument is clear: public money had been wasted by withdrawing an anti-untouchability poster to appease a sectional objection.

What other efforts against untouchability were undertaken? When were opposition parties invited? Last year I said Harijan Day was celebrated among four or five officials, ten or fifteen contractors, twenty or thirty voters and fifty or sixty policemen. Such a gathering cannot draw public attention to the people's grievances. It should become a national festival involving every party.

What was done toward that goal? Despite our saying party differences should be set aside, many have not escaped them and may never do so. In vital fields, however, we must treat the occasion as a national festival rather than make party discipline supreme.

Both the current and previous reports of the Commissioner for Scheduled Castes state that schemes were not properly implemented. The English quotations in the scan were processed by Tamil-only OCR and are not recoverable verbatim. Their surrounding Tamil says State Governments reported rising expenditure without demonstrating planned and efficient execution.

We hear only that expenditure rose from Rs 17 lakh to Rs 18 lakh or from Rs 10 lakh to Rs 20 lakh. It is not we but the Commissioner who says State Governments did not show that schemes were clearly planned and effectively completed.

Members must recognise two parts of the problem. One is freeing Harijan people from poverty. The other is freeing socially oppressed people from social cruelty. They are connected, but distinct, with substantial differences between them.

Years ago, a municipal commissioner from an oppressed community came to Kanchipuram. He raised house tax. When owners petitioned for reductions, he went to inspect their houses. Although he had completed advanced schooling and held high office, residents of upper-caste houses barred his entry.

He said, “I am the municipal commissioner.” They replied, “We know; that is why we do not admit you.” When he asked whether they excluded him despite knowing his office, they said they knew both his office and his community, and excluded him for the latter.

One may ask why an incident from ten or fifteen years earlier matters. Untouchability does not disappear merely through wealth or office. There is no permanent remedy until people's minds change. There is no sound path to abolishing untouchability without abolishing caste itself.

The Commissioner for Scheduled Castes likewise stressed this in the report. Its damaged English passages say caste dominates election time and that caste prejudice persists even in cities. The report also proposes means of abolition. Invite our party to participate in such schemes.

Before Congress began speaking against caste difference, the movement to which I belong was already doing so. Ask whether we dug as many wells, built schools, supplied breeding bulls or distributed selected seed: we did not, because we did not sit beside the Treasury. You did.

Finance Minister Subramaniam once discussed our party's ancestry. Congress's own history includes orthodox members who resisted social reform, and Bal Gangadhar Tilak argued that Congress was a political organisation and need not reform society. Congress later changed and eventually acquired the generosity to reserve rights for Harijans.

Before Congress took such steps, and before it seated the Honourable Kakkan as minister, M. C. Rajah had held responsibility in this field. People of the oppressed communities had occupied distinguished offices. The Self-Respect and Justice Party traditions from which our party arose had long attended to Adi Dravidar grievances.

A member made an allegation that someone associated with our side failed to treat these people equally. If he gives a genuine and responsible explanation, I am prepared to remove the offender from the party. Radicals in every party must unite to abolish caste practice and thereby remove long-standing oppression.

Moving from one party to another is common, and can even be profitable; I see examples in this very House! But I neither ask nor invite anyone to change party. Within party discipline, we can cooperate in a specific good cause.

Working for the advancement and liberation of oppressed communities is such a reforming service. The DMK will support every Government effort toward it.

But as a whip is raised over a running horse without scarring it, to urge good work to be done still better, we have moved this cut motion. With that explanation, I conclude.

The source uses period terms including “Harijan,” “Adi Dravidar,” “Scheduled Castes,” “oppressed communities,” and “tribal/ancient people” with some overlap. The translation follows each local usage rather than replacing the historical vocabulary in the Tamil source.
