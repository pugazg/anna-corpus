## English Translation

Debate held on 13 July 1967.

**The Hon. Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** Mr. Speaker, two privilege motions are before the House. One is the privilege issue raised by Thiru Martin concerning the *Murasoli* newspaper. Believing there were certain defects in the privilege issue brought by Thiru Martin, the Speaker has raised another privilege issue.

Our Leader of the Opposition has now said that, instead of discussing both together, it would be better first to examine whether there is cause for raising a privilege issue against the hon. Martin.

The Leader of the Opposition pointed out that the hon. Martin cited an earlier ruling by the Speaker. He says it is proper to cite it and that no privilege issue arises from doing so. I accept that completely. It is not only proper but necessary to cite a ruling of the Speaker, a ruling of a former Speaker, or any relevant ruling. But one must cite the ruling the Speaker actually gave. One must not cite as his ruling something he did not rule.

The Leader of the Opposition says that Thiru Martin stated only the ruling the Speaker gave. The Speaker had ruled that certain things published in *Navasakthi* gave rise to a privilege issue and, on a preliminary view, that there was a prima facie case. Thiru Martin pointed to a sentence in *Murasoli* and cited the earlier decision as though a similar sentence in *Navasakthi* had led to the privilege issue.

Sentences of that kind did appear in *Navasakthi*. But the Speaker has expressed the view that Thiru Martin distorted his ruling: he attributed to the Speaker something the Speaker had not said. To understand this fully, the second issue must be connected with the first.

If what appeared in *Navasakthi* is exactly what appeared in *Murasoli*, the ruling applied to *Navasakthi* must also apply to *Murasoli*. Conversely, if what appeared in *Murasoli* appeared in *Navasakthi*, the ruling must be the same. Everyone may agree that if the wording published in *Navasakthi* also appeared in *Murasoli*, the same ruling should be given to both. But we must determine whether the wording in the two publications is in fact identical.

The Leader of the Opposition referred to one or two words. In informal conversation there may be such latitude. But when issues are raised in a court or Legislature, every word certainly has its own meaning.

For example, in Tamil only one letter distinguishes “கொல்வது” (to kill) from “கொள்வது” (to take). Nobody would say that, because only one letter differs, the meanings should not be treated as different. Even students would not argue that a single-letter difference leaves the meaning unchanged. The Leader of the Opposition therefore should not—and, I believe, would not—argue that it is only one word.

What appeared in *Navasakthi* was: “When I say Congress, I will take seriously only what Kamaraj, Bhaktavatsalam or Karuthiruman says. I regard the other speeches as those of people who live off the Congress.” The construction “only what they say” means that, apart from those three, the speech of everyone else is regarded as speech by someone who lives off the Congress.

To explain it in English, the emphasis excludes all Congressmen except those three. Like the emphatic repetition in the phrase “learn thoroughly what is worth learning,” it makes the sentence mean that I regard the speech of every other Congressman as that of someone living off the Congress. But I did not speak in that manner.

They said the same thing appeared in *Murasoli*. What *Murasoli* published, however, was: “When I say Congress, I look to people such as Kamaraj and Bhaktavatsalam. I regard all the other speeches as the speech of those who make a living from the Congress.”

The single word “such as” removes the poison from this. “Such as” requires others to be included in the list, like “et cetera” in English. Once the other people who belong in the “such as” list are included, the remaining speeches are separately characterised as those of people making a living from the Congress. There is therefore a difference between the two versions. They stand separately.

It is the Speaker's task to decide whether a privilege issue arises here. The use of that one word certainly creates a difference in meaning between the two passages.

There is another statement in *Navasakthi*: it reported me as saying, “Except for Kamaraj, Bhaktavatsalam and Karuthiruman, the others are people who live by their stomachs.” If it says “except Kamaraj, Bhaktavatsalam and Karuthiruman,” it means that the hon. Vinayakam and others have been placed on the other side.

The wording in *Murasoli* includes everyone in the comparison: Kamaraj, Karuthiruman, Bhaktavatsalam, Ponnappa Nadar and others. Using “except” pushes those three aside and treats everyone else differently. However the editors may have written it, Tamil is a refined language. This illustrates the need to use words with an understanding of their meaning.

Thus the word “except” is one thing, while *Murasoli*'s “people such as” is another. I merely submit that the two passages have different meanings.

**Thiru P. G. Karuthiruman:** Mr. Speaker, I express my appreciation for the Chief Minister's observations. The issue now has two parts. The first is whether a member of our party made an “allegation” against the Speaker's ruling. The other is the ruling that must be given on the publication itself. I first seek your ruling on the first part.

The Speaker had already said there was a “prima facie case.” We did not say he had delivered a final ruling. If the hon. Martin referred to your statement that there was a prima facie case and you call his presenting a similar message an allegation…

**The Hon. Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** Mr. Speaker, citing an earlier ruling does not create a privilege issue. That is not what the Speaker said. He said: “The wording in *Murasoli* is not the wording on the basis of which I found a privilege issue in *Navasakthi*. Saying that I gave a ruling on wording like this is itself the privilege issue.”

For example, had the hon. Martin simply submitted the words published in *Murasoli* and said, “This affects privilege,” none of these issues would have arisen.

**Thiru P. G. Karuthiruman:** All we ask—and all our party and I desire—is whether we do not even have the right to cite as an example a ruling already given by the Speaker. To say so affects our privilege. Two aspects must be clarified.

Today it has become difficult even to inspect the proceedings of this House. Previously, the day's proceedings would be placed in the library the next day, and we had the right to inspect them. Even that right has been removed. The Deputy Speaker has said they will be placed there after correction.

If each day's proceedings were made available immediately, we could conveniently check, “What did they say? What did we say?” Now we must speak from memory. If tape recording were introduced, no dispute would arise. We could play the tape to determine who spoke and what was said. It would serve as evidence.

Acting suo motu under Rule 192, the Speaker spoke as though something would happen if the hon. member did not express regret by the following day. This threatens not only privilege but democracy itself. Even if there were errors, there should be generosity in allowing them to be corrected…
