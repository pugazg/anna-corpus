That shows the difference between Governments: the former stopped at “What can we do?” while this one recognised a larger duty and acted.

Mr Alagarsamy, I think, said we should have pressed the Union Government. I did. Officials did too. To ensure that I pressed properly, I took labour leader and Left Communist P. Ramamurthi with me to meet the Union minister. He said legislation was being introduced to deal with such mills. When I specifically sought assistance for the Cambodia Mill, he agreed to send a special officer, who inspected it. The difference lies in intention, action and result.

The Leader of the Opposition's “double bolt” was not imposed for a song. Mill owners say yarn worth between Rs 6 crore and Rs 9 crore remains unsold. Communist leaders say the owners suffer no loss and merely claim one. Leave aside who is right. Owners argue that continued production amid stagnant sales would cause loss and announce a ten-day closure, not permanent shutdown.

Labour leaders say even ten days will hurt workers. Arrangements are being made to bring both sides together. Labour leaders should help create a climate for talks. Instead, some announce picketing before the Collector's office from a fixed date.

When this Government formed, a friend said its horoscope was bad. Perhaps it is, but that is also the intention of its opponents: everything others did is protested against us. They imposed Hindi, but we must answer. They neglected agriculture and left a food crisis, but we must answer. The Union failed to organise export markets for yarn, but we must answer.

Mr Karuthiruman and others may regret being out of office, but perhaps they also feel relief: when the burden became heavy, the right people arrived and it could be transferred. Rajaji said during the election at the Lakshmipuram Youth Association that coming to office would be difficult because the new rulers must bear twenty years of accumulated damage. Likewise, labour leaders should not fight the State over failures belonging to the Union.

Congress friends warn that Left Communists will join us, topple us and establish their dominance. If we join them, you say they dominate; if we let you grow, you dominate. We cannot see which dominance brings us profit or loss.

But we have firm evidence that Congress government stood beside employers for twenty years. Congress members themselves say in their Socialist Forum that their party fostered capitalists. The warning resembles a robber shouting that another robber approaches and asking us to hand everything to him for protection. If Congress says, “Beware the Left; let Congress grow,” both sides know I am capable of vigilance. (Applause.)

Left Communists and other labour leaders say officials have not acted properly. Officials are skilled at adapting to the outlook of those governing. If that adaptation takes time, delay should not be turned into a basic accusation.

At the same time, industrial peace is urgently needed. I fully welcome the Leader of the Opposition's point. Without peace, we shall be severely hampered in finding work for labourers. Every effort on their behalf must be peaceful and constructive.

We have seen strikes in the past and see them now. Labour leaders know the difference. Formerly, when a factory struck, the industrial area became silent: neither workers nor production moved. An owner seeing profits decline gradually came to negotiate.

Today a strike may move immediately into agitation and then violence, accompanied by abusive language no one should utter or endure. I do not blame labour leaders automatically. Some told me such acts happened without their knowledge and that they condemned them.

A member mentioned Cellpark Company. As soon as I heard that workers had attacked employees who came to work and members of management, I intended to summon the union leader. Before I could, he came and said, “You will hear another matter; I came first to tell you. What happened at Cellpark was wrong. Someone did it, but not at our instruction and not through our union.”

I offered a suggestion. When a political-party member violates a party decision, the party acts against him. Likewise, if a union member commits violence or conducts degrading abuse, and leaders truly disapprove and did not direct it, announce that he has been expelled. I would praise that stand. I have not yet heard that this was done.

The right to strike is among labour's most important rights. This Government will not obstruct, and will support, a strike conducted without violence, abuse or disturbance of peace. But if a strike develops into law-breaking violence, no one should expect this Government to ignore it. Even Governments where Communists hold greater power do not ignore such conduct; do not expect more indulgence here.

Another general issue was raised through an imaginative argument: if computers come, what of spinning? One could likewise remove the electric fans above us and employ many people with palm-leaf fans, as in religious halls and temple processions. No one opposes computers through ignorance or hostility to science.

The question is at what stage a country should adopt a labour-saving scientific instrument. A country with too few workers needs it. A country where waves of people seek jobs does not need it at this stage. That has been the DMK's view since before taking office.

Members say, “Keep the computer without reducing employment.” That is meaningless. Retaining a thousand employees while adding a computer creates unnecessary expense and no economist would welcome it. A stage may come when employment is abundant and scientific progress justifies computers, but not now.

When surgeons transplanted a heart, none of us asked to have a healthy heart removed simply because the procedure existed. Likewise, the existence of computers in America or Germany does not prove we need them here. America has higher average income, population control and more employment.

Unfortunately, Indian management may receive salaries comparable to management abroad and import the same scientific equipment, while Indian workers do not receive foreign workers' wages. Management or production costs are then cited. In a country that must create jobs, computers are presently unnecessary. Taking office has not changed our party's view.

Member C. Balasubramaniam said labour can win nothing without agitation and would continue it. He must also ask what happens if agitation turns violent. Peaceful agitation causes this Government no difficulty. But Government cannot be conducted without authority any more than labour movements can exist without agitation.

The OCR severely fragments the next transition, but the recoverable argument says this Government has offered to bring the parties together, talk and arrange compromise; that is one difference from the former Government.

Some friends say this Government fired on people just as the previous Government did. I am not prepared to lose my humanity by arguing that gunfire was right or just. I shall make every possible effort, now and always, to prevent it.

Yet Congress leaders outside this House repeatedly ask from platforms why police “stand with hands tied,” even speaking directly to policemen. Perhaps members here lack power to restrain those leaders, or perhaps one side has agreed to say one thing while the other says another. In either case, for members of a party that ruled twenty years to ask why police are inactive is an indirect incitement. I do not say it caused the firing.

If Congress approaches us in a cooperative spirit and advises labour, a good settlement can be found. No one objects to nonviolent agitation.

Finally, members requested compensation for families of those killed by police firing. I am pleased to inform the House that the Government has decided to compensate them. (Applause.) Details are being collected to decide the amount and method. The Government has accepted the sound principle that bereaved families must receive compensation.

The Minister will answer members' specific doubts about this Government's actions during its first year. With that, I take leave.
