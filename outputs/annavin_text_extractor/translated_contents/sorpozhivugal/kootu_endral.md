## English Translation

The following is a summary of the speech delivered by Arignar Anna in Chennai’s 63rd Ward on the date printed by OCR as 24-33-1961.

The Congress people suffered greatly to buy the shirt called “Swaraj.” That hard-won shirt loses its colour after a single wash. We point this out, and they become angry.

Just as one becomes angry with a friend who asks, “After all that, was this the only shirt you could find?”, Congress becomes angry with us when we ask, “Was this the only Swaraj we obtained? Do we not need another kind?” They endured much to obtain Swaraj; that is why they are angry.

I put it in terms of a shirt. Apply it to a house, a cow or a wife and you will understand its truth.

They think, “We endured so much; so many bore lathi blows and gunfire to obtain this Swaraj. Yet now, after all the suffering and sacrifice through which it was won, all sorts of people criticise it.” That is the source of their anger.

But genuine Congress workers today privately ask one another what benefit Swaraj has brought. Outside, however, they say, “What can surpass Swaraj?”

When genuine Congress people speak privately, they say, “What Annadurai says is true. Anbazhagan was not wrong when he said the North thrives while the South declines.” Then they add, “It is true, but do not say it outside; we will lose votes.” That is why they remain silent.

A man who struggled to buy a coloured shirt may know it is true when someone says its dye will fade, yet hide the fact and abuse the speaker. In the same way, Congress people who struggled to obtain Swaraj become angry and abuse us.

Because of that anger, Congress causes us harm. Its anger turns into posters, stones, soda bottles, knife thrusts and acid attacks. According to each person’s capacity, they may use every means available to cause suffering.

Only those capable of enduring this belong in the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. Those who cannot are not DMK members.

We entered this work treating lathi blows, gunfire and prison as trifles. Those who cry, “Lathi blows? Guns? Oh no, we want none of that!” are not what the Kazhagam needs.

We saw with our own eyes the cruelties Congress workers endured during the Swaraj struggle. Even innocent people were killed in large numbers. Congress volunteers continued their struggle without becoming enraged, bearing it all. Our comrades must acquire the same character.

In whose hands is Congress today, though it once possessed such steadfast fighters? In the southern Pandya country, Congress once needed Pasumpon Muthuramalinga Thevar to gain strength. After Swaraj he is no longer in Congress. Instead, Ramanathapuram Raja, who in those days sharpened the sickle to uproot Congress, is now inside it.

Who is Ramanathapuram Raja? Did he go to Alipuram Prison during the Congress struggle? Did he accompany Mahatma Gandhi on the Dandi March? No. He tried to eradicate Congress then, but joined it after Swaraj. How do genuine Congress people bear to see this? Even we cannot bear it.

During the anti-Hindi agitation, the man then called Kumararaja Muthiah, now Raja Sir Muthiah Chettiar, marched street by street carrying the Tamil flag. How did Congress give him an MLA seat? Those who once wished Congress destroyed came after Swaraj to warm themselves by its fire. Why do people who feel no anger at their presence in Congress become angry with us?

What connection does shipping-company proprietor Haja Sheriff, elected to the Legislature from Triplicane, have with Congress? What does he say in the House? “If production is to increase, workers must not demand higher wages. There must be regulation. Production will not rise if they seek more pay.” I alone opposed him there; no Congress member did. His speech appears in the newspapers.

Should genuine Congress people not become angry when they read it? We once demanded Swaraj for workers, yet betrayers of labour are now in Congress. Should they not ask whether this is their party? Today, only those who wish to throw dirt into workers’ mouths find a place in Congress.

What greater disgrace can there be than Draupadi garlanding Dushasana, who stripped her, and serving him a feast?

People who treat politics as a pastime have captured Congress today. If genuine Congress workers reflect upon this for a moment, they will understand the truth and their anger toward us will diminish.

The Swaraj won through so much suffering now benefits the Tatas and Birlas. The Annamalai Chettiar and Alagappa Chettiar families have seized Congress and flutter eagerly to enjoy its benefits.

Our Finance Minister asks, “Is it a crime for a rich man to grow?” Speaking last week, he said, in effect, “Under Swaraj, the rich will naturally grow richer because they already possess money. Let them. We shall tax them and recover it.”

This resembles the story of placing butter on a crane’s head, waiting for it to melt and cover the bird’s eyes, and then catching it. Let wealth accumulate with the rich, says the Finance Minister; afterward we shall seize them by the neck.

Was it because they did not know this method that revolutions in Russia rolled upon blood and in France upon corpses?

The Finance Minister says he will recover the money through taxation. How can he? The rich can be taxed only upon the income they report. What accounts will they show? Every rich person today keeps three sets of books: one for the income-tax officer, one to deceive his business partner and one for himself.

How will they show their true accounts? Whom do you fool by saying you will recover the money through tax? Congress now belongs to the rich and has become a tent protecting capitalists. There is no further use in trusting it. I tell genuine Congress workers that it is not the people’s party; it is the party of the rich.

One example is enough. In 1944, taxes upon food and commodities brought Delhi Rs. 47 crores. Today, such taxes reportedly bring Rs. 400 crores. Who is harmed by commodity taxation at that scale? Does it not harm the poor? Is this just?

When I spoke in the Legislature, I asked that at least foodstuffs not be taxed. The ministers did not remove the tax.

The Swaraj won through the suffering of some now benefits others, while poor people cannot improve their lives. That alone is why we oppose Congress administration and seek an understanding with other parties.

Why should Kamaraj and others be disturbed by such an understanding? Why do they ask whether we can join this party or that?

Suppose we went to seek a bride for a man. A neighbour ought, if possible, to recommend that the bride be given to him. If instead he asks, “Will they give you the bride?”, what does it mean? I would suspect that, had I been a little less alert, he himself would have tried to negotiate the match.

What moral standing do you have to tell us not to join them—the Communists or Swatantra Party? You entered a coalition in Kerala. Withdraw from that coalition, then come and tell me not to join Ramamurthi or Rajaji; I will respect and praise your integrity.

When Finance Minister Subramaniam accused us of forming a coalition with the Swatantra Party and Communists, I said it was not a coalition but an “adjustment,” a mutual concession. He maintained that both were the same. Anbazhagan cited this and said the Finance Minister did not know the difference between coalition and adjustment.

It is not that the minister does not know the meaning of adjustment. Of course he knows; could a B.A., B.L. not know even that? But many people in the country do not. To deceive them, he pretends not to know and says both are identical.

We go to a shop to buy fruit. Guavas lie on one side and mangoes on the other. Pointing toward the mangoes, we ask their price. The seller says one costs two annas. We pay two annas and ask for one, but he hands us a guava. When we say we asked for a mango, he replies, “You asked for fruit; both are fruit, so take this.” The Finance Minister similarly says both are the same. Is this the hurried evening trade of China Bazaar, or is it politics?

What is a coalition? One understands it when eating at home. A properly prepared mixed vegetable dish combines particular ingredients in their proper proportions. One substance becomes inseparably blended with another; that is a true mixture. But if someone takes the lentils on the leaf, pushes in the mixed vegetable and curry, pours water over everything, stirs it and calls that the dish, what does it mean? Only two people would call it so: one intoxicated, the other insane.

If, for the election, we said that no party would retain its principles, that the Swatantra Party, Communists and DMK would no longer need separate boards and would become a single party, that would be a coalition. If the DMK threw away its two-coloured flag and abandoned the North-South question, the Communists discarded their red hammer-and-sickle flag and their principle that democracy must mean the people’s Government, and Acharya abandoned his star and blue flags and the principle of individual liberty so that all could merge, that would be a coalition.

But what do we propose? If a Congress candidate contests Washermanpet, there should not also be separate DMK, Communist and Swatantra candidates opposing one another. The three parties should confer, assess each party’s influence and previous vote, and avoid splitting votes through wasteful mutual competition. That is all we say.

Kamaraj opposes even this. He wants all three to contest so that some votes go to the DMK, some to Swatantra, some to the Communists, and the remainder come to him, allowing him to win. That is why he says we must not unite.

Like a fish imagining it can escape a net if there is even a small hollow, Kamaraj thinks he can escape. This time, even if the net is torn, we shall repair it and leave no such opening.

Let Congress declare that it will nominate only those who suffered in the Swaraj struggle, not select candidates by caste and money, but by lathi blows endured, sacrifice made, prison terms and labour. Show me such a list and I will bow my head. Instead, if the candidate is Kumararaja—Raja Muthiah Chettiar—who came to warm himself in Congress only after Swaraj, would I be mad enough to yield?

Anbazhagan said Kamaraj asked whether four lame people could combine to oppose a man with two sound legs. I think Anbazhagan did not know the history. Kamaraj did not originate it. Pandit Nehru said it four years ago about northern parties, and *Swadesamitran* illustrated it. Kamaraj saw it and thought he could repeat it against us.

The three may even be lame, yet they can oppose Congress. For today Congress itself cannot see properly; flesh has grown over its eye and hangs down. Therefore, even the lame can manage it.

## Translator's Notes

The source date is printed by OCR as “24-33-1961,” an invalid date; 24-3-1961 is probable but requires scan-level confirmation. “Coalition” translates `கூட்டு`, while `adjustment` is retained as Anna’s English loanword for electoral seat accommodation without ideological merger. The speech’s food metaphor depends on `கூட்டு` also meaning a mixed vegetable dish. Historical caste, disability and mental-health comparisons are translated as spoken for documentary fidelity and do not reflect current preferred language.
