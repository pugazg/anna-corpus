## English Translation

Speech 1, dated 17 March 1967

Subject: Congratulating the election of the Speaker and Deputy Speaker

The Honourable C. N. Annadurai:

Mr Speaker, today we have reached the moment when I offer my congratulations to you and to Pulavar Govindan on your election as Speaker and Deputy Speaker, after friends from the various sections of the House have warmly praised you and indicated that we shall ultimately conduct ourselves in obedience to your rulings.

All those who occupied the office of Speaker before you have added celebrated chapters to the history of this country, and especially to its parliamentary history. There can be no doubt that during your tenure, and during the tenure of Pulavar Govindan as your Deputy Speaker, many more new and distinguished chapters will be added to that illustrious history.

The country knows well that you possess in abundance the training and qualities required for that task. You are well trained in parliamentary procedure. You have also acquired direct knowledge of the ways in which those procedures have been refined in countries where parliamentary government is conducted efficiently. There can be no doubt that your distinguished intellect and the practical wisdom you have gained will be of great use in enhancing the dignity of this House.

Pulavar Govindan, who has been elected Deputy Speaker, knows well, through literature and history, the precious greatness of Tamil Nadu, which conducted government in a dignified manner admired by the whole world at a time when many other countries on earth had not yet established systems of governance. Through literature, he has gained a thorough knowledge of Tamil Nadu's unique distinction and its treasury of ideas, which understood two thousand years ago how assemblies ought to be conducted. During the past five years, while serving as a member of this House, he has further refined the ideas he acquired from those treasuries of thought. His assumption of office as Deputy Speaker of this Legislative Assembly provides a fine opportunity for us all.

Although we belong to different parties in this House, as our friend Navalar Nedunchezhiyan, the Leader of the House, said, our friend Karuthiruman, the Leader of the Opposition, the Speaker, and the Deputy Speaker are all devoted to Tamil. They are not merely devoted to Tamil; we are also deeply attached to one another. There is therefore no room for conflicts or complications in the proceedings of this House. Not only is there no such room, but this House has already demonstrated sound practices. Although many of us have newly arrived here, taken as a whole, we are people who have come along an established path, not people who have lost their way. I therefore state firmly at this moment that we will not stray in the conduct of this House and create difficulties for you.

At times, enthusiasm may cause us to depart slightly from the straight path and lean this way or that. If that happens, the great responsibility of restraining us and guiding us along the proper path rests with you. Expressing our confidence that you will discharge that responsibility ably, I wish to convey my good wishes and appreciation to you.

Members of the Opposition have said that their rights must be protected. When they said so, I was reminded of the position in which I made the same plea in this House in 1952. I say with pride that during those five years, although I sat in the Opposition, the late and respected Dr U. Krishna Rao, who was then Speaker of the Legislative Assembly, never once acted in a way that gave any of us in the Opposition cause for distress. Remembering that, I pay tribute at this moment to that departed elder. Such an excellent tradition has been established in this House.

I remember what I said then: “We sit in the Opposition and are a small party. You are an expert in medicine; it is you who must give the proper medicine to those who are weak.” Today, however, I will not say the same thing to you on behalf of the Opposition parties. There is a good proverb: even when a mustard seed is small, it does not lose its pungency. I will therefore not say that they have grown weak. They, too, have come to guide us, and they have no doubt that you will remain diligently attentive to protecting their rights. Nor do I have any such doubt.

Therefore, I offer my heartfelt appreciation and congratulations to our Speaker and Deputy Speaker, who have taken their places in those offices worthy of everyone's praise. I also give the assurance that we shall conduct ourselves in obedience to their rulings, and I resume my seat.
