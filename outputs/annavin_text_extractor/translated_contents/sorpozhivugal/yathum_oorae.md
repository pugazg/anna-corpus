## English Translation

The incomparable presidential address delivered by Perarignar Anna, the foremost son of Mother Tamil, when he inaugurated the historic Second World Tamil Conference in the capital of Tamil Nadu on 3 January 1968.

Comrades!

Our Tamil language is ancient and gentle; it has acquired richness and strength. Possessing both vigour and splendour, Tamil has lived and enabled other languages to live. People in many regions know to some extent the excellence of Tamil, a language that has been the mother of many languages. I trust that this conference and the research conducted here will help make the world acknowledge the greatness and dignity of Tamil on a broad and complete scale. We may hope that this Tamil research will usher in a new and worthy stage. There can be no doubt that the benefits of this research will guide the life and growth of Tamil and the life of the Tamil people for a long time to come.

Congress leader Mr Kamaraj, speaking here, said that the first conference itself ought to have been held here. That is true; for some reason, that opportunity did not arise. Yet even when an elder brother shows no concern, it is customary for the younger brother to take an interest. In that manner, the second conference is now being held here. Twenty foreign delegates participated in the first conference held in Malaysia. More than thirty people from overseas have taken part in the second conference. Research scholars have come to Madras from Russia, Czechoslovakia, Austria, Hungary, England, America, and other countries.

It is deeply regrettable that Tamil, a language known in so many countries, has still been unable to find a place in many parts of India itself. Tamil is admired and praised in Czechoslovakia. Americans know and honour it. Tamil is taught in British and American universities. When we realise all this, our hearts swell with emotion and carry us to all sorts of imagined heights. Only when we consider the present condition do we return to where we actually stand. If Tamils learn about Tamil culture and Tamil history, unite with strength and knowledge, and undertake a great effort, we will certainly reach that higher position.

## That State and This State

At a time when linguistic knowledge had not yet arisen elsewhere in the world, Tamil possessed both literature and grammar. We possessed the *Ettuthokai*, the *Pattuppattu*, and the *Pathinenkilkanakku*. We were the inheritors of the *Kural*, which teaches the world the lofty principle: “Whatever the matter and whoever the speaker, wisdom is to discern the truth in it.” There can be no doubt that this conference will bring renewed splendour to such a language.

By witnessing the rare events that have taken place and are taking place at the Tamil Conference, and by listening to the excellence of the Tamil research seminar addresses, we can gain fresh benefit.

I wish to give an assurance that the heartfelt joy we have attained will not remain as others may imagine it, but will be used to discover the means and methods by which Tamil may be enriched and developed.

Let us firmly declare that we will use the research and conclusions concerning our Tamil language solely for its growth.

Tamil culture accepts every human being in the world as a sibling and a comrade. It greets and welcomes those who come to it. It accords every language its proper respect. Wherever in the world the wealth of knowledge may be found, it will seek it out and bring it home.

But Tamils will never consent to lose what is their own. When experts coming from England and other countries say that Tamil is such a precious language, can those who realise that they are the inheritors of that language fail to develop affection and devotion toward it?

If, at any time, for any purpose, and at the hands of anyone whatsoever, such a language is denied the distinct respect due to it, Tamil Nadu will not tolerate it.

The deep sea is always turbulent. If one wishes to cross it prudently, one may do so in a sound vessel, a ship.

Similarly, when Tamils embrace others and move forward together, no one can surpass them.

Although the United Nations works with unity as its objective, and although the flags of all countries fly outside while representatives speak inside and depart, there is still discussion about who may invade which country.

“Good and evil do not come from others.” My heart swells again and again when I think of the magnanimity of the Tamils, who a thousand years ago fashioned the true spirit of unity into the golden saying, “Every place is our town; everyone is our kin.”

The poet who sang “Every place is our town; everyone is our kin” sang in the very next line, “Good and evil do not come from others.”

Do we want good to come? We ourselves must bring it about. Do we want evil to come? We ourselves must invite it. Therefore, there is no need to wonder whether this or that may happen. Harm will not come to Tamils from others; unless we seek it out ourselves, harm will never come to Tamils.

A time will come when, besides our own recognition of the precious greatness of Tamil, others too will recognise it, look toward us Tamils, and say: “This Tamil language does not belong to you alone. It is our language too. It is the link language, the common language, and the language of administration.”

That time will not arrive through a hurried march. It cannot be reached through haste.

Just as an angler waits patiently until a fish approaches the hook, we must practise such patience. Tamil scripture and Tamil ethical teaching tell us to act with knowledge of time, place, method, and subject. Let us follow that path and create the auspicious day on which we can seat the Tamil language upon the throne.
