## English Translation

Speech delivered by Arignar Anna at the Education Conference held in Ooty on 19-5-1967:

Comrades!

You must give me time to understand the State’s financial position. The Government of Tamil Nadu is now engaged in determining the condition of the finances and what measures may restore them. Therefore, after the position is put in order, teachers’ problems will be considered one by one according to their urgency and in a manner suited to the finances. Teachers’ grievances will be removed.

A request has been made here that the retirement-age limit for teachers be relaxed and fixed at sixty. I wish to state that I shall examine this matter carefully and reach a decision.

To prevent the quality of primary education, the condition of secondary education, and the advancement of college education from deteriorating, the teaching profession must be accorded respect and made attractive so that qualified people in the field of education enter it.

Only when teachers’ salaries rise, giving them freedom from anxiety and a sense of fulfilment, will people of intellectual ability, those skilled in teaching, and highly qualified persons of distinction enter the profession.

If we wish to prevent educational standards from declining, use the service of educated people for society, and lay the foundation for a distinguished society, we must attract people of learning and ability to the profession and use their talents for society’s advancement.

I shall never say that the salary now given to teachers is sufficient for life, fulfils their needs, or provides peace and a satisfying existence. We know that only a substantial increase in teachers’ salaries will attract able people to this profession, encourage them to perform their service, and thereby enable the country to advance intellectually.

The Government of Tamil Nadu will give priority to teachers’ problems while also considering its own circumstances and financial position. It will undertake appropriate measures to accomplish every benefit it can provide to the teaching community and the education sector.

When we assumed responsibility for governing Tamil Nadu, the State Government’s financial position was greatly depleted. It did not possess sufficient funds to meet the demands raised by the country’s various sectors.

The Government of Tamil Nadu will examine the position and fulfil teachers’ demands in a manner that gives satisfaction. The State receives revenue from four sources: land tax, sales tax, entertainment tax, and its share of the income tax collected by the Central Government. The sectors from which these four revenues are collected depend upon landowners, traders, and the general public. No other field is visible from which taxes may be collected to increase revenue.

I therefore ask respected teachers to understand how many difficulties surround the Government of Tamil Nadu as it confronts this financial strain. Even so, do not imagine that I shall ignore the shortcomings you have stated or disregard your demands. As long as we remain in office, we shall do everything possible. We shall examine every problem and resolve them one by one.

I state that we shall agree and cooperate with you in fulfilling your aspirations, and I ask you to give your wholehearted affection and cooperation to the good measures undertaken by the Government of Tamil Nadu.

I myself once worked as a teacher. The hardships and troubles teachers undergo are not unknown to me. Teachers are the people capable of creating a good society in the country. A society’s rise or decline lies in the hands of its teachers. Therefore, I shall consider teachers’ problems with the greatest care.

Editorial note: Anna worked in 1934-35 at a school in Madras belonging to the Pachaiyappa’s Trust.
