Friends!

The comrade who had agreed to preside over this conference was unable to attend because of ill health, and this presidential position unexpectedly came to me. I am doubly happy about it.

## Our Opponents and Us

The “national fighters” of this country consider us, members of the Self-Respect Movement, their enemies. That is, they think and allege that we do not desire national liberation and that we are somehow reactionary.

Not everyone who has come to this conference accepts our policy. I know that some here harbour hostile thoughts about us. But it gives me great happiness that they think about us constantly. Even those wishing to condemn us would do well to learn our true principles before doing anything.

Our movement has survived in Tamil Nadu for these thirteen years only by confronting many kinds of difficulty. It has witnessed many fierce storms and conducted many struggles without tiring.

Today it has reached a condition in which even our opponents accept our principal policies to some extent. Such success will certainly delight the workers of our movement. The reason for this success, and for the movement’s survival despite immense opposition, is its vital force.

## After the Trial by Fire

Its founder, leader E. V. R., participated in what Congress members call the liberation struggle, led it, spread its call through every corner of Tamil Nadu, and went to prison many times as a result. He has passed the Congress “trial by fire.”

Therefore neither he nor anyone else in his movement will retreat from fear of hardship, loss, trouble, or imprisonment. Our movement’s leader and comrades once went to prison in the name of nationalism. Why then do they hesitate today to enter legislatures in the name of nationalism? Why this bitterness?

When nationalism brought hardship, why did they leave Congress, yet decline to join today’s ornamental Congress when using its name can easily secure a place in the legislature or ministry and countless newspapers are ready to sing its praise? I ask our political opponents to reflect upon this.

## The Conclusion of Our Inquiry

Through inquiry aided by history, we discovered the cause of foreign rule. We learned that indigenous corruption caused a foreign government to arise in this country. Our enslavement occurred because of division and confusion among our own people. History declares this truth.

In the name of religion, caste divisions were created here. One class suppressed others, exercised an exclusive monopoly, established class arrogance, produced a thousand and eight castes and divisions, and built fortresses of *shastra* to preserve them. Those divisions enslaved us.

The society that instilled a servile mentality in the majority of this country’s people was the misfortune afflicting us from the beginning. Because of it, the country’s condition deteriorated. Foreign rule and the white man’s bombs and guns were consequences.

Congress members say they will conduct a liberation struggle by setting the bomb against the spinning spindle and bring about “Rama Rajya.” We cannot accept this. We began our movement by asking how there could be a liberation struggle without removing the causes of division, servility, hierarchy, and exclusive monopoly. We explained our policies and laboured arduously to free our people from priestly rule.

Did we succeed? Yes. We gained immense success in the social sphere, but suffered political defeat, and today we again live under priestly rule.

## Forever Agitators

When politics is mentioned, the “nationalists” say that they adopt the policies of our movement but oppose only our political association with the Justice Party. I wish to explain that we did not associate with the Justice Party without reason and that the relationship arose for the sake of our movement’s principles.

## We Do Not Want Rule by a Pope

History says that the earliest form of rule to arise in any country was priestly rule. European history, for example, shows how much dominance the Pope exercised in politics. Those holding progressive policies destroyed that rule. We do not want such government, and therefore opposed the foundation of that kind of system.

The Justice Party acted forcefully in politics to abolish class arrogance, one of our principles. Supporting it therefore became necessary. Through it, we attacked priestly government.

Nevertheless, whatever system of government arises, we remain agitators. People ordinarily value neither agitators in politics nor greens among foodstuffs. Yet when political harm must be removed they seek the agitator, just as they seek greens when bodily illness must be cured.

Who introduced entirely new ideas in Tamil Nadu? Was it not us? Who conducted agitations? We did.

When Sir Ramaswami Mudaliar, a Justice Party leader who had received our support, said religious instruction was needed in schools, we condemned it. We are not people who say “so be it.” Our aim is to agitate constantly in accordance with our principles. Accordingly, we regard it as our duty to explain the present ministry, its consequences, and the political deception now taking place.

## The Doubt of the *Dinamani* Editor

What are the creation, composition, programme, and policy of the Congress ministry formed after a major election? Let us leave aside whether accepting office was right or wrong. Let us avoid debating even the promises and quarrels. Let us not discuss the ministers’ personal traits. Let us consider only one policy clearly visible in the ministry’s composition.

The Honourable Dr. Rajan, Minister for Health, broke Congress discipline and committed betrayal. He was censured by the All India Congress Committee. Non-Brahmins who committed lesser offences were punished; even recently such “punishment” was imposed in the Chennai Corporation. Yet Dr. Rajan, who committed an offence for which he was excluded throughout India, was made an Honourable Minister. Is this proper? Is it not a disgrace, a form of Manu’s justice? Must Tamil Nadu tolerate it? Is the policy of priestly rule not visible in the very composition of the ministry?

I ask the nationalist youth. They said, “The new reform is bad. We shall enter the legislature and smash it to pieces. Cast your vote in our yellow box.” What is breaking now: the new reform or their election promise?

When I recently asked a student of Annamalai University about this, he said angrily that the college’s militant youth were disgusted because the Congress ministry had not broken the new reform.

I ask the elders who voted. They said that voting in the yellow box would bring great benefits, reduce land revenue, and end the suffering of the unemployed. What have you seen today?

The Honourable Raman Menon threw up his hands at Tiruvannamalai and said, “We wish to reduce land revenue, but there is no money.” He says self-government has not arrived. Yet the Honourable Munisami Pillai climbs to the summit of Ootacamund and says, “My government.” The editor of *Dinamani* suffers from doubt about whether the present government is British or Congress.

Comrade Batliwala has been punished, under Congress rule. That is why *Dinamani* has this doubt. Thus the new reform has not been broken, self-government has not arrived, and land revenue has not been reduced. In the end, we have reached a condition in which people doubt what government is actually governing.

This is what we have been saying. We say exactly what the Honourable Raman Menon says, but one group calls him a “patriot” and us “traitors.”

## Prohibition or the Abolition of Education?

The ministers say, “We have introduced prohibition.” I support the ministry’s prohibition; let drinking disappear. But I condemn their use of it as an excuse to say there is no grant for education.

If there is no money to spread education, it is the ministry’s duty to discover proper means. If it has courage, let it use legislation to stop a portion of the crores spent in temples in the name of religion and spend that money on education. Who will accept a situation in which drink disappears and education disappears with it?

## A List of “Ignorant People”

Likewise, Achariyar says the country needs unity and therefore needs a common language, namely Hindi, which he will make compulsory.

What is the common language of Canada? There is no country more advanced than Russia, and it has many languages. Switzerland, almost a birthplace of nationalism, has many languages. There is no such compulsion in those countries. Even primary education has not spread through this country. Why spend many lakhs on Hindi in this condition?

## Kalidasa and Manu

Comrade Varadachariyar says, “If Hindi is compulsory, interest in Sanskrit will arise. By studying it, Aryan life and civilisation will become known, and an Aryan institution will arise.” That is precisely why we oppose it.

We know about the Aryans. Aryan intrigue is the original cause of our harms. That is why we condemn the scheme to revive it.

When Comrade Sathyamurti spoke at Pachaiyappa’s College, he praised Sanskrit and drew attention to Kalidasa. That is fair: when Iyar remembers Kalidasa, we remember Manu. When he notices refinement, we remember the harms of Manu’s system of priestly rule.

That is why we oppose Hindi. People of every kind participate in this struggle: Tamils, scholars, those attached to Congress, people who have never before entered politics, newspapers such as *The Hindu* and *Educational Review*, and most recently Comrade T. R. Venkatarama Sastri. Is it proper for Achariyar to call all these people “ignorant”?

## It Is Enough for the Flame to Enter

Our movement therefore continues to explain both religious and political deception. A provincial conference will soon be held in Tiruchi to prepare a programme for future work and examine the situation. I ask for your support.

It is enough for the flame of self-respect to fall within your mind. Little by little it will burn away all your old thoughts, conclusions, and policies. If you harbour futile hatred toward us, the flame will burn that too. I ask you to support this great movement.

## Translator's Notes

The first scan clearly dates the conference to 14 November 1937 despite damaged OCR. Its footnote identifies this as Anna’s second presidential address. All seven Tamil OCR pages remain verbatim.

“ச. ம. இயக்கம்” is expanded contextually as the Self-Respect Movement. “ஜஸ்டிஸ் கட்சி” is rendered by its established English name, the Justice Party.

The contrast between “வெடிகுண்டு” and “தக்ளி” is between the colonial bomb and the nationalist spinning spindle. It is translated as a political image rather than corrected into a factual programme.

The comparison between political agitators and “கீரை,” edible greens, is a wordplay about things ordinarily undervalued but sought when harm must be cured.

“மஞ்சள் பெட்டி” refers to the colour-coded ballot box associated with Congress in the source’s election context. “Manu’s justice” translates “மனுதர்மம்” as the caste-law critique intended here.

Political, party, caste, religious, educational, linguistic, electoral, biographical, and historical assertions are translated in the speaker’s voice and have not been independently verified.
