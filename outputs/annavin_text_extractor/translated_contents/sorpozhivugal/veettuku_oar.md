## English Translation

Anna's battle cry at the anti-Hindi meeting held on 26 June 1960:

Comrades!

"Pandit Nehru gave an assurance in Parliament not once but twice: 'I will not impose Hindi without knowing the views of the non-Hindi-speaking people; I will not remove English, which serves as the official language.'

"But where is that assurance today? President Babu Rajendra Prasad has issued a directive declaring, 'Hindi will become the official language.' That directive violates the pledge given by the Pandit. Where is the pledge now?

"Was the assurance Pandit Nehru gave merely rubbish? Or was the place in which it was given an ordinary place?

"If Parliament so decides, it can enable the thirty-six crore people living in India to live, or cause them to die. Was the assurance given by the Pandit, as Prime Minister in a Parliament possessing the power to determine such a destiny, merely a game of hide-and-seek? I ask our Congress friends to reflect upon this situation.

"The Pandit speaks sweetly and gives an assurance. In violation of that assurance, the President issues an order. If the Pandit truly possessed a sense of honour and self-respect, should he not have resigned his office by now?

"Therefore, it is precisely to protect the Pandit's honour that we are beginning this struggle. Babu Rajendra Prasad must withdraw his order. If he does not, the movement has decided to begin an agitation.

"The question arising today is: who governs the country? The Prime Minister gives an assurance and says, 'I will not impose Hindi.' The President, however, says, 'Hindi must take the place of English.' Is this politics? Is it civilised politics? Is it moral? Is it just? These are the questions I wish to ask.

"In truth, Pandit Nehru himself would have been delighted today upon hearing our announcement of the agitation, thinking, 'At least you have come forward to protect my honour!'

"Indeed, I believe, I dream, that Pandit Nehru may summon Brother Sampath, the leader of our agitation committee, seat him in a private room, stroke his chin affectionately, and say, 'Good men, you have done a good deed. By launching this struggle, you have come forward to preserve my honour. How can I ever thank you?' That scene appears before my mind's eye.

"For an assurance given by a Prime Minister in Parliament is no mere piece of playful talk.

"When that assurance is not honoured, how can we remain idle? That is why our General Secretary Nedunchezhiyan, speaking here, said that the movement has raised the flag of battle today.

"Yes! Today we have raised the flag of battle. We have raised it against the imposition of Hindi and for our liberation. If the Delhi government does not withdraw Babu Rajendra Prasad's order, battalions of Tamils will rise throughout the land. I believe those who govern the country know well what form our struggle will take.

"Brother Sampath remarked that Kamaraj intends to frighten us by displaying his guns.

"The only reply I can give Kamaraj is this: 'You possess bullets, but we possess hearts capable of bearing those bullets.'

"Kamaraj must also remember that bullets and guns are nothing new to us.

"Tamils will consent to lose anything. They will lose property and comforts; they will even consent to lose kingdoms and palaces. But they will never consent to lose precious Tamil.

"Kamaraj and his masters in Delhi must understand this truth clearly.

"If we accept the imposition of Hindi today, with what face and with what tongue could we speak about the domination of an adversary?

"Did we begin opposing Hindi only today or yesterday? We have fought Hindi from the very day it set foot in this land. Those who spoke before me explained that history in detail. We have already stated all the arguments and evidence that could be stated. What remains for us to do except raise the flag of battle?

"Therefore, we firmly declare that even if we ourselves must perish, we will not accept Hindi. Withdrawing the order is the only prudent action the government can take.

"In the coming anti-Hindi struggle, one person from every family must participate. Unless we make great sacrifices at this time, our objective cannot be attained. Comrades must therefore begin preparing for the struggle from this very day.

"Kamaraj is described as a 'true Tamil.' If all of us are sent into prison, the whole world will learn through our moral struggle that he has become a 'dried-up Tamil.'

"Cruel repression, baton charges, tear-gas shells, and bullets may all be unleashed against us. Many thousands among us may be thrown into prison. But through this struggle we shall demonstrate that the DMK is a camp of valiant people capable of enduring them all!"

Source footnote: In 1938, Anna was imprisoned for four months for speaking about the anti-Hindi agitation.

## Translator's Notes

The scan clearly prints the unusual headline “வீட்டுக்கு ஓர் மறவார் வாரீர்!” The Tamil source is retained exactly, while the English title conveys its call for one warrior from every household.

“Thirty-six crore” reproduces the population figure used in the historical speech; it is not converted to a modern population estimate.

The contrast between “பச்சைத் தமிழர்” and “உலர்ந்த தமிழர்” is a rhetorical play on “green/fresh” and “dried-up.” It is translated with a brief cue rather than silently regularised.

“அறப்போர்” is rendered as “moral struggle,” reflecting the source's presentation of the agitation as principled and non-criminal.

Political and historical claims, including statements attributed to Nehru and Rajendra Prasad and the source footnote about Anna's imprisonment, are translated in the speaker's or editor's voice and have not been independently verified.
