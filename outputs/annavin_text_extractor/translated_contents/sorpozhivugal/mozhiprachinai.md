## English Translation

Speech delivered at Pachaiyappa’s College in 1967:

Previously, only the Tamil people opposed Hindi. Now the Government of Tamil Nadu also opposes Hindi! The people’s feelings have been vindicated. In this situation, I am confident that the Central Government will come to our side. I also have confidence in Indira Gandhi, who has become Prime Minister. I think she will not remain so obstinate on the language question.

Having travelled with her father and seen countries throughout the world, she will realize that no country in which several languages are spoken has made one language alone its official language. Moreover, I have asked Hindi speakers about the Hindi she speaks. They say, “It is a kind of Hindi!” But I am not saying that Indira Gandhi does not know Hindi.

Hindi spoken in Punjab is not understood by those in Uttar Pradesh. That is the nature of the language. Let us expect that, after Nehru, his only daughter Indira will possess a similar quality of respecting democracy.

The Government of Tamil Nadu is immediately faced with resolving the food problem. Next comes the problem of prices. To resolve the problems that arise while controlling prices, attention must be given to industrial development and the burden must be reduced.

Should we resolve these problems and then attend to the language question? Or should we resolve the language question and then take action on these matters? Let the students say. Although only the students of Pachaiyappa’s College are present here, I ask the whole student community: tell us which problem must be resolved first.

If no solution can be found to the language question, this government will not hesitate to resign. I believe a situation must be created in which we resolve the present problems while also finding a solution to the language question.

Even those of the Jana Sangh who have been extremely obstinate on this issue now appear to have changed to some extent. State categorically which problem should be resolved first. If you believe that “only doing this will bring benefit,” explain it. We shall listen.

This is the difference between those who ruled before and those who rule now. Those in power previously would not listen, no matter who said what. They would say, “We know everything; no one need tell us.” But we are not like that. We shall listen to everyone’s views.

The Government of Tamil Nadu desires not only the friendship of students but also their assistance. Give us your advice and your views on how it should be carried out. If we cannot do it, we shall not be ashamed to say, “We could not; these are the reasons.”

We are not seeking advice only from students. We ask every section. Where possible, on certain issues, we shall even try to implement methods of referendum used in European countries.

Only after paying homage to the martyrs who died in the anti-Hindi struggle did we go to Fort St George and assume office. We consider the D.M.K. ministry to be alms bestowed by those martyrs. We do not imagine that it was achieved solely through our own intellectual ability.

For democratic culture to endure, show consideration for the problems now confronting Tamil Nadu’s new government. Give us your support. Offer us your affection.
