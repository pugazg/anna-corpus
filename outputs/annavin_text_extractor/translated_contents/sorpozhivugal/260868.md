## English Translation

Speech record 52, dated 26 August 1968.

The third day of debate upon the no-confidence motion against the Ministry began at about 11:30 a.m. on 26 August 1968. Deputy Speaker Pulavar K. Govindan presided. The debate began with DMK member L. Ganesan.

Mr Ganesan said Congress members engaged in violence under the guise of showing black flags. He compared Central Minister Morarji to Shylock, the Shakespearean character. With statistics, he described the extensive work of the DMK Government in food and agriculture. He accused Congress of playing a double role and concluded with an English quotation that is illegible in the Tamil OCR.

Congress member R. Ponnappa Nadar, who followed, regretted that a member of the DMK, an organisation he described as an abode of culture, had spoken in that manner. He blamed the DMK Government for failing to extend the one-measure-of-rice scheme throughout the State, said the Shylock analogy was inappropriate, alleged internal conflict within the DMK, criticised its approach to labour problems and condemned the lottery scheme.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** The Deputy Leader of the Opposition wishes to know whether it was proper for the Inspector-General to issue a statement. During Congress rule, the Police Commissioner, the Inspector-General and similar officers issued statements of this kind. Comparing those earlier statements with the present one, one can see that there is no great difference or contradiction.

[Interruption.]

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** They are using an incorrect proverb. [An English phrase is illegible in OCR.] On that basis, I consider this an instrument of reassurance.

[Interruption.]

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** I wish to know whether Congress is prepared to enter into an agreement that senior officials shall not issue statements of this kind amid disputes between political parties.

The Opposition speaker said the absence of a sound and stable economic policy had created unemployment. He alleged administrative paralysis and said statements by senior Government officials would adversely affect the Government itself. Arignar Anna intervened.

**R. Ponnappa Nadar:** The Chief Minister brings up what happened before. In English there is a saying [the quotation is damaged]. I consider this an excuse.

**R. Ponnappa Nadar:** To call a person [English phrase illegible]—if they do not accept that calculation, it may fit this situation.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Congress governs on an all-India scale. If Congress agrees that henceforth, wherever it governs, officials will not issue statements explaining a situation or position, we can sit down and discuss an agreement.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** His wish is that his party should spread throughout the world. For the present, let it remain as far as it has reached. Congress governs in many places in India. The DMK does not possess that position. So far as I am concerned, I can give an assurance myself. But Mr Karuthiruman must speak while taking the All India Congress into account. [Laughter.]

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** He has gone to a very high position in the investigative branch of today's Union Government.

**R. Ponnappa Nadar:** He went of his own choice.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** He went of his own choice, with this Ministry's good wishes and the goodwill of the people.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** They asked whether Mr Narayanaswami Mudaliar was appointed Advocate-General as a reward because he had argued for this party many times and in many cases. If so, I wish to tell the Deputy Leader of the Opposition this: if the appointment of Mr V. P. Raman was a reward for arguing on behalf of former Chief Minister Bhaktavatsalam, then this appointment too may be called a reward. In truth, neither was a reward. Both men received professional fees independently.

**P. G. Karuthiruman:** Forgive the interruption. On behalf of my party, I wish to state that Congress will support an agreement that such conduct should at least be prevented hereafter.

**P. G. Karuthiruman:** This is the source of the present dispute. Fortunately, he has not gone throughout the whole world; I am pleased about that.

Mr Ponnappa Nadar continued, alleging that police work had become paralysed under DMK rule and asking why a senior police officer had gone to Delhi.

He then questioned the appointment of a judicial officer, and Arignar Anna intervened.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** When Mr Mathiazhagan spoke earlier, the former Chief Minister asked, “Is this the way a responsible Opposition member should speak?” I do not wish to say that our Deputy Leader of the Opposition is speaking in the same manner. I merely bring to his attention what the former Chief Minister said.

**R. Ponnappa Nadar:** By way of explanation, the present Chief Minister himself has said so. “Periyar” does not mean Ramasamy alone. Advocates receive fees. You have made an appointment.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Even though it has taken time...

**R. Ponnappa Nadar:** For my part, I state that what he did was wrong.

**P. G. Karuthiruman:** To err is human. If these people commit errors at eighty per cent, what he did would have been only ten per cent.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Thank you for attempting to protect your leader by doing the arithmetic.

Mr Ponnappa Nadar continued. He alleged excessive waste in financial administration and said two additional Public Service Commission members had been appointed with the election in mind.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** The appointment of Public Service Commission members has no connection with the Tenkasi election. If their party workers raised this matter during the election and thereby secured for us the votes of the communities to which those two members belong, I thank them for it.

Mr Ponnappa Nadar alleged that PUC examination papers had leaked. Hon. R. Nedunchezhiyan intervened to say that the papers were the University's responsibility and that action had been initiated. Mr Ponnappa Nadar then made several allegations against a senior Electricity Board officer. Anna intervened.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Every allegation against him will be investigated properly. There is no rule that an allegation by itself prevents promotion. A person senior to him was not passed over. That senior officer was posted to Erode and promoted; this officer was not. Therefore, there is no irregularity here.

**R. Ponnappa Nadar:** I accept that the Government's policy on prohibition is excellent. But—

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Forgive my interruption. He raised a question and I gave an explanation. It would not be proper to move onward without knowing whether he accepts that explanation.

**R. Ponnappa Nadar:** I accept the explanation. But the inquiry should have been completed before making such an appointment.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Whatever allegation may be made against an officer, the Government retains authority even after he has left office. The allegations will be considered according to procedure.

Mr Ponnappa Nadar continued, asking that prohibition be strengthened. He quoted an article written to *The Indian Express* by an office employee. He then alleged that the Corporation failed to act at the proper time. Anna intervened.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** The article says that a citizen goes from one official to another and then to the Corporation. It does not identify this particular State. He himself accepts that. There was therefore no need to cite it as evidence in a debate about this Ministry.

**R. Ponnappa Nadar:** We wrote many letters and received no reply. That is why I quoted it.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Even without the quotation, if the honourable member says his letters received no reply, we are prepared to treat that statement with respect. He need not cite Iyengar because he imagines he himself lacks standing. We have great respect for Mr Ponnappa Nadar.

Mr Ponnappa Nadar concluded by alleging that the DMK Government had not fulfilled its promises. The debate continued with many participants.

Congress member S. Ramalingam recalled that during the election the DMK had accused Congress rulers of allowing poverty to dance wildly throughout the country. He asked whether the same condition did not continue under DMK rule and displayed a photograph of an emaciated mother.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Within one year, the feeling has arisen in you that this question must be asked. Why did our friend remain silent during twenty years of rule when immeasurable horrors of this kind danced throughout the country?

**S. Ramalingam:** We accept the consequences of the sin we committed. As penance for it, we now sit in the Opposition benches. To remove that sin—

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Our friend says they now suffer for their past sin. I did not even describe it that way; his own conscience seems to trouble him. I asked why, within fifteen or sixteen months—or [an OCR-damaged number]—he now feels moved to ask why some child lacks milk, while for twenty years, when such horrors strutted arrogantly through the country, he did not make a representation in his committee, on a public platform or after entering the Legislature.

**S. Ramalingam:** I have spoken in our party meeting. Even in the Legislature, I—

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** I am deeply sorry that even after he spoke in the party meeting, his party paid no attention.

[Loud commotion from the ruling benches.]

The member continued, asking why the problem of beggary had not been abolished.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Give us fifteen years. Then ask whether we have transformed all those conditions.

[The editorial summary of the following exchange is severely damaged. A member urged that the problem receive priority.]

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** If the money spent on this lamp would complete that task, our friend could do it. There is a difference between the six rupees spent on this lamp and that immense problem.

The member continued. Hon. Mrs Sathyavani Muthu rejected an allegation that the problem of Harijans had not been addressed. Anna then explained the Electricity Board matter raised by the member.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** The member said the Electricity Board sent home eighty supervisors last year and one hundred this year, telling them there was no work. The information received from the Board is that thirty-three supervisors have so far been removed from work on the Kundah hydroelectric project during 1967–68 and arrangements are being made to re-employ them.

Many members continued to speak. Congress member R. Ramachandra Durai discussed prohibition. His speech contained words unsuitable for the House. When the presiding officer pointed this out, the member withdrew them and the Chair ordered the phrases expunged. Similar language appeared again, and Anna intervened.

**Hon. C. N. Annadurai:** Mr Speaker, the previous member used the expression “[expunged portion].” Since it diminishes the dignity of the Legislature, I ask that it be removed from the Assembly record.

Speaking as the Minister responsible for the Police Department, I state that if information is given that anyone comes here after drinking, suitable and strict action will certainly be taken against that person.

**Hon. Deputy Speaker:** I have already ordered it removed from the Assembly record.

The debate continued with many participants. The third day's proceedings ended at 2:15 p.m.
