## English Translation

Speech delivered at the inauguration of the Sankaralinga Nadar School in Madras in 1967:

Comrades!

Tamil Nadu has never said no when good people asked for help toward a good cause. Tamil Nadu will withhold money only from those who receive it but neither carry out the work nor render accounts.

In that manner, many schools are functioning today through donations obtained from philanthropists and through Christian priests. Many such schools operate in Tamil Nadu. I too studied in such a school.

Although Sankaralinga Nadar himself did not study extensively, he established this school so that others might receive an education. Though the name Nadar appears in it, the institution is not narrow in outlook. Everyone is admitted here. As this school grows, Sankaralinga Nadar’s fame will grow, and so will our feeling of gratitude toward him.

Only when the present generation undertakes constructive work for the education of future generations will the new generation attain distinction.

Political parties that appear once every five years cannot conjure up progress through magic spells alone.

Progress lies in the hands of students. When I see students, I behold in my mind’s eye the very future of Tamil Nadu.

If one-fourth is spent on education this year, a still larger sum will be required next year to continue educational work at the same scale. A small child needs a small shirt. When that same child grows, it needs a larger shirt. It remains one shirt, but its size is greater.

Even this simple arithmetic is unknown to the Delhi government. Having said that it would provide eighty crore rupees for education under the Five-Year Plan, it has now reduced the sum and says it can provide only sixty-five crores. Though I may not have money in my hand, I have strength. Therefore, even amid financial scarcity, I shall do what I can.

Some say that although expenditure on education has increased, its quality has declined. I do not think so. The difference between education in the past and education today is one of kind, not a deficiency in quality. Since the world is advancing, their statement may be tolerated, but it contains no substance.

It was said here that Tamils are adept at learning any language. Ability is one thing; compulsion is another. To say that one can live in India only by studying one language, Hindi, is a command. In the same way, learning is one thing and imposition another. Even a child spits out food when its mother forces it into its mouth.

That is why the students of Tamil Nadu stand united in saying there must be no compulsion in the matter of language. In them we see devotion to Tamil and its development. Because we remained vigilant over language, our mother tongue is protected and continues to develop.
