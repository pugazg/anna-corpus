அதேபோது, மாணவர்கள் தமக்கு அளிக்கப்பட்டுள்ள
வாய்ப்பு எத்தனை அருமைமிக்கது என்பதனை உணர்ந்து

கேட்டுக் கொள்கிறேன்.

முத்து எடுப்பர்? தனக்கென வந்துள்ள தையல் அன்றோ அதற்கு

அறைத்தெடுத்துப் பூசி மகிழ்தற்கன்றோ சந்தனம், அடுப்பு

நாட்டினருக்கு நல்வாழ்வு தந்திட, எனக்கென்ன தருகின்றனர், ஏன்
இந்தத் தாமதம் என்ற கணை தொடுக்கமட்டுந்தானே? எவரும்
அதுபோல் கூறார்.

உம்மிடம் மிகுதியான நம்பிக்கை கொண்டுள்ளோரில்

சென்ற பாவலர், உம் போன்றோரை மனத்திற் கொண்டாற்றும்

குறிக்கோள் தெளிவாக அமைந்திடின், மாணவர்களிடம்
மாண்புமிகு செயலினை எதிர்பார்க்க முடியும் என்றுரைத்தேன்.
இந்தக் குறிக்கோளை மாணவர் பெற்றிடத்தக்க விதத்தில்

கூடங்கள் எவ்விதமான சூழ்நிலையில் இயங்கவேண்டுமோ
அவ்விதம் இருந்திடச் செய்வதும் இயலாததாக உள்ளது.

அடிப்படைத் தேவைகளையேனும் அளித்திட முடிவதற்கு.

வரிப்பணமே, கோட்டையாய், கொடி மரமாய், பாதையாய்,

இந்நாடு இந்நாளில் உள்ளது என்பதனை, எத்தனை ஏழ்மைக்கு

மிகுந்திடும், ஏற்புடைய செயலில் ஈடுபடுவதை, இத்தனை தந்திடும்

முடியாது, தன்மானமுள்ள எவரும் எதிர்த்தாக வேண்டியது -
இதனை இலட்சியம் என்றோ திட்டமென்றோ கூறுவது,
இலட்சியம் என்பதற்கே களங்கம் தேடுவதாக முடியும்.

## English Translation

Address delivered by Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu Dr. Anna to the "gems among students" at the convocation of Madurai University held on 7 September 1967.

Governor, Pro-Chancellor, Vice-Chancellor, distinguished professors, and young friends who have come to receive your degrees!

I am greatly delighted to have the opportunity to deliver the convocation address of Madurai University. This university is only a year old, and this is its first convocation address. I convey my gratitude to the university's elders who have enabled me to receive this opportunity.

I stand in Naanmaadakoodal. I stand filled with the inspiration born of the thought that this city of Madurai once possessed a unique distinction praised by the whole world and shone as Tamil Nadu's citadel of learning.

### Naanmaadakoodal

No one standing here can fail to recall the history of the great Pandya kings who once ruled while giving scholars their protection, fostering Tamil, safeguarding the distinction of the Tamil people, planting the flag of fame, and holding just sway to the admiration of the world. Waves of memory that make the heart melt must arise.

Today I rejoice to see those who have come here to receive the distinction of a degree and the eminent professors. I reflect that it was here, in those days, that people conversed with the poets of the Pandya court, bathed in the stream of knowledge, and rejoiced inwardly; I taste the sweetness of that delight.

With my mind's eye I see Tiruvalluvar coming before the assembly of venerable poets from three hundred miles away, declaring: "Behold the Tamil way, the good path I have composed, a noble order worthy of the world's praise and acceptance."

I see the proud scene in which great poets delivered discourses explaining why their respective works deserved the praise of the learned. There can be none who do not see it.

What intellectual power and what varieties of critical inquiry existed here in those days!

### The Event of That Day

I see Nakkeerar, the lion among great poets, who, even after hearing that the poem was composed by the Lord and even when shown the eye upon the forehead, declared, "A fault is a fault." I see the dignity possessed by good Tamils. Does not that event of old command all of us: cast off fear; refuse to lie prostrate even if the nine treasures are offered; acquire the strength to defend truth; dare, for its sake, even to swim through a river of fire; prove what Tamil valour is?

All of us born in this sacred land understand that preserving the precious tradition established by the wise is an enduring and noble duty. The great poets seem to stand before us saying, "Walk with the stride of a lion! Let your ability serve to win an exalted station for this land." Though we cannot see them, we hear their words.

Great Madurai, which lent distinction to the Pandya land; Madurai, which had an academy that guarded Tamil tradition; sacred Madurai, which made the sweet sound of Tamil rise in every direction: it was a fortress of victory that earned greatness not merely through heroic warfare but also through the dignity of launching arrows of knowledge. Anyone who knows the historical distinction of this great city will be stirred by the feeling, "Will that day not return?"

Yes. But why should we yearn? We can see that former distinction even today. We can make that distinction endure forever. All these people have dedicated themselves to precisely that task. With their ability as our support, Tamil tradition and Tamil dignity can be made to shine forever. I gain that assurance; you, the great scholars and sovereigns of learning who have fittingly assembled at this convocation, give us that assurance. What remains for me to tell you? Your own hearts know it, and your professors have given you their counsel. My duty is only to remind you once more of what you already know.

### What Is a Degree For?

You have obtained degrees. You are becoming experts in many branches of learning. You have become fine gems brought forth by the university. Yes, but is this the end or the beginning? That is the question. You have degrees and deserve congratulations; there is no doubt. But what is a degree for? Is it to display with delight, or to regard as a mandate received for service? Is it for yourselves or for the country? Is it to earn wealth or to preserve the country's honour? For what purpose will this degree be used? That is the question that falls upon our ears mingled with the joy of this celebration.

We know that a cloud travels about gathering water only to multiply what it has consumed and give it back for the world's delight.

Does the peacock spread its beautiful tail and dance merely to enjoy the sight itself? It does so for others to see and rejoice.

### An Army of Knowledge

Does the earth use the treasure of gold she has guarded to make ornaments and adorn herself alone? No; she gives it for others to receive.

Why does the sacred lamp emit light? To remove the distress of those in darkness.

Distinguished graduates, you are the sacred lamp, the treasure of gold, the richly plumed peacock, and the rain cloud. You are experts who must enrich the country. You have come to contribute your ability so that the people may live in harmony. These degrees are the permits for that work. The university's special distinction lies in training and sending out people of this kind. You are the common property of the nation. You are not people who will forget this because the worthy degrees you received today belong to you; nevertheless, I have come to say it and I say it now.

If any country is to escape distress and degradation, dispel darkness, and attain advancement, generations of intellectuals must continue to arise within it. A powerful army of knowledge must continually emerge to examine and explain what is good and what is evil, what is ours and what was given by others, what is tradition and what is delusion, and to walk accordingly so that the people attain dignity. What other training ground could there be for that task? This university is that training ground, a fortress of Nakkeerar established to teach the good arts by which the country may acquire manifold prosperity.

The National Education Commission of 1964-66 emphasised this. In substance, it stated that a university exists to produce people who submit to no one, do not lean whichever way they are pulled, think and discover the truth, explain clearly what they have learned, refuse servitude, and stand without fear.

There must be inquiry and the courage appropriate to it. Otherwise, we shall become accomplices in imprisoning thought and allowing cruelty to hold sway, warns the great Bertrand Russell.

Two thousand years ago, the learned who sat here said the same. We forgot many such elevating teachings in the intervening years and suffered. Today the wise in other parts of the world proclaim them. We realise that this strength has long been ours. We understand that we are Tamils, and we resolve to labour so that the whole world may know it.

The sacred day on which you receive your degrees is undoubtedly a day of joyful celebration. Yet it is not meant for joy alone; it is also a good day for making a resolve, a day to realise that we dedicate ourselves to the noble work of protecting the country.

### An Old Story

Madurai University is only a year old. There are universities in our country that have already celebrated their centenaries. Madurai University was not created merely as one more added to the ten already in existence. It was founded with a particular need of the Tamil people in mind.

When universities were founded a hundred years ago, the country had lost self-rule and stood with its head bowed. The people's hearts were in anguish. Disorder prevailed in many fields. It was a time not merely of general ignorance but of a spreading belief that the people of this country were incapable of knowing anything. People entered universities with an overriding sense of wonder at the arts that Westerners supposedly introduced, arts which, it was imagined, no one here had ever contemplated before. Even those who undertook to teach in those days approached their lessons with pity. Wonder at what had been seen in foreign lands received first place; the question of what our own country had known was pushed aside.

The older universities grew in these circumstances. They were regarded as institutions established by masters to impart knowledge to their subjects out of kindness and to obtain workers suited to their needs.

Very many of the scholars trained and graduated there fashioned themselves, in gait, dress, and outlook, into foreign forms living in the land of their birth, and took great pride in doing so. That condition lasted a long time. Today it is an old story.

### A Distinct Character Is Needed

Madurai University arose in days when a people who had broken the chains of slavery walked abroad awakened, proclaiming that we had gained both the right and the fitness to govern.

Ranks of heroes gathered in response to Bharathi's valiant summons, "Come with eyes filled with light! Come! Come! Come!" This university arose afterward.

We saw not only the liberation of the country but also the gradual breaking of mental fetters. This university was born in that atmosphere. We understand it to be a fortress dedicated to the principle that thought is our right.

The beggar's mentality that everything exists abroad and nothing in our country has died. We possess a treasury of worthy ideas for which people of every country may come asking, "Give us some." It lies like a tarnished gem or a faded painting. This is a time in which awareness has risen sufficiently for us to declare, "We shall wipe away the stain and reveal the gem's radiance." This infant, Madurai University, has received that awareness as its foster mother.

Therefore, this university must have a distinctive character and a path of its own. It must become a citadel that enables the world to see Tamil Nadu's treasury of ideas. We thrill when we read the history of those who engraved the tiger, engraved the fish, defeated the Aryan army, conquered the Ganges, and vanquished Kadaram. In a manner worthy of that history, we must create a new history that makes scholars throughout the world praise those who gave the Kural, those who shed the light of Manimekalai, and those who revealed the inner and outer domains of life. To marshal the forces required for this must become the university's special ability. Its curriculum, method of training, and system of examination should all serve this distinctive purpose. Those responsible must study this. I express the wish in the confidence that those entrusted with giving it form will act.

Cast off fear, inquire, raise the condition of the country, display and share our unique wealth with other nations: declare these to be our tasks. I warmly invite you to prove through word and deed that the degrees we have received certify that we have reached this state.

### Our Task

These ideas are sweet to read on paper, suitable for explanation in speech, and worthy of admiration. But putting them into practice is not easy, and following them continuously is difficult. Unfailing determination, undiminished enthusiasm, tireless labour, steadiness of purpose, and rejection of selfish interest are all urgently needed. I believe the university will yield, and has yielded, this benefit. I ask you to prove the answer "Yes" through your distinguished service.

When things that are needless, harmful, meaningless, or inappropriate are found lingering under the name of antiquity or protected by affection as our possessions, an ethical struggle must be organised to remove them without indulgence.

Do not assume that something must be wise simply because a particular person said it. Give first place not to who said it but to what was said. If it is true, let us preserve it even at the cost of life; if it is not, let us oppose and remove it even if life itself must go. With that cry, I call upon the worthy graduates to rise and join the revolution in the realm of knowledge.

If a scorpion comes from our own country, will its sting feel cool? If honey comes from another country, will it taste bitter?

Whatever its source, if it is appropriate, we need the outlook that makes it our own. We must work earnestly to provide it and to derive benefit from it.

### The Vanguard

Universities have grown and are growing; educational institutions are increasing. Yet when we compare the number of graduates with the population even today, we can understand how small our progress remains. That is why the people expect so much from you, who have received an opportunity denied to very many good people, to raise the country's condition. You are the vanguard setting forth to defeat the enemies that surround this land: deprivation, insufficiency, ignorance, and baseness. Remove the reproach that graduates merely hurry to use their certificates to obtain comfortable lives. I bless you: may you succeed.

If graduates remain without a goal, the country will lose its stability and the future will not be beautiful. I believe that aimlessness gives rise to mental confusion, agitation, shock, clamour, disorderly actions, harmful conduct, the creation of strife, and refusal to accept discipline.

Many have expressed concern that harmful tendencies have become widespread among the youth of our country, particularly among students. I have not lost faith in our young people or students. On the contrary, I believe there is no fundamental defect in their nature. Their nature becomes distorted and the straight path is blocked because a worthy goal has not been presented to them properly and because they see that many who offer advice are trapped by selfish interest and commit improper acts. Harmful ways then appear sweet.

### The Duty of Elders

Therefore, if young people and students are to possess good character, it is today's urgent need that others ensure their words and actions serve the public good.

At the same time, I ask students to understand how precious the opportunity given to them is and to shape their conduct accordingly.

Do people dive into the roaring sea and retrieve pearls merely to fasten them around a parrot's neck? Is not the woman for whom it was brought the one entitled to it?

Is sandalwood ground into paste so that it may give delight when applied, or is it meant to fuel a stove? Degrees and training are obtained to give the people a good life, not merely to shoot arrows of complaint asking, "What are they giving me, and why this delay?" No one should speak in that manner.

I am among those who have great faith in you. We can expect the ability to act from people such as you whom the poet had in mind when he sang, "Let us make a new world."

I said that we can expect noble action from students when the goal is clearly set. The curriculum, training methods, and habits cultivated in educational institutions must be arranged so that students can acquire this goal.

As the number of educational institutions grows without a corresponding increase in government revenue, it has become impossible to provide them with all the conditions under which they ought to function. The government is working to improve this condition gradually. It may take many years even to provide the basic necessities. Great sums must be given for that alone. We must also consider the circumstances in which this money is obtained and provided.

### This Is Not a Dream

The tax money paid by those who never see a full stomach, those who labour until their backs break, those who never taste life's pleasures, those who are pawns of the powerful, and the downtrodden becomes our forts, flagstaffs, roads, displays of splendour, officers who administer the law, and institutions in which knowledge is acquired.

Sweat becomes money and is collected as tax. With it, institutions from universities down to primary schools have been built. If students understand what a fortress of sacrifice this country is today, and understand that amid such poverty its people give us a great opportunity they themselves never received, their sense of responsibility will increase. They will know that engaging in appropriate work is the gratitude we show to the poor who have given us so much. That will bring a new turn in the student world. Am I dreaming? No. I speak with knowledge of the minds of students. The future belongs to them. It is their responsibility to free it from darkness, and they have the ability needed for it. This is my faith. History shows that in every country students have stood in the forefront and served in distinguished movements. The period in which you are students today is a time when new history is being written in our country.

Ideals that can be won only through hard labour stand before us. Yet beside them are placed beguiling attractions and intoxicating speeches under the name of goals. We must discern clearly.

### What Is an Ideal?

There must be unity in the country. That is an ideal acceptable to everyone, and it is the duty of students to perform arduous work for its success.

But to say that for national unity you must consent to the destruction of your language and tradition and accept the domination of another language is something no intelligent person can accept and every self-respecting person must oppose. To call that an ideal or a plan only stains the very meaning of "ideal."

The country's common wealth must grow every day, and everyone must labour in the manner required for it. That is an ideal all can accept. But to say, "Let the nation's wealth increase; do not worry even if it becomes trapped in the hands of a few," is not an ideal. It makes a mockery of one.

"If we live together, joy will multiply a millionfold" is a sweet and simple ideal.

But arranging matters so that what is yours is mine and what is mine remains mine is not living together. Those who seek "unity" must not behave like the dog that devoured a lamb and then declared, "The goat has now become one with me; we have become inseparable siblings."

### Tamil Is Our Language

Graduates, I desire that through your clear explanations the people should receive understanding.

I have elaborated upon these matters because the university is the proper place to assemble a powerful force capable of driving out ignorance and poverty.

We see the phrases "medium of instruction," "language of study," "official language," and "link language" circulating rapidly.

No language needs any qualification equal to that of being one's mother tongue. Yet our Tamil wins the wonder and praise of everyone not merely because it is our mother tongue but also because it is rich. It is your duty to perfect the measures that will preserve that richness and enable it to grow further. Your sincere enthusiasm is greatly needed to succeed in creating the conditions for Tamil to function as the official language in every field.

Knowledge of English is indispensable if we are to live in step with the world and draw upon the world's treasury of knowledge.

Let English remain the link language among the various parts of India and between India and foreign countries. Some describe this position as unpatriotic and lacking in national self-respect. I say only that they "describe" it so; with reason, I do not say they truly "think" so.

### The Language Question

Those who say that accepting English as a link language is devoid of national self-respect because it was formerly the language of the rulers do not call it destructive of national self-respect when they receive money from that English country as loans and grants to develop industry. Nor do we hear this argument when they seek English assistance to obtain technical knowledge. They make this argument only about language. That is why I said they do not actually hold the view that English must not be used; they merely say so.

When, along with saying that English must not be used, they insist upon another language as suitable for linking India, the speaker's real intention becomes quite clear.

I know that the student world stands firm in its determination not to let this tendency succeed. I convey my appreciation to the students who stand as examples of devotion to language.

At the same time, I ask you confidently to entrust to the Government of Tamil Nadu, which has complete faith in devotion to language, the efforts to remove both the complications inherent in the language question and those deliberately introduced into it.

### Go Forth! Bring Back Victory!

While studying at a university, it is proper to understand that this is the period spent in camp before the long, great journey of life.

Friends receiving degrees, I ask you to recognise that the country expects much from you and to shape your methods of action accordingly.

History tells us that in the days of crowned kings, armies that departed from this great Madurai won victories admired throughout the land.

The army departing today is an army of knowledge, the first contingent formed by this university and setting forth into the world. I am truly proud to have the opportunity to bless, welcome, and praise it, and to say: go forth and bring back victory.

Let Tamil be your drum, culture your armour, knowledge your weapon, and the path of virtue your companion. Go with determination; labour with enthusiasm; win exalted victories. Go forth so that our homeland of the language of song may flourish and gain the worthiness to support the world. Go forth! Be victorious!
