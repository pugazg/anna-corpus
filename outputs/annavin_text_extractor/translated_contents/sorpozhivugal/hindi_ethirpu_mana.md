என்னை வற்புறுத்துகிறார்கள் என்று கொஞ்சம் ஆச்சரியப்
பட்டேன். இப்பொழுது அது தீர்ந்து விட்டது.

என்னை உட்கார வைத்துக் கொண்டே என்னைப் பற்றிப்
பேசிய தம்பிமார்களெல்லாம் “வழிகாட்ட வேண்டும்; திட்டங்கள்

இங்கே யாருக்கும் தெரியாத ஒன்று என்னிடம்
மறைக்கப்பட்டுக் கிடப்பது போலவும், எல்லோரும் அதை
எதிர்பார்த்து ஏங்கிக் கிடப்பது போலவும், நான் அதைக் காட்ட

உங்கள் முன்னிலையிலேயே கூறித் திகைப்படையும்படிச் செய்யும்
காரியத்தைத் தம்பிமார்களெல்லாம் மிகச் சாமர்த்தியமாகச்
செய்து முடித்துவிட்டார்கள். இங்கே இவ்வளவு பெரிய
மாநாட்டினைக் கூட்டிச், சோர்வுற்ற நெஞ்சம் களிப்படையவும்,
புதிய நம்பிக்கை ஏற்படவும், அந்த நம்பிக்கையைத் தி. மு. கழகம்
பெறவும் நல்ல வாய்ப்பை ஏற்படுத்தியிருக்கிறார்கள்.

இப்பெரிய மாநாட்டில், தாய்மார்களும், வாலிபர்களும்
மட்டுமின்றி வயது முதிர்ந்தவர்களும் கூடியிகுக்கிறீர்கள். நீங்கள்
காட்டும் உற்சாகமும், உறுதியும்தான் எங்களுக்கு உதவும். அறப்போரில்
நாங்கள் ஈடுபட இம் மாநாடு வழிகோலும் என்று நம்புகிறேன்.

ஈடுபடுத்தித் தொல்லை தரக்கூடாது என்பதுதான். இதனாலே

நேரத்தில், அதே மேடையில் நான் வேண்டாம் என்னுடைய

## English Translation

Summary of Arignar Anna’s presidential address at the Anti-Hindi Conference held at Anna Nagar, Kodambakkam, Madras, on 1 August 1960:

Comrades!

“I wondered a little why they compelled me to preside over this conference, a task I did not desire. Now the reason has become clear.

“All the younger brothers who spoke about me while keeping me seated here asked me to show the way and provide plans.

“They have very cleverly made it appear that something unknown to anyone here lies hidden within me, that everyone longs for it, and that I keep saying, ‘I will not reveal it.’ They have put the matter before you and left me astonished. By assembling this great conference, they have created a fine opportunity for weary hearts to rejoice, for new hope to arise, and for the DMK to receive that hope.

“At this great conference, not only mothers and young people but the aged too have assembled. Your enthusiasm and resolve will help us. I trust that this conference will guide us into a moral struggle.

“Ever since the DMK arose, I have never suffered an itch to conduct agitations. The movement does not exist only if it conducts a struggle; circumstances themselves will carry it to that point.

“The DMK has developed into a great movement with a distinctive character. It will not surrender its mind to futile agitations.

“If opponents try to destroy the feeling of liberation and erect barriers against it, we will struggle only to cut apart that net of affection and shatter their cunning into fragments.

“Whenever my younger brothers cried, ‘Struggle! Struggle!’ I hesitated and told them, ‘Wait, be patient.’ I do not deny it, nor am I ashamed. My conduct demonstrates the profound respect a leader must possess. My brothers were deeply disappointed that I had not given them a plan of struggle. Scattering plans for agitation is no great accomplishment; it is very easy. But the DMK is not such a movement.

“In the present situation, we resolved at Kumarapalayam that a struggle must be conducted. A separate committee was formed under the chairmanship of E. V. K. Sampath, not merely so that we could display an account showing that a committee existed, but because no path other than struggle remained.

“There is no way except a moral struggle. Once every door has been closed, failing to struggle would leave us without honour, righteous anger, or dignity. That is why we resolved to enter the campaign.

“We must win this struggle, or else dissolve this movement. We are not people who run a movement merely as an eleventh item added to ten others.

“All my plans have sought to avoid involving the people in struggle and causing them hardship. This does not mean I doubt my younger brothers’ courage. I do not want the knowledge and ability the people can receive through them to be wasted in a futile agitation.

“Our General Council at Kumarapalayam resolved to enter a moral struggle if the President’s order imposing Hindi was not withdrawn.

“If they refrain from trying to suppress us through repression and from political meanness, I am prepared to accept a challenge offered in an honourable manner.

“Let Congress members explain in a public forum that Hindi poses no danger. On the same platform, do not invite me; choose one of my younger brothers, even the weakest among them, and ask him what harm Hindi will cause. Let him explain its dangers before the public, and let the ministers answer by explaining its supposed benefits. Then hold a vote at that very meeting on whether Hindi is needed. If the ministers are ready, we offer them a golden opportunity.

“Minister Subramaniam, speaking on opposition to Hindi and the language issue, asked, ‘Will Anna contest an election on the demand for national separation?’ Because he asked, I have decided definitely to stand in the same Kanchipuram constituency. I do not worry about what I will do if defeated; I will certainly stand. Had the Finance Minister said that he would oppose me in that constituency, one could say he possessed political understanding and courage. He did not. I declare that I will stand and ask whether he is ready to stand too.

“They have become northern overseers and resolved to remain in office even by betraying Tamils. We oppose Hindi. Yet some imagine the problem will be solved if, as a leisure amusement, they gather old men under the name of a youth forum and speak two words against Hindi; write two editorials in a newspaper; or, after seeing countries governed through their mother tongues, place a few people in an obscure little hall and say that imposing Hindi is wrong.

“Because the DMK has accepted responsibility for conducting a moral struggle against Hindi, some think they can unleash hooliganism and suppress it.

“This time, repression will run rampant against our moral campaign. Many among us may lose arms, legs, or other limbs; some may lose their very lives. I do not say this as ornament. You must prepare yourselves for every sacrifice.

“If our comrades are beaten, doctors will be needed to treat them. If false conspiracy charges are laid and cases filed against them, lawyers will be required to argue for us. That is why we resolved to exempt doctors, lawyers, and actors from direct participation.”

Summary of Anna’s instructions at the conference on conduct during the black-flag demonstration against the President:

“Whatever struggle we conduct must stand upon morality, do no injury to peace, and allow not the smallest violence to raise its head.

“If noise arises during our campaign, it will resemble the childish game of young cyclists who, finding the ordinary sound insufficient, tie a palm strip to the wheel to make more noise and delight in it. I ask that no one indulge in such childishness.

“If violence truly arises, it may arise from the police or the government, but it must never arise from our comrades. We must endure violent acts and repression from the government and never behave as counterattackers. We need not ask whether we possess the power to resist; what we must ask now is whether we have the strength of heart to endure.

“While displaying black flags, no one should tell the President, ‘Go back.’ We must shout only: ‘Down with Hindi! Withdraw the order!’

“No one must throw anything at the President’s car, and no one must approach the building where he stays.

“If anyone disobeys me and does something of that kind, I will not even call them traitors. I will simply say that they are not my younger brothers.”

When Arignar Anna concluded, he summoned the struggle committee’s members, including Mayor Abdul Khader, and personally distributed black flags.

The people cheered and shouted: “We too are ready! Long live Anna! Dravida Nadu for the Dravidians!”

Across the country, Kazhagam comrades studied the details of the President’s tour in preparation for participating in black-flag events.

English and Tamil newspapers wrote clearly that the President’s directive violated Prime Minister Nehru’s assurance and supported the Kazhagam.

Pant’s Explanation

In Parliament, Pandit Pant explained that the President’s order imposed no condition requiring non-Hindi-speaking applicants for Indian government employment to pass a compulsory Hindi examination. He said that the government had not accepted the Official Language Commission’s recommendation to make the Hindi examination compulsory.

After this, the Madras Finance Minister stated that the DMK no longer needed to display black flags, that Nehru’s assurances had not been violated, and that any desired change could be discussed collectively and requested from the Indian government.

On 3 August 1960, struggle committee chairman Sampath wrote to the Indian Prime Minister. Pandit Nehru replied:

“The government will never have occasion to implement anything contrary to the assurance I gave in the Lok Sabha concerning the language problem.

“We remain bound by the assurance we gave.”

In this situation, an emergency meeting of the DMK Executive Committee was convened on 4 August 1960.

The committee expressed happiness that those in power appeared eager to honour the new assurances faithfully.

It stressed that the Constitution must certainly be amended to resolve the language problem and that this was the best course.

The committee resolved that the President’s assurance that he would act according to Pandit Nehru’s word, and Pant’s announcement that Nehru’s promise had not been violated, constituted a major victory for the Kazhagam. It concluded that the situation and necessity for displaying black flags no longer existed.

Thereafter, the country and its people rejoiced at the Kazhagam’s victory. The President completed his tour of the state peacefully and returned to Delhi.

Victory Celebration

On 7 August 1960, a victory meeting was held at Madras Marina under the chairmanship of General Secretary Navalar.

More than two lakh people gathered at Triplicane Beach to celebrate the first victory in the anti-Hindi campaign, achieved by the Kazhagam without the black-flag agitation taking place.

The immense gathering rejoiced as Kazhagam leaders explained the nature of that wholly unexpected victory.

When Anna finally declared, “This victory will be inscribed in golden letters in the history of the Kazhagam,” all those assembled welcomed it with a great outburst of joy.
