## English Translation

[On Tuesday, 19 February 1943, at 4:30 p.m., a debate was held in the Madras Law College hall on the *Kamba Ramayanam* and the *Periya Puranam*, described as Aryan texts. Comrade C. M. Ramachandran Chettiar, B.A., B.L., chairman of the Hindu Religious Endowments Board, presided. C. N. Annadurai, M.A., Eelathadigal, B.A., R. P. Sethu Pillai, B.A., B.L., and Srinivasan took part and presented their views.]

## Proceedings

Although the meeting had not been advertised, people assembled in great numbers. Several hundred members of the Self-Respect Movement, Law College students, scholars, and many women filled the hall.

Proposing the chairman, Venu Gopalan, secretary of the Law College Tamil Association, said: “It is natural for Tamil people to be angry when Periyar E. V. Ramasamy says that the *Kamba Ramayanam* and *Periya Puranam*, works treasured by Tamils, should be burned or destroyed. But the Self-Respecters' resolution must not be dismissed. We have therefore invited Comrade Annadurai to explain their view. I would compare him to Indrajit. Mr Sethu Pillai has come like Lord Rama to refute him, and the distinguished Ramachandran Chettiar has come like King Janaka to stand between them. I hope the debate will be conducted at the highest level.”

The chairman, C. M. Ramachandran Chettiar, said: “The subject is extremely important, and those who have come to debate it are able men. The discussion will therefore remain on a high plane. It is proper for me to speak only after hearing them. Without saying anything in advance, I first invite Comrade Annadurai to speak.”

## 1. Annadurai's Address

Mr Chairman, mothers, and comrades! I thank the Law College Tamil Association for arranging this debate and inviting me. The secretary called me Indrajit, but no one need imagine that I shall launch magical weapons. Nor is today to be a *Yuddha Kanda*. I hope my friend Sethu Pillai, who follows me, will live up to his name by building a bridge across the gulf between Puranic scholars and rationalists.

I do not grow angry at debates; I welcome them. It gives me great pleasure to debate the learned Sethu Pillai under the chairmanship of the eminent Tamil and Saivite Ramachandran Chettiar, in a Law College that prepares judges and advocates for the courts. The debate will be conducted on a high plane.

When we criticise the *Ramayanam* and *Periya Puranam*, the learned people gathered here will understand that we condemn the falsehoods and obscenities that entered them. Ordinary crowds become angry at the mere mention of criticism. There is a saying that Hanuman is present wherever Rama's story is read. No roaring Hanuman is present here at the criticism of that story, so the discussion can proceed at a high level.

The secretary said it was natural for the public to be angered by E. V. Ramasamy's demand that these works be burned. We know that. Every cause we began and every idea we introduced first met opposition; the people grew angry and later joined us in our camps. We have not begun this in order to wound anyone.

We know the accusation that we destroy art and obscure Kamban's fame. But if those deeply devoted to art think that Kamban's *Ramayanam* and Sekkizhar's *Periya Puranam* can be destroyed by one elder's struggle or one Annadurai's flame, what they call art must be very frail indeed. Such art need not exist. We do not seek to wreck art; with good reason, we seek a revolution within it.

Art expresses a people's mental character and exemplifies its clarity and courage. It changes and expands with the people's growth. Art also develops according to place, climate, and temperature. Arab art cannot abound in poetry about a cool southern breeze; Eskimo art cannot abound in verse about the brilliance of the sun. The dance of the Zulu will appear in African art. Aryan art similarly contains the fascination of the Ganges, its banks, and its groves.

India is a subcontinent inhabited by many peoples and therefore contains many arts. Yet scholars identify two great formations: Aryan art and Dravidian art. Hindu and Islamic art may remain distinct without colliding, but Aryan and Dravidian art have been mutually opposed. As a result, Aryanism came to dominate Dravidian art and law. Mr Nelson, once a legal authority at this college, pointed out that Hindu law rests on Aryan works by Manu, Parasara, and Yajnavalkya, and that imposing it upon the non-Brahmin majority of the south, who are not Aryans, is wrong. His warning became a cry in the wilderness. Hindu law, Aryan justice, governs us today; Tamils possess neither a law like their national wealth nor the justice of the *Kural*. This is what resulted from allowing Aryan dominance in art.

We need art that gives Tamils Tamil ethics, customs, morality, courage, chastity, and love. We demand the destruction of stories, epics, and literature that glorify another people, establish their dominance, rob Tamils of confidence, and make them despise their own community. By “Tamil” I mean not merely speakers of the Tamil language but the Tamil people.

We are not hostile to all art, literature, and imaginative writing. We have not touched the *Tolkappiyam*, *Natrinai*, *Kuruntokai*, *Kalithokai*, *Akam*, or *Puram*. We condemn works that insert Aryanism beautifully in order to destroy Tamils. If the *Tolkappiyam* is a treasury of sayings by earlier scholars, the *Kamba Ramayanam*, composed seven hundred years ago, must draw upon much older literature. When the ancient originals survive, will literature or art die if a later *Ramayanam* that introduced Aryanism is destroyed? Are these two books all the literature and art Tamils possess?

We did not invent this controversy. Max Muller argued for Aryan art and Sir John Marshall for Dravidian art. There was a time when Indian art was assumed to mean Aryan art and when Triplicane and Mylapore rejoiced in Aryan religion and civilisation. As a child I studied a work called *Arya Matha Upakhyanam*. Later, Professor Sundaram Pillai, V. P. Subramania Mudaliar, and Maraimalai Adigal explained the greatness of Dravidian civilisation. They spoke even more severely than we do of Aryanism making an Aryan prince, Rama, into a god and reducing Tamils to worshippers of lesser deities. Even Pandit Jawahar, who differs from us politically, called the *Ramayanam* the story of an Aryan-Dravidian struggle.

Research therefore concludes that the *Ramayanam* is an Aryan story and a record of that conflict. Kamban's treatment encourages Tamils to accept Aryanism and confess that the Tamil people were defeated by an Aryan leader. Readers lose self-confidence and dignity. To revive Tamil, we say this Aryan art must go. This is the consequence of ethnic awakening. Whether it can be done is not a question for idealists. Our victory depends upon your cooperation; opposition will only give us the strength to meet it. We do not seek to destroy art or literature. Through a revolution in art, we demand the awakening and liberation of a people.

Sethu Pillai will speak of Kamban's poetic genius, ornament, and similes. I have often enjoyed hearing him do so and know his “Kamban paintings” in *Senthamizh Selvi*. But neither he nor this audience should misunderstand us. We do not dispute Kamban's poetic ability. This is not an academy of Sattanar, Ottakoothar, and Pugazhendi deciding whether he possessed talent. Ability is one thing, character another, and effect a third. We admire Kamban's ability, are dismayed that it supports Aryanism, and suffer because its effect has lowered Tamils into subjection. Why did Kamban sing Rama's story?

Ilango Adigal clearly declared that he composed the *Silappathikaram* so that the world would praise the chaste wife, a government that departed from justice would perish, and each person's deeds would return upon him. Kamban gives no such purpose. Instead, with a troubled heart, he wonders whether the world will mock him and whether stain will attach to him. Why such anxiety about singing a story praised as the Lord's incarnation? Did he sense that singing an Aryan tale and decorating it would injure Tamils?

Kamban says three poets composed the story in the “language of the gods” and that he took the first, Valmiki, as his source. Aryans call Sanskrit the divine language and themselves earthly gods. By accepting that label without qualification, did Kamban accept them as gods and Tamils as inferior, and make Tamils accept it too? Can that aid a people's awakening?

The very talent for which scholars praise Kamban helped ruin Tamils. His skill in portraying motives and actions transformed flawed Aryan leaders into noble men worthy of Tamil worship. Had he followed Valmiki in Sita's words when Rama left for the forest, Lakshmana's denunciation of Kaikeyi, and Ravana's seizure of Sita, Tamils would have seen the obscenity in those characters and not worshipped them. Kamban's poetry instead made Aryan Rama faultless and divine.

He describes Ravana as powerful, learned in the Vedas, a devotee of Siva, and ruler of a prosperous Lanka, but ends by saying that this gifted ruler saw an Aryan woman, succumbed to lust, abandoned virtue, and perished. That wounds Dravidian dignity. Does it suggest that however capable and learned we are, we will lose control before an Aryan woman? Ravana was lord of the beautiful Mandodari and attended by celestial women; if even he was bewitched by Sita, are ordinary Dravidians to think themselves helpless? Such a story harms Dravidians and Aryans alike.

People say the epic contains heroism, sacrifice, friendship, chastity, and love. Our *Akam* literature contains subtler and finer poetry on love and chastity, so scholars need not fear their disappearance with the *Kamba Ramayanam*. They praise the friendship of Rama and Guha, formed on sight. The Sangam poem about Kopperuncholan and Pisiranthaiyar celebrates an unchanging friendship between two men who never saw each other, knowing one another only by reputation. Friendship will not be forgotten if Kamban's work is removed.

They praise Rama's sacrifice in giving up the throne and entering the forest with a face like a newly blossomed lotus. Valmiki says his face changed when he learned he had lost the kingdom; Kamban polished it. Even accepting Kamban's version, greater sacrifices exist. Rama renounced the kingdom under his father's order and stepmother's cruelty; Ilango Adigal voluntarily renounced it. Why must Tamils learn sacrifice through the hardship of an Aryan prince? In our own time we saw Edward give up his crown for love.

Compare Sita's words to Rama when he tells her not to enter the forest with Kannagi's gentle reception of Kovalan after he squandered youth, beauty, and wealth on a courtesan and returned poor. With the *Silappathikaram* before us, do we need Kamban's Aryan story to teach chastity?

Nor is an Aryan Rama story the only way to teach courage. Rama's war was not between humans: an incarnation of Vishnu won through magical weapons. That is divine power, not human valour. When Ravana took Sita, courage would have meant marching straight to Lanka or sending Lakshmana to Ayodhya for the kingdom's army. Bharata, who crowned Rama's sandals, would not have refused. Instead, like Hitler acquiring the Romanian army, Rama obtained a monkey army by entering a quarrel between brothers and sent it against Lanka. Call that statecraft or Aryan dharma, but do not call it courage.

Call Cheran Senguttuvan's march to the Ganges courage. Call Rajendra's fleet and conquest of Burma courage. In the *Kalingathupparani*, a king orders his commander to break Kalinga's mountain fort with elephants while the fleet accompanies the land army; there we see courage, strategy, and the excellence of warfare. Which quality makes the *Kamba Ramayanam* indispensable?

Do those who cry “art” understand the public? Ninety-three out of a hundred are unlettered. Scholars discuss metre and ornament only among people with leisure and preparation, then despair that the rest cannot understand. When the ordinary person hears “Ramayanam,” he knows to fall at the feet of a gigantic Hanuman and worship Aryans, not to enjoy the work as poetic fiction. Have our scholars gone among the people and taught them that Puranas and epics are imaginative literature offering moral reflections, not gods to be housed in temples and worshipped?

Under Sun Yat-sen, the Chinese reportedly worshipped many gods. He asked the learned whether one supreme god was enough. When they agreed, he told them to retain one name and strike the others out in red. The people then accepted one god. Here Saivite leaders have proclaimed Siva the sole supreme deity and condemned lesser worship for fifty years, yet have not stopped even the custom of wearing neem leaves for the goddess of Periyapalayam.

In countries with developed public reason, people retain the poetry of old legends but discard obscenity and superstition. Greeks and Romans left Venus and Apollo after accepting Christ; Britons forgot Thor and Odin. Here people refuse to remove even one Aryan lesser deity. What have scholars done to correct the minds that treat Aryan inventions as gods?

People still call Ravana's taking of Sita a lustful act. Yet seizing cattle, women, or forts belonged to the warfare of that age. After Rama dishonoured Ravana's sister, Sita was the only valuable thing available to seize in order to draw him into battle. Why may that account not be stated?

Can any humiliation be imposed upon a people in art's name? We are accused of atheism and of destroying morality by touching art. Ignore our description and even Gandhi's severe words; hear a Saivite elder's description of temples: towers overgrown with plants, broken walls, thorn-filled corridors, rubbish in halls, cattle grazing, tanks covered in moss, dark spaces filled with bat droppings, dangerous paths, oil-blackened walls, smoke, dust, stench, worms, insects, rats and cats in sanctums, dirty bodies and mouths intoxicated by powders. A worshipper who returns without encountering all these “holy” things is fortunate. Those are the words of today's chairman at Mailam. If morality collapses when we touch art, who created such decay before we touched it?

We condemn the *Periya Puranam* because it lowers the people. If it was written to show that a devotee will do anything for faith, why portray God imposing ordeals filled with cruelty? Nowhere else are devotees tested by demands for a child's flesh, a wife, or an eye. What happens to the minds of people who believe such stories? These Puranas destroy public reason, subordinate Dravidians, erase their character, and corrupt common minds. That is why we condemn the *Kamba Ramayanam* and *Periya Puranam* and say: burn them.

After Sethu Pillai answers, I hope to reply. Scholars should not mistake us. We are their allies and they our defence. Others wait to create hostility between us, like Rama shooting from behind a tree. By failing to tell people that these Puranas are fiction, scholars have allowed their minds and condition to decline. Tirumular spoke elsewhere of those who reject the guru who removes blindness, follow a blind guru, and fall blind into the blind pit. Recognise that as our people's condition.

## 2. R. P. Sethu Pillai's Rebuttal

You enjoyed Annadurai's long address, rich in meaning and verbal beauty. Debate with such eminent men is pleasurable. But he spoke for more than an hour and a half. Perhaps his case was weak and required so much time to make falsehood resemble truth. I shall speak for ten minutes and not test your patience.

My friend cited ancient Tamil works, called Kannagi one of his lineage, and spoke well of Kopperuncholan and Pisiranthaiyar. He said a northern Pandit Jawahar, southern Sundaram Pillai, and V. P. Subramania Mudaliar between them called the *Ramayanam* an Aryan-Dravidian war. He is supposedly of Nakkirar's line, which says a fault remains a fault even when shown the forehead-eye. Why rely on northern Jawahar rather than state the view as his own?

They call Ravana a Dravidian. The term was not used by the ancients; did Gnanasambandar say “behold the Aryan, behold the Tamil”? Some hold that Dravidam is merely a changed form of Tamil. I deny that Ravana was a Dravidian or Tamil. He possessed all the abilities described, but lacked one thing and was destroyed by its absence: compassion. Kamban called him a demon without compassion. Had he possessed it, he could not have been destroyed. Is this compassionless Ravana to be called the ancestor of Tamils? Are Tamils without compassion?

Though a devotee of Siva who knew Mount Kailasa to be Siva's abode, Ravana tried to uproot the mountain. What sort of devotee was he? He then sang the Sama Veda and received from Siva a long sword with which to kill others. Ravana was not Tamil; he spoke Aryan language. When Hanuman found Sita in the Asoka grove, he wondered what language to use. If he spoke Aryan, Sita might suspect that Ravana, master of disguises, had come as a monkey, so he did not speak the northern language. Kamban says Ravana descended from Pulastya, an Aryan. Ravana was therefore Aryan. The *Thevaram* says Rama incurred *brahmahatya* by killing Ravana and performed Siva worship to remove it. How could that sin arise unless Ravana was a Brahmin? I therefore reject my friend's case.

Kamban lived when Tamil Nadu had fallen into disorder and wished to restore public pride. He took a story known to all and filled it with Tamil art. In his poem Sita appears a Tamil woman. Valmiki says Rama broke a bow and married her; Kamban, valuing the Tamil ideal of a love marriage, made Rama and Sita see and love one another: “The noble one looked; she too looked. Eye caught eye.” Here we see Tamil dignity.

Kamban understood Kannagi's greatness and portrayed Sita overflowing with the chastity Ilango celebrated. His Sita bears the form of the Tamil chaste wife. The *Kamba Ramayanam* is therefore a treasure for Tamils. My friend says Kamban coated defective Aryan characters with polish. It is the duty of the great to forgive the small faults of the lesser; this demonstrates Kamban's magnanimity. Ravana had courage and honour but no compassion, and was destroyed by arrogance. I regret that I cannot speak longer and shall return to the subject. I deny that Ravana was Dravidian.

## 3. Eelathadigal's Address

I intended to explain the subject fully, but the chairman has restricted me to five minutes because time has passed. I regret that I cannot make it as clear as I wished.

Some Tamil scholars are now anxious to suppress our Self-Respect agitation. Newspapers write condemnations and scholars show agitation and alarm. Why? We have campaigned for years to abolish the *Manusmriti* and *Ramayanam*, and scholars knew it but remained silent. They act now because we have added one more work, which they guard as life itself: the *Periya Puranam*.

The *Kamba Ramayanam* is Vaishnavite and the *Periya Puranam* Saivite. Saivite scholars welcomed the accusations against the former, but when defects in their own work were exposed, those who call Saivism the foundation of love and compassion fired arrows at us. Their anger is only the disorderly, selfish outcry of pretenders unconcerned with public welfare.

Annadurai must answer Sethu Pillai's unsuitable claims, and my time does not permit me to do so. I shall make only one point. Sethu Pillai has studied and lectured on the *Ramayanam* for more than twenty years, yet never before today established the subtle discovery that Ravana was Aryan. No comparable Tamil scholar has done so either. Calling the Dravidian king Ravana Aryan is unsupported by research or reason. But if these scholars produce evidence that he was Aryan, then Kamban, a Tamil poet, degraded Dravidians still further by singing of two Aryan kings. We shall regret not having burned that unalloyed Aryan tale sooner and hasten to put it in the fire. The intelligent world will nevertheless never deny or forget the virtues of Ravana, who ruled without departing from justice.

Sethu Pillai said nothing about the *Periya Puranam*, so we must conclude that he accepts our charges against it. If anyone can prove our charges wrong, we are ready to answer. We will allow not one month but two or three, patiently hear the case, answer rationally, and only then burn the books. Do not delay your case; act quickly.

A Law College student named Srinivasan then said that Aryan and Dravidian blood had mixed from ancient times and that it was improper to distinguish them now. Members of the audience called the claim baseless and a small disturbance arose, whereupon he stopped speaking.

## 4. Annadurai's Reply

Comrades, Sethu Pillai told me he was unwell and left, so I shall not reply at length. He answered none of my arguments about the nature of art, peoples, or the harm Aryan art caused Tamils. Like someone who cuts a rope when unable to untie a knot, he simply called Ravana an Aryan. That is neither argument nor evidence.

He asked why I cited Pandit Jawahar. You understand the political satire. I disagree with Jawahar politically; I cited him for friends here who admire him. Many other authorities could be named. Ravana's knowledge of Sanskrit does not make him Aryan. Does speaking English turn a Tamil into an Englishman? Tamil scholars know Sanskrit even today; the Sanskrit pandit at Pachaiyappa's College is himself Tamil.

Sethu Pillai said that completing the deficiencies of Aryan characters was Kamban's magnanimity. Gilding a base metal and deceiving people is an offence in law. Similarly, decorating Aryan fictions with Tamil art is the harm Kamban did Tamils. He says Kamban's Sita displays Kannagi's greatness. When the work about Kannagi already exists, why is this needed? I regret that he avoided my many points with the unsupported assertion that Ravana was Aryan. Let the assembly recognise it.

Chairman C. M. Ramachandran Chettiar said: “Both spoke beautifully. I hope they will speak again, so I shall give my opinion later. I say nothing about the subject now. I am pleased that the debate remained on a high plane.”

The meeting ended pleasantly with the secretary's vote of thanks.

## Translator's Notes

The title's “fire” refers to the controversial proposal to burn the *Kamba Ramayanam* and *Periya Puranam*. The translation retains period political categories and the speakers' polemical terms as historical language. Titles of Tamil and Sanskrit works are transliterated; short classical references are translated for sense. The source's four separately labelled contributions are preserved as one complete debate.
