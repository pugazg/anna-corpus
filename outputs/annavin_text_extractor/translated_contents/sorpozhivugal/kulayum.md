## English Translation

Anna visited Anakaputhur on 28 October 1961.

That day, people gathered like a flood at Pallavaram railway station and welcomed him.

A great procession was held under the leadership of comrade C. K. G. Sami. Movement comrades had arranged representations of the Hindi demoness and a northern Marwari, a bear dance, tiger dance, silambam, karagam, kavadi, and other performances.

Anna travelled seated upon a peacock throne in a flower-decorated vehicle. To his right sat C. V. M. Annamalai, secretary of the DMK’s Chengalpattu district organisation, and to his left sat circle secretary K. Ramalingam. District deputy secretary Munu Adhi also travelled with them.

The Whole Town in Festive Dress

The procession passed through the principal streets of Pallavaram. Men and women stood all along the route, saluted Anna, and delighted in the splendour of the procession.

As it passed through Pallavaram, branch comrades and prominent residents honoured him with garlands. Merchants garlanded him in the bazaar at Cantonment Pallavaram. Abdul Razak, chairman of the local council at Eesa Pallavaram, presented him with a champak garland.

When the procession reached Pammal village, local-council members Johnny, Nagarathinam, and others garlanded Anna and read an address of welcome.

Anna was then garlanded on behalf of the Pammal branch, the Pammal Sambandha Mudaliar Cultural Forum, and the Tamil Forum.

Pallavaram, Pammal, and Anakaputhur wore a festive appearance. Every street was decorated with two-coloured flags and green-leaf festoons. Along the route, important associations and branches had erected facades and welcome arches illuminated with electric lights.

Upon reaching Anakaputhur, Anna opened the Alagiri Forum and raised the Kazhagam’s two-coloured flag.

The procession moved through the main streets and reached the public-meeting platform. Branch president G. V. Devendran presided. After comrades R. Mani, T. K. Kabali, M. C., C. Ramalingam, Munu Adhi, C. V. Rajagopal, and district secretary C. V. M. Annamalai had spoken, Anna rose amid prolonged applause.

Branches from the surrounding area presented Anna with garlands and handloom shawls. Garlands were offered on behalf of branches and forums from Gowul Bazaar, K. Pallavaram, Nagalkeni Masjid Forum, Chromepet Nedunchezhian Forum, Tambaram, Alandur, Mannivakkam, Thiruvanmiyur, Meenambakkam, Pazhavanthangal, Kundrathur, Thiruneermalai, and Pazhanthandalam.

Anna then explained the cruelties committed during the previous fourteen years by the Congress members entrenched in power and said that the poor had received no benefit under their rule.

He observed in his speech:

“Congress members look at us and say, ‘Did we not win independence? Did we not give everyone the vote?’

The Priest Who Asks for the Woman

“It is like this. A beautiful woman was possessed by a spirit. Do not infer from this story that spirits exist; we do not believe in them. I use it only as an example. Her parents searched everywhere and brought a priest to drive it out. The priest expelled the spirit. But instead of asking for his customary offering, he told the parents, ‘Was it not I who drove out the spirit possessing your daughter? Give me the woman herself.’ That is how Congress speaks to us.

“Let us concede that the white man fled the country because of them. At least, when he departed, he left behind more than two hundred crore rupees. After coming to power, these people spent that two hundred crore and have now borrowed about five thousand crore abroad. They have not only made us debtors but made our children and grandchildren debtors too.

“Whatever plan is undertaken, we must carry the kavadi to the North. We have no government of our own. All we say is that we must govern ourselves. That is why we demand Dravida Nadu for the Dravidians. I ask all of you to cooperate in achieving it.”

Alapakkam

Anna next travelled to the village of Alapakkam in the Saidapet circle.

C. V. Rajagopal, C. V. M. Annamalai, T. M. Parthasarathi, K. Rajaram, Mullai Sakthi, circle secretary C. Ramalingam, T. Rajarathinam, and C. Ko. Viswanatham accompanied him.

Anna addressed the gathering without a loudspeaker.

Comrade Chinnathambi presided. After comrades T. Kasi, V. P. Akilan, Alamelu Appadurai, M. C., Kumar, P. Chinnasami, C. V. Rajagopal, and C. V. M. Annamalai had spoken, Anna delivered a rare and thoughtful address.

When he rose to speak, branch comrades from the surrounding area garlanded him.

The Alapakkam branch presented Anna with an OCR-obscured sum as an election-fund contribution.

The Subordinate Minister

Anna then spoke:

“Our friend Annamalai mentioned that we arrived late. We were delayed because a great procession lasting three hours was held in the town of your circle secretary, Ramalingam. Annamalai also said that the police refused permission to install a loudspeaker. There is no benefit in blaming the police for that. We must blame the ministers in power.

“Mr Bhaktavatsalam, today’s Police Minister, is a subordinate minister without authority.

“Tamil Nadu has a Police Minister. Andhra, Mysore, Kerala, Bombay, Gujarat, and the other states likewise have separate Police Ministers. Over all of them stands another Police Minister in the central government, and he is the minister with authority. Our Bhaktavatsalam is a minister subordinate to him.

“I will give an example. If a police constable summons him, the circle inspector will not come. If the circle inspector summons him, the constable will come. That is also the condition of our Police Minister.

Merry-Go-Round Government

“The government ruling us resembles a merry-go-round. When children are taken to a festival, the merry-go-round there has toy horses, elephants, and camels. Children sit upon them and go around asking, ‘Am I ahead, or are you?’ Only when the operator turns the mechanism do the horse and elephant run, and he too goes around. To the children, it appears that the animals are racing to overtake one another. If the operator stops it, the merry-go-round stops and the toys do not move.

“Likewise, the mechanism that turns and stops this government is in Nehru’s hands. Nehru is the operator of the merry-go-round, while Tamil Nadu’s ministers are like children riding toy horses. That is why I call it a merry-go-round government.

“Even our tax money does not reach their hands, and they possess no authority to spend it.

Only the Banana Sheath Remains

“A farmer’s child says to his father, ‘Father, the banana tree in our garden has produced a bunch. Let it ripen on the tree and give me the fruit.’ The father consoles him: ‘All right, son. Where could I find money to buy you fruit? I will let the bunch ripen just as you wish.’

“The next morning, the landlord comes to the garden, sees the bunch, calls the farmer, and says, ‘Muniya, the bananas have matured well. Cut the bunch and bring it to my house.’ Unable to say anything, Muniya cuts it and delivers it to the landlord’s house.

“After finishing his outside work and returning home, the landlord’s wife says, ‘It has been a long time since I ate banana stem. Tell him to cut and bring that too.’ The landlord immediately comes to the garden. At that moment the farmer’s son asks, ‘Father, why did you cut the banana bunch?’ The farmer’s wife consoles the child: ‘Let the fruit go. The stem remains; I will cook it for you.’ Then the landlord arrives and says, ‘Muniya, my wife likes banana stem very much, so give me the stem.’

“The farmer immediately strips away the sheath, removes the stem, and sends it with the landlord. When the child comes and asks, ‘What is left, Father?’ he gives him the banana sheath. The boy takes it away to play with the other children.

“The farmer’s child sits upon that sheath. Another boy asks, ‘Where did you get that banana sheath?’ He replies, ‘My father gave it to me.’ ‘Where did your father get it?’ the other asks. ‘It grew in our garden. Our landlord took the bananas and the stem. This sheath remained, and my father gave it to me.’ The condition of the Tamil Nadu government is just like that.

We Must Enjoy It Ourselves

“They spend the tax money they collect in the North and give us only a little here, like the banana sheath.

“All we say is this: we should not be left to enjoy only the banana sheath. We ourselves must enjoy the bunch and the stem.”

Continuing, Anna appealed to the people to support the Kazhagam in the forthcoming election. Secretary A. P. Gengappan proposed the vote of thanks.
