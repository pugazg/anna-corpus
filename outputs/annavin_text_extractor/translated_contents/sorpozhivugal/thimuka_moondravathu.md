## English Translation

The following is the speech delivered by Arignar Anna at the Third General Conference of the DMK on 13 July 1961:

When I behold this vast gathering of people conscious of duty and discipline, I experience boundless joy. Your enthusiasm as frontline fighters of the liberation movement has further strengthened my own commitment to the goal of liberation.

Within twelve years, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has enabled the Kannada, Kerala, Andhra, and Tamil peoples to recognise their ethnic unity.

Responsible leaders in all four Dravidian states frequently point out that South India is being neglected in the Five-Year Plans framed by the Government of India. This lends strength to the policy of Dravida Nadu. The DMK has therefore resolved to work with redoubled energy in the other states.

Although we have not yet dealt fully with every force opposing the policy that Dravida Nadu must be established, the strength of the opposing forces has greatly diminished. Those forces have reached a position in which they are occupied with protecting themselves. Even if we are left utterly alone, subjected to repression, or abandoned in a desert, we shall fight for the liberation of the Dravidian land until our final breath and achieve victory. We shall devote all our ability and labour to that purpose.

Our growth itself is evidence of this. Today we see our movement functioning not only in the towns and cities of the country but even in remote villages. Dravidian national consciousness has reduced even the dominance of landlords in rural areas. That is why village people recognise the necessity of Dravida Nadu's liberation and firmly support the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam.

Elections are certainly very important. But no party can abandon its fundamental principles merely for the sake of electoral advantage. At the DMK State Conference held in Tiruchi in 1956, the assembled people decided that the DMK should participate in elections. The movement contested the general election, received more than seventeen lakh votes, and seated fifteen members in the Legislative Assembly. Every impartial observer knows the valuable work that the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has performed as the Opposition. The work of our members in the legislature has earned the people's esteem.

The movement has decided to continue its parliamentary work. In the coming general election, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam will contest by placing its policy before the people.

Those who examine the results of the previous general election can see that although the Congress party secured a majority of seats, it did not receive a majority of the votes. Under the present electoral system, the Congress party, without winning most of the votes, is able to secure most of the seats through a minority of votes. Because several parties contest the election, the votes are divided and Congress comes to office. This is compatible neither with democracy nor with political integrity. Democracy thereby becomes meaningless and lifeless.

Vinoba Bhave himself says, “Democracy is full of confusion and devoid of meaning; therefore, what we need are Sarvodaya and self-rule.” Jayaprakash Narayan says, “We need partyless democracy.” Some say that we need a form of basic democracy like the one advocated by Ayub Khan. Viewed philosophically, it is true that all these proposals contain certain merits. But none of them is suited to practical application.

The Communist Party says that all opposition parties should come together and form an electoral alliance on a minimum programme. But it imposes the condition that the Swatantra Party cannot be accepted as a progressive party.

In a country such as India, where forces with many different policies coexist, proportional representation should be provided. Only then will genuine democracy blossom. I request the leaders of the other parties also to reflect upon this proposal of mine.

If defeating Congress is necessary, the best course is to make constituency arrangements suited to the circumstances. This is also an appropriate way to prevent votes from being split. There is nothing to say now about an electoral alliance. The Parliamentary Board of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is about to begin considering the details relating to the election. Only after it completes its work and submits its report, and after the Executive Committee and General Council examine it in due form, can we decide whether an alliance is necessary.

I am not in a condition to deliver a long address to you today. I stand here speaking before you, but my heart and thoughts remain with the people recently affected by the devastation of floods in the Tiruchi and Thanjavur districts. I must travel there shortly. Therefore, before concluding my speech, I will go there only after receiving whatever you intend to give the people affected by the floods. It may serve as the first assistance we provide.

The joys and sorrows of the people have always been our concern. We cannot forget the effort Brother Karunanidhi undertook for the Thanjavur cyclone relief fund. He collected and gave twenty-five thousand rupees. We distributed it in the form of dhotis and saris. Now once again we must perform our duty toward our people. Those wishing to contribute to the flood-relief fund may come forward and give their contribution to me at once. I shall receive it with affection.

## Translator's Notes

The scan confirms the conference date as 13 July 1961. The OCR transcription reads 1967; the verified date is used in English while the Tamil source remains unchanged.

The OCR also reads “1256ம் ஆண்டில்” for the Tiruchi conference. The surrounding chronology and printed scan support 1956, which is used in English and separately logged.

The political term “திராவிட நாடு” is retained as *Dravida Nadu*, the proper name of the movement's contemporary territorial demand. The speech's claims and programme are translated in their 1961 historical voice.

“குறைந்த அளவுத் திட்டம்” is rendered as “minimum programme,” the established political sense in the surrounding electoral-alliance discussion.

The OCR phrase after the proportional-representation proposal is visibly damaged (“மண்னை வலை...”); the complete syntax securely conveys a request that other party leaders consider the proposal, and the English remains limited to that meaning.

Political, electoral, and historical assertions are translated in the speaker's voice and have not been independently verified.
