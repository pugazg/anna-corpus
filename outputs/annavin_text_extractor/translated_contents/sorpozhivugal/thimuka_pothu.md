## English Translation

The following was Arignar Anna’s presidential address at the D.M.K. General Conference on 31-12-1966:

“When Hitler of the Nazi Party ruled Germany, the party that spoke of ‘living space’ gained the support of the people. That party said it would enable the people to live and provide them with the space needed for life. Hitler of the Nazi Party then asked the people, ‘Is not National Socialism the policy of the Nazi Party? Will the Nazi Party not give the nation life?’ He said, ‘If I choose, I can make Spain submit within ten days. I can make Norway surrender over the telephone.’

“It was enough for him to say, ‘Will you submit, or shall I send the army?’ and countries would submit the very next moment. Even now, it has not been possible to determine exactly how Hitler, the dictator who exercised domination to that extent, died. Because Germany trusted him, the country was destroyed. Mansions were levelled to the ground; no trace remained of where forts and battlements had stood. Children were killed, heaps of corpses accumulated, and mothers became widows who had lost their husbands. Hitler’s dictatorial drive for domination turned Germany itself into a graveyard.

“Congressmen too follow Hitler’s course. Whatever fate befell the country that accepted Nazism will befall our country as well. Pakistan and China stand at the borders. I consider Nepal, Sikkim, and Bhutan to be sitting on the fence. World political circles too view the situation with dissatisfaction.

“The rulers may be described as capable in speeches, but one cannot believe that they possess any other strength. Therefore, if the country is entrusted to a Congress that speaks of democratic socialism, it will cast the nation into an abyss. If dictatorship is established in India at this time, when democracy has not matured, India’s condition cannot even be imagined.”

## Editorial Conference Record

Kalaignar, the Kazhagam treasurer, presented Arignar Anna with eleven lakh rupees as an election fund.

Arignar Anna issued the affectionate invitation, “Come, classical Tamil! Toward the city of Madras.” Treating the fourth General Conference as a great festival of every family, people arrived and gathered in vast numbers in Madras.

Crowds overflowed from every train. Hundreds of buses arrived from outlying places, decorated with Kazhagam flags, publicity boards, banana trees, and mango-leaf festoons. Delegates came from Bombay, Ahmedabad, Andhra, Karnataka, and other states.

Enthusiastic comrades of the Kazhagam also came to the conference from foreign countries including Ceylon and Singapore.

A vast procession seven miles long took place in an unprecedented manner, making one wonder whether the whole city of Madras had assembled and leaving no place without festivity. It took about five hours for the procession to pass a single point. A flood of people lined both sides of the streets. Their cries split the sky. Elderly people marvelled and said that they had never witnessed such a procession in their lives.

If even national daily newspapers praised the procession in editorials, what further proof is needed of our Kazhagam’s growth?

“We shall transform a land that was a wilderness into a country! I take pride in inaugurating this conference presided over by Arignar Anna, who opened the eyes of Tamil Nadu,” said Kalaignar when inaugurating the conference.

The people realized that they had reached a new stage in history. The Kazhagam became a star of hope. Kazhagam comrades engaged in election work with confidence that Anna’s government was certain to be formed and that no one could prevent it.
