## English Translation

Speech No. 51, 24 August 1968

The debate on the no-confidence motion against the Ministry began for its second day at about 10:30 a.m. on 24 August 1968 and continued until 2:00 p.m. The Honourable Deputy Speaker, Pulavar K. Govindan, presided.

At the beginning of the debate, DMK member K. M. Kathiravan delivered his maiden speech opposing the motion. He said the motion had been brought merely to accuse the ruling party and divert the people’s attention. A workers’ strike was an expression of the workers’ awakening, not a sign of administrative failure. He described the hardships workers had endured under Congress rule. He explained that unemployment had not been created by the DMK Government but had resulted from deficiencies in the three Five-Year Plans implemented during Congress rule.

Thiru P. G. Karuthiruman intervened. When the Chair intervened and asked that K. M. Kathiravan’s maiden speech be heard without interruption, members pointed to an interruption made by the Honourable Chief Minister the previous day. *[The concluding exchange on the first scan is damaged.]*

Thiru K. M. Kathiravan then resumed his speech and explained the shortcomings of the Five-Year Plans. He said the Tamil volunteer force differed from organisations such as the White Pigeon Force and was a shield, not a sword, for Tamils. Speaking about prohibition, he used statistics to explain that the DMK Government was more firmly committed to prohibition than other State Governments. He concluded by showing that all the accusations in the motion lacked substance.

Congress member Thiru D. Martin next spoke in support of the motion. He described DMK rule as dictatorial. Speaking about the fire incidents, he alleged that the fires were a conspiracy by the ruling party and that its members had set huts ablaze so they could replace the burnt dwellings and gain a good name. As evidence, he cited the line “The huts will be burnt” from the film *Nadodi Mannan*.

**The Honourable Thiru M. Karunanidhi:** In the film *Nadodi Mannan*, did it say that the huts must be set on fire as soon as the word “hut” occurred...?

**Thiru D. Martin:** They will be burnt...

**The Honourable Thiru M. Karunanidhi:** He says the line was “They will be burnt.” The person who wrote that dialogue is now the commander of their own party.

**Thiru D. Martin:** In which party is he? This arose suddenly under a Government that came suddenly...

**The Honourable Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** By referring to the story of *Nadodi Mannan*, in which a wanderer became a king, and saying that wanderers have come to rule the country, if they think the nation will therefore accept that they descend from a royal line, I consider them mistaken. Even in that film, he did not come to rule merely because he had been a wanderer. The two men in it had the same appearance, and that is how he came to rule. He did not come to rule because he was a wanderer. It is not enough merely to watch a film; one must understand it.

**Thiru D. Martin:** I speak only after understanding it. I say this knowing that the Constitution gives everyone the opportunity to govern.

**The Honourable Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** When that is so, I wish to know whether it seems courteous to point to what occurred in the film and say that this is what happens when people who were wanderers come to rule.

**Thiru D. Martin:** When the Chief Minister tells us what an old woman supposedly said about the fire, why is there no room for me to mention what appeared in a film?

**The Honourable Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** What I wish to ask is simply this: by pointing to a film in which a wanderer came to rule, and speaking generally while keeping us in mind as people who were wanderers and have now come to rule, is that courteous speech?

**Thiru D. Martin:** My purpose in mentioning a scene from the film in this House was that the scene’s important point was this: as soon as the wanderer became king... he said the huts would be burnt...

**The Honourable Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** *[Part of the reply across the page boundary is damaged.]* I do not wish to pursue the film. I ask whether it is right to speak while keeping us in mind.

**Thiru D. Martin:** I did not call the Chief Minister a wanderer. Such a scene appeared in *Nadodi Mannan*...

**The Honourable Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** Look again at what you said when you have time. There is honour in people who were wanderers coming to rule. There is no honour in those who once ruled becoming wanderers.

**The Honourable Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** When he supplies incorrect information and ministers offer an explanation, he should say whether he accepts it. I did not say that he must accept it. He may say that he does not. It is not right to say neither and simply stop at that point and proceed.
