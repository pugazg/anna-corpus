**The Hon. Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** Mr. Chairman, I have come before you to place the first supplementary estimates for 1968-69 before this House. A detailed statement explaining the demands for grants has been laid before the House. These supplementary demands total Rs. 6.40 crore, of which Rs. 3.32 crore falls under the revenue account. They include the “new service” schemes sanctioned after the current year's Budget was placed before the Legislature last February. We have also used this opportunity to seek additional provision under certain grants where expenditure is now expected to exceed the amount initially provided in the Budget.

The largest additional liability reflected in these estimates arises from the increase in dearness allowance rates sanctioned with effect from 1 June of the current year. As a result, the dearness allowance of our employees now corresponds to that of Central Government employees in the same grades. As usual, this increase also applies to local-body employees and teachers in aided schools. The resulting additional net expenditure during the current year is estimated at Rs. 2.70 crore. The necessary additional provision has been sought under the different grants. We expect the Fifth Finance Commission, while determining our State's financial needs, to take this essential liability into account and make adequate financial provision for our State in its interim report expected in September.

I wish to remind hon. members that, in my Budget speech, I referred to the Government's major policy decision to obtain institutional credit on a large scale for implementing the extensive food-production schemes now under way. Provision has been made for this under Demands 16 and 19.

Since special development schemes have also been undertaken for South Arcot and Chengalpattu districts, additional provision is sought under Demand 16. The total investment in these schemes is estimated at more than Rs. 2 crore. Under them, 2,000 private tube wells and point tube wells will be sunk in South Arcot district, and 2,000 private tube wells and three hundred point tube wells in Chengalpattu district. We must undertake such schemes to develop new and substantial sources and make full use of the groundwater available in these areas. We must also implement swiftly the connected programmes for extending electric power to irrigation.
