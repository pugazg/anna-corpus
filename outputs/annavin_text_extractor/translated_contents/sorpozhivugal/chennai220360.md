## English Translation

Speech 35, dated 22-3-1960

C. N. Annadurai: Honourable Speaker of the Legislative Assembly, I wish to state at the outset that if the Corporation’s adoption of a resolution concerning members who pointed out, as they saw fit, shortcomings in the Corporation’s administration in this House is found capable of undermining the dignity of this House, I shall never be party to supporting it to that extent.

Those who criticized and spoke of shortcomings in the Corporation’s administration in the Legislative Assembly were not merely the three members mentioned in the Corporation’s resolution. Many others here did the same. In particular, the leader of the government party, the Leader of this House, our Finance Minister, spoke on it specifically, prominently, clearly, and vigorously. One or two other members too criticized the Corporation’s administration here.

I believe the reason the Corporation referred there only to three members, without mentioning all these people, was its grievance that they had spoken not merely in their capacity as members of the Legislative Assembly but in relation to their own participation in the Corporation’s administration; it criticized them in that context.

Therefore, the principal purport of that resolution clearly reveals only the Corporation’s distress that certain members closely connected with its administration had criticized it. I cannot infer in any way that it regarded this House or the members of this House with diminished respect.

When I examine the rules prescribing the grounds on which a question of privilege may be raised, I do not feel that this matter falls within them even to a small degree. But trusting that your good and clear ruling will stand on the side of justice, I do not wish to say anything further about this issue.
