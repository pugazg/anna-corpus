## English Translation

Date: 18 July 1967

**Chief Minister C. N. Annadurai:** Mr Speaker, the naming of this state as Tamil Nadu, a measure accepted to a good extent by every party in this House and now about to be passed with their support, makes this a sacred day capable of bringing great happiness and noble inspiration into the life of every person who is a member of this Assembly today.

Amid our happiness, our only sorrow is that we had to wait so many long years to see this sacred day. We are accepting today, after a very great delay, something that ought to have occurred long ago.

Nevertheless, it is highly commendable that every party has united and given this resolution its support.

Even our Leader of the Opposition, Karuthiruman, has supported the resolution. When he says that certain suggestions have been offered, those suggestions arise only from the duty of members of the opposition to offer counsel; they do not constitute opposition to the measure.

The resolution will therefore receive everyone's support and be sent to the Government of India.

When I had opportunities to converse with one or two leaders who possess great influence in the Union government, they told me beforehand that if the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly passed and sent this resolution, there would be no hesitation in amending the Constitution.

Ten days ago, when an opportunity arose in Parliament to speak about this state, the Home Minister, Mr Chavan, who had been accustomed to saying "Madras State," spoke with great care and attention using the name "Tamil Nadu." This gives us reason to believe that many leaders there, including those conducting the government, accept it.

Thus, today in this Assembly we have obtained a good opportunity for them to accept the change and amend the Constitution.

It is natural that the respected M. P. Sivagnanam should feel especially inspired. For many years he worked with immense concern to have this country named Tamil Nadu.

When some wondered whether members of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, having joined the word "Dravida" to the name of our organisation, might lack concern for Tamil Nadu, we of the DMK had already insisted for many years that it must be named Tamil Nadu.

Members of Congress and others may previously have opposed it because someone else introduced the proposal, and may have searched for literary evidence. But today they too take great pride in accepting the name Tamil Nadu.

Therefore, I consider that this resolution is about to be passed. If such a victory is obtained, it is not a victory for the DMK alone, nor for the Tamil Arasu Kazhagam alone, nor for any other party. It is a victory for Tamil, a victory for the Tamil people, a victory for history and a victory for Tamil Nadu. Everyone must share in this victory.

It has been said not only that people abroad would fail to understand the name "Tamil Nadu," but also, as our former Industries Minister Mr Venkataraman argued, that agreements made between one country and another would all have to be rewritten and complications would arise among nations. Such remarks draw attention only to the fact that those speakers have travelled abroad; they do not examine whether any real complications exist.

As our respected friend Balasubramaniam pointed out, the Gold Coast became "Ghana," and no international complications arose from it. Tamil Nadu is not discarding the name of an independent country; it is giving a name to a region that remains part of India. There is therefore no basis for international complications. I am very happy that everyone has regarded supporting this resolution on behalf of their respective parties as a duty.

Our friend Aadhimoolam observed that a memorial should be raised to Sankaralinganar, who sacrificed himself for the change of name to Tamil Nadu. I firmly believe that everyone will keep that suggestion in mind and heart. That his aspiration has today reached a position where it can be fulfilled, and that all of us have taken part in creating that position, is something in which we may take pride throughout our lives. Long afterward, when our children and grandchildren sit in our homes and converse, they will be able to say with pride:

...They will say in the future, "It was in my grandfather's time that our country was named Tamil Nadu. My grandfather Karuthiruman, who sat in the opposition, supported it."

If Mr Karuthiruman considers all the happy circumstances under which both his grandchildren and ours may speak in the future, I have not a particle of doubt that he will accept it without even offering that suggestion.

I therefore humbly request everyone to pass this resolution unanimously.

The resolution was put to the decision of the Assembly and passed unanimously.

Mr Speaker, on this day when we have passed a historic resolution, I seek your permission for members of the Assembly to answer "Long live!" when I say "Tamil Nadu."

**Chief Minister C. N. Annadurai:** Tamil Nadu!

**Members:** Long live!
