## English Translation

Speech delivered by Arignar Anna at the College of Social Work convocation held on 20-4-1967:

Comrades!

The degrees awarded here are not degrees conferred for seeking employment. They are credentials given for performing social service.

The greater the number of students who go out with the feeling that they must serve society, the greater the benefit to the country.

Today, work of this kind to safeguard social welfare is easier than the work of social reform.

When debate is continuing over society’s old principles, and new principles are entering without our noticing them, living within society itself is an arduous undertaking!

To labour for that society’s welfare is a challenge.

For ours is a strange country!

It is in this country that abundance flourishes, yet the food problem has raised its head.

It is in this country that great figures in education have arisen, yet it is here that the uneducated are numerous.

It is in this country that religious doctrines abound, yet it is here that moral decay is widespread.

It is in this country that perennial rivers flow, yet it is here that famine runs rampant.

It is in this country that harvests are seen, and it is here that rationing exists.

It is in this country that fine doctors live, yet it is here that diseases remain unresolved even now!

Our first duty is to discover the meaning of this “yet” and resolve it.

Why scarcity exists amid abundance is not merely a question; it is a challenge issued to us. We must answer that challenge through our work.

If young people work with sound training, firm hearts, and no thought of self-interest, a good answer can be given to that challenge.

Two different attitudes exist in our country.

One is an attitude born of despair.

The other is an attitude born of excessive estimation.

To abandon effort believing that nothing can happen in this world is the mentality of despair. To make no effort believing that everything is already fine is the mentality of excessive confidence.

Our aim must be to serve society between those two extremes.

Reforming society is difficult work. Many rocks of conflicting thought stand in its way. The very word “reform” produces a different kind of outlook.

Social work is easier than that.

In such work, you can provide education to the uneducated and medicine to the sick. No question of this doctrine or that party is needed.

A smile on the face, confidence in the heart, purity in action, and selflessness in thought are enough!

This College of Social Work has thanked the Government of Tamil Nadu and the Central Government.

For institutions of this kind to offer thanks is remarkable; my thanks go to those who expressed them.

I wish you distinction in your work and hope that you will make this country one with an advanced society.

## Translator's Notes

The source is a complete convocation address ending with a direct wish to the graduates.
