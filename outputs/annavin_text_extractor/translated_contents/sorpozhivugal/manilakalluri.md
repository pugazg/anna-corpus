## English Translation

Speech delivered at Presidency College, Madras, on 24-11-1967:

The president of the Students’ Forum said that students would support me in protecting democracy. I have great confidence in educated intellectuals. That is why, whenever I come to colleges to speak, I emphasize the necessity of protecting democracy. Where else should I go to honour and safeguard democracy and carry its message throughout the country?

Democracy is not merely an organization, nor is it an inanimate object. No one need have any doubt about its benefit or excellence. However noble and excellent the system may be, the human being who puts it into operation must possess equal excellence.

We have preserved for a long time, without serious damage, the system left to us by the British. Just as a bulb and wire do not by themselves become electricity, and only when a mechanism supplies electrical energy do we receive it as a blaze of light, so too democracy has its conditions.

An election every five years and polling stations cannot by themselves become democracy. They are only structures. For them to endure and flourish, democratic feeling must become deeply rooted and cultivated in all our hearts. Wherever that feeling fades or is scarce, the democratic system will decay and collapse.

We are striving to make this country’s democratic system complete and free of defects. In Pakistan, where democratic feeling faded, military dictatorship arose. Even in a country such as Burma, where national leaders like U Nu were celebrated as living gods, democracy failed because the people lacked democratic disposition and emotional commitment.

Democratic feeling did not grow to match the scale of the democratic system. Therefore, democracy fell there.

We see the same condition in Sukarno’s powerful Indonesia. Sukarno, who laboured greatly for that country’s liberation, has now been made to stand somewhat aside by the present rulers.

We must not become complacent imagining that democracy is securely protected here.

Democracy is not in such a safe position. Therefore, for it to flourish, democratic disposition, courage, and temperament must grow in each of us. We must acquire the democratic awareness needed to accept every challenge.

We cannot obtain this merely from books or classrooms.

Nor should temporary surges of feeling alone become our measure. Suppose ten thousand people gather and shout, “Down with Hindi.” Will that one event alone protect us from Hindi? That feeling must be cultivated continuously. Enduring courage grows only through exchange of ideas, debate, respect for an opponent’s view, and mutual respect among ourselves.

The president of the Students’ Forum mentioned my speech at Annamalai University. There I explained that caste discrimination and democracy cannot function together. If democracy is to possess life and remain protected, caste difference must be destroyed.

Some may ask, “Are you still speaking about caste difference? Where does it exist?” Caste discrimination has not yet disappeared. But it has now been reduced to wandering in search of ways to protect itself.

Ten years ago, caste seized and shook society. Twenty-five years ago, it stood as the very emblem of cruelty.

Within the next ten years, it may be destroyed. Until then, we cannot rest.

During college, a young person may remain like a free sparrow, untouched by caste discrimination. But when he leaves college and steps into life, caste meets him at three stages and attempts to possess him: when he applies for a job, when he seeks a bride, and when he stands for election. This is the condition of sixty people out of a hundred.

For democracy to flourish and rise, the caste system must be broken. Some say that if economic inequalities disappear, everything else will vanish by itself. That merely reflects their desire; nothing else will occur. I do not thereby say that economic causes create no differences.

We must bring about the natural death of the caste system. Only if a better institution fills the place caste now occupies will the caste system fall and die naturally. We must achieve this within the next ten years, in our lifetime. Where else but in colleges shall I seek the warriors capable of conducting this great struggle?
