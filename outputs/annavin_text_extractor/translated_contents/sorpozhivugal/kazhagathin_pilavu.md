## English Translation

The following is a summary of Arignar Anna's speech at the meeting held on the beach on 26 September 1960:

I have taken office as General Secretary of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, and some of my younger brothers have assumed several other responsibilities.

Many younger brothers here spoke in praise of me, placing me in a very uncomfortable position. I have rebuked many of them before, and I shall rebuke them again. Yet they spoke in praise only because the decision that has been reached had to be explained to the people of the country.

This morning, I saw a newspaper report about the decision. It described the DMK election as a “sudden turn.” I do not mention this to find fault with them, but “sudden turn” is not very good Tamil. A turn is inherently sudden. Write merely “turn,” and at least from now on, please write in good Tamil.

Another newspaper wrote, “Annadurai has cleverly managed the competition and conflict that arose in the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam.” Brother Sampath has explained to you that there was no such competition. But for the sake of argument, I shall accept the claim. Is it intelligent or foolish to manage such a crisis cleverly? If friends and adversaries reflect upon it, they will understand that the person running that newspaper is no friend.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is a fine veena. When its resonance diminishes, its strings and keys are adjusted from time to time. If someone therefore imagines that it is about to snap, it means that person cannot appreciate the strength of its strings, the manner in which the veena was made, or the music it produces.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is not merely a political party; it is a liberation movement.

For all those involved in it, the important matter is not who occupies which place, nor even who assumes which responsibility at what time. Each person taking up a responsibility is like a battle camp established within the liberation movement. If adversaries try to enter it, they will be caught amid our closeness and crushed to pulp.

I do not say this to great political leaders. At this moment, however, I wish to make it clear to those who think they must make their living from such claims.

If all my younger brothers are present in the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, I tell you and the members of other parties: the tenderness within me exists in the form of Brother Karunanidhi. The spirit of uprising within me exists in the form of Brother Sampath. The sense of restraint within me exists in the form of Brother Mathiazhagan. My love for Tamil exists in the form of Brother Anbazhagan. The Tamil within me exists in the form of Navalar Nedunchezhiyan. The humour within me exists in the form of Chitrarasu.

When I grow weary, Brother Karunanidhi plays the Mohanam raga for me. If our adversaries call it Mukhari, it means that Mukhari is what they need at present.

If I begin speaking about my younger brothers, I could continue without end. Everyone involved in this movement comes running whenever the call arrives.

When Brother Mathiazhagan was studying at Annamalai University, opponents shut him inside a room and beat him. The scar remains upon him even now.

Likewise, our Brother Karunanidhi was chased and beaten in Pondicherry. Thinking he was dead, they abandoned him and left.

Our friend N. V. Natarajan was initially in Congress and even tried denouncing me. When he realised that it was not right, he came and joined us.

In this way, each person has endured great suffering. Recounting all this would be sweet news to us, but if time passes, this meeting of praise may afterward become a meeting for bathing in the rain.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is an organisation structured so that each person works from an assigned place. If opponents imagine that there are other differences of rank or resentments among us, we must say that they do not know the history of our growth.

We are people capable of eating from the same leaf. The only reason we were not born from one mother's womb is that one mother's womb could not bear so many children.

Only those who know this historical truth can understand us.

That is why the DMK has possessed strength not merely today but throughout its life in the country. This organisation is as though it were cast from molten metal. If it were otherwise, it would have broken apart over a single election. Those who, without knowing this, imagine that a split exists within it have failed to understand our growth.

Today Brother Sampath has become the presiding chairman. If someone asks, “Who is this Sampath?” you may answer, “A Member of Parliament.” As far as I am concerned, I would say he is the younger brother who, frightened of his father, used to lie beneath my cot.

Our relationship, to use Navalar's language, is bound together by tears and red fire.

Brother Nedunchezhiyan said that he could not bear this burden, that I alone possessed the strength to bear it, and that he had therefore handed it to me.

This is not an unbearable burden. There are a hundred thousand people capable of bearing it. I recognise the great achievements Brother Nedunchezhiyan accomplished after assuming this responsibility.

He has guided the movement to a position where it has two Members of Parliament, fifteen members of the Legislative Assembly, forty-five members of the municipal corporation, one Mayor, one member of the Legislative Council, comrades in many local councils, and a treasury balance exceeding one lakh rupees. He also said that he would stand beside me. I am not going to allow him even a little rest. Nor is my younger brother at an age when his interest will turn elsewhere. What he has entrusted to me is like having a child and joyfully showing that child to me when it reaches the age of six.

None of these achievements is enough to satisfy us. At this moment, all of us must take a solemn vow to attain our fundamental objective, Dravida Nadu. Many among us may be beaten and kicked, but we must pass beyond all that and attain our goal. Let everyone return home and labour for victory.

## Translator's Notes

The source explicitly calls itself a summary of Anna's speech. The English preserves that editorial status rather than presenting it as a complete verbatim transcript.

The headline OCR reads “பிதற்றுபவார்கள்”; the clean grammatical form “பிதற்றுபவர்கள்” is used in title metadata and English while the source block remains unchanged.

Mohanam is associated here with a pleasing or uplifting musical mood, while Mukhari supplies a punning contrast of lament. Both raga names are retained.

The line about a praise meeting becoming a “நீராட்டுக் கூட்டம்” is a rain-related joke: if the meeting continued too long, those present would be drenched. It is translated with that context visible.

The term “செந்தீர்” is literally red fire; it is retained rather than silently replacing it with the more familiar political phrase “red blood.”

Political, organisational, historical, and biographical assertions are translated in the speaker's voice and have not been independently verified.
