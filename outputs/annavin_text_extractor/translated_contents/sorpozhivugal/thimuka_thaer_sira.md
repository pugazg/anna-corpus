## English Translation

Anna's speech at the DMK Election Special Conference held at the S.I.A.A. Grounds in Madras on 10 February 1957:

For the DMK, patience is its armour, dignity its sharp sword, and discipline its sound shield. Therefore, the first training that members of the movement must acquire is inner strength. When we say that one needs a heart capable of bearing anything, that includes ridicule, slander, and much else besides.

Our growth during the past five years is something no one else has achieved or could have achieved. The first capital we invested in all this growth was tears. We were born amid tears, and we asked for tears as an offering. The people of the country gave them. Then we asked for their sweat, and our comrades gave it tirelessly. Later, the movement asked for blood itself as an offering, and Joseph has given his very hand along with his blood.

The treacherous fox has a thicket. The venomous cobra has an anthill. Even the tiger that kills and drinks blood has a cave. But the good people of this Dravidian land have no place in which to live.

The lines of sorrow spread across the faces of the Dravidian people must be erased, and the light of a smile must shine there. The weariness in their gait must be removed, and they must walk with majesty. To achieve all this, we must offer our tears, our sweat, and our blood.

The DMK now stands in the position of a young woman who has come of age. If she smiles, they call her flirtatious; if she adorns herself, they call her affected; if she stands on the terrace, they ask, "Why are you looking at those who pass along the street?" We therefore carry a great responsibility. We must not speak irresponsibly and then struggle and stumble afterward. Just as gold does not lose its quality no matter how often it is refined in fire, our movement must become a mountain of virtue.

If the Dravidar Kazhagam believes in breaking Pillaiyar idols, let it do so thoroughly; we are not going to find fault with it. But we will neither break Pillaiyar nor break coconuts for that Pillaiyar. Thus, if people possess firmness in their convictions, working according to those convictions is the justice and integrity required in public life. It is improper for them to make a decision themselves, act upon it, experience its gains and losses, and then turn to us and ask, "What is your decision?"

We say that Brahminism must be abolished; Periyar says that Brahmins must be abolished. If you ask me, I would say that the two amount to the same thing. If so, how is it that we admit Brahmins into our movement? They must cut away the sacred thread. They must abandon the deceitful Aryan ideas in their hearts and their attachment to the Vedas and Agamas. They must wholeheartedly declare, "The Dravidian land belongs to the Dravidians." It is only Brahmins who have changed in this way whom we accept.

To establish how just our demands are, we call upon the towering mountains, the low-lying valleys, the fertile fields, and the perennial life-giving rivers as our support. We place the traces of history in the witness box and make them speak. Dig at Kolar and there is gold; dive at Kumari and there are pearls; cut in the forest and there is sandalwood. We point as well to Salem's iron ore and Neyveli's mines and ask that our land be ours.

This morning, our friend Natarajan spoke about the distress of Tamils living across the seas. I ask: what does this country lack that would prevent them from living here? Why must they travel to another land for food, wear away their bodies, lose their dignity and rights, and then be kicked out? Why does the wretched condition prevail in which people lose their love of life and welcome death because they cannot find work or a means of living in this country, their own country?

Is the Five-Year Plan the only remedy offered for all this? The First Five-Year Plan has ended and the Second Five-Year Plan is being drawn up; why, then, do these degrading conditions continue?

That is why we say that the people of this land can obtain security in their lives only if our country belongs to us, only if the Dravidian land belongs to the Dravidians.

In the Dravidian land, food, shelter, and a dignified life can be guaranteed to the people. The government itself will undertake the production of goods required for people's essential needs, consumer goods, and ensure that every necessity is available to everyone. We will certainly not leave the production of these goods in the hands of any private capitalist. Agriculture will be conducted cooperatively, and under government supervision food will be made available to everyone in the required quantity without scarcity or obstruction. Private individuals may be permitted to manufacture luxury goods if they wish, but they must not be permitted to control essential goods.

If a worker loses employment while willing to work, there must not be a condition in which work is unavailable. Even if such a condition arises, the worker must be given unemployment assistance. Only then can that person live as a human being like everyone else. Receiving such assistance is also the worker's right. This is the system followed in England.

All industries and agriculture will be run by the government in keeping with contemporary scientific development and will belong to the people. For the people of our country to obtain all these things, their country must come into their own hands.
