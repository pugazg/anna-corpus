ஜனநாயகத்தின் பாசறை

23-04-1961 கழகம் ஜனநாயகத்தின் பாசறை என்பதற்கு

நான் மிகமிகச் சாமானியன் - ஆயினும், திரியும் எண்ணெயும்
முறைப்படி. இருந்திடின், இருட்டினை விரட்டிடும் ஒளியினை
உள்ளங்கை அளவுள்ள அகல் விளக்கும் தருகிறது அல்லவா?
அதுபோல என்னை ஓர் மாபெரும் விடுதலை இயக்கத்தை
நடத்திச் செல்லும் பொறுப்பினை ஏற்கத்தக்க நிலைக்கு நீங்கள்,
உங்கள் அன்பைப் பெய்து அளாக்கிவிட்டி ரக்கிறீர்கள்.

நான் மிகமிகக் கூச்சப்படுபவன் - உங்களுடைய உற்சாக
மூட்டும் தன்மையும் உறுதி தரும் பேச்சும் செயலும், உடனிருந்து
பணியாற்றும் திறனும் சேர்ந்துதான், என்னை இந்த அளவுக்குப்
பொதுப்பணித்துறையிலே ஈடுபட வைத்த து.

அவ்விதம் ஈடுபட்டதிலே, பலனேதும் நமக்குக் கிடைத்து
விடவில்லை என்று எவரும் துணிந்து கூறமாட்டார்கள். ஒரு
'பாசறை' அமைக்கப்பட்டி ருக்கிறது; படைக்கலன்கள் குவிக்கப்பட்
டி ருக்கின்றன; நோக்கம் செம்மைப்படுத்தப்பட்டி ருக்கிறது; நஈடு
கூர்ந்து கவனித்துத் தீரவேண்டிய நல்லிடம் கிடைத்திருக்கிற து.

இவ்வளவும் இந்தப் பன்னிரண்டே அண்டுக்காலத்தில்!

இவ்வளவும் பல்வேறு வகையான எதிரிகள், நமது
கழகத்தைச் சூழ்ந்திருக்கும் நிலையில்!

இவ்வளவும் - இருட்டடிப்பு, இஹழிமொழி, பழிச்சொல்,
ஏளனம் எனும் இன்னல்களுக்கிடையில்/

அன்று நாம் கழகத்தைத் துவக்கியபோது, நாட்டு மக்கள்,
ஐயோ பாவம், இதுகள் முயற்சி செய்து பலன் என்ன காணப்

கொண்ட கொள்கை, திட்டம், போர் முறை ஆகிய எதிலாகிலும்

மற்றவர் அறியார்' என்ற மமதையுடன் நடந்துகொண்டதுண்டா?
இல்லை என்பதை எவரும் கூறுவர்.

என்னை உங்களிடம் ஒப்படைத்துவிட்டவன்.

நீங்கள் தேர்ந்தெடுக்கும் திட்டங்களை நாட்டுக்கு
அறிவிப்பவன்; உங்களோடு சேர்ந்து, அந்தத் திட்டங்கள் வெற்றி
பெற உழைப்பவன்.

ஒவ்வொரு கட்டத்திலேயும் உங்களை தாடி யோசனை

## English Translation

Arignar Anna’s explanation, dated 23 April 1961, of why the movement is a camp of democracy:

I am a very ordinary man. Yet if the wick and oil are properly in place, does not even a little earthen lamp no larger than one’s palm give light that drives away darkness? In the same way, by pouring your affection upon me, you have made me fit to accept responsibility for leading a great liberation movement.

I am an extremely shy person. It is your capacity to inspire, your speech and action that give assurance, and your ability to work beside me that together have drawn me this deeply into public service.

No one will dare say that our involvement has yielded no result. A camp has been established; weapons have been gathered; the objective has been refined; and we have secured a sound place from which to observe the country closely and reach decisions.

All this in only twelve years.

All this while many kinds of opponents have surrounded our movement.

All this amid the hardships of suppression, abusive words, slander, and ridicule.

When we founded the movement, the people of the country thought, “Alas, what result will these poor fellows obtain from their efforts?” Some even said so. Rival parties mocked us. Even members of our own families spoke in voices mingling fear and affection, saying that we were embarking upon a dangerous undertaking like taking a catamaran into a turbulent sea.

Today there is no hamlet or village that does not know our movement, no one who has not heard our heroic song, and no heart into which our ideas have not entered.

## Blossomed Through Collective Labour

The movement is something all of us united and worked to create. As Navalar has said, we nurtured it by shedding tears and blood.

The beauty, advancement, strength, capacity for action, and power to secure the victory of ideals possessed by this movement are all the result of collective labour; they blossomed from collective strength.

We obtained that collective strength by speaking together in a spirit of comradeship and properly implementing the plans we selected.

During these twelve years, in any policy, plan, or method of struggle undertaken by our movement, have I ever acted according to my own whim, autocratically declaring, “There is no need to ask anyone,” or with the arrogance that “I know everything and others know nothing”? Everyone will say that I have not.

I am one who has entrusted himself to you.

One who has made your thoughts his own.

One who announces to the country the plans you select and works beside you for their success.

At every stage, I gladly wait to seek you out for advice and to receive corrections and changes of direction.

Even when an idea occurs to me, I remain confident and patient until that idea occurs to you as well.

## The Election Plan

Examine any action undertaken by our movement and this will become clear.

Let me give an example. The plan to participate in the general election, which enabled the political world to form a very high estimate of our movement, was not a decision I made myself. All of you assembled at the General Conference, held a vote, and adopted the plan.

I did not forcibly impose upon you who should contest elections or where they should contest. The inquiry committee established for that purpose examined the prevailing circumstances and reached its decisions accordingly. I was the announcer.

You decided that we should contest elections, and I took part. I did not consider it beneath me to travel from town to town, stage plays, collect money, and add it to the general election fund. I regarded it as my duty.

From the question of whether we should enter the assemblies in which rulers sit to the question of whether the journal *Nam Nadu* should be published on a larger scale, decisions were taken through proper procedure after consultation.

## The Rules Committee

Whether it was the first set of rules framed for the movement or the present rules, they were examined and prepared by separate committees of which I was not a member.

It is now said that amendments to the rules are needed. This too has been entrusted to a committee.

Examine the by-elections at Thoothukudi, Yercaud, and Chidambaram, the Chennai Corporation election, or the leadership election. You will see that each was an issue handled by a separate committee.

The reason I have conducted myself this way in every matter is my unwavering trust that our comrades and front-line workers know the measure and manner of things and can carry them out skilfully and with dignity. It is also my conviction that everyone possesses the same responsibility and concern for the movement’s affairs that I do.

Even now, I have not departed from this nature by the smallest measure.

Nor will I depart from it by even a drop in the future.

To tell the truth, I am incapable of changing in that way.

All the happiness available to me lies in being able to say, “Everyone came together and said this; everyone came together and did this.” This is my nature. I am always meticulously watchful that it should not be damaged in the slightest.

If this is my nature, everyone should also kindly understand how, because of it and because of ideas produced by developments in political circumstances, our movement has step by step acquired orderly organisational forms and grown sound over these twelve years.

## The Inquiry Committee

Branch organisations have been established according to procedure and connected with each higher body. Today there is an excellent organisational structure extending all the way to the highest body, the General Council.

If complications, disorders, irregularities, or indiscipline arise in the movement’s functioning, there are methods, opportunities, and arrangements for identifying and removing them.

There are separate procedures and bodies for auditing the movement’s finances and examining expenditure and income.

An Executive Committee exists to deal with election issues.

The General Council exists to weigh and examine all these matters, set them right, make new arrangements, create new bodies, and introduce changes even at the level of policy.

## It Can Be Done If We Try

By placing a matter before the General Council and obtaining its consent, there is a path for doing anything from changing the membership fee to giving a new interpretation to the movement’s policy.

There is no justification for fearing that the General Council might never be convened. Even if the General Secretary does not convene it, the members possess authority to convene the General Council through a specified procedure.

I explain both my own nature and the nature of the movement’s organisation to show that anyone desiring any reform or change can very easily win me to their view, discuss the matter with members of the General Council, obtain their agreement, and accomplish it.

Even now, I have not lost my nature and become an autocrat who tells anyone in a high-handed fashion, “Do not say this! I will not accept it!” I shall not become one, and I cannot become one.

Am I a permanent leader? Have I become a dictator who no longer needs the agreement of fellow comrades?

Have all the bodies of the movement been dissolved? Are the fewer than two hundred members of the General Council incapable of knowing good from bad? Are they people who sit with their eyes closed and mouths shut?

No proposal has been submitted and rejected.

No plan has been submitted and abandoned.

No demand has been made for a change in policy.

Through the well-structured organisation of our movement, there is a proper path by which anyone may pursue any change or plan.

## The Duty of Democracy

When participating in a large organisation, if ideas that occur to us appear correct, just, and necessary, democratic duty consists in working through the means by which the organisation can be made to accept them and achieving success.

Properly fulfilling that duty may take time. Yet a democrat can consider nothing greater than the happiness and pride of having made fellow comrades and the organisation accept one’s ideas.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is a fitting place for all such democrats.

## Translator's Notes

The source presents this as Anna’s explanation dated 23 April 1961; it does not supply a venue or separately label the text as a verbatim transcript. All six Tamil OCR pages remain unchanged.

“பாசறை” is a camp or encampment, historically a place where an army gathers and prepares. “Camp of Democracy” preserves the source’s linked imagery of weapons, struggle, and organisational preparation.

“பரணி” is a Tamil heroic poetic genre. “Heroic song” conveys its function in this political passage without replacing the Tamil literary term in the preserved source.

“நாவலர்” is retained as Navalar because the source gives only the honorific. *Nam Nadu* is retained as the title of the journal.

Organisational names such as General Conference, General Council, Executive Committee, and General Secretary are contextual translations. The source’s claims about the movement’s history, rules, elections, finances, and internal democracy have not been independently verified.
