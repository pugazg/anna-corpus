## English Translation

Speech delivered at the first Chennai District Conference after leaving the Dravidar Kazhagam, on the date printed in the OCR as 22-71-1950:

Comrades!

I am delighted to see you taking part in this, our first Chennai District Conference, and standing here with enthusiasm and excitement.

Though we live in the country, we have assembled amid lions, tigers and bears. On this side is a circus and behind us is a zoo; there are animals in both! We are here among them. The political life of the great mass of this country’s people is just the same.

## A Fine Future

This is the first conference to be held in Chennai after we left the Dravidar Kazhagam. The more than four hundred branches established by the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam are evidence enough that it is receiving strong support in the country. More than 330 great public meetings have been held so far. Many who shine like bells of propaganda are travelling from place to place explaining our principles. All this confirms that the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has a fine future.

We bear no hostility toward the Dravidar Kazhagam. Nowhere have I displayed a feeling of enmity. When I had the opportunity to leave the Dravidar Kazhagam, four paths were open to me. One was to use this as a pretext and enter some other field of politics. Another was to use it as a pretext and enter some other avenue that would bring me personal gain. The third was to capture the Dravidar Kazhagam.

I rejected all three opportunities. I chose the fourth and founded the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam to relieve the suffering of the people. I am not one who is ignorant of profit and loss. Nor am I one who knows nothing of forts and ramparts.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is carrying on a campaign for sound knowledge throughout the country. It has created a new awakening and a new vitality. The young people of the DMK are the spearheads on the battlefront. They are capable of accomplishing their task with vigilance and concentration. They are noble people who turn their words into deeds. They are not indolent by nature. They are capable of creating a new force in Tamil Nadu.

## Many Wealthy People

When we separated from the Dravidar Kazhagam, many people formed a mistaken impression. We demolished that false impression and founded the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. When we began, we had no newspaper, no printing press and none of the instruments needed to establish a party. There are many wealthy people in our organisation, but that wealth will not serve this cause. We run a newspaper without financial strength. We maintain a printing press without machinery. Even without many of the facilities a party requires, we can accomplish our work.

Near Tiruchirappalli is a beautiful village called Thavuttupalayam. The bank of the Kaveri lies nearby. We held a meeting there until midnight, slept there after midnight, crossed the river the next morning in a small boat and reached the opposite bank. There was a meeting on this bank at ten at night and another on the far bank at eleven the next morning. Between them flows the beautiful Kaveri.

## What Our Force Can Accomplish

Our force will accomplish all the things the Government cannot. There is nothing our force cannot achieve! The Government has no money to remove mythological notions from the country, eradicate the affliction of Sanatana orthodoxy and spread sound knowledge. When the provincial Government asks the Delhi Government for money, the only answer is, “No! No!” Our Government showed them the Kaveri, the Thamirabarani and the abundance of the Vaigai and asked Delhi for a loan of six crores, but the answer was still no.

Do not send us to Delhi to ask for a loan. Send us to perform our duty. We shall accomplish what the Government cannot. Will the Government send us to fight Delhi?

## The Breeze and the Storm

If our Government makes use of the breeze, it will remain a breeze. If it fails to make use of it, the breeze will turn into a storm. If the breeze is allowed to wander through the flower garden, the people of the country will receive great pleasure. What became of Chiang Kai-shek, who turned the breeze into a storm? What became of Hitler? Was he not transformed and destroyed? What was Mussolini’s fate? Because he turned the breeze into a storm, Chiang Kai-shek now lives in hiding in the capital of Formosa. The mind of Chiang Kai-shek, who turned the breeze into a storm, is now confused.

We are not engaged in hunting for votes. Does the Government wish to turn the breeze into a storm? If it does, we wish to ask it only this, in the words of Sundaram Pillai, author of *Manonmaniam*: “If this force is defeated, what force can triumph?”

## Do You Think We Do Not Know How?

Do we not know how to derail a train, as was done in 1942? Do we not know how to cut telegraph wires? We know very well. But I have stated openly in many places that we have no desire for such destructive force. If the Government makes use of us, who have no interest in destruction, it can open the way to constructive power.

When lions are here and peacocks are in their own land, why should our Government worry? From the very day we founded the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, a new responsibility has rested upon us.

For the *Ariya Mayai* case, I repeatedly leave important duties and travel to Tiruchirappalli. They keep granting adjournments. Comrade Bhaskara Thondaiman, an admirer of the *Kamba Ramayanam* and a connoisseur of the arts, has been appointed to hear this case. If they ban *Ariya Mayai* and send me to prison, the very next day it will be printed in thousands of pamphlets and fly everywhere!

I am very happy that the Government is now reading good books such as *Ariya Mayai*, *Latchiya Varalaru* and *Ravana Kaviyam*.

They know very well that “Annadurai wrote only what is there.” The Government would do well to stop banning books and turn its attention to the important tasks that must be done.

## Tasks That Must Be Done

If one travels through Ramanathapuram District, the goats and cattle appear to be nothing but skin and bone. They are on the verge of going to the cremation ground. The fields are cracked. The crops have withered and lie dead in the fields. The people lack rice, employment and clothing, and suffer the torment of hunger. Our Government has a responsibility to remove all these afflictions. If this poverty continues to grow, who knows whether the famine that struck Bengal might arrive here—

*[The available scans omit the continuation here. Images 4 and 5 duplicate the same printed page; Image 6 begins in the middle of a later argument about Tamil names.]*

Minister Madhava Menon says Tamils are fanatics because Maraimalai Adigal changed his name from Vedasalam. Would the Malayali Madhava Menon understand the nature, distinction and beauty of Tamil?

Can you now hear titles such as “Sri-la-Sri,” “Srimath” and “Sriman” anywhere? You might hear them in thousand-pillared or hundred-pillared halls, but you cannot hear these words in the people’s forum. Six or seven years ago, people said “Sri Sathyamurthi,” “Srimath Rajagopalachariar” and “Sriman Srinivasa Iyengar.” Does anyone speak that way now? No. Why? Even the newspaper *Swadesamitran* has changed its old usage: it writes *arappor*, righteous struggle, for *satyagraha*, and *thalapathi*, commander, for *senathipathi*. When we change a name, we change it with an idea behind it.

## Sankranti and Pongal

Many years ago it was called the Sankranti festival. If you now say “Sankranti festival,” people will ask whether such a festival exists. Today it is known only when called Pongal, and more particularly the Festival of the Tamils. When it was called Sankranti, people worshipped the Sun God and imagined that the sun travelled in a chariot drawn by seven horses from east to west. How beautifully we have now transformed it into the Festival of the Farmer!

When people heard “Sankranti festival,” did our forebears not believe that the sun raced from east to west in a single-wheeled chariot drawn by seven horses? Today many know that it is the earth that moves, not the sun. Has the notion that the sun runs from east to west not now been shattered to pieces?

We have transformed the Maha Sankranti festival in Tamil Nadu into the Festival of the Tamils. Does our Government wish to turn the breeze into a storm, or will it allow the breeze to roam as a breeze? It would be best for our Government to let the breeze remain a breeze. We can turn it into a storm if we choose. Will the Government offer us friendship or hostility?

## The Intellectual Movement and Repression

Is ours not the intellectual movement founded by Socrates two thousand years ago? Is ours not the intellectual movement praised and honoured by Martin Luther? At one time Raja Ram Mohan Roy was part of our movement. Ambedkar is carrying out the work of our movement in North India. In Tamil Nadu it was Periyar E. V. Ramasamy who founded the intellectual movement. This movement cannot be suppressed with the weapon of repression.

The words we speak travel through the people’s ears, settle in their hearts and become songs of the heart. Our plans and ideas have given the country new life. When Chiang Kai-shek was in China, who knew the name of Mao Zedong? Where is Chiang Kai-shek now? Political prophecy is dangerous, but it cannot be discarded for that reason. Would anyone once have imagined that Omandurar himself would say, “Farmers, farmers, do not sleep! Rise up!”?

Acharya Kripalani declared from a public platform, “Incompetent Government, get out!” How patiently did Nehru listen to it? In political conditions, no one can foretell what will or will not happen. Our entire purpose is righteous struggle, righteous struggle, righteous struggle! A righteous struggle to cast away the old ideas born among the people from blind customs and practices!

## What Is Democracy?

In a town in Kerala, an important event during an annual temple festival is the erection of the flagstaff by an elephant. A large pit is dug, a great timber is placed in the elephant’s trunk, and the animal is made to lower it into the pit. One year, as the elephant refused to lower the flagstaff and created a commotion, the mahout looked inside the pit. A kitten had accidentally fallen into it.

They immediately removed the kitten and told the elephant to erect the flagstaff. In the same way, true democracy means governing so that the welfare of the minority is not harmed, even when one possesses majority strength. Those who conduct themselves like that elephant are the true democrats. I conclude by saying that we shall work to establish good government in the country.
