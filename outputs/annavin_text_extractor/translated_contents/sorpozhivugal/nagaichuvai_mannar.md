## English Translation

The moving address delivered on 14-1-1969 by Chief Minister Dr Arignar Anna at the grand arts festival unveiling the statue of Kalaivanar N. S. Krishnan, the last public event Anna attended in his lifetime:

Beloved Chairman, elders, and mothers!

It is most fitting that a statue-unveiling ceremony has been arranged on this Tamil festival day for the late King of Comedy, N. S. Krishnan. Though many feel that this should have taken place several years ago, our friend Vasan observed that the proper time had to arrive.

I do not think his statement that the proper time must arrive was mere fatalism.

I believe its true meaning could only have been that the good time must arrive when all of us, his friends, would govern the State. Kalaivanar possessed close friendship and association with the comrades of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, which governs today, and, as Revolutionary Actor Ramachandran said, with good people in every political party.

When we recognize that it was in N. S. Krishnan’s time that, for the first time, an artist attained a position worthy of respect from every political party, we have reason to rejoice again and again over his exceptional qualities.

Those who remain merely artists and perform artistic service serve only the world of art. Those who remain merely artists and increase their personal earnings place their families and friends in a good position.

But Kalaivanar chose the arts with the conviction that he must serve not only the artistic world but society as well, and that art must become a path toward that service. Today is the auspicious day of Pongal, a day that proclaims to the world the noble ideal that the whole of society must receive the true benefit of labour.

Kalaivanar’s success was a success founded upon labour. His close friends have spoken of this many times.

His exceptional labour secured a distinct place not only for himself and the arts, but for the comic role itself. A pasted moustache would suddenly fall off: that was a comic role. Someone would stumble and fall while walking: that was a comic role. N. S. Krishnan, the King of Comedy, transformed this and showed that a comic role could make people think, laugh, and gain a good benefit.

At this moment when his statue is being unveiled, a thousand and eight thoughts inevitably surge in the hearts of those like me who knew him closely. There is no time to explain all of them, nor is my physical condition suited to it. Yet, in our hearts, today and forever, not only we but all the people of the country will remember that he lived as an outstanding son of Tamil worthy of honour and praise.

It is also fitting that the procession committee of the World Tamil Conference has erected his statue. Through the conference procession, they gave the World Tamil Conference a distinguished standing; through a procession of the artistic world, they gave the world an outstanding genius.

Just as they completed this statue through their efforts, they have been carrying out a continuing constructive task for the arts. I convey my heartfelt thanks to that committee.

I state that I have not the slightest doubt that, today and forever, those in the world of art, those who have benefited from it, those in the world of reform, and every servant of society will honour N. S. Krishnan. With joy and pride, I unveil his statue.
