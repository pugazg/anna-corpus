## English Translation

Speech delivered among lawyers at Kanchipuram on 23-4-1967:

Judgments are delivered according to the law as it exists. They are not delivered after understanding the circumstances of the case. Instead of merely saying that the judgment must be whatever the law is, those giving judgment must also understand what the law ought to be. Legal experts must undertake appropriate efforts to amend the law accordingly.

Although the judiciary is subject to the legislature, we must not forget that the court is the foremost institution that dispenses justice to everyone and in all matters. Whether it is the court in Kanchipuram, a court in Calcutta, the supreme court in Delhi, or tribunals in the cities, all of them are courts: exalted institutions possessing the honour of dispensing justice.

If the laws enacted by the legislature contain faults or wrongs, legal experts must strive to amend the law, elevate its excellence, and strengthen respect for it. I wish to state that this is the duty and responsibility of lawyers.

In England, a country where revolutions and disorder have not occurred frequently, an agitation once arose in connection with the law.

The king imposed a tax. The court pointed out that “the imposition of the tax was wrong.” The king disregarded the court’s judgment and boasted, “I am the one who governs.” The Chief Justice replied, “You are the one who governs, but it is the court that must pronounce justice. If whatever you say is itself justice, it makes no difference whether I exist or not,” and resigned his office.

A great agitation followed. English history states that the matter was tried before the tribunal of justice established in front of the legislature and that the king was punished.

Therefore, courts must act with an understanding of their own high standing. Judges must deliver judgment without favour or prejudice and with an awareness of the justice of the case. They must create a condition in which no room for doubt remains in the minds of the people.

Legal experts know how a large majority in the legislature can lead to laws being passed in haste without listening to the opposition; how various political mishaps can result; and how High Court judges point out in their written judgments the errors that arise when some exploit loopholes in the law.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has now come to power, and many lawyers have been elected. Therefore, unlike before, we shall accord greater respect to the law. The laws we enact may take a little longer to pass, but we shall not create loopholes. We shall confer with legal experts, use the experience of those learned in law, and enact legislation. I assure you that we shall create respect and regard for the law among the public.
