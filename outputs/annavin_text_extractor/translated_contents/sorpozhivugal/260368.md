## English Translation

Statement delivered on 26 March 1968.

**The Hon. Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** Mr. Chairman, under Rule 82, I wish to place before the House an account of a regrettable sequence of events.

Yesterday evening, a clash occurred between a Law College student travelling on a Government bus and the conductor and driver.

Following this, the students pushed the bus into the Law College hostel and held it there. The clash between the student and the bus conductor and driver continued. A conductor and a driver were assaulted and admitted to hospital.

In connection with this, bus drivers and conductors went on strike. Leaving buses at various points along their routes, they marched in procession demanding an inquiry into the attack. One section went to the Chief Minister's residence and expressed its condemnation of the assault.

As soon as information about this regrettable incident was received, all the Ministers assembled at the Chief Minister's residence to discuss the situation.

All the Ministers met the workers who came there. The Chief Minister assured them that a proper inquiry would be conducted and justice secured, and appealed to them to maintain peace. The workers expressed agreement and left.

Meanwhile, senior Transport Department and police officers met the workers, promised to ensure justice, and asked them to bring the buses abandoned on the roads back to headquarters. The workers accordingly returned many buses.

The students refused to surrender the bus they had detained.

When workers travelled in two other buses to retrieve buses parked near Kellys, another clash arose between students and workers.

In the commotion inside the hostel, two workers and two students were injured. All were admitted to hospital.

I am compelled to convey the heartbreaking news that one of the workers who was attacked died in hospital. I am sure this House will join me in expressing its profound sympathy.

Following the assault, workers gathered near the Law College students' hostel. The Inspector-General and the Commissioner remained there and worked with great concern to establish peace and prevent a terrible confrontation between the students inside the hostel and the workers outside. Peace was not restored, however; the situation worsened from minute to minute.

At about three in the morning, Public Works Minister Karunanidhi and Minister Govindasamy went to the location. Minister Karunanidhi assured the workers that the events would be investigated, justice rendered, and action taken against those responsible. He appealed to them to restore peace. The workers refused to disperse and insisted that the students be punished immediately. The Minister's assurance that the matter would proceed according to due process did not satisfy them. The crowd did not disperse.

A similarly alarming situation developed near the Madras Medical College hostel. Conditions near the Arts College hostel also assumed a worrying form.

The Inspector-General and Commissioner undertook the task of dispersing the crowd and restoring peace. Police officers attempted to disperse it with a baton charge and tear gas.

Meanwhile, the Chief Minister summoned Left Communist Party leaders and members of the Legislature Thiru Kothandaraman and Thiru Govindarajan. He explained the situation and asked them to persuade the workers to disperse. They agreed, went to Kellys, spoke to the workers and laboured to restore peace. The Government conveys its appreciative thanks for the concern and ability they showed in this matter.

Their appeal, however, was not accepted by the workers.

Throughout the night, the Ministers concentrated anxiously on preventing this sorrowful incident from reaching terrifying proportions.

I ask the members of this House to support me and the Government in restoring the situation.

This is neither a party conflict nor an ideological confrontation. I therefore request responsible members of the House, committed to social welfare, justice, fairness, order and peace, to offer their valued support.

Everyone will feel inconsolable sorrow over the heartrending death of a fellow worker. Yet I appeal to all to ensure that the emotion surrounding it does not lead to a further breakdown of peace.

I inform the House that the Government has decided to conduct a judicial inquiry into the events.

The Government will undertake every measure needed to establish peace and justice.

A police investigation will also take place, and there will be neither hesitation nor partiality in taking action according to law.

**Thiru P. G. Karuthiruman:** Hon. Deputy Speaker, we have submitted an adjournment motion on this matter. The Chief Minister has presented the situation very directly and conveyed deeply moving news. On my own behalf and that of my party, I express with profound sorrow my deepest sympathy for the deceased comrade.

We too must accept social responsibility so that an amicable situation may be created. On my own behalf and that of my party, I assure the Minister that we will certainly help restore an amicable situation and that there is no political objection whatsoever.

I wish to bring one matter to their attention. Somehow the situation has gone out of control. Conditions at the Medical College hostel and Veterinary College hostel are reportedly serious. Even now a telephone call has arrived saying that some people have entered the Veterinary College and are attacking others.

My request is that they create conditions in which nobody and no force can enter student hostels and cause trouble. We must ensure that conditions in our country do not sink further. I wish to say that everyone bears that responsibility. Whenever wrong is committed in this manner, whoever commits it, immediate action must be taken.

In particular, some people are said to have entered the Medical College hostel and behaved in an uncivilised manner; this must be stopped. It is said that the same has happened at the Veterinary College. I trust the Chief Minister will immediately inform the police, provide the students proper protection, and take every measure to establish peace.

**The Hon. Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** All the measures mentioned by the Leader of the Opposition are being properly undertaken. But while these events continue to unfold, many different complaints keep arriving. We are examining all of them carefully.

I intended to mention one other matter, but while recounting this sorrowful incident I naturally omitted what was in my note. I wish to assure society that the people must remain calm and that appropriate action will be taken.

Not only we, but the leaders of all parties who have assumed great responsibility for this country, should gather at about one o'clock this afternoon, confer together, and issue a statement appealing to the public. I therefore warmly invite you to discuss it with us.

**Thiru P. G. Karuthiruman:** I assure you that all parties fully accept the Chief Minister's invitation and that we are ready to undertake every effort to establish peace.
