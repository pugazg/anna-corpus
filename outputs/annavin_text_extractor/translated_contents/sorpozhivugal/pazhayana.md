## English Translation

Speech delivered at Pachaiyappa’s College, Kanchipuram, in 1967:

Ten years ago, *namaskaram* and “good morning” had occupied the country. At that time, if one said “vanakkam,” people would look the speaker up and down and glare! It was not that there were no Tamil teachers then. Even here in Kanchipuram there were Tamil teachers such as Thirugnanasambandam. Yet they could not say “vanakkam.” How did that word enter usage? When innovation is needed, no one should prevent it from entering.

There was a time when people understood only if one said *manthiri*. Do we not now say *amaichar* and *amaicharavai*? The revolutionary poet said that even a maiden clothed in garments would not prevent the breeze from embracing her.

Old ideas that are no longer needed are being destroyed. At the same time, we have not grasped the new ideas either! We remain in an intermediate state. Somewhere in a corner of the newspaper, a single paragraph has reported the new spacecraft that reached the Moon. That Surveyor 3, which reached the Moon, has taken photographs of it and sent them back. But what had we been saying?

“The Moon’s sphere indeed! Soil indeed! Gold indeed! Digging indeed! Bringing anything back indeed!” Is that not what we had been saying? Without directly calling someone a madman, did we not say, “the man in the Moon”? Can we speak that way now? Knowledge is growing today to the point where tomorrow we shall travel to the lunar sphere.

A lesson may contain the line, “O white moon upon the Lord’s crown.” Ten years ago, a teacher explaining it would have said, “A crescent rested in Parama Siva’s matted hair, and the Ganges flowed there.” Today, he will say with some doubt, “The myth-makers say that a crescent rested there and that the Ganges flowed there!” Let another ten years pass. He will explain only that “the poet imagined it to be so.” Even if he does not interpret it that way, a situation will arise in which the teacher cannot manage the students!

Therefore, we must learn to remove the unnecessary old and embrace necessary innovations. Only then can we too advance in competition with other countries.
