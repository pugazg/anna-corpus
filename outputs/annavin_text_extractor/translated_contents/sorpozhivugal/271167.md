## English Translation

**The Hon. Thiru C. N. Annadurai:**

Mr. Chairman, while the discussion on this department was under way, our Leader of the Opposition ingeniously brought two points into it, although they were not relevant. I wish only to offer an explanation regarding them. Before that, several good suggestions were made here with the object of increasing the revenue earned through the Electricity Department. I thank the members of the Swatantra Party, the Communist Party and the Congress Party for suggesting that the electricity tariff charged to industries may be raised, so that this department's revenue may increase and it may develop further.

The second point made by our Leader of the Opposition was connected with a particular line of argument: "Who was responsible for the generation of electricity? Who was responsible for its development? Was it not we?" Even today the British may say, "Were we not the first to introduce electricity here?" Before them, even Edison's descendants may say, "Was it not we who discovered electricity?" There is no loss in periodically recalling the milestones along the way. They may claim that credit.

But what did they say in between? They said, "Electricity may now be plentiful. The reason is that industry is not functioning. Many factories have closed under your rule. That is why electricity cannot be used." Far from factories being closed during our administration, our achievement has been to reopen many factories that were closed when the government was handed over to us.

When we assumed office, many factories in Coimbatore were closed. We have now reopened some of them. Somasundaram Mill has reopened; Janardhana Mill has reopened; Ambal Mill has reopened; Cauvery Mill in Pudukkottai has reopened; and the Textool Company, which was closed during their time, is working today. Central Government Minister Thiru Dinesh Singh has assured us that, within another week or two, ways will be found to reopen Ranga Vilas and Cambodia Mills. All these are due to reopen.

When we point out that so many factories have reopened, they ask, "But are some others not still closed?" Reading the newspapers may create that impression. If Cellpark Company has closed, how many workers are employed there? Fewer than sixty. If Union Accessories has closed, how many workers are connected with it? Eighty. Such small establishments remain closed because, although the establishments are small, the obstinacy of those who run them is great.

Our Leader of the Opposition does not appear to have information that any factory employing one thousand or two thousand people, or even five or six hundred people, has closed.

**Thiru P. G. Karuthiruman:** Enfield!

**The Hon. Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** They must understand one thing. In industrial terminology, when a factory is described as "closed," it does not mean that it has been shut for good. There is a distinction between "closed" and "closure." Even "closure" need not mean that the factory has been pulled shut and will never operate again. It may remain under closure until a dispute is settled. The Government may inquire whether even that closure is justified or unjustified and have the factory reopened.

The office of *The Hindu* newspaper itself was under closure for about a month. *The Hindu* was not published during that time. It has now reopened. Therefore, if the Leader of the Opposition wants a line to appear in an evening newspaper saying that factories have closed on a large scale, and wishes to read it and rejoice, I lose nothing by his manufacturing that happiness for himself. It is enough if he understands that it is not the truth. He may tell his friends in the evening that numerous factories have closed. Let them even exclaim, "Oh! Is that so? What a dreadful government!" if they wish.

**Thiru K. Vinayakam:** New factories have opened too.

**The Hon. Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** Our friend always comes to our assistance. With great modesty, he points out that I omitted to mention that new factories have opened. I have been saying in public meetings as well as in the Assembly that I give first attention to agriculture, not industry. On hearing that, they assume that I pay no attention whatever to industry. I know that they will form such a mistaken impression. But I have neither obstructed nor failed to encourage the starting of new industries.

Two months ago, a pesticide factory belonging to the Tata company opened here in Ambattur. A new plastics factory began operations in Mettur three or four months ago. My friend, the Minister for Industries, has opened a bottle factory at Mettupalayam. I opened an expanded section of another factory in Ambattur. Besides these, he has opened no fewer than six tea factories operated on the cooperative model in the Nilgiris region. It is therefore wrong both to say that there has been no industrial growth and to say that all existing factories have closed.

They said that there is industrial unrest. I accept that. There is unrest in industry. I too reflected: "Unrest has arisen even after we came to office. I have always desired peace; should this happen under our administration?" I examined the lists for the previous two or three years. No year's list was shorter than this year's. In other words, a bonus dispute arises around Deepavali. Just as the sound of firecrackers is certain to reach our ears during Deepavali, the workers' demand too is certain to reach us.

One such dispute occurred at the office of *The Hindu*. It did not happen only now. Last year too, during this same month and Deepavali season, that newspaper office was closed over a bonus dispute. The difference between then and now is that this time it remained closed for nearly forty days, yet there was no disorder of any kind. Last year, under that "righteous government," its glass windows were broken on the third day of the closure.

**Thiru K. Vinayakam:** The manager was assaulted.

**The Hon. Thiru C. N. Annadurai:** What Thiru Vinayakam says is true. But what was said at that time was that those workers had acted with the support of some important person connected with the Government. He will deny that portion; I know it. He denies it because that part affects them. But I wish the Leader of the Opposition to understand that the dispute has now been settled peacefully.

Apart from this, the electricity tariff now charged to industrial establishments is very low. I thank the members for their excellent suggestion that it should be increased.
