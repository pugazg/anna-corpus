ரங்கநாதர்: ஆமாம் சாமி! சந்தேகமென்ன, ஆனால் எங்கே இந்த ஜனங்களுக்கு இந்த ஞானம் வரப்போகுது. என்னமோ எல்லாவற்றையும் தங்கள் தலைமேலே மூட்டை கட்டி வைத்து தூக்கிக் கொண்டு போகப்போவது போலத்தான் அலைகிறதுகள்.

ராக: ஞானம் இல்லை, முதலியாரவாள்! ஞானம் ஏற்படவில்லை, ஏன் என்கிறீர்? சத்சங்கம் இல்லை. சத்விஷயங்களைப் பத்தின கேள்வி ஞானம், இல்லை!

ரங்: நீங்க ஒரு பைத்தியம் சாமி! அவனுங்கதான், நம்மை எல்லாம் ஞான சூன்யங்கள்னு ஏசறானுங்க... நம்ம பேச்சையாவது அவனுங்க கேட்பதாவது...

ராக: ஒரு யோசனை சொல்லட்டுமா?

ரங்: எதுக்கு?

ராக: ஜனங்களோட க்ஷேமத்துக்குத்தான்.

ரங்: என்னது அது...

ராக: ஸ்ரீமத் ராமாயண காலட்சேபம் ஏற்பாடு செய்துடுவோம் - ஆறு மாதம் - சித்ரகூடப் பாகவதர்னு கேள்வியுண்டோ, மகா சிலாக்கியமான உபன்யாசம்...

ரங்: யாரு? யாரோ, உங்க பந்துன்னு சொல்வாங்களே...

ராக: ஆமாம்... கலெக்டராகப் போயிருக்க வேண்டியவர், எம்.ஏ., பி.எல்....

ரங்: கேள்விதான்... ரிவின்யூ இலாக்காவிலே இருக்கச்சே என்னமோ இரண்டு மூணாயிரம் கையாண்டதாக...

## Image 1: 001-da90ed7660.png

### The Canto of Defeat

**Raghavachariar:** Mudaliar! What is there in any of this? It is all a puppet show lasting but a moment. What else!

**Ranganathar:** Yes, Swami! No doubt about it. But how are these people ever going to acquire such wisdom? They roam about as though they are going to bundle everything upon their heads and carry it away with them.

**Raghavachariar:** There is no wisdom, Mudaliar! Why, you ask, has wisdom not arisen? There is no fellowship with the virtuous. They have no knowledge gained by hearing about noble matters!

**Ranganathar:** You are mad, Swami! They are the ones who abuse all of us as empty-headed fools... As though they would ever listen to what we say...

**Raghavachariar:** Shall I offer a suggestion?

**Ranganathar:** What for?

**Raghavachariar:** For the welfare of the people, of course.

**Ranganathar:** What is it?

**Raghavachariar:** Let us arrange a six-month discourse on the sacred Ramayana. Have you heard of Chitrakooda Bhagavathar? His exposition is most excellent...

**Ranganathar:** Who? The man they say is some relative of yours?

**Raghavachariar:** Yes... A man who ought to have become a Collector. M.A., B.L....

**Ranganathar:** I have heard of him... While he was in the Revenue Department, did he not handle some two or three thousand improperly...?

## Image 2: 002-01a1599b5b.png

**Raghavachariar:** Wicked men laid a false charge upon him! Had he fought the case, he would certainly have won. But do you know what he did? He cried, “Rama! Is this your trial of me? Here I come to serve you!” He took a vow, paid the entire three thousand without opening his mouth, and left. People said whatever came to mind: that payment would not save him and that he would be sent to prison. But is Rama's grace an ordinary thing? His superiors withdrew the case. Do you see the glory of Rama's name?

**Ranganathar:** You think it would be good to arrange a religious discourse by him?

**Raghavachariar:** I have already arranged it, Mudaliar! He is coming here next Wednesday...

**Ranganathar:** Is that so? Very well...

**Raghavachariar:** Altogether, one thousand and one...

**Ranganathar:** Rupees?

**Raghavachariar:** We could even give sovereigns... But would that be possible?

**Ranganathar:** How could it be? Look at me. For the reading of the Mahabharata at our village Draupadi Amman festival, I engaged our Padavettarayar. Now I am suffering no end. Not one fellow will give a paisa. The entire expense has fallen upon my head. It has become terribly difficult...

**Raghavachariar:** The Mahabharata is indeed a sacred tale... Nevertheless...

**Ranganathar:** You mean it cannot equal the Ramayana. Yes, no doubt. In keeping with my means, I have arranged the Mahabharata. In keeping with your means, you arrange the Ramayana. Only when each of us takes up one such undertaking will things come together. Shall I leave? The fire-walking is the day after tomorrow; I must go by the firewood shop...

**Raghavachariar:** Very well...

### The Canto of Commotion

**Kamatchi:** Fruitful or fruitless?

**Raghavachariar:** If I went to him, even a ripe fruit would rot. He is an obstinate miser...

**Kamatchi:** If you went to work before dawn, how could you possibly succeed? Where would cleverness ever come to you?

**Raghavachariar:** Be quiet! I spoke for four long hours, saying this and that, and proposed holding the Ramayana discourse. He drove me away saying that he was already conducting the Mahabharata in his village...

**Kamatchi:** So what? You should have said, “With your wealth and income, is this such a great matter? If a son of Lakshmi like you will not undertake such a cause, who else will come forward?” You should have spoken like that and softened his heart... Where would such skill come to you? The moment people see your face and that jutting chin, they pull the door shut and bolt it...

**Raghavachariar:** Enough of your chatter. Your face is shining so brightly; go show it to him... I do not know how to speak cleverly. You do, do you not? Go, speak to him, win him over, and come back. Let us see...

**Kamatchi:** You know how to speak abusively and pick quarrels like this. What else do you know? Though you lack the skill to finish the task for which you went, anger wells up in you at once...

**Raghavachariar:** Rama! Rama! Rama! Better to beat my head and go wandering across the land than to lose my life trapped with her. Why not call your brother and ask him to conduct the Ramayana? The whole town knows that he was caught in a bribery case and dismissed from his job...

**Kamatchi:** What if they know? It was merely an unlucky time, a period of trial... Did he ever go to prison and return?

## Image 4: 004-424101b009.png

### The Canto of Counsel

**Kamala:** Mother, why is Father sitting there sulking?

**Kamatchi:** He will sit and sulk, then spring up and snarl. What else does he know?

**Kamala:** Why did you quarrel with Father?

**Kamatchi:** If I do not quarrel with him, am I to romance such a fine specimen as your father? A good-for-nothing creature... It is my fate...

**Kamala:** You keep piling it on. Tell me what happened.

**Kamatchi:** Is it so very necessary for you to know?

**Kamala:** Tell me, Mother. Please tell me...

**Kamatchi:** Did I not tell him all day yesterday to go and see that Mudaliar about holding a Ramayana discourse?

**Kamala:** Yes. Did he not go?

**Kamatchi:** He went! And came home empty-handed. The man would not agree, it seems. Are we not supposed to make him agree? One must possess the ability for that. When a case was brought against my brother, if I had sat like such a simpleton, my brother would still be in prison...

**Kamala:** Yes, even the old woman next door says that you spoke to the Collector, won the case, and returned.

**Kamatchi:** You make it sound so easy: “You won the case and came back.” Did that Collector soften and come around so readily? At first he sent word through the peon that he did not make a practice of seeing women. I waited at the doorway... I went to Amba's shrine, had an offering performed, and then went to his bungalow. The peon came and said the master would not see me, because he was not accustomed to seeing women. “Good heavens! Go tell your master that we too are not accustomed to seeing men.

## Image 5: 005-92ff7a0880.png

“But this is a matter of grave danger, and that is why the Iyer's wife has come,” I told him to say. Only after that could I enter. The Collector spoke like sparks flying from a fire. “The bribe has been proved. I cannot let it pass. Crying is no use,” he thundered and raged. Afterwards, I spoke gently to him...

**Kamala:** Did he know Tamil?

**Kamatchi:** He spoke it haltingly. The man had a good heart. He roared only at first. Then I spoke to him tactfully, dispelled his anger, and softened his heart. He said, “Very well! I shall do it for your sake.” The case was dismissed. Your father does not possess even one-hundredth of a woman's cleverness...

**Kamala:** Everyone says that nobody can equal your cleverness!

### The Canto of the Coronation Song

**Ranganathar:** I thought that Iyer was merely chatting to pass the time, but he began trying his scheme on me...

**Sankarasivam:** Is that so? What did he ask for? Some kind of loan?

**Ranganathar:** Not that. He says the townspeople are perishing without wisdom. So the Ramayana must be conducted for six months, and I must provide the money.

**Sankarasivam:** That Brahmin is a clever fellow...

**Ranganathar:** Yes, and what does he think we are, fools? I kept saying, “Yes, yes. Certainly, Swami, it must be done. The Ramayana is sacred. I too am conducting the Mahabharata in my village and bearing all the expense myself.” I delivered that one blow...

**Sankarasivam:** Is the Mahabharata really being conducted there?

**Ranganathar:** The Bhagavatham is being conducted... Look at the fellow! As though we have no other work... I simply made it up, man... The moment I said it,

he shut his mouth and left. Good heavens! If we are not vigilant in every way, these fellows will grind chillies upon our very heads. The elders did not call the Brahmin a deadly schemer without reason...

### The Canto of Appropriating Money

**Kamala:** This is Ranganatha Mudaliar, the great lord of our town, a man of land and charity...

**Ranganathar:** Oh, please, you are piling it on too high... Both of you sit down... Sankarasivam, the accountant! Run and buy some coloured drinks; come back quickly... And who are you?

**Kamala:** She is my friend, the social worker Charubala...

**Ranganathar:** I do not know her... I am not in the habit of watching films and such...

**Kamala:** Mudaliar! Must you speak in such haste? Do you know who Charubala is? She is the daughter of Income-tax Officer Kothandarama Iyer...

**Ranganathar:** Is that so? Forgive me...

**Charubala:** Never mind, Kamala! Seeing my dress, he must have taken me for a cinema star...

**Ranganathar:** Yes... No... It was my mistake... Is your father keeping well? Your father has a very good name in this town...

**Charubala:** Why only here? It is the same in every town where he works.

**Ranganathar:** He is fire itself when he sees people doing wrong... For some people, the mere mention of his name is a nightmare...

**Kamala:** That is how he is... But his heart is gold. One learns that only after knowing him. Now our Charu has begun an undertaking. She has arranged to conduct a complete Ramayana discourse...

## Image 7: 007-113f783c4e.png

**Ranganathar:** I am delighted... Tell me without hesitation where it will be, when it will be, and what I must do...

**Kamala:** Would anyone hesitate over a righteous cause? Look, in this matter I have even set myself against my own father. Charubala said that if this discourse was to be truly distinguished, Chitrakooda Bhagavathar himself must come. My father had apparently already arranged to bring him here. I decided that we would offer five hundred more than my father was giving, sent a telegram, and obtained the Bhagavathar's consent... My father's face is blazing with rage.

**Ranganathar:** You have acted very cleverly... Does the officer, that is, our Income-tax Officer, like the Ramayana very much?

**Kamala:** Tell him, Charu! The Ramayana is life itself to her father... When the story reaches the exile in the forest, tears pour from his eyes in streams...

**Ranganathar:** At that point, the anger I feel toward Kaikeyi is beyond words... Tell the officer that he should come often to hear the discourse...

**Kamala:** Will he merely come often? You are quite innocent, Mudaliar! He himself is going to preside over the opening ceremony...

**Ranganathar:** Splendid! Then it is settled...

**Kamala:** Guess who is coming for Sita's wedding.

**Ranganathar:** Who?

**Kamala:** Our Rajaji!

**Ranganathar:** Ah! What an excellent arrangement! Rama! Rama! Your greatness is indeed great.

**Kamala:** We do not know whether our plan for the coronation will succeed... There is no harm in telling you: our Governor...

**Ranganathar:** Our Governor? What has happened to him?

**Kamala:** Oh, no! Nothing has happened to him, Mudaliar! We are inviting him to the coronation...

**Ranganathar:** Wonderful! If you accomplish that, our whole town will overflow with pride...

**Kamala:** It will cost a great deal... Look, we described all this at length to Kannaiya Chettiar. That miser gave a hundred rupees and told us not to trouble him again.

**Charubala:** Kamala! Some people will always be misers. Their hearts will not permit them to give to Rama's cause, but they will pour out money for income tax...
