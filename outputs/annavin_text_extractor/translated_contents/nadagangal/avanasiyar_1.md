அவனாசியார்

காணவேண்டிய காட்சி

இடம்: தமிழாசிரியர் வகுப்பு.

காலம்: பிற்பகல்.

நிலைமை: தமிழாசிரியர், புதுப்பாடம் கற்றுக் கொடுத்தான
பிறகு, களைத்து, இரண்டோர் நிமிஷம், மெளனமாக இருக்கிறார்.

உடனே, மாணவர்கள் பேசத் துவங்கி விடவே, வகுப்பிலே
இறைச்சலாகி விடுகிறது. இந்த இறைச்சலைக் கேட்டு, கோபம்
கொள்கிறார்; தமிழாசிரியர். மேற்கொண்டு பாடம் நடத்த விரும்ப
வில்லை. எனவே, பழைய பாடங்களிலிருந்து, கேள்விகள் கேட்க
லானார்.

த.ஆ.: லோகங்கள் எத்தனை சொல்லு! டே! சுப்பு உன்னைத்
தாண்டா, மண்டு.

மா: வெள்ளி, பித்தளை, செம்பு. . .

த.ஆ: தலை தலை என்று அடித்துக் கொண்டு எங்காவது
தேசாந்திரம் போய்விடலாம் போல இருக்கிறது, இந்தச் சனியன்
களோடு மாரடித்துக் கொண்டிருப்பதை விட. ஏண்டா, சுப்பு
உன்னை நான், லோகங்கள் எத்தனை என்று கேள்வி கேட்
டேண்டா, உலோகங்களை அல்ல.

மாண: நான், உலோகங்களைத்தான் கேட்கிறீர் என்று
எண்ணிக் கொண்டேன்.

த.ஆ. சரி, சொல்லு எத்தனை லோகங்கள்.

மாண: கீழ் ஏழு, மேல் ஏழு, ஆக பதினாலு, அதல, சதல,
விதல, தராதல. . .

த.ஆ.: சரி, உட்கார், டே! பரந்தாமன் பள்ளி கொண்டிருக்கும்
கடலின் பெயர் என்ன? உன்னைத் தாண்டா, உன்மத்தம்!

மாண: திருப்பாற் கடல்

புரட்டுமாக இருக்கிற பழைய சரக்குகளை எல்லாம் பயங்காட்டிக்
காட்டி எங்க மனசிலே புகுத்தினாக் கூட, அதெல்லாம் ஜீரண
மாகுமா, வாந்தியாகிவிடும்.

[மறுபடியும் வகுப்பிலே சிரிப்பு]

ஆசிரியர்: அப்படியானா, மந்திரி அவினாசிலிங்கமும் உங்க
அம்மாவும் ஒரே விதந்தான்.

மாணவன்: அது எப்படி, சார், இரண்டு பேரும் ஒண்ணு.
எங்க அம்மாவுக்கு, மாசாமாசம் ஆயிரம் ரூபா சம்பளமா வருது?

[மீண்டும் சிரிப்பு]

வேறு மாணவன்: டே! எங்க அம்மா, எங்க அம்மான்னு
சொல்கிறயே, நிஜமாகவா...?

மாணவன்: எங்க அம்மான்னா, சொந்த அம்மா இல்லே,
எங்க அப்பாவுக்கு இப்ப வந்தவங்க - இரண்டாந்தாரம்.

## English Translation

Place: A Tamil teacher's classroom.

Time: Afternoon.

Situation: After teaching a new lesson, the Tamil teacher is tired and remains silent for a minute or two.

The students immediately begin talking, and the classroom becomes noisy. Hearing the noise, the Tamil teacher grows angry. He does not wish to continue the lesson, so he begins asking questions from earlier lessons.

Tamil Teacher: Tell me, how many worlds are there? Hey, Suppu, I mean you, fool!

Student: Silver, brass, copper...

Tamil Teacher: Rather than struggle with these wretches, I feel like beating my head and wandering off somewhere. Suppu, did I ask you how many metals there are? I asked how many worlds there are.

Student: I thought you were asking about metals.

Tamil Teacher: All right. Tell me how many worlds.

Student: Seven below and seven above, fourteen altogether: Atala, Sutala, Vitala, Talatala...

Tamil Teacher: Fine, sit down. Hey! What is the name of the ocean upon which Paranthaman reclines? I mean you, Unmatham!

Student: The sacred Ocean of Milk.

[The school bell rings.]

[Several students]

Student: Sir! Sir! The bell has rung.

Tamil Teacher: Nobody here is deaf. I know the bell has rung. Tomorrow, *Nalavenba*. Remember?

Student: Yes, sir!

Tamil Teacher: Hey! The passage where the swan is sent as a messenger is tomorrow's lesson.

[The Tamil teacher leaves.]

[The classroom immediately erupts in noise as the children begin playing.]

One Student: Hey! Can a swan talk?

Another Student: Get lost! How could a swan talk? It is only a story.

Another Student: A lie.

One Student: All right, but why does the teacher make our lives miserable by asking us to study lies and fabrications like this?

Another Student: What can he do? He can teach only what is in the book.

One Student: But wasn't that book also written by a teacher like him?

Another Student: Yes.

Another Student: Why did that man lie? I do not know why all these people conduct nothing but a trade in bundles of lies...

[Some students]

Hey! The teacher! The teacher!

[A teacher approaches the classroom.]

[After the teacher sits down]

Teacher: Questions first, then the new lesson. Close your books. Hey! How many continents are there?

One Student: Atala, Sutala, Taratala...

[The teacher angrily walks over and slaps the student on the cheek.]

Teacher: You monkey! Are you being insolent? Are you wagging your tail at me?

[The student does not cry. He gives the teacher a mischievous look. The teacher grows still angrier and speaks while rapping the student on the head.]

Teacher: Atala, Sutala, Taratala, you fool...

Student: Sir! I spoke without remembering that this was the geography class.

Teacher: I will cut your tail clean off. Be careful.

Student: I did not say it deliberately, sir. In Tamil class, the teacher asked how many worlds there were.

Teacher: Spill your Atala, Sutala and all that nonsense there. Here...

Student: Here, sir, we must answer by looking at the pictures in the atlas.

Teacher: In geography class the names are Africa, America, Asia and so on. There is no Atala, Sutala or Taratala here.

One Student: We are only small children, sir. Why do they confuse us by teaching us two different sets of answers like this?

[Forgetting his anger, the teacher laughs.]

[The student too forgets the beating and laughs.]

Student: Sir! The Tamil teacher says there is an Ocean of Milk. You say there is a Pacific Ocean.

Teacher: Yes. What can be done about that? The Tamil teacher will even tell you the story of the ten-headed Ravana. Can all that be true?

Student: Then why does the Tamil teacher tell lies, sir?

Teacher: [Laughing] Go on! Is he the one who invented the lie? That is how it is written in the book...

Student: If we make even one spelling mistake, you rap us on the head and make us stand on the bench...

Teacher: All right, all right! Sit down.

Student: Sir, the ten-headed Ravana, the Ocean of Milk and the netherworld: all these are false, aren't they?

Teacher: Yes. But from now on, none of us may speak to you about them in this way.

Student: Why, sir?

Teacher: Government orders.

Student: Has it ordered you to teach lies and frauds as lessons without explaining any of them?

Teacher: Sit down! You will not understand.

Student: Sir, please explain it so that we can understand.

Teacher: The old stories, Puranas and epics contain plenty of lies and fabrications. But if we explain that, it will be treated as mocking religion and speaking disrespectfully of it. Therefore teachers must no longer talk like this. The minister has ordered that anyone who does so be dismissed.

Student: Come on, sir! Even in a play there would not be a minister who issued an order like that.

Teacher: It is true. There is a minister named Avinashilingam. He issued this order.

Student: What an injustice! Then it means the government wants to fill our minds with lies before sending us away.

Teacher: The government thinks that questions and answers would destroy belief in the Puranas and religious faith.

Another Student: My mother thinks exactly the same way.

Teacher: Your mother?

Student: Yes, sir! Sometimes she serves curry made in the morning. I usually smell it first. She says, "You donkey! Why are you smelling it? Just eat it without talking."

Teacher: Why?

Student: The curry may have gone sour. If I smell it, I will know. That is why she orders me not to smell it.

[The teacher and students laugh.]

Student: If I fear my mother's authority and eat it, I become nauseated and vomit afterward.

Teacher: Yes. If you eat spoiled food, you will vomit. It will not remain inside or be digested.

Student: Exactly, sir! In the same way, even if they frighten us and force all this old stock of lies and fabrications into our minds, can we digest it? We will vomit it out.

[The class laughs again.]

Teacher: In that case, Minister Avinashilingam and your mother are alike.

Student: How can the two be alike, sir? Does my mother receive a salary of one thousand rupees every month?

[Laughter again.]

Another Student: Hey! You keep saying "my mother." Do you really mean...?

Student: When I say my mother, I do not mean my own mother. I mean the woman my father has now married, his second wife.

[Laughter again.]

[The teacher strikes the table. Silence settles over the classroom, and he begins the lesson.]

Teacher: The perennial rivers Ganges, Brahmaputra, Indus and others rise in the Himalayas...

[While he teaches, the students still hear in their ears the lesson previously given by the Tamil teacher.]

Tamil Teacher's Voice: In the Himalayas, Lord Shiva stands with Goddess Parvati beside him and Goddess Ganga in his matted locks, watching Arumugan and Vinayaka playing before him...

[The class continues.]

*Dravida Nadu*, 19 October 1947.

## Translator's Notes

The printed title `அவனாசியார்` is a satirical coinage that echoes Education Minister T. S. Avinashilingam's name; it is therefore transliterated as *Avanasiyar* rather than normalized into a different Tamil phrase. Images 2-5 were recovered directly from the scans after the original OCR pass missed their transparent-background text. The humour in the opening depends on the near-homophones `லோகங்கள்` (worlds) and `உலோகங்கள்` (metals); the translation explains the distinction through context.
