## English Translation

Madurai, June 27.

A case is under way against twenty-three people accused of deceiving the public by claiming that their magical power could double currency notes.

## Scene 1

**Time:** Night.

**Place:** The grocery market.

**Present:** Proprietor Mannarsami Chettiar and accountant Kannusami.

**Mannarsami Chettiar:** Pull the lock and make sure it is secure.

**Kannusami:** I locked it and shook it hard three times, sir!

[Chettiar begins speaking as they walk along the darkened road.]

**Mannarsami:** Accountant, they say some swami has come to town. Have you gone to see him?

**Kannusami:** Will seeing the swami put food in our stomachs?

**Mannarsami:** Hey, the whole town praises him. They say crowds stand there cheering every day!

**Kannusami:** Ten people gather around a madman, and around a rich man...

[The accountant swallows the words that have slipped from his mouth.]

**Mannarsami:** They say he is extremely powerful.

**Kannusami:** Somehow, whenever I see fellows of this kind, I dislike them. Thieves, murderers and men escaped from prison wrap themselves in ochre, chant “Om Namah Sivaya,” and deceive the town. Why waste time talking about them?

**Mannarsami:** Why do you chatter all at once? When I try to learn a little about the holy man, you evade me and pour out endless talk.

[Chettiar laughs. The accountant grins.]

**Kannusami:** Forgive me, sir. It has become late. If you wish, I shall learn everything and return tomorrow.

[Chettiar calls the accountant close and speaks in a lowered voice.]

**Mannarsami:** Hey, is it true that this swami manufactures brand-new currency notes?

**Kannusami:** I do not know for certain, sir. There are twenty-two sturdy disciples there. I shall bring one of them. We can discuss everything face to face and settle it.

[They reach the street leading to Chettiar's house, and he leaves. The accountant walks alone.]

**Kannusami:** He roams about possessed by greed for money. The money he already has would last another eight generations, yet he wants to print new notes. Madman!

[He reaches his home.]

## Scene 2

**Time:** Dawn.

**Place:** Riverbank.

**Present:** Accountant Kannusami, disciple Vayalappan and Mannarsami Chettiar.

[Kannusami sees the disciple Vayalappasami finish bathing and climb onto the bank.]

**Kannusami:** Swami, I must speak with you privately.

**Disciple:** Devotee, do not fear. You may ask me anything.

**Kannusami:** Your guru...

**Disciple:** A great saint who has attained a glory no one on earth has ever attained.

**Kannusami:** He...

**Disciple:** Cures incurable karmic afflictions and removes diseases that refuse to depart.

**Kannusami:** Is that all?

**Disciple:** There are a thousand things to say, but no time to recount all his magnificence. Through Gurusami's exceptional gaze, hunchbacks stand upright and walk. By the glory of his sacred ash, the blind receive sight and rejoice. Through the magic of his noble speech, the deaf gain hearing, drink in music and delight.

**Kannusami:** And then?

**Disciple:** He removes witchcraft and grants the boon of children.

**Kannusami:** Hmm.

**Disciple:** He turns copper into pure gold and black stone into diamond.

**Kannusami:** Does he indeed?

**Disciple:** Why doubt, Muruga?

**Kannusami:** Not that, sir. If old currency notes are given, an equal quantity of new notes...

**Disciple:** Devotee, you are intelligent, extraordinarily intelligent. Why stop what you came to say? Do not hesitate to tell the truth. The power of our great guru is known to the world.

If he wishes, forget new notes: before one closes and opens one's eyes, he can build a fortress that makes everyone tremble.

**Kannusami:** You must show us a little mercy.

**Disciple:** We are messengers sent by God solely to bestow compassion. Why hesitate to show mercy? Ask freely. The boon asked without hesitation will be granted.

**Kannusami:** Come with me to the house of my employer, Mannarsami Chettiar of the grocery market.

**Disciple:** Whether there is a car, a bullock cart, or we must walk, there is no obstacle, devotee. Come.

[Upon reaching Chettiar's house, the accountant rushes inside and reports the matter. Chettiar runs out to welcome him.]

**Mannarsami:** Swami, please come, please come!

**Disciple:** May every good thing increase.

**Mannarsami:** Sit here. Accountant, bring milk. Give the swami fruit if he wants it, and purchase sweets if he desires them.

[Kannusami brings a plate piled with idlis, dosas and fruit, and a vessel full of milk. The disciple consumes everything and belches.]

**Mannarsami:** Swami...

**Disciple:** Ask without multiplying words. You want Gurusami to grant you the boon of doubling currency notes, is that all?

**Mannarsami:** Speech compressed like a jewel!

**Disciple:** Then tomorrow bring the ritual articles and however many notes you wish doubled, tied inside a silk bag.

**Mannarsami:** Very well.

**Disciple:** I shall take leave. Send a disciple's offering through your servant. I shall speak to the great guru and see.

[The disciple leaves, with the accountant following.]

## Scene 3

**Time:** Evening.

**Place:** The “Double” Swami's ashram.

**Present:** The swami, his disciple Vayalappan, Kannusami and Mannarsami Chettiar.

**Disciple:** Chettiar, have you come with all the arrangements I specified?

**Mannarsami:** Every ritual article on the list you sent has arrived. I did not omit a single thing.

**Disciple:** [In a low voice] The currency notes...

**Mannarsami:** I have them, twenty thousand rupees.

**Disciple:** Do not imagine that Guruji is an ordinary man. He is a master of doubling. Through his grace, many people now live in eight-storey mansions. Yet he is a saint and lives only in a mud hut. If he sets his mind to it, he can make the sky a roof, clouds its walls, lightning its electric lamps, and the air itself into a moving city like a Pontiac!

**Kannusami:** It is getting late. If the proper hour passes, the undertaking may be reduced to ashes.

**Disciple:** Do not panic, devotee! The hour and the stars are not unknown to me. Behold the saint's greatness. Guruji's very name is “Double” Swami. He earned that special name by dividing into two the wealth entrusted to him and returning it doubled!

**Kannusami:** “Duggle” Swami?

**Mannarsami:** You great fool! He said “double,” and you babble “duggle.” In English it means two.

**Disciple:** Chettiar, there is the prayer room. Go inside!

[The heavy scent of incense fills the room. As they enter, the swami opens his eyes.]

**Swami:** Devotee, what is your request?

**Mannarsami:** Swami, you must save me by doubling this bundle of money I have brought.

**Swami:** Is that so? Place the bundle here. Wait outside for half an hour and take it when summoned. Perform worship for fifteen days, then open it. You will be astonished. You will come running in search of me, leaping and dancing. But who knows whether your sins may perform some divine sport of their own!

**Mannarsami:** Sins...?

**Swami:** You are no sinner, devotee! But even the deeds of those around you may come to surround you.

**Mannarsami:** In that case...

**Swami:** Do not fear, devotee. Go outside. Wait patiently and smile. I shall call you in just a little while.

[As Chettiar goes outside, the disciple enters. The accountant watches the scene with amusement. Later...]

**Swami:** Chettiar, come and take your bundle of money. On the fifteenth day, what you desire will happen.

**Mannarsami:** I shall come, sir.

[Chettiar and Kannusami walk away. The disciple doubles over with inward laughter.]

## Scene 4

**Time:** Midnight.

**Place:** Mannarsami's house.

**Present:** Mannarsami, Kannusami, the swami and the disciple.

[Chettiar's impatience torments him. On the night of the fourteenth day itself, he unties the bundle and looks inside. His eyes dart in bewilderment. He strikes his mouth and stomach. Afraid that his sleeping wife in the next room may wake, he suppresses his cry, crosses the hall and comes outside. The accountant is sitting there.]

**Kannusami:** Well, sir, was it there?

**Mannarsami:** What was there? Nothing! The thieving rogues have cheated me. It is all gone. The bundle contains nothing but blank paper!

**Kannusami:** Alas!

**Mannarsami:** Come with me. We shall go and confront those thieving fellows!

[The two hurry to the place, half running and half walking. They see no one. They enter. The swami and Vayalappan are smoking cannabis.]

**Swami:** Hey, gentleman... what do you want?

**Mannarsami:** Keep some of my money and at least return the rest, you sinful wretches!

**Swami:** Do not talk too much. You came without being invited! I did not ask for it. You gave it yourself. I did not deceive you. But you were deceived!

**Mannarsami:** I will tell the police...

**Swami:** The police station is not here.

**Mannarsami:** You thieving rogues!

[Chettiar collapses in despair.]

**Kannusami:** I told you so at the very beginning, sir!

[At that moment someone shouts, “Police!” The swami and disciple do not move. They continue drawing on their cannabis. Chettiar and Kannusami look for a way to flee.]

*Dravida Nadu*, 1 July 1950.

## Translator's Notes

Images 1 and 5-7 were recovered from transparent-background scans after the original OCR pass treated them as blank. The title `எத்தன் திருவிளையாடல்` combines `எத்தன்` (swindler, trickster) with `திருவிளையாடல்` (a deity's sacred or divine sport), satirically transferring the religious expression to a confidence trick. The short Madurai news item preceding Scene 1 supplies the real-world premise. The source's `பாண்டியாக்` is conservatively rendered “Pontiac”; its precise intended comparison is uncertain. The mistranscription “Duggle” represents Kannusami's comic mishearing of the English word “double.”
