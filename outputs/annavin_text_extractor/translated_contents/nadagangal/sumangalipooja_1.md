## English Translation

**Characters**

**Pakkiyanathan:** A wealthy man  
**Andal:** His wife  
**Sankaralingam:** A poor Brahmin  
**Ekambaram:** A poor man  
**Ellamma:** His wife

**Ekambaram:** Mirashdar! I have been trying to see you for a week...

**Pakkiyanathan:** Have you been coming and going? What can I do? My work never ends.

**Ekambaram:** Yes! You have to attend to the fields and look after the shops...

**Pakkiyanathan:** Good heavens! Not that kind of work, man! By God's grace, we have none of that work. There is a perfectly honest manager to look after it. Have we not started a new association?

**Ekambaram:** Which association?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Why, when cows grow very old these wretched people sell them for slaughter, do they not? We have founded a new association to put an end to that sin: the Gomatha Association.

**Ekambaram:** Yes, yes, I heard about it. An excellent undertaking, a service that ought to be done.

**Pakkiyanathan:** I thought I would do whatever I could, and even agreed to become its president.

**Ekambaram:** Is that so? Then what could the association possibly lack now!

**Pakkiyanathan:** It is for that undertaking that I have been running about for a week. We must collect one lakh of rupees within this month.

**Ekambaram:** One lakh of rupees? Why, sir?

**Pakkiyanathan:** What a madman you are! I said one lakh, but our Navaluthu village headman still insists that the work cannot be done for less than four lakhs. You nearly faint as soon as I say one lakh! We need twenty acres of land in one continuous square. There must be a large pond in its middle, without even steps, so the cows can walk in and drink. There must be grazing facilities and people to supervise all this. And every Friday there will be a Sumangali Puja. One lakh is for all these things together.

**Ekambaram:** Oh, is that so? Very well. It will cost that much; it certainly will.

**Pakkiyanathan:** In one week of going around for this lakh, we have obtained pledges for sixty thousand and received forty-two thousand in hand.

**Ekambaram:** Forty-two thousand rupees?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Yes! I have put in six thousand myself, and that was a cash contribution. But...

**Ekambaram:** If your services were valued, they would amount to many thousands more.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Leave even that aside! Whatever we do, we are seeking merit for our journey beyond this life, are we not? Good dry-crop land, our own, twenty acres in one square: even if one bought it from a thief, it could not be had for less than four lakhs. I have given such land to the Cow Service Association for fifty thousand.

**Ekambaram:** You have given it away?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Yes! Had I not, however many lakhs were collected, where could they find one continuous tract?

**Ekambaram:** They could not, sir. They could not.

**Pakkiyanathan:** If no land were available, what could the Cow Service Association do? Could it let the cows graze?

**Ekambaram:** It could not. It could not.

**Pakkiyanathan:** That is why I gave the land to the association, even though it meant some financial loss to us. Our manager too was rather distressed. He had planned to put in machinery and turn that tract into a field of maize. I told him, "Enough, man. What God has given us is sufficient. Let us give this land for a meritorious cause," and pacified him.

**Ekambaram:** Very well... I came, sir...

**Pakkiyanathan:** You too must offer whatever help you can to this meritorious cause.

**Ekambaram:** Certainly, certainly. But what could a man like me do?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Do not say that. Even a tiny splinter given with a good heart becomes a bar of gold.

**Ekambaram:** Yes, sir, yes.

**Pakkiyanathan:** You need not undergo any great hardship. Take charge of the arrangements for this Sumangali Puja.

**Ekambaram:** What puja is that?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Fool! The finest part of this undertaking is the Sumangali Puja. We keep cows in the association and protect them from death and from being sent to slaughter. Every Friday, all the sumangalis in our town must come to the association grounds and perform cow worship. Nothing else yields the blessings of Gomatha Puja: sons, growing wealth, improving health, every divine favour.

**Ekambaram:** Very well. What articles are needed for the puja?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Is there some fixed rule about articles for worship? Devotion is what matters. Just a little camphor, a bundle of agathi greens, kumkum and turmeric. Those are all.

**Ekambaram:** Yes, they say that offering agathi greens to a cow is especially important.

**Pakkiyanathan:** They say that giving agathi greens to a cow is equal to giving gold to a Brahmin in Kasi. It is apparently written in one of the four Vedas.

Therefore, you need only explain this distinction to the sumangalis of our town and arrange the puja. You will earn more merit than we do by giving thousands and thousands of rupees.

**Ekambaram:** That is true, sir.

**Pakkiyanathan:** You have arrived at a good time. Attend to this task for four days beginning tomorrow. Hey! Narayana! Narayana!

**Ekambaram:** I came about another matter, sir.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Naturally. Let it wait; we shall discuss it after four days.

**Ekambaram:** It is a little urgent.

**Pakkiyanathan:** It may be. But at present my whole mind is immersed in the affairs of the Gomatha Association. I tell you truthfully, whether awake or dreaming, all I see before my eyes is a herd of cows.

**Ekambaram:** Once you have made up your mind, could an undertaking ever remain unfinished?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Can we simply say so? Look, even the collection work is not yet complete.

**Ekambaram:** The collection will come through, sir.

**Pakkiyanathan:** It will. But only when the collection is over will my mind be at peace and I be able to attend to anything else.

**Ekambaram:** Yes, peace of mind is needed for everything.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Look here. I may appear to be talking with you, but do you know where I truly am at this moment? I am at Rudra Koteeswara Mudaliar's bungalow. He said he would give the money after eight tonight. I must send our boy Narayanan. That is why I called him. He seems to be occupied with something else. I must go towards the garden, find that boy and hurry him along. Shall I leave?

**Ekambaram:** I wanted to discuss one matter...

**Pakkiyanathan:** Did I not say that no other talk will enter my ears now? Let four days pass. Let the plan I have made take effect; then we shall speak at ease.

**Ekambaram:** So you are asking me to return after four days?

**Pakkiyanathan:** What is this? Am I giving you an instalment date? Come every day. Come and go, and tell me how far you have carried out the Sumangali Puja arrangements I assigned you.

**Ekambaram:** Very well. Shall I begin that task at once?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Do it. Afterwards we can attend to all our affairs. Go and return. Hey, Narayana! Narayana! The senseless fool has no sense of time or circumstance. He has no thought of the meritorious work for which I labour. Food and comfort: those are the only two things he seeks.

**Ekambaram:** Then I shall take leave, sir.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Do it, man! I need not tell anyone else about the Sumangali Puja, need I?

**Ekambaram:** No, sir. I shall attend to it.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Will you do it generously?

**Ekambaram:** Ah, certainly! I shall leave now.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Do it, man. Do it. Go and return.

**Andal:** Yes.

**Pakkiyanathan:** A business opportunity has come to him.

**Andal:** A wholesale shop?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Yes, at the Gomatha Association itself. A Sumangali Puja will be held every Friday. All the married women in town will come carrying money. They will need coconuts, fruit, camphor, flower garlands, agathi greens, horse gram, bran and many other things. Before some other fellow scents the opportunity, if we make Narayanan set up a shop there, the weekly trade will be no less than three hundred rupees.

**Andal:** It is a good idea. He wanders about idly and is being ruined.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Once he sits in a shop and starts putting money into the cashbox, he too will become as good as gold, foolish woman! Worship Lakshmi, and Saraswati comes of her own accord.

**Andal:** Our priest said last week that Narayanan's planets were unfavourable.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Forget his chatter. Ah, I nearly forgot. I am going to ask our Sankaralinga Iyer to oversee the Sumangali Puja. Even if I forget, remind me.

**Andal:** Very well. Look...

**Pakkiyanathan:** He has arrived himself. Welcome, priest, welcome. May you live a hundred years!

**Sankaralingam:** Madam, why should I live a hundred years only to be devoured by poverty?

**Pakkiyanathan:** Do not weep, Iyer. I have made an arrangement especially for you.

**Sankaralingam:** An arrangement for me? Whatever path you show, I am ready to follow.

**Andal:** It is a good path, priest. Do not worry.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Iyer, do you know of Gomatha Puja?

**Sankaralingam:** Certainly. There are sixty-eight slokas in all.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Never mind the number. Can you write out clearly the manner of performing the puja and the benefits it bestows?

**Sankaralingam:** I can explain it well. There are Sanskrit slokas. The benefits of the puja are exceptionally great.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Good. Explain all that, add the sacred stories of who performed this puja in the different ages and what benefits they received, and have your son make it into ten pages. Where does your son work now?

**Sankaralingam:** That dullard is keeping accounts for some Marakkayar.

**Pakkiyanathan:** What business does the man conduct?

**Sankaralingam:** A leather warehouse.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Forget it. From now on, let your son stop going to that job and assist you. The two of you prepare it together. We can print ten thousand notices about Gomatha Puja. We have founded an association for that very purpose. Every Friday there will be a Sumangali Puja, and you shall be its priest. I shall build a small temple. Take care of it.

**Sankaralingam:** Ah! A splendid arrangement, an act of great merit.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Let the merit be mine; at least you will gain a livelihood!

**Sankaralingam:** Everything depends upon your kindness.

**Pakkiyanathan:** The income will not be trifling. Women will gather for the Sumangali Puja. Merely place a donation box there.

**Sankaralingam:** I shall do exactly as you direct.

**Pakkiyanathan:** I shall give you money for the monthly expenses. If the donation-box collections bring in more, there will be something in it for you too. You know my nature: I speak sparingly, but my giving is not like that.

**Sankaralingam:** Have I ever spoken a word of refusal? I shall conduct the arrangement you have made properly and devoutly.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Do so! The temple will be ready within another month.

**Sankaralingam:** I shall bring the notice tomorrow morning. There are rare sacred stories concerning cow worship.

**Pakkiyanathan:** Very well. Go and return.

**Sankaralingam:** I shall take leave. My season of suffering has ended today. Madam, it is all because of you.

**Andal:** God is there, priest. What power do we possess?

* * *

**Ellamma:** Why are you returning with your face hanging so low?

**Ekambaram:** What can I do?

**Ellamma:** Why? Did that sinner say he could not lend you two hundred rupees?

**Ekambaram:** Why should he be the sinner? I am a sinner; you are a sinner. All those who have nothing, like us, are sinners. That is what fate has written for us. He alone is a man of merit. He seeks merit, and it comes to him of its own accord. Even merit looks at a man's house, property and comforts before entering.

**Ellamma:** What are you saying? I cannot understand any of it. Are you exhausted?

**Ekambaram:** How could I fail to understand? I understood very well. I simply lacked the courage to ask. How could I possess courage? I have no money in my hand and no means to live. He lies fluently and without the least shame.

**Ellamma:** Stop and tell it calmly, without wandering into side stories!

**Ekambaram:** I went to ask him for a loan. I thought: after all, did we not sell him our grove and property? Would he refuse even this help? But he would not even let me speak about it.

**Ellamma:** Your tongue always stumbles at the important moment.

**Ekambaram:** My stomach burns, Ellamma! I went to ask for a loan, but he made me sit and told stories.

**Ellamma:** What story?

**Ekambaram:** All about the fraud he himself is committing. Apparently there is an association to protect cows, and he is collecting a lakh of rupees for it. After boasting of that greatness, he says he sold a palmyra tract he owns, a place where jackals howl at night, to this association for fifty thousand.

**Ellamma:** He sold a palmyra waste for fifty thousand? What about our coconut grove?

**Ekambaram:** What can we do?

**Ellamma:** Who are the people in that association?

**Ekambaram:** Who? He himself is that too.

**Ellamma:** What is this? I do not understand.

**Ekambaram:** No one in the whole town understands. Look here, Ellamma, at what he has done. He gathered ten people, named it the Gomatha Association, and collects a lakh of rupees in charity to protect aged cows. When a great rich man goes through town calling it a meritorious cause and asking for charity, who will say no? Money pours in. He proposes that the association needs a large place in which the cows can graze. Then he says, "My palmyra tract is just such a place. It is worth three or four lakhs, but for this meritorious cause I shall give it for fifty thousand." By deceiving them in this way, he consigns the collected money to the sacrificial fire.

**Ellamma:** The sinner!

**Ekambaram:** You call him a sinner, but he says he is seeking merit, and the town believes him. He beat me down with that story without letting me open my mouth. I went to him in distress to ask for help, and he has even found a job for me.

**Ellamma:** A job? Must you work for that sinner too?

**Ekambaram:** Why would he say it is for himself? He asks me to earn merit. Every Friday I am to arrange a Sumangali Puja in the palmyra tract and marshal all the women in town to attend it.

**Ellamma:** You? He asked you?

**Ekambaram:** Yes. I went to ask for a loan, and he gives me this task. I agreed, thinking I could explain our distress afterwards. When I raised the subject again, he said, "Ekambaram, until this association work is finished I cannot attend to anything else. No other talk will enter my ears. Tie all your troubles into a bundle and put it aside. Attend to the Sumangali Puja work I assigned you; we shall discuss your matter afterwards." Then he drove me away.

**Ellamma:** The sinner! How he torments a poor man!

**Ekambaram:** There you go again, calling him a sinner! He is seeking merit, woman.

**Ellamma:** Seeking merit indeed! He roams about seeking the property of the whole town.

**Ekambaram:** Who will believe you if you say that? The whole town will praise the founder of the Gomatha Association as a man of merit. Every week all the sumangalis will gather and bless him.

**Ellamma:** Let them bless him. Let them bless him.

**Ekambaram:** The people will see neither his fraud nor the deception by which he seizes others' property. Every Friday the sumangalis will set out in procession, rank after rank. What will the town think when it sees them? Will anyone discover his trick of selling a palmyra tract worth less than ten thousand for fifty thousand? They will say, "Look, look! Our Pakkiyanathan is a man of merit. He has performed this charitable deed!" Who will know from what fraud the Sumangali Puja sprouted? Will there be any shortage of people for a puja? Even you, after saying all this, will one day set out for the Sumangali Puja!

**Ellamma:** Very well. Get up. Let us eat whatever there is and go to sleep.

## Translator's Notes

`Sumangali` denotes an auspicious married woman whose husband is living. `Gomatha` literally means “Mother Cow”; `Gomatha Puja` or `Go Puja` is ritual worship of a cow. `Agathi` is the edible leaf of *Sesbania grandiflora*, traditionally offered as cattle fodder. `Mirashdar` denotes a substantial hereditary landholder. A `Marakkayar` is a member of a Tamil Muslim mercantile community. The Sanskrit cry `svaha`, used when an offering is consigned to sacrificial fire, is employed satirically for making the charity's collected money disappear.

The play satirizes the conversion of public piety and charitable institutions into private profit. Pakkiyanathan sells his nearly worthless palmyra tract to the association at an inflated price, reserves the associated trade for his son, and recruits poor dependants to manufacture religious legitimacy.

The cast list describes Ekambaram as `ஏழை (ஆனவன்)`. The parenthetical word is legible in the scan but its editorial purpose is uncertain; the translation conservatively renders only the unambiguous description, “a poor man.” Speaker names are inferred from the dialogue sequence because the printed text presents most turns without labels.
