## English Translation

*(An imagined exchange that offers food for thought)*

The Honourable Kamaraj and the Honourable Ramasami meet. An imagined scene!

What do they discuss? This and that. It is only an imagining.

Yet if one examines the situation, it becomes clear that this is how they could speak.

Please do not imagine that this conversation actually took place.

This imagined conversation has been drawn only because, if such an exchange did take place, it might bring a little honesty and clarity to politics.

**Ramasami Padayachi:** [Eagerly] Did you hear? I gave those DMK fellows a thorough thrashing. They called me a firebrand. I was furious. I said I would chase and beat them until they ran.

**Kamaraj:** [Mockingly] They say Vinayagam spoke even more fiercely!

**Ramasami:** [Dismissively] Yes. That fellow is always like that. He loses all sense of proportion. But everyone was astonished only after hearing my speech. After all, I hold the position of “leader” to ten million Vanniyar people.

**Kamaraj:** [Teasing] Our Manickavelar too still calls himself a Vanniyar leader.

**Ramasami:** [With a scornful laugh] In North Arcot! He has no influence in the other districts. I have one person in Tiruchirappalli district, Subramaniam MLC in Salem district, Sambandam MLA in Thanjavur district, and so on.

**Kamaraj:** [His attention seemingly elsewhere] So I was told.

**Ramasami:** [Delighted] Even the Dravidian Legislative Party supported me. You read it in the newspaper, did you not?

**Kamaraj:** [Frowning] I did. You still have closer ties with Congress's opponents. Ramasami, do not be angry. At that conference you did not include Congress leaders even as a token gesture. Many comrades asked about it; some even condemned it. You said that if S. S. Ramasami were made a minister, the whole Vanniyar association would join Congress. They complained: “At the Tindivanam conference Ramasami Padayachi staged his display by assembling opponents of Congress and gave Congressmen no place. Why make him a minister?”

**Ramasami:** [Worried] What could I do? I could not tell them to merge into Congress in one stroke. Our people would say, “See, see, what the DMK said has come true.”

**Kamaraj:** [With some authority] Was the arrangement not that all Vanniyars would join Congress and that the Toilers Party would be dissolved?

**Ramasami:** [Worried] Yes. Am I denying it?

**Kamaraj:** [Eagerly] Did you see what happened in North Arcot? Manickavelar announced that his party would not contest the district board election. And he did not receive a ministry after I came; Rajaji had already given it to him.

**Ramasami:** [Proudly] His case is different. What influence does his party possess? It managed to win four or five places. Besides, Vanniyars will not listen to him.

**Kamaraj:** [Mockingly] Yes, yes! Gopala Gounder, Tirukkural Munusami, Kacharayam and the Vanniyars in the DMK all obey every word you say, do they? Why this empty pride? You struggled so much to hold a conference, yet in that very town Gopala Gounder opposes your course, and you could not even pass a resolution condemning him.

**Rama:** [A little angrily.] We did not pass one. Why say we could not?

**Kama:** [Angrily.] What use is this lawyer's argument? I understand that my calculation was wrong.

**Rama:** [Angrily.] You too listen to this DMK talk and believe it. If you speak without knowing my influence, I am forced to explain it myself. If I do, you will regard it as boasting. Do you know that even Periyar Ramasami did not issue a statement condemning me?

**Kama:** [Laughing.] Good heavens! What is so extraordinary in that? Do you know that he issued a statement supporting me?

**Rama:** [Boldly.] I saw it. But the Dravidar Kazhagam, the DMK and the Communists can do nothing against me. Did they show black flags? Could they? Can they?

**Kama:** [Angrily.] The DMK showed black flags even to Jawaharlal Nehru, the Light of Asia. What would you do if they showed them to you? Is it political speech to say, "I shall stab and cut"?

All the DMK leaders were imprisoned. Those outside showed black flags to Nehru in Madras and Madurai. Does Nehru lack in Madras the influence you possess in Cuddalore and South Arcot district? Yet you speak as though no one could show you a black flag.

**Rama:** [Sadly.] If even you speak this way...

**Kama:** [Mockingly.] I do not speak; you make me speak. They showed Manickavelar black flags at Walajapet. Did Manickavelar lack at Walajapet the influence you possess at Cuddalore? Yet you speak somehow.

**Rama:** [Humbly.] That is not what I mean. Generally, no one can work in South Arcot district while maintaining hostility towards me.

**Kama:** [Majestically.] I myself worked against you, and my head is still upon my shoulders!

**Rama:** [With a faint smile.] It appears the moment at which we began speaking was inauspicious.

**Kama:** [With disgust.] What is wrong with your time? It is perfectly sound. Mine is the time that has gone wrong.

Considering the circumstances in which I stand, there must be no opposition. Rajaji watches for an opening through which to enter, and a faction within Congress waits for the moment it can trip me.

Therefore, it is best to keep patting the Dravidar Kazhagam and DMK on the back and carry them along. I act with the thought that we need everyone's cooperation. I gave you a ministerial position with that very purpose. What you do destroys my purpose.

**Rama:** [Sadly.] What harm have I done?

**Kama:** [With disgust.] You seek out and cultivate enmity. If you abuse the DMK however you please, will opposition not arise?

Let us assume that through your tremendous influence you beat and chase them until they run. Will that opposition end with you? Will it not come and attach itself to me?

I gave you a ministry thinking it would add new strength. You poke at the DMK while it is sitting quietly and then watch the entertainment.

**Rama:** [Wearily.] If they condemn me, how can I remain silent?

**Kama:** [Angrily.] What condemnation have they inflicted? When Manickavela Naicker became a minister, whatever you said in condemning him is now being returned to you, and your honour and anger boil over.

Only in Manickavelar's case does caste disappear? Is he not a Vanniyar too?

**Rama:** [Sadly.] If we stir up old matters, this conversation will reach no conclusion.

**Kama:** [Wearily.] You have not abandoned the old matter even now. At the Tindivanam conference you said you joined the ministry for the sake of the Vanniyars.

You did not find the heart to say: "Congress is a sacred organisation. I have joined it. We must not ruin the nation by speaking uselessly of caste and community. Everyone must join Congress." It was the same old speech.

**Rama:** [In a pleading voice.] At a Vanniyar conference, one has to speak that way. Elsewhere I have spoken only in support of Congress.

**Kama:** [Angrily.] We do not need your favour! Is it not among your own caste people that you must speak to seek support for Congress?

**Rama:** [Humbly.] As time passes...

**Kama:** [With disgust.] My worry is what further troubles you will create as time passes. You do not know how to conduct yourself with the maturity of Manickavela Naicker. He too has a Vanniyar association.

**Rama:** [A little angrily.] Repeatedly holding up Manickavelar's speech feels like a mocking stab at me. This office is not so important to me.

**Kama:** [Growing angry.] Yes, yes! You even said at the conference: "Kamaraj searched for me and gave me office. Office is nothing important to me. Is the salary enough for my expenses? Every month I spend one or two thousand rupees of my own money."

That speech insults all of us together.

**Rama:** [Startled.] Alas! Did I insult you? I?

**Kama:** [Still angry.] If you say office is not important to you and money does not matter, does that not imply indirectly that office alone is important and money paramount to all of us?

**Rama:** [Attempting conciliation.] Is it right to speak after taking the wrong meaning?

**Kama:** [Still angry.] Publicly at the conference, you said I searched you out and gave you office. But when you see your community privately, you will probably spin the story that I fell at your feet and begged you to save me.

**Rama:** [Placatingly] Would I say such things? I spoke that way only to silence those who condemn me for changing parties to obtain a ministry.

**Kama:** [With disgust] Then will my party members not question me? Will they not condemn me by saying, “We already have a majority. Why did you make Ramasami a minister and give him room now to speak against you?” I gain honour only if I can show that by giving you a ministry I dissolved the caste association and brought everyone into Congress. Your speech now damages my honour.

**Rama:** [Trying to clarify] I did not utter even one word of condemnation.

**Kama:** [With disgust] This is a Congress ministry, and Congress has a proper majority. You and your colleagues now sit on the Congress side in the Assembly. In that situation, is it proper for you to preside over a caste conference, beat your chest and say that you became a minister for the good of your caste, and proudly declare that you did not approach me but that Kamaraj himself summoned you and gave you office? If Rajaji's faction tells Nehruji this, what will he think of me? Will he respect me?

**Rama:** [Sadly] I did not think that far. I was angry with those DMK fellows.

**Kama:** [Mockingly] Why would you think? You want both a ministry and a caste association!

**Rama:** [Frowning] Was it not after considering my caste association and influence that you included me in the ministry?

**Kama:** [Sheepishly] Yes. Only now is its true character becoming clear. I also see that you are disturbing peaceful politics by abusing the DMK.

**Rama:** [With a faint smile] What can the DMK do? Why treat them as important and speak about them so much?

**Kama:** [Still angrier] Enough. Be quiet! Do you ask now what the DMK can do, after all the time you associated with them?

**Rama:** [Dismissively] They came to me of their own accord...

**Kama:** [With disgust] Yes, yes! Tomorrow you will say the same of Congress, that Congress itself surrendered to you. Why tomorrow? You say it already. Did you not say exactly that at the conference? At a Dravidar Kazhagam conference they passed a resolution supporting our ministry. You are a minister, yet at your own conference you did not even wish to pass a resolution supporting the ministry.

**Rama:** [Placatingly] It was forgotten.

**Kama:** [Disgusted] Such a trivial matter to you, isn't it? The conference existed only to attack the DMK. Was there even a trace of policy explanation in it? While serving in a Congress ministry, your conscience permits you to preach caste politics. I too have a caste, sir. Count on your fingers: have I ever once said that I became minister for the advancement of the Nadar community?

**Rama:** [Angrily] Who told you not to?

**Kama:** [Still angry] That is my principle. I have never thought it a great distinction to boast of my community, my caste and my influence within it. Look at the constituency from which I now stand for election: Gudiyatham, where Senguntha Mudaliyars are the majority. It is not a Nadar constituency. I stand there, and I am going to win. *That*, sir, is what influence means.

**Rama:** [Disgusted] It is not good for this conversation to continue. I...

**Kama:** [Mockingly] Are you going to say that you will resign your ministry?

**Rama:** [Startled] I shall come and speak later.

**Kama:** [Mockingly] You frightened me. I thought you might actually resign. Let our conversation rest. Please do not abuse the DMK needlessly and bring trouble upon me. You will earn ten million blessings.

[The Honourable Ramasami takes his leave with a slight smile.]

*Dravida Nadu*, 4 July 1954.
