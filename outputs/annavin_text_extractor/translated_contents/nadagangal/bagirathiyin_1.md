எஜமான் அதிகாலையில் எழுந்தார் - குளித்தார் - தனியறைக்குப் போய்விட்டார்!

“யார் வந்தாலும் என் அனுமதி இல்லாமல் உள்ளே
விடாதே” - இது அவர் எனக்களித்த கண்டிப்பான உத்தரவு!

எனக்குத் தூக்கிவாரிப் போட்டது! காரணம், அவர்
எப்போதும் இப்படிச் சொன்னதில்லை!

யார் வந்தாலும் அவரோடு இனிக்கப் பேசுவார்-அரட்டை
அடிப்பார் - பொழுது போக்குவார். நேரம் போனதே தெரியாமல்!
அப்படிப் பொழுதைப் போக்குவது, அன்றாடக் கடமைகளில்
ஒன்று அவருக்கு!

எதையெதைப் பற்றியெல்லாமோ பேசுவார் - பற்பலர்
அடிபடுவர் அவரது பேச்சில்!

“மந்திரிகளாம், மந்திரிகள்! என்ன சாதிக்கிறார்கள், இவர்கள்!
'பைல்'களிலேயே என்னேரமும் புதைந்து கிடக்கிறார்கள்! இந்த
இடத்திலே இருக்கிற 'கமா'வை அங்கே போடு, அங்கேயிருக்கிற
அரைப்புள்ளியை இங்கே மாற்று, இந்த வாசகத்தை இப்படித்
திருத்து, என்று ஆணையிடுவதோடு முடிந்து விடுகிறது அவர்களது

பொல்லாத கோபம் வந்துவிட்டது. வராமல் எப்படி இருக்க
முடியும்? சட்டம் இடங்கொடுக்கவில்லை, என்றல்லவா அந்த

விளக்கம் இல்லையா? சட்டங்களை உண்டாக்குவதற்குத்தானே
அமைச்சர்கள், அந்தப் பீடங்களில் அமர்த்தப்பட்டு இருக்கிறார்கள்
- அதற்காகத்தானே ஏராளமான பணமும் அவர்களுக்கு
தரப்படுகிறது, சம்பளமாக!

கோபமடைந்த போலாநாத்தைச் சாந்தப்படுத்த, அப்பப்பா
நான் பட்ட பாடு! மணிக்கணக்கில் பேசி வாதம் செய்து, குணக்
குன்றே கோமானே என்று துதிபாடிய பிறகல்லவா அவரது
கோபம் தணிந்தது?

கரீம்கானை எடுத்துக் கொள்ளுங்களேன், முஸ்லீம் லீக்

பாடுபட்டிருக்கிறேன்!. . . காங்கிரசு என்றால் ஏறெடுத்துக் கூடப்
பார்க்காத அவரது உடலை இன்று கதர் அலங்கரிக்கிறதே, எப்படி?
எல்லாம் என் கைத்திறன்!

- இப்படி எஜமான் எத்தனையோ தடவைகள் தனது
பிரதாபங்களை, கைத்திறத்தை நாலு பேரைச் சேர்த்து வைத்துக்

பிறப்பித்தால், எனக்கு ஆச்சரியம் ஏற்படாமல் என்ன செய்யும்?

### Bagirathi's Wager

Presented here is a translation of a story written by C.N.A. in *Homeland*.

[He was only an attendant, but he worked in the house of one of the Congress party's “big men.” Only men like him could know the true, undisguised form of those “big men.”

Here is one day from his experience.]

My master rose before dawn, bathed, and went into his private room.

“Do not let anyone in without my permission, whoever comes.” That was the strict order he gave me.

I was startled, because he had never said such a thing before.

Whoever came, he would speak sweetly, chat with them, and pass the time until no one noticed how many hours had gone by. Passing the time in that fashion was one of his daily duties.

He would talk about all manner of things, and many people would be struck down in his conversation.

“Ministers, they call themselves! What do these ministers ever accomplish? They remain buried in files all day. Their work ends with orders to move the comma here over there, bring the semicolon there over here, and revise a sentence in some particular way.

## Image 2: 002-a574777d97.png

“What else do they accomplish? How can the party grow if things go on like this? Men like me have to intervene, set matters right, do what must be done, meet the people who must be met, say things tactfully, and make the party grow. A party has to be run by patting some people on the back, lifting others onto one's shoulders and praising them, tempting still others, and drawing every one of them in.

“Had I not intervened, that Polanath would have left the party by now, causing it a tremendous loss.

“If not for that same Polanath, could we have arranged a six-course feast for the minister, the deputy minister who came from Delhi, and gladdened his heart? Polanath had apparently asked the minister for something, and the minister refused. Naturally, Polanath flew into a rage. The minister's explanation was that the law did not permit it. Is that not a remarkably amusing explanation? Ministers have been seated on those pedestals precisely to make laws, and are paid enormous salaries for that very purpose.

“Ah, what I endured to pacify the furious Polanath! Only after arguing with him for hours and singing his praises as a mountain of virtue and a noble lord did his anger subside.

“Take Karim Khan. What have I not done to bring that Muslim League man into our party? A man who would not even look in the direction of Congress now has khadi adorning his body. How did that happen? It was all my skill!”

My master would often gather four people around him and measure out tales of his glory and prowess in this fashion.

How could I not be surprised when that very man ordered me not to admit anyone?

I sat in my chair outside the room. The door was closed.

Pot-bellied Parangusam arrived. That was how my master had taken to describing him lately, but only in his absence. Would he dare say it when Parangusam was present?

I stopped him.

“What insolence! Just watch what I do to you!” Parangusam roared.

“Sir, forgive me, sir! Those are the master's orders, sir. What can I do?” I pleaded.

“The master's orders! Who is the master here? That fellow inside, that dimwit? We provide his food and clothes. How can he be my master? He is your master, and that is all. Understand? Cross my path because he told you to, and I will crack your skull. Be careful!” He sprang at me as he spoke.

“Sir, sir, let me go!” I cried.

Fortunately, my master noticed the commotion from inside the room.

“Parangusam! What is this? Are you displaying your rowdyism here?”

“Oh! Since when did you become such a great man? I was wrong. Look, I struck him when I should have struck you. Just wait and see what I do.”

“Master, shall I call the police?” I asked.

“Do not panic. Why call the police? Parangusam behaves like this when he is drunk.”

“I am drunk? Yes, I am. So what? You took two hundred rupees from me to obtain that permit! The law is on my side. And besides, look here, I have this walking stick too.”

“Something? She knows all of it. Do you know that Soori is trying to become chairman in order to humiliate Bagirathi?”

“What has Bagirathi to do with this?”

“Do you truly know nothing? Bagirathi adores you and always speaks highly of you. One day she met Shoba, Soori's wife. As usual, Bagirathi began praising you. Shoba interrupted her: ‘What is difficult about it? Anyone can become chairman by spending money.’ Bagirathi denied it, and a wager was made. Do you know its terms? She challenged Shoba: ‘If Parangusam alone fails to become chairman, slap me publicly on the cheek, and I shall accept it.’”

“Good God! Has the matter gone that far? I knew nothing of it.”

“Soori has no liking for politics. He is now throwing himself into it only to fulfil his wife's wish. Bagirathi has become frightened. She was saying that she wanted to meet you alone and speak with you.”

“I saw her recently, at the exhibition.”

“Saw her? Did you expect her to run up to you at once, especially in such a place? She is deeply distressed. It was she who asked me not to send Soori's proposal to the board.”

“Shoba slap Bagirathi? Impossible! It shall never happen. Her cheeks are not meant to be slapped! Ah, yes. I have been a fool all these days. Tear up Soori's proposal. I am ready to become chairman again.”

“Truly? Then I must leave for Madras immediately. When is the first train?”

“Take my car. There must be no delay. Before you leave, meet Bagirathi and tell her my decision.”

“Bagirathi will leap for joy. She may even say that she wants to come with us.”

“Why, would it not be better if I came with you too?”

“Excellent. We shall leave at exactly eight.”
