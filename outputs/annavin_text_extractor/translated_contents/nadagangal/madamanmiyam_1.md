தான் இருக்கிறார்கள் - என்பதை, மடாலய நிர்வாக மசோதாவை
எதிர்க்கும் மகானுபாவர்கள் கூட ஒப்புக் கொள்கிறார்கள்.

அவ்விதம் மட அதிகாரிகளிலே ஒருவர் வாழ்க்கை எப்படி
இருக்கிறது என்பதை விளக்கும் கருத்தோவியம் இது.]

## English Translation

[An imagined tableau illustrating conditions within monasteries.

Even the eminent men who oppose the Monastery Administration Bill admit that some monastic authorities are morally deficient.

This is a dramatic sketch showing how one such monastic authority lives.]

## 1

**Place:** The ceremonial hall

**Present:** The head of the monastery, Selvaan, and the abbot's broker

**Abbot:** He who restrains the unruly horses called the senses with the bridle called wisdom, and drives them along the path called righteousness, shall reach the holy abode known as the feet of Hara. On the way lies the poisonous creeper called desire. If one puts it to the tongue, thinking how fresh and green it looks, it will ruin whoever is caught by it. One must cross it. For that, the horse must be struck with the whip called renunciation. True devotee! All who received the Supreme One's grace attained liberation in this manner.

**Selvaan:** Ah! I am blessed! Master, I understand.

**Abbot:** Virtuous man versed in the Saivite path! Praise the One with the golden form. Change your inclination towards worldly pleasures. Then see: happiness on earth, and boundless bliss in the supreme abode!

**Broker:** His Holiness has a special affection for our Pillai. For a long time, he has desired to do something for the monastery.

**Abbot:** Ambalavana! Only one who has received His grace will have such a thought. Can a barren cow yield sweet milk? We have heard about him.

**Selvaan:** Your will is my good fortune, Swami! Is there anything that is truly my property? Everything came through His grace and belongs to Him!

**Abbot:** We commend your devotion! Perform any service you desire.

**Broker:** If His Holiness grants permission, Pillai wishes to rebuild the spring pavilion.

**Abbot:** What difficulty is there in that? Let him do it.

## 2

**Place:** The monastery veranda

**Present:** The broker and the wealthy man

**Broker:** Pillai! You are greatly blessed. The sermon His Holiness gave today, ah, ah, ah, magnificent, magnificent!

**Wealthy man:** The songs of the Four Saints dance upon his tongue!

**Broker:** Today's appearance was exceptional too. Did you see the adornment?

**Wealthy man:** Need it be said? He looked like the very fruit of Saivism. I shall take my leave...

## 3

**Place:** A private room

**Present:** The abbot and the broker

**Abbot:** Is it unripe or ripe?

**Broker:** Even the unripe will ripen at the touch of these saffron robes!

**Abbot:** Where is the fruit?

**Broker:** In a splendid orchard! But there is a fence and a guard.

**Abbot:** A fence and a guard! Even against whom?

**Broker:** Can there be an obstacle to His Holiness? Arrangements have been made to behold the fruit in the spring pavilion. We await only your command. The fruit is golden; as for its taste, only you shall enjoy that rare sixfold flavour.

**Abbot:** Does it bear the grace of Mother and Father?

**Broker:** The father was seized by confusion. We spent a little money and cleared it away. The fruit is Vaishnavite.

**Abbot:** Hari and Siva are one!

**Broker:** A merchant maiden!

**Abbot:** As though you did not know!

**Broker:** A graceful creeper!

**Abbot:** Let there be no poison!

**Broker:** Was she not struck down by famine? A parijata blossom that has ripened perfectly.

**Abbot:** Her name?

**Broker:** Vilvamohini. They did not keep the name they first chose. You may name her; that would be fitting. The name they gave her is Amsa.

**Abbot:** Amsa! A beautiful name! Make every arrangement in the spring pavilion. Let there be no shortage of intoxicating drinks. Amsa! What an elegant name! No matter how finely I dress, this matted hair and crown remain an affliction, like an old attachment that will not leave! Will the maiden's eyes and mind be disturbed, Kachithakamba?

**Broker:** I shall come and see you tonight.

**Abbot:** Good! Go now. What about expenses?

**Broker:** I have received the money.

**Abbot:** Do not economise. Spend somewhat liberally. This is a new vessel!

## 4

**Place:** The spring pavilion

**Present:** The abbot and Amsa

**Abbot:** The reality is more extraordinary than its description. Golden image! Sit here. If you stand for long, your flower-soft feet will grow weary.

**Amsa:** More than these eyes have?

**Abbot:** What is wrong with your eyes...?

**Amsa:** They have blossomed from lying in wait and watching for you, wondering when you would come.

**Abbot:** Why this pretence after seeing me?

**Amsa:** It is like beholding the Lord of Sivaloka.

**Abbot:** Enchantress! I have pined since morning to see you. At the monastery, all those venerable fellows kept coming and going without end. But my heart did not remain there... Mohanangi...

**Amsa:** Beautiful one!

**Abbot:** My darling!

**Amsa:** Light of my eyes!

**Abbot:** My fruit!

**Amsa:** Here is fruit nectar brought especially for you in a golden goblet. Please drink it.

**Abbot:** Is that goblet more special than your coral lips? Can that fruit nectar give greater pleasure than tasting your lips...?

**Amsa:** Shall I sing something?

**Abbot:** You know music too? Let me hear a song.

**Amsa:** Your servant studied for five years under Pannaiyur Parthasarathi Iyengar.

**Abbot:** Oh! Pannaiyur Iyengar? Do you know a woman named Padma who studied singing under him?

**Amsa:** Yes, she is thin.

**Abbot:** Fine features!

**Amsa:** Her face lacks radiance.

**Abbot:** Could she possess a face like yours? Among all the beauties I have seen, you are the finest!

**Amsa:** You speak only of matters such as these. Afterwards...?

**Abbot:** What can I do? I have to carry the burden of Saivism throughout the entire morning.

*Dravida Nadu*, 27 February 1949.

## Translator's Notes

The title `மட மான்மீயம்` is a satirical formulation meaning “the glory or greatness of the monastery”; “Monastic Glory!” preserves its mock-panegyric force. The source repeatedly uses fruit, creeper, nectar and religious imagery as sexual double meanings. All four source images had incorrectly been recorded as containing no OCR text; their Tamil text was manually restored from the scans before translation. A few proper names and deliberately ornate expressions, including `கச்சிதக்கம்பா`, remain transliterated or conservatively rendered where the satire does not establish a unique literal reading.
