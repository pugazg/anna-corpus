## Part 1

**Place:** The hall of Morarji Desai's mansion  
**Present:** Morarji Desai and Pandit Nehru

[Pandit Nehru appears highly cheerful. His pure white khadi clothing and rose-coloured complexion captivate Morarji. Seeing Desai sad, Nehru speaks teasingly.]

**Nehru:** Morarji-bhai! Why this old sorrow still? I have arrived, have I not? What more is there? They created rather too much trouble for you. That is the rash speed of youth. We must not be shaken by it or lose courage.

**Morarji:** Nehru-bhai, I have neither fear nor terror. But can I bear it when my own Gujarati people humiliate me in my own province? That is my sorrow. Why should fear arise? Will those fellows stage a French Revolution? Could they answer even one division of troops?

**Nehru:** Yet they behaved with extraordinary boldness. I hear they chased and beat everyone wearing a khadi cap.

**Morarji:** Their lawlessness knew no limit. They dared anything. Do you know how far they went? I feel shame and pain even to say it.

**Nehru:** What do you mean?

**Morarji:** They made an effigy in your likeness and burned it.

**Nehru:** [Angrily] Has the situation gone that far? You allowed such savagery to grow.

**Morarji:** Were my efforts to suppress it slight? I imposed a curfew and ordered them chased and beaten.

**Nehru:** Cries of "massacre" and "repression" arose, increasing hatred of our government. What else did we gain?

**Morarji:** I never expected such frenzy in my Gujarat.

**Nehru:** What is this "my Gujarat," Morarji-bhai? Your own obsession has not disappeared at all! You speak possessively and assert a claim over it. If even you are like this, why should the young men not speak defiantly?

**Morarji:** I merely said "my Gujarat" from habit. You must not read any special meaning into it.

**Nehru:** [Mockingly] My Gujarat! My Maharashtra! My Punjab! My Bengal! Shame, what lunatic speech! One must raise one's head and say, "Our India!" In my U.P. they would never speak so.

[Morarji laughs. Nehru becomes angry.]

**Nehru:** What is there to laugh at?

**Morarji:** Panditji, while arguing that it was wrong for me to say "my Gujarat," you yourself just said "my U.P."

[Nehru too laughs heartily.]

**Nehru:** That is habit. Very well. What happened at your meeting that day? Tell me.

**Morarji:** What meeting took place? I went, but the people did not come. How could a meeting occur?

**Nehru:** Did they boycott it with such discipline? Morarji, I am deeply sorry. How much affection Gujaratis have had for you, and for how long!

**Morarji:** They consider none of it. They shout, "Long live Maha Gujarat! Down with Morarji's rule!"

**Nehru:** They should say, "Long live India!" Never mind. Today I shall advise the students and set the situation right. They are hasty, but if the matter is presented with maturity, they will understand.

**Morarji:** One could make them understand if they allowed one to speak. They did not even come to the meeting.

**Nehru:** What is the situation now?

**Morarji:** It has become normal. Those who engaged in disorder now hang their heads in shame. The fast has set matters right.

**Nehru:** They will no longer think of Maha Gujarat, Mahakoshal and all that. The Suez question, Algeria, and my visit to Saudi Arabia: those are what the students will follow with interest.

**Morarji:** Yes, yes. They are eager to hear about what you saw and learned in Saudi Arabia.

**Nehru:** Did the newspapers not report it in detail?

**Morarji:** They did, but people naturally wish to hear it from your own lips. I do too.

**Nehru:** The Saudi visit was highly important. I have received many royal honours, but the reception there astonished even me. I stayed in the palace. The Saudi king had never before granted anyone that status.

**Morarji:** I hear he gave you a Cadillac.

**Nehru:** Yes, one specially built for him, a gold watch, and many other gifts. Leave those aside. We boast that India is a land where gold grows, but, Morarji, they held a banquet in Saudi Arabia. Good heavens, what a sight! There were more than a thousand people.

**Morarji:** Ordinary people?

**Nehru:** Grandees! What magnificence and pomp! What electric decorations: fifty thousand lights.

**Morarji:** It must have blazed like universal illumination.

**Nehru:** No emperor could have received such treatment. Do you know something? I had given the Saudi king an elephant.

**Morarji:** What happened to it? Did the desert air not suit an elephant?

**Nehru:** [Laughing] Nonsense. The elephant is well. It played an instrument to welcome me.

**Morarji:** The elephant played music?

**Nehru:** Yes, they had trained it. They placed an instrument in its trunk; it put it to its mouth and played splendidly.

**Morarji:** We should teach our country's elephants too.

**Nehru:** How can you teach them? You have taught your Gujaratis to raise a din! [Laughs.]

**Morarji:** The entire town is laughing at me already.

[A police officer peers in quietly. Morarji rises and leaves. Nehru examines one of his own books.]

**Place:** A private room in the mansion  
**Present:** Two senior police officers

[Morarji enters with another officer. The senior officers rise and salute. Morarji sits on a sofa and gestures for them to sit.]

**Morarji:** Are all arrangements correct?

**Police Officer:** Every arrangement is A-one.

**Morarji:** How many divisions have you alerted?

**Police Officer:** Eight divisions around the meeting place, with another six held ready at a distance.

**Morarji:** What is the public mood? Panditji wants this meeting to be magnificent. Only if the first meeting after his Saudi visit proceeds without the slightest blemish will its prestige...

**Police Officer:** Yes. Nothing remains undone. All suspicious persons...

**Morarji:** Have been locked up, I hope? Enough people will attend the meeting, will they not?

**Police Officer:** Panditji's meetings are always an ocean of people.

**Morarji:** We cannot assume so. If what happened at my meeting happens again, it is over. Panditji will panic and say, "I do not want Bombay State; I shall send Pantji instead." Watch everything carefully.

**Police Officer:** We have made adequate security arrangements.

**Morarji:** There must be no effigy burning, throwing of bulbs, garlands of sandals, or such disgrace.

**Police Officer:** There will not be.

**Morarji:** Even a drop of disorder would damage the world situation. They now seek our Panditji's counsel even on Suez. Saudi Arabia welcomed him as the messenger of peace who would prevent world war. If he loses respect at a public meeting in his own country, his towering worldwide prestige will fade. Exercise the utmost vigilance. Not merely Bombay's honour but India's honour is now entrusted to you.

[The officers leave. Morarji returns to the hall. Nehru lights a cigarette while reading.]

**Nehru:** Who were they, reporters?

**Morarji:** No, police officers.

**Nehru:** [Dismissively] Nonsense! Why this fear, Morarji? Why does my meeting need police? It is utterly disgraceful.

**Morarji:** Not for protection, not for us. A vast crowd will gather. They are there to prevent injury from people crushing one another.

**Nehru:** Oh, for that? Very well. But why even then need police? If an uncontrollable crowd presses forward, I shall say "Order! Order!" once or twice. If order is not restored, I shall leap into its midst and establish it myself. Does that require police? Never mind; let them come. They too may wish to see the meeting and hear the speech. Let them. Shall we leave?

[They depart.]

**Place:** Near the meeting ground

[Police vans and jeeps arrive. Above even their sirens rise the slogans: "Long live Maha Gujarat! Down with Morarji's rule! Long live Maha Gujarat!" The police beat their batons together to create fear. As the Pandit's car draws nearer, the slogans grow louder.]

The Pandit mounts the stage and, smiling, says, "Namaste."

"Long live Maha Gujarat!" rends the sky. Thousands of black flags are visible. Police officers struggle to restore order. Morarji sits on the stage, terror on his face.

**Nehru:** Everyone knows why I have come here. Friends, in our country...

[The slogan grows louder. Nehru flounders, unable to speak. The officers wring their hands. He raises his voice.]

**Nehru:** I have come to speak affectionately to the students about important matters. What students particularly need today is...

**Crowd:** Long live Maha Gujarat! We want Maha Gujarat!

**Nehru:** [Slightly angry] If you raise such a wild din, how can you hear me? What is this rowdiness? Why demonstrate like this?

[The slogan and the police shouting "Sit down!" compete to drown each other out.]

**Nehru:** [Anger mounting] Fools! Ruffians! Are you showing black flags to me? Have you that much audacity?

[The police demand the flags and order them lowered. The students insist they will show them and never lower them.]

**Students:** Nehru, go back! Down with Nehru's verdict! Down with Morarji's rule! Long live Maha Gujarat!

**Nehru:** [Face reddening] Savagery! You wag your tails at me? If I wished, I could wipe this out. Yes, I could order the university itself closed.

**Demonstrators:** Do it! Try it! Beat us! Shoot us! Kill us! We will not fear! Long live Maha Gujarat!

**Nehru:** Nonsense! What barbarism is this? Are you students at all? Fools, fascists, communists, hooligans!

**One Demonstrator:** Did you hear all the names he called us? Hooligans and savages.

**Another:** Let him rave. Do we become hooligans merely because he calls us so? His trick has been exposed and he is irritated. Let him abuse us. Maha Gujarat...

**Many:** Long live!

**Nehru:** [With some emotion] Friends, why create such disorder? Your vice-chancellor invited me to say something to the students. I came to show affection and love, but you create this disgrace.

**One:** See how his tone changes.

**Another:** He knows every trick. He is quite an operator.

**A Third:** Are we all going to be deceived by this? Come, Maha Gujarat...

[Police rush forward to disperse them.]

**Police:** Silence! Silence! Silence!

## Part 2

**Many Students:** Long live Maha Gujarat!

**Police:** We shall now be forced to take severe action. Disperse with dignity and do not invite trouble.

**Some Students:** You are the ones troubling us. We display our dissatisfaction. What of it? Gujarat expresses its condemnation; what is that to you?

**Police:** Move far away from the meeting and raise your slogans there.

**Student:** Who will listen there? Officer, if we cannot show Nehru our burning anger, to whom should we show it in an empty field?

**Police:** This has become a terrible nuisance. How will his speech be heard?

**Student:** We do not need his speech to reach our ears. Our cry of rights must reach his. Comrades, shout so Nehru hears: Maha Gujarat!

**All:** Zindabad!

**Nehru:** [Enraged] Look at them jumping like monkeys! Has someone opened a menagerie? You hooligans! I do not fear your hooliganism. I opposed the whole British Empire. Do you know that?

**Student:** [Mockingly] Oh, we know, opponent of the British lion! Gujarat has assumed its battle form. Chacha Nehru, your tricks will not work here.

**Nehru:** If you shout like this, I shall stop speaking and leave.

**Student:** Hurray! Go then. Will the nation perish? Down with the whole gang that betrays Gujarat!

**Another:** Down with them all!

**Student:** Maha Gujarat!

**Nehru:** Shame! What hooliganism, what brutishness! Monkeys!

**Student:** Did you hear? He calls us monkeys, beasts, savages.

**Another Student:** Terrified by opposition, he raves.

**Nehru:** Wild men! Beware!

**Many Students:** You are wild! Morarji is wild! The Congressmen are wild!

**Nehru:** The world praises me, you useless fellows! You behave like hooligans towards me?

**Student:** It is no hooliganism. It is a cry of rights, an uprising for freedom!

**Nehru:** Do you know what Russia would do if you behaved so? Do you know what China would do?

**One Student:** [To another] Chacha is threatening us fiercely.

**Another:** They say Russia and China shot people like us dead.

**Student:** Is he threatening to do the same? Let him shoot. Have they not already fired on us? Shoot! If you have courage, shoot us down.

**Another:** He says the world praises him.

**A Student:** Why would it not? We closed our eyes and made him an uncrowned king. The world saw that glitter and praised him. And now?

**Student:** Now it laughs. Maha Gujarat!

**Nehru:** You want Maha Gujarat? Is this the way to obtain it? Maha Gujarat indeed! Foolishness. India alone is the nation. To speak of Gujarat and Punjab is lunacy. The bilingual state has been established, and no one can prevent it.

**All:** We shall stop it.

**Nehru:** [Furiously] Never! Let us wager and see. The new state will certainly come into being on the first of November.

**All:** We will not let it.

**Nehru:** You cannot stop it.

**All:** [Furiously] We will stop it. Maha Gujarat...

**All:** Zindabad!

[Supporters in one corner shout, "Long live Chacha Nehru!" Hearing them, the irritable Nehru speaks sorrowfully.]

**Nehru:** Enough of your praise! "Long live Chacha Nehru," indeed. Chacha Nehru! You have made Chacha Nehru into Nana Nehru and diminished my dignity. In this condition, what use is a greeting? At least you people keep quiet. Look at those little fellows leaping about. They have lost their senses. Hey, who are you all? Communists?

**All:** No!

**Nehru:** Socialists?

**All:** No!

**Nehru:** Jana Sangh?

**All:** Not that either.

**Nehru:** Ramarajya Parishad men?

**All:** Not that either. We are Gujaratis.

**Nehru:** [Mockingly] Gujaratis... monkeys.

**All:** Mind your tongue! Are you bold because the police are here? Long live Maha Gujarat!

**Nehru:** [Weary] I came here eagerly to speak, but you created disorder and prevented me. I wished to speak of so many things.

**Student:** Speak of our concern! We need Maha Gujarat. You must grant Maha Gujarat.

**Nehru:** For now there will be only the new bilingual state. We will not and cannot change it. If necessary, let another ten years pass; after five years we may see.

**Student:** Nehru is bargaining.

**Another:** He tries to deceive us.

**A Third:** After ten years, he says! Why? What pierces him if Maha Gujarat is formed now?

**Student:** His prestige is pricked.

**Another:** What does he say of our honour being stripped and our right destroyed?

**Nehru:** I thought I might speak briefly about Suez.

**Student:** Speak of Surat, sir. Do not run off to Suez.

**Another:** He is the peace envoy in the Suez question. He will boast of that glory.

**Student:** Our ears have soured from hearing it.

**Nehru:** Student friends, when I was in Saudi Arabia...

**Student:** He has begun boasting! He will tell us of the banquet there and the laughter of the grandees who attended.

**Another:** Say it properly. Why should he care about a gentleman's laugh? Tell us of the lady's laugh!

**Nehru:** Our prestige has risen in the Arab countries.

**Student:** Here our prisons overflow! Maha Gujarat!

**All:** Zindabad!

**Nehru:** [Furiously] I have tried for ninety minutes, but the hooliganism has not stopped. There is no purpose in trying to speak here any longer.

[He leaves. Police whistles and motor horns shriek, but above them rises "Long live Maha Gujarat!" Nehru departs with his entourage.]

* * *

**Place:** Morarji's mansion  
**Present:** Morarji and friends

[Morarji eats a feast with his friends.]

**A Gentleman:** Trouble upon trouble for Morarji-bhai, one after another. Panditji became very angry.

**Another Gentleman:** Even as he boarded the aircraft, his customary smile was absent.

**Morarji:** How could it remain after all that happened? Eat, eat. Is there enough chutney? Have some chapati.

**Gentleman:** Morarji-bhai, Panditji is angry only with the people, is he not?

**Morarji:** You ask whether he is angry with me? What if he is, and what if he is not?

**Gentleman:** It would have been better had people boycotted Panditji's meeting as they did ours. Coming there, creating such trouble, and making the whole affair end so badly was deeply disgraceful.

**Morarji:** That is how public anger works. Have you not eaten the rasgulla? Radharamanji, you have eaten nothing. Are you dieting?

**Radharaman:** Ram! Ram! No diet. It is worry. The people had such devotion and loyalty towards Nehruji, and already matters have become like this. When I wonder what will happen as time passes, Ram, Ram, my heart pounds.

**Gentleman:** Will all the newspapers print it in enormous type, Morarji?

**Morarji:** Not only here. By now the news will have reached America.

**Radharaman:** Americans will marvel that Panditji faces such great opposition in Bombay State. Is that not so, Morarji?

**Gentleman:** America may now even reconsider giving us loans. Is that not so?

**Morarji:** Nehru's worldwide fame has suffered somewhat. But, Sethji, observe one thing. When I was humiliated and hooliganism was directed against me, they said I lacked influence among the people, maturity, and the ability to govern.

**Radharaman:** Did Panditji say so?

**Morarji:** Leave aside who said it. He too must have thought it. They said Morarji had lost influence and therefore the people dared riot. Only now will they know the truth. Only after Panditji himself suffered so much will they understand the actual condition in Gujarat.

**Gentleman:** That is true. But forgive me, Morarji-bhai: I too thought that however angry they were, the people would calm down if Panditji spoke. Truly...

**Morarji:** What is surprising in your thinking so, Sethji? Nehru himself thought the same. He believed people would show all their anger only towards men such as Dhebarji, Morarji and Patilji; that at the sight of his face they would fall utterly silent, suppress their anger, listen to what he said, and do what he ordered. He believed he possessed such influence. But the people showed: "We do not oppose Morarji personally. We do not care how much influence any individual possesses. Our demand is what matters. Nehru is great, but Maha Gujarat is greater to us than Nehru."

**Radharaman:** They did not allow Panditji to speak at all.

**Morarji:** They thought that allowing him to speak would imply agreement to abandon Maha Gujarat.

**Gentleman:** The cries of "Zindabad" made it feel as though one's eardrums would tear.

**Morarji:** Had the meeting proceeded peacefully and without disorder...

**Gentleman:** Panditji would have said many things.

**Radharaman:** He would have attacked the Communists fiercely.

**Morarji:** Not only that. He would have rebuked me: "Morarji, did you see? This is how one must speak maturely and set the people right. Was there even a drop of disorder? I struck their heads with the truth that demanding Maha Gujarat is foolishness and savagery, and did they not fall silent? Now make sure the situation does not deteriorate again." He would have said all that.

**Gentleman:** Yes.

**Morarji:** What greater humiliation could I suffer? How could I bear Panditji saying, "You could not even hold an orderly meeting in your own Gujarat; I had to come and set matters right"?

**Radharaman:** That is true.

**Morarji:** And now? Thinking, "A greater disgrace has happened to me than happened to Morarji," Panditji left too ashamed even to look at my face. What can one do? For how many days can Panditji's influence remain unshaken? It too has its allotted time.

**Gentleman:** Yes, Morarji. In general, we must call this a season of hardship for us.

**Morarji:** Yes. What can one do? Sethji, you are very fond of soan papdi. Why have you not eaten any?

**Gentleman:** Here I am.

**Radharaman:** What happened has happened. What use is worry? Eat, Sethji!

*Dravida Nadu*, 14 October 1956

## Translator's Notes

The printed title is `மொரார்ஜி விருந்து` (“Morarji's Feast”); the archive filename `morarji_thesai.md` is retained only for stable linking. `Maha Gujarat` refers to the movement demanding a separate Gujarati-speaking state during the reorganisation of Bombay State. `Zindabad` means “long live,” while `murdabad` means “down with.” `Chacha Nehru` (“Uncle Nehru”) was Jawaharlal Nehru's popular honorific. The source's contrast with `Nana Nehru` is retained as wordplay, although its exact intended nuance is uncertain.

`Panditji`, `bhai`, `Sethji`, and the names Dhebarji, Patilji and Pantji retain their period forms. U.P. means Uttar Pradesh. The Suez references concern the 1956 crisis. `Rasgulla` and `soan papdi` are sweets; chapati and chutney are retained in their familiar English forms.

This is political satire, explicitly introduced as an invention mixed with truth. The dialogue attributes exaggerated private speech to public figures; it should not be read as a documentary transcript. Apparent OCR damage remains in the verbatim Tamil source, while the translation follows the recoverable meaning without silently rewriting that source.
