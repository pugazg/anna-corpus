வளைத்துக் கொள்ளப்படுமோ என்று திகில் தருகிறது. ஸ்டாலின்

## English Translation

## Image 1: 001-f734999622.png

**Mussolini:** Adolf! A flood of destruction has broken through the dam and is rushing towards us. Important bases in Sicily have been lost; the wicked British forces are hissing like cobras emerging from their anthills.

**Hitler:** Yes. Seeing that fury, I have become deeply troubled. I went to capture them and have been caught myself.

**Mussolini:** What is this, man? What subject are you talking about?

**Hitler:** Why, the Orel front, of course. I thought our army would annihilate the Russian army there. The present situation makes me fear that the German forces may themselves be encircled. Before the scar of the suffering at Stalingrad has faded, another danger is emerging.

**Mussolini:** What is this? You speak of your own grievance. Is that what I asked about? My empire in Africa has crumbled. I thought, “Let that cursed thing perish with it.” But now the blow falling upon Sicily is shattering my mind.

**Hitler:** Oh! Was that the story you meant? Why, our battalions are there too, are they not?

**Mussolini:** Even so...?

**Hitler:** Why? Are they not fighting?

**Mussolini:** Only three things are happening to your troops as well: surrender, a headlong retreat, or descent into the grave.

**Hitler:** Is that so? But you could not endure for even a second the kind of suffering we face on this Russian battlefield, my friend. They have recaptured a hundred places. Fierce warfare. Enormous losses. My stomach aches when I think of that horror.

## Image 2: 002-b9db8c6164.png

**Mussolini:** Adolf, they say that once Sicily falls, they will pounce upon Italy.

**Hitler:** Let them! There is a great distance between Rome and Berlin. They would have to cross all of it before they could see my city.

**Mussolini:** From what you say, it seems that you yourself have concluded that Italy's fall is certain and cannot be prevented, and that you do not care even if Italy falls. Adolf, is this the mark of friendship? How many times must I plead with you? Matters appear likely to pass beyond remedy. Tell me, should I seek refuge?

**Hitler:** Would that not be the act of a coward?

**Mussolini:** If we remain warriors, it appears we shall be burnt to ashes. The Allied troops are advancing so swiftly.

**Hitler:** What do you know of the speed of Timoshenko's attack? You would understand if you stood on the Russian battlefield. Is your grievance alone so great?

**Mussolini:** Adolf, do you know how many times I have come to see you now?

**Hitler:** The fifth time.

**Mussolini:** What is to become of us?

**Hitler:** Did you read the American minister's speech? He says there are eight million Axis troops to oppose the Allied armies. When he comes to know our strength...

**Mussolini:** The army that once numbered more than ten million has now diminished. We have suffered losses, Adolf.

**Hitler:** What of it? I am the ruler of Europe. I shall fight for as long as it remains.

**Mussolini:** War, war, must it be war for all eternity? Must it still be war after taking a beating that has squeezed us dry? Without understanding, I fell into the net. I am suffering.

[A telephone rings. Hitler goes to it.]

## Image 3: 003-1855d90abf.png

**Hitler:** What? What? Is the Orel front in danger? Are the Russians attacking fiercely? Alas! My plan is being ruined.

[He puts down the telephone. A short while later, it rings again. Hitler answers and listens.]

**Hitler:** Musso, someone is calling you.

**Mussolini:** [Taking the telephone] Who is it? The commander? What is the news, man?

**Commander:** The situation in Sicily keeps growing worse.

**Mussolini:** To hell with it. Is there no other good news?

**Commander:** There is no good news. There is terrible news. Allied aircraft have dropped seven hundred and fifty thousand pounds of bombs upon the city of Rome.

**Mussolini:** What? What? Seven hundred and fifty thousand?

**Commander:** Yes. Rome has caught fire and is burning.

Mussolini puts down the telephone and looks at Hitler. Hitler looks at a map of the Orel front. The eyes of the world's people look upon those two roaring men. The chapter of the fanatics' downfall is beginning.

Having met somewhere in Italy, what else could the two men do but converse as we have portrayed above!

Yet who knows how much further the Axis nations' agony may spread, and in how many places, before our journal appears?

They say that Nero, the king, played the lyre while Rome burned. His successor now sits in conference as Rome burns once more. Destruction keeps drawing nearer, ready to embrace him.

*Dravida Nadu, 25 July 1943.*
