## English Translation

This is a little portrait that may be seen in the world of the wealthy. It did not appear as a motion picture; it is one of the scenes that escaped the camera.

Our country's cameras are still engaged in the sacred task of photographing Kailasa and Vaikuntha. Where is the time to photograph society?

These things escaped the camera, but thinking people can see them.

Where? Can one name a particular place? One can say only that the scene may be seen in the world of the wealthy.

Place: The front of a rich man's house.

Characters: The rich man and his friend; later, the servant Kuppan.

Time: Evening.

Situation: The rich man is ready to go out, suitably adorned. Driver Mohan has said the motorcar is being repaired, so a phaeton stands ready for the rich man's journey.

The visiting friend is the rich man's school-day companion. He has come to stay quietly at the house and write a book.

The rich man has treated his friend hospitably.

The two walk towards the mansion entrance. The rich man chats cheerfully. The friend considers it his duty to speak in a manner that pleases the heart of his host.

The rich man takes a peculiar delight in displaying his wealth, status and authority before his friend.

In these circumstances, their conversation begins.

**Rich Man:** [Pointing out his man approaching them.] Have you seen that fellow? What does he look like?

**Friend:** Like a lump of charcoal.

**Rich Man:** Leave colour aside, sir. I too am black.

**Friend:** [With a foolish laugh.] I forgot; forgive me. But although your complexion is black, see how it shines. He is not like that.

**Rich Man:** Leave that aside. In spite of being in such a condition, this fellow has now gone and married.

**Friend:** What was the hurry?

**Rich Man:** He has come of age, like a young bull. But creatures like these ought to work from morning to evening. They have no money. Yet he kept longing for marriage and somehow went and conducted one.

**Friend:** Yes, marriage is a great responsibility. They have neither concern nor forethought about whether they can manage it now.

[The servant approaches and bows.]

**Rich Man:** Hey, Kuppa! So you have married. Are you happy?

**Servant:** [Shyly.] Yes, master.

**Rich Man:** Hey! What is that in your ear? [To his friend.] A piece of bidi.

[The servant removes it and hurriedly hides it.]

**Rich Man:** Look at your face and your worth, and yet you have gone and married. Does your wife value you even at a quarter-anna?

[The servant grins.]

**Rich Man:** Why are you showing your teeth?

[To his friend.] The woman has fine features, sir, and is somewhat fair too.

[To the servant.] Your wife is fair, is she not?

**Servant:** She is wheat-coloured.

**Rich Man:** Is this fellow some maharaja? Nothing of the sort. Somehow he brought her home. Such a proper young beauty.

**Servant:** She is my aunt's daughter, sir.

**Rich Man:** Aunt's daughter or uncle's daughter, she is a fine woman.

[To his friend.] If she fell into the hands of a good film director, she would become a star, sir.

**Friend:** Village women...

**Rich Man:** Possess a beauty all their own. I do not know how this fellow will keep house with her.

**Friend:** Hey, Kuppa! The master praises your wife very highly.

**Servant:** Yes, sir.

**Rich Man:** Is she fond of you?

**Servant:** [Shyly.] Yes, sir. She is young, and newly married, you see.

**Rich Man:** Very well. Keep her carefully and protect her, understand? She is a wonderful woman. She is not an object a fellow like you could ordinarily obtain. Do you understand?

**Servant:** Yes, sir.

**Rich Man:** Do not remain complacent merely because we tied the marriage thread, understand?

[To his friend.] I have my doubts, sir, whether that young woman will spend her life with this fellow. In his present state he is not even slightly suited to her. You should see her one day wearing a blue sari, her hair dressed, carrying a pot of water...

**Servant:** May I go now, sir?

**Rich Man:** What, are you angry? Are you upset that we are talking about your new wife?

**Servant:** No, sir.

**Rich Man:** Go, go!

[The servant leaves.]

**Rich Man:** [To his friend.] The fellow is somewhat angry. Let him be. Truly, sir, this fellow is not suited to that woman. She is very beautiful. I do not know how she will remain with him. [Looks at his wristwatch.] Oh, it is late. Vela will be angry. May I leave?

**Friend:** Please do.

**Rich Man:** I suppose you are going to struggle on with that book.

**Friend:** Yes, about fifty pages remain.

**Rich Man:** What purana did you say it was called?

**Friend:** Not a purana: *An Explanation of Yoga*.

[The rich man takes his leave. After he has gone, the servant returns and addresses the friend.]

**Servant:** Is this decent conduct, sir?

**Friend:** What is it, Kuppa? Why are you angry?

**Servant:** Nothing, sir. We work somehow to survive, for the sake of our stomachs. Was my father a zamindar, so that I could drink and visit a mistress's house? I labour hard and maintain a family. Because a man is poor, may the wealthy say anything they please to him?

**Friend:** How did he abuse you just now?

**Servant:** Must he call me names? I would not mind even if he called me donkey or dog a thousand times. However arrogant his money makes him, right in front of me he spoke about my wife as though speaking of a woman wandering the streets: that she is not suited to me, that I must guard her carefully, that she looks like a film actress. What degrading talk from that man! I have surrendered this body to labour for food. Must I surrender my honour and shame as well? Does money, however plentiful, give him the right to speak to a husband about his wife in this manner?

**Friend:** Do not be angry, Kuppa. Good heavens, why are you crying? He was merely joking.

**Servant:** Is this a joke, sir? Is it a joke to summon a poor man, speak disgracefully about his wife before his own eyes and mock him? You read all manner of great books!

**Friend:** I did not imagine you would be so hurt.

**Servant:** How could you imagine it? You join him in his abuse and enjoy whatever hospitality you can obtain. How could you know how a poor man's stomach burns? He asked whether I was fit for her and whether she would remain with me. You say he was joking. Speak justly: was that a joke?

**Friend:** The game has turned disastrous.

**Servant:** What a senseless man you are! Why did you study? To beat an accompanying drum beside that sack of money? Did you decide never to distinguish justice from injustice before reading that great book? You felt not the slightest compassion when a man was humiliated before his own eyes.

**Friend:** What can I do? That is his nature.

**Servant:** You speak until a poor man's heart trembles and call it a joke. Fine justice you have, sir.

**Friend:** Leave him be. Let it go; do not distress yourself.

**Servant:** Come with me if you wish. I shall show you now with your own eyes.

**Friend:** Show me what?

**Servant:** He said that woman would not remain with me. Come, and I shall show you the conduct of his own wife.

The servant Kuppan angrily seizes the rich man's friend by the hand and rapidly pulls him towards the motor shed behind the house.

The shed door is not fully closed but slightly open. They stand beside it and hear voices from within.

**Woman:** I have not a moment's peace, Mohan. Listen to me. Let us leave quietly.

**Woman:** I cannot remain with him any longer.

**Man:** What hardship do you have now?

**Woman:** Do not joke. Is he a suitable man for me?

[The sound of bangles; a kiss; a deep breath; a soft laugh; bangles again; a song in a low voice. The motor horn becomes a gentle musical note.]

The rich man's friend covers the servant's mouth with his hands and drags him away.

After they have gone some distance, the servant shouts like a man possessed.

**Servant:** He degraded my wife before my own eyes. Did you see what his lawful wife is doing?

**Friend:** I saw. What can be done?

**Servant:** Is there not even one line about such a world in the book you are writing?

**Friend:** There are many. If I were to say everything I have read...

**Servant:** You could no longer remain the pampered son of a rich man's household.

The friend lowers his head in shame. The servant departs angrily.
