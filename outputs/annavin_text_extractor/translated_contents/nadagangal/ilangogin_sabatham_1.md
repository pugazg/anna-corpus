சேர: மூத்தவன் முடி புனைவது முறை - அறம் என்றாலும்

நாட்டை மறந்து, நாவலர், பாவலர் போல, ஏடெடுத்து, பாட்டெழுது,
பதவுரை பகர்ந்து, விழுப்பொருள், கருப்பொருள் பேசி,
பொழிப்புரை, கருத்துரை வழங்கிக் கொண்டிருந்தால் எட்டும்
போதுமா?

செங்: ஆம் தம்! ஆளப் பிறந்தவன் நானாக இருக்கட்டும்.
அதற்காக அண்ணனை விட்டுத் தம்பி தனி வழி நடக்க

சேர: ஒரு நாட்டிற்கு முடிசூடும் மன்னனும் வேண்டும் -
இன்னும் எதிரியின் இதயத்தைச் சுவைக்கும் ஈட்டியை, பளபள
வென ஒளிவிடும் வாளை, கேட்டவர் கிடுகிடுத்து நடுங்கும்
ஓசையை வெடிக்கச் செய்யும் கேடயத்தைத் தாங்கி நடக்கும் சேரர்
படைக்கு ஒரு சேனாதிபதி அவசியமல்லவா? கடகட, தடதடவென
ஓடி எதிரிப் படையைக் கண் திறப்பதற்குள் கலங்கடிக்கும் தரைப்
படையைக் கட்டியாள ஒரு வீரன் வேண்டாமா? யானைச்

தேவையாயிற்றே!

## English Translation

*A Screen for Thought*

## Scene 1

**Time:** Evening.

**Place:** The king's private flower garden.

**Present:** Chera sovereign Imayavaramban Neduncheralathan; his sons Senguttuvan and Ilango; and an astrologer.

**Cheralathan:** My sons, look there at the sun!

**Ilango:** He casts yellow rays, enchants the mind and gathers up delight.

**Senguttuvan:** That is the magnetic radiance of the sun's last glance!

**Cheralathan:** Yes, my son, its final light. The sun's journey is ending, just like mine.

**Ilango:** Father, what riddle are you setting us?

**Cheralathan:** It is no riddle or incomprehensible sermon. The morning of my life has passed and evening has arrived. Only the night called death remains. It too will come very soon. From moment to moment, I await that final embrace.

**Senguttuvan:** Can even those who rule the earth understand the coming and going of death, father?

**Cheralathan:** They cannot, Senguttuva. Yet my age, the bodily suffering it has caused and my failing spirit repeatedly command me to take precautions. I have therefore come here to speak privately with you, the future kings.

**Ilango:** At this lovely hour of twilight, why debate an unnecessary death? Why investigate it? Father, look at the vast sky, glowing crimson and making us swoon in its beauty; at the sun, appearing as a crystal sphere bathing in a coral sea; at the flowering vines that bloom and rejoice in this golden hour where darkness and light meet; at this pool of colour resembling a young face that captures the heart's thoughts; at the lotus, which lowers its head like a woman who has not seen her lover, folds its radiant face and offers painters and makers of epics an inexhaustibly sweet sight; and at the lily that bestows a smile!

**Senguttuvan:** Brother, what a rain of poetry you pour down!

**Ilango:** Yes, brother. I intend to become a poet.

**Cheralathan:** Then what will become of this earth?

**Ilango:** My brother must answer that question.

**Cheralathan:** It is custom and righteousness that the elder wear the crown. Yet, Ilango, you too descend from guardians of the land. Is it enough to forget the country in pursuit of epic and painting, to take up manuscripts like scholars and poets, compose verse, offer glosses, discuss theme and substance, and provide explanations and interpretations?

**Senguttuvan:** Yes, brother! Let me be the one born to rule. Must the younger brother therefore leave his elder and follow a separate path?

**Cheralathan:** A country needs a crowned king. Does not the Chera army also require a commander to bear the spear that tastes an enemy's heart, the gleaming sword and the shield whose thunder makes listeners tremble? Does it not need a warrior to command the land forces that race forward and throw the enemy into confusion before they can open their eyes? Does it not need a general to oversee the elephant corps and chariot host?

**Ilango:** My brother is more accomplished than I in the work of war. I trust that he will choose worthy warriors for those duties.

**Senguttuvan:** Leave the army aside. Is there not the treasury? Is there not a council needing wise ministers? Within it...

**Cheralathan:** Ilango, why hesitate to advise your brother? When the king's mind is clouded, your jewelled words can restore clarity.

**Senguttuvan:** You can be an expert who explains the state of the treasury, a discerning minister who corrects us by declaring justice, and a master who interprets conditions abroad. You can speak about food and clothing and show the proper path through difficulties in governing towns.

**Ilango:** I have allowed my desire to become a poet to grow beyond measure. Brother, every pulse of my spirit cries, “Create an epic,” and only then subsides. My ears will not accept your arguments.

**Cheralathan:** Remain a poet; I do not object. But remain beside your dear brother, who will govern this earth. My son, this is the request of your aged father, an old man tottering at death's threshold. It is also the wish of your loving mother, Narchonai. Do not cast aside my final desire. Do not refuse it, my son. Accept it.

[Ilango's face withers. Senguttuvan watches his brother eagerly.]

**Ilango:** So be it. Brother, do not fear. Father, I shall act according to your wish.

**Cheralathan:** A short while ago we arrived in a...

**Senguttuvan:** Golden chariot.

**Cheralathan:** And it had...

**Ilango:** Two wheels. You intend to ask us to become the two wheels of the chariot that is the Chera nation. Is that not so, father?

**Cheralathan:** You are wise, my son. That is precisely what I meant. Not only that: there were two horses drawing it. In the same way, a nation needs two powers to prosper. A chariot needs two wheels to roll; a country needs valour and wisdom to proceed well. Here is valour embodied in Senguttuvan; wisdom takes form in my youthful younger son Ilango. I overflow with pride when I look at you. My heart rises and my shoulders swell. My sons, I tell you this: like running horses, you must move united in one direction without crossing each other and creating perverse disputes. Otherwise the administration will collapse.

**Senguttuvan and Ilango:** So be it, father.

[The astrologer enters. Hearing him, the three turn.]

**Astrologer:** Namo Narayana! Namo Narayana!

**Cheralathan:** Welcome!

**Astrologer:** My salutations.

**Cheralathan:** Are you well?

**Astrologer:** May God bless you with many years in which to transform this wicked world into a golden one, make everyone good, cause joy to overflow and life to flourish.

**Cheralathan:** What brings you here?

**Astrologer:** To praise and bless you. Nothing else. Is that not the path ordained by the Vedas for wearers of the sacred thread? I came accordingly, and now I leave.

[The astrologer turns. He studies both princes at length from head to foot. The king's sons look upon him with distaste.]

**Ilango:** Brahmin, have you completed your work? Though you have taken leave, why do you remain and stare at us?

**Astrologer:** Future king, your golden body made me forget myself and stand transfixed!

**Ilango:** Ah! You have the glory of possessing, by divine grace, the power to foretell what is to come. Yet you have erred. Your astrology has betrayed you and your Veda has fallen into a pit. My elder brother, who will rule, stands nearby, yet you have called me the future king! And people call you an astrologer. Is this claim that you can know the future anything but fraud?

**Astrologer:** Prince, you speak too much in haste. You are the one who will rule. Royal fortune is yours. Such is God's decree!

[The astrologer realises that he has mistakenly blessed the wrong man and tries to recover. Senguttuvan's face reddens, whether from anger or jealousy. Ilango understands.]

**Ilango:** Astrologer, speak with deliberation. You say that while the elder lives, the younger will wear the crown. That directly violates the custom of our royal line.

**Astrologer:** I merely broadcast what astrology declares! You belong to royalty; I belong to the priestly caste, which lives by trusting the sacred thread, bundles of grass and piles of manuscripts. Is it proper to be angry with me?

**Cheralathan:** Ilango, the Vedic scholar is your elder in age, learning, experience and even caste. Why argue with him?

**Ilango:** Father, be patient for a moment. I shall test this astrologer's expertise. I know their scripture. It is a collection of a crowing cock's clucks; their Veda is a dry exercise in recitation.

**Senguttuvan:** Brother, why quarrel needlessly with a Vedic scholar?

**Ilango:** It is no quarrel, brother, only a peg to stop his reckless tongue. Astrologer, I am the one who will rule. Is that what you say?

**Astrologer:** I do not say it. Astrology says so!

**Ilango:** If I do not rule, that means your astrology is false, does it not?

**Astrologer:** Yes...

**Ilango:** To tell the country that every one of your words is a distilled lie, from this day I take up the saffron robe and become an ascetic. I shall renounce not only desire for the kingdom but desire for every object in this fourfold world. Wearing saffron, I shall wander as a poet.

**Cheralathan:** Idle words have brought us to calamity. My son, why take the guise of an ascetic?

**Senguttuvan:** It does not suit you at such a young age!

**Ilango:** Astrologer, leave! If you meet me again someday, you will certainly ask my forgiveness.

[The astrologer leaves, blinking in confusion.]

**Senguttuvan:** Brother...

**Ilango:** Do not be alarmed, brother. Even if I live in the forest wearing saffron, I shall remain watchful of your welfare. The three of us gathering here, the astrologer approaching us, his false praise, my seeking the saffron robe, and that priest who spoke mischief then fleeing: all of it forms a fine prologue to a delightful drama. I swore to become an ascetic perhaps because I feared that desire for land might enslave me and make this almanac's babble come true. Abandon your anxiety!

**Senguttuvan:** What does it matter who rules? Why this extraordinary vow?

**Ilango:** For that very reason, I say that you must rule.

**Cheralathan:** Who can defeat you in argument? Follow the path shown by your wisdom.

[The three mount their chariot and leave for the palace.]

## Scene 2

**Place:** The town's public hall.

**Present:** Elders, scholars, guards, bards, artists, poets, warriors and the astrologer.

**Poet:** The two of them are truly a pair!

**Scholar:** They are hills of diamond and gems!

**Poet:** Is this not how an elder and younger brother should be?

**Scholar:** Yes. One governs the country; the other, a man of letters, guards the world itself.

**Artist:** One rules the earth; the other rules poetry!

**Poet:** In the history of this land we have seen so many kings of kings, but...

**Warrior:** We have neither seen nor heard of one like Cheran Senguttuvan!

**Elder:** Some loudmouths from the north spoke scornfully of our country. Those worthless Aryan kings were hastening towards their own ruin!

**Warrior:** He set out for war at once!

**Poet:** Was it merely war? It was a wondrous event the whole earth should honour in letters of gold.

**Elder:** Those arrogant northern corpses carried the stone for a temple to heroic Kannagi, while the entire world laughed! The community of warriors mocked them and laughed until its ribs ached.

**Artist:** Only a Himalayan stone suits Kannagi; and its glory lies in crowned kings bearing it.

**Poet:** That is the elder brother. And the epic written by the younger...

**Scholar:** An incomparable epic, a jewelled necklace worthy of Mother Tamil: the illustrious *Silappathikaram*.

**Poet:** The three forms of Tamil unite within it and become a surging sea!

**Bard:** Poetic sweetness spreads throughout it, pouring refined honey into the listener's ear and sugarcane juice into the mind.

**Poet:** They praise it as a dramatic epic. It has remedied every deficiency the dramatic arts possessed until now.

**Man of Letters:** How can we adequately praise its verbal beauty, subtle meaning and firmness of thought? Words fail description.

**Scholar:** From now on, the epic he created will itself become the hero of our homeland's glory.

**Poet:** *Silappathikaram* is an unequalled summit of epic poetry for the classical Tamil land, a mine of ideas and a treasury of imagination!

[The astrologer enters.]

**Astrologer:** Why such delight? The crowd can hardly be contained!

**Poet:** We are going to see Ilango Adigal, sovereign of epic poetry and the noble creator of *Silappathikaram*.

**Astrologer:** Why?

**Poet:** To express our appreciation for the immortal fame he has attained in the world of knowledge.

**Warrior:** Astrologer, why do you stare so blankly? Have you not been in town? Not merely the citizens; Chera sovereign Senguttuvan himself comes today to see his younger brother.

**Astrologer:** Oh!

[The astrologer slips away.]

**Elder:** It is late. Everyone set out. Let us see our young emperor of epic poetry, the royal poet who has mingled with our very souls.

[They leave together, raising cries of “Long live Ilango Adigal!”]

## Scene 3

**Time:** Evening.

**Place:** The secluded forest where Ilango Adigal resides.

**Present:** Ilango Adigal, Senguttuvan, townspeople and the astrologer.

[The townspeople are returning after seeing the poet. The astrologer enters.]

**Astrologer:** Salutations.

**Ilango:** Well! Is this the old astrologer?

[Adigal laughs mockingly.]

**Astrologer:** Yes, the old astrologer, but with new clarity of mind!

**Ilango:** Have you heard the news that your astrology proved false? Have you seen what became of the royal fortune I supposedly possessed?

**Astrologer:** Until now I was merely a turner of pages. I had cast my own thought far away. I have gained clarity. I sought you out and came now to ask you to grant me a new life.

**Senguttuvan:** Astrologer, though my brother does not rule a country, he plants his banner over the whole world of poetry. The epic world worships at his feet!

**Ilango:** My brother possesses a royal fortune absent from your scripture. Not only royal fortune, but heroic fortune; not only heroic fortune, but victorious fortune. You have seen it and rejoiced!

**Astrologer:** Adigal, forgive my old error and show me mercy by allowing me to remain with you.

**Ilango:** I am no religious preceptor.

**Astrologer:** You may not be a religious preceptor, but you are a teacher of humanity. Even if not, you are certainly my teacher and guide.

**Ilango:** If the priestly realm saw this conduct of yours, it would roar! The world of Vedic reciters would rise in fury!

**Astrologer:** I do not care about that, and you need not think of it.

**Ilango:** I have praised Buddhism, celebrated Jainism as the home of gracious principles, blessed Vaishnavism and not rejected Saivism in my epic. I have therefore become one who accepts every faith.

**Astrologer:** That itself gives me comfort.

**Ilango:** I wear a saffron robe, yet I have expressed ideas at which many other bearers of water pots tremble.

**Astrologer:** I came knowing all of it.

**Ilango:** Even when I sang the invocation to my epic, I did not summon gods.

**Senguttuvan:** “Let us praise the moon! Let us praise the moon! Let us praise the great rain! Let us praise the great rain! Let us praise Puhar! Let us praise Puhar!” That is what my brother sang.

**Ilango:** Astrologer, observe carefully. I did not worship nature; I praised it. I did not bow down; I blessed it. That is the very opposite pole from your practice.

**Astrologer:** I am your slave.

**Ilango:** The very word “slave” is bitter to me.

**Astrologer:** I do not know what name you would give me. You must accept me somehow.

**Ilango:** Very well. You have realised that what you trusted until now was false.

**Astrologer:** Yes, yes.

**Ilango:** You say my ideas are not bitter neem to you, but sweet jaggery syrup. Is that so?

**Astrologer:** Ah, without doubt!

**Ilango:** Then rather than remain with me, speak to the thousands within your own community who still trust the old arrangements and beliefs as you once did. Perform that service from this day until your life ends.

**Senguttuvan:** That is the right judgement for this newly enlightened astrologer!

[The astrologer stands like a statue. Silence lasts a moment.]

**Astrologer:** Adigal, you are a lamp of light. You have driven away the darkness of delusion in my mind and intellect. I am forever obliged to thank you. However much opposition arises, I shall labour greatly to spread your ideas among my people.

[The astrologer leaves.]

**Senguttuvan:** Though you are a saffron-robed poet, you are the sovereign best suited to this earth!

**Ilango:** That is why I appointed you! This is the command of this sovereign, so obey it without transgression. The astrologer too has acquired sense.

[Ilango Adigal smiles. The Chera king takes leave.]

*25 June 1950.*

## Translator's Notes

`நிமித்திகன்` denotes an astrologer or diviner who claims to read future signs. “Astrologer” is used consistently while the source's satire of inherited authority remains explicit.

`பூசுரன்` and `மறையவர்` are caste-marked honorifics for a Brahmin or Vedic priest. They are translated according to context rather than flattened into a single neutral title.

The vow joins the traditional account of Ilango's renunciation to Anna's rationalist critique: Ilango takes saffron robes specifically to falsify the astrologer's prediction and later directs the converted astrologer to reform his own community.

The English retains the drama's partisan descriptions of northern and Aryan kings as rhetoric spoken by its characters; those descriptions are not presented as independent historical verification.
