இரக்கம்

உச்சரிப்பதற்கு எளிய, பதம் இனிமையுங் கூட.

வாழ்க்கையிலே, இரக்கத்தைத் தேடிப் பயணம் நடத்துவதோ,
அலை கடலில், பாய்மரமற்ற கலம் செல்வது போலிருக்கும்.

இளமங்கையைப் படுகிழவருக்கு மணமுடிப்பது தவறு என்ற
கொள்கையை மறுப்பார் கிடையாது. இரு இளைஞர்கள், இந்த
இலட்சியம் வெற்றி பெற வேலை செய்கிறார்கள்.

எந்தத் தாயும் தன் மகள், வயதுக்கொத்த மணாளனுடன்
வாழ்வதைக் கண்குளிரக் காண வேண்டுமென்றுதான் விரும்ப
முடியும்.

மரகதம், அவ்விதமானவள்தான்! ஆனால், தன் ஒரே மகள்
அம்பாளை, ஒரு முதியவருக்குத் தரச் சம்மதிக்கிறாள்!!

இலட்சியவாதிகளான, சோமுவும், பாலனும், கோபமும்
வருத்தமும் அடைகிறார்கள்.

நேரிலே, சென்று விளக்கி, இந்தக் கலியாணம் நடைபெற
வொட்டாமல் தடுத்தாக வேண்டும் என்று இருவரும் தீர்மானிக்
கின்றனர்.

அங்ஙனமே சென்று பேசிப் பார்க்கின்றனர்.

இதோ, அந்த உரையாடல்.

* * * * *

சோமு: என்ன அநியாயமம்மா! எப்படி அம்மா, உங்கட்கு
மனம் வந்தது; இவ்வளவு, இளமங்கையை, தொண்டுக் கிழவருக்கு
- பணம் இருக்கிறது என்ற காரணத்துக்காக - மண முடிக்கச்
சம்மதிக்கிறீரே - நியாயமா? அந்தப் பெண்ணின் வாழ்வு,
சந்தோஷமாக இருக்க முடியுமா? மனம் எரிமலையாகும்! கண்கள்
குளமாகும்!

பாலு: ஏரியோ, குளமோ. . .

சோமு: ஆமாம்! உடைந்த மனதினர், அங்கெல்லாம் சென்று
உயிரைப் போக்கிக் கொள்வார்கள்.

போரிடும் புனிதவதிகளின் விழிகளிலே வழிந்தோடிய நீர் இந்த
நாட்டையே அரித்து வருகிறதே அது தெரிகிறதா அம்மா, உனக்கு!

சோமு: அம்மாவுக்கு இதெல்லாம் தெரியாதப்பா தெரியாது
- அவர்கள் ஒரே ஒரு விஷயத்தைத் தெரிந்து கொண்டார்கள் -
ஆகவே வேறு எந்த விஷயமும் தெரிய வேண்டாமென்று

பாலு: எதைத் தெரிந்து கொண்டார்கள். . .?

சோமு: ரூபாய்க்குப் பதினாறு அணா, என்பதை!

மரக: போடா, படித்த முட்டாளே! பணப்பேய் நான் என்று
பரிகாசமா செய்கிறாய்? கற்றதை எல்லாம் கொட்டி அளக்கிறான்

கொண்டிருக்கிறீர்கள் - ஆனால் ஏழையின் மனவேதனை எப்படி
இருக்கும் என்பதைத் தெரிந்து கொள்ளாத மூடர்கள் - தெரிந்தாலும்
என்ன செய்வதென்று தெரியாமல் அரண்டு ஓடும் கோழைகள்.
பேசவந்து விட்டார்கள் - பேச. நியாயம் - நேர்மை - இரக்கம் -
என்று வார்த்தைகளை வீசுகிறார்கள் - முட்டாள்களே!

என்றான் - புன்னகைக்கு ஈடு இந்தப் பூலோகத்தில் கிடையாது
என்றான் - இந்த வீராதி வீரன். இவளை மயக்கினான்.

## English Translation

Compassion.

Easy to pronounce; the very word is sweet as well.

But to journey through life in search of compassion is like sailing a vessel without a raft or sail upon a wave-tossed sea.

No one would deny the principle that it is wrong to marry a young woman to a decrepit old man. Two young men are working to make this ideal prevail.

Any mother can only wish to behold her daughter living happily with a husband suited to her age.

Maragatham is just such a woman. Yet she consents to give Ambal, her only daughter, to an old man!

The idealists Somu and Balu are angered and distressed. They decide that they must go in person, explain matters, and stop this marriage from taking place. They do so and speak to her.

Here is that conversation.

* * * * *

**Somu:** What an injustice, mother! How could you bring yourself to do it? You agree to marry so young a woman to a decrepit old man merely because he has money. Is that fair? Can the girl's life be happy? Her heart will become a volcano! Her eyes will become a lake!

**Balu:** A reservoir or a pond...

**Somu:** Yes! People with broken hearts go to such places and end their lives.

**Maragatham:** I know all of that! Who could care more about my daughter than I do? Do you know how much thought I gave before reaching this decision? Do not inflame the anguish in my heart.

**Somu:** Mother says she knows everything! Your daughter is eighteen, while that old bridegroom is two years short of sixty. Do you know that? You agree to give this flowering vine to a grey-haired man, and then say you know everything. What is it that you know...?

**Balu:** To marry a young woman to an old man is to torture her life! Mother, did you know that? An ill-matched marriage could even ruin your daughter's character and conduct; did you know that? The tears streaming from the eyes of virtuous women who struggle in agony after marrying old men are eroding this very country. Can you see that, mother?

**Somu:** Mother does not know any of this, Balu. She learned one single fact, and therefore decided that she need know nothing else...!

**Balu:** What fact did she learn...?

**Somu:** That there are sixteen annas to a rupee!

**Maragatham:** Get out, you educated fool! Are you mocking me as a money-fiend? This young bull empties out everything he has studied and holds forth. You both imagine that you know everything, but you are fools who do not know the pain in a poor person's heart; even if you knew it, you would panic and flee, not knowing what to do. Yet you have come to talk. You fling about words such as justice, integrity and compassion, you fools!

[She goes inside, returns with a photograph of a handsome young man, and shows it to them angrily.]

**Maragatham:** He praised her beauty. He called her eyes red lotuses. This greatest of heroes said that her smile had no equal in this world. He bewitched her. With his honeyed words he even deceived me, though I am not easily taken in. He swore that the marriage was certain, whoever might oppose it.

**Somu:** Who is he? What is his name?

**Maragatham:** His name? Man! Why does he need any other name? A male who does not know how to care for a woman! His name will be Rama, Krishna or Govinda; it is hardly going to be Hiranya or Ravana! He is just one among them.

**Somu:** Did he promise to marry your daughter?

**Maragatham:** He swore it, invoking the names of five or six gods.

**Somu:** Then...?

**Maragatham:** What then? My daughter remains a maiden to this day, while he is away in the hills with his wife.

**Balu:** Why?

**Maragatham:** Your friend just mocked me, saying that the only thing I know is that there are sixteen annas to a rupee. That very thing: he abandoned my daughter out of greed for money. I said he was away in the hills; the woman he married there has three bungalows!

All that cajoling about lotus eyes, coral lips and a body the colour of gold flew away the moment he obtained those bungalows, gardens, motorcar and money. You useless, overeducated blockhead, you mock me as a money-fiend! Say, if you like, that because I have no money I wander about thinking that, if only I could obtain some, we too might live comfortably like other people. This man already had money, an abundance of it, and still wanders about seeking to heap up more and more. Will you call him a money-fiend? You will not! You boldly condemn only the poor. Before the rich, all of you are cowards.

**Somu:** It would have been a very suitable match if he had become your daughter's husband.

**Balu:** Yes! In beauty, age, education, in every respect.

**Somu:** Was it only because she had no money?

**Maragatham:** That was the true reason, the one in his mind. Outwardly he gave another: “She is of a different caste; my family is angry,” he said. While courting her, he used to boast, just like you, that he was not a man who cared about caste. He must have been educated like you too, for he said all manner of things: marriage is not enslavement, but the victory of love and the glory of womanhood. In the end he deserted her. “You are of one caste and I of another. Personally, I dislike caste distinctions, but I am forced to submit to the elders at home,” he said, and left.

**Somu:** Fool! Coward!

**Maragatham:** Call him whatever you please.

**Somu:** A man with a heart devoid of compassion!

**Maragatham:** Now you can see it! Did he stop to think, “I made that woman trust me; will she not be shattered if I abandon her?” Did he show compassion?

**Somu:** He is a man without compassion, mother! But must you too abandon compassion on that account? You dare cast your daughter into a pit without compassion. Is that right?

**Maragatham:** Son, speak calmly! Think before you speak! You have not a drop of compassion. If you did, you would not have called me a woman without compassion. Wait, I will return.

[She goes inside, brings out a sari with a decorative border, and displays it.]

Look! This is the finery in my house, son, the very last of it! This is the sari she owns. Even this lay in pawn with the Marwari for three months. Have I not gone blind in one eye? Why? Because the staff of poverty struck it. A broken chair, a torn mat, a filthy pillow, a leaking roof, half-starvation: who showed compassion to us while we lived in this condition? Who did? Who paid any heed to the agony in a poor person's heart?

Who came forward to find my daughter a station suited to her beauty and a position suited to her youth? You revile me as heartless, young man who knows nothing! I ask you: answer me. The creditor is about to auction even this house in which I live. We face the cruelty of having to live in the shelter at the edge of the village. Who will save us from this condition? Can you? You are a compassionate man, are you not? Will you stop the auction? Will you appease the creditor's anger? Will you put an end to poverty? Can you come forward and provide us a way to live without losing our life and honour?

**Somu:** That is true. But if one stood firm in principle...

**Maragatham:** What principle are you talking about, son?

**Somu:** The principle that there must be no ill-matched marriage...

**Somu:** Yes...

**Balu:** And some idiot went and called him “Karunatma”...

**Somu:** Damn it, shut your mouth and walk! It was my father who gave him that title.

[Seeing his friend's predicament, Balu closes his mouth, though his mind continues to work.

**Somu:** Whatever else may be said, what you did was utterly wrong, an act without compassion!

**Somu:** Whom do you hope to deceive with such talk? If you had felt even a little compassion at the thought that the woman's life would be ruined and her heart broken, would you have abandoned her? It was greed for money, was it not...?

**Wealthy Man's Son:** You fool! Did I abandon her out of greed for money...?

**Somu:** Did you not obtain abundant wealth by marrying Mangai...?

**Wealthy Man's Son:** I did. But that was not why I abandoned her.

**Somu:** What other reason was there?

**Wealthy Man's Son:** Compassion was the reason. Do you know what my mother did? One evening she locked herself inside a room. A noose was ready. She stood by the window and called me.

**Somu:** Why? What happened when she called you?

**Wealthy Man's Son:** She called me, showed me the rope and said, “Boy, I am going to sacrifice my life.”

**Somu:** Alas! Why?

**Wealthy Man's Son:** I cried out in just that way. “You must swear that you will do nothing to dishonour our clan, and you must act by that oath. Otherwise you will see only my corpse,” my mother said, not angrily, but in tears.

**Somu:** What a predicament!

**Wealthy Man's Son:** I stood outside, pleading and imploring. “Mother, no! Do not do any such thing. Do not hang yourself. I cannot bear it,” I said, weeping too. “My life is in your hands, son. Only if you swear that you will not marry that woman of another caste will I open the door and untie the rope. Otherwise I shall die right here. This is the truth,” she said. What could I do?

**Somu:** It truly is a moral dilemma!

**Wealthy Man's Son:** Merely hearing it leaves you in a moral dilemma. Imagine what it was like for me to witness the scene. The mother who bore and raised me stood before my eyes ready to end her life. I alone could keep her from dying. “Will you abandon that woman or kill me? Choose one,” she asked. Think what my heart must have suffered at that moment.

If I married the woman I loved, I would become a son who caused his mother to die. My mother stood before me begging for her life: the mother who bore and raised me; the mother who protected me from so much as a fly or ant landing upon me; my mother who took pride in me and praised me; the mother who loved me as her own life. “I shall end my life; that woman of another caste is no good,” she said. I pleaded, “Mother, do not impose this one condition. Ask me for anything else.”

**Somu:** What did she say to that?

**Wealthy Man's Son:** Listen! “Son, have I ever before asked you to give me any particular thing? This is the first time, and it will also be the last. The mother who bore you begs you for her life. Will you grant it, or will you harden your heart and kill me, thinking it does not matter if your mother becomes a corpse?” she asked. Think how my heart must have suffered. Nothing I said persuaded her. How could I bring myself to lose my mother? Even a man who kills for a living would show compassion to his mother! I abandoned Ambal in order to save my mother. Otherwise the town would have reviled me as a man who killed his mother, a brute whose deed caused the mother who bore him to die, a man without a drop of compassion. Even you would have condemned me, and my own conscience would have burned me. That is why I sacrificed my love: not for lack of compassion, but because I possessed it. What do you say now?

**Somu:** I do not know what to say. I thought Ambal's mother lacked compassion and rebuked her. She said that her daughter's life had been ruined by your lack of compassion. Yet you did this to save your mother, so you too are not without compassion. I cannot understand who is guilty! Seth Rathanlal shows compassion even to cows and ants, but not to that poor woman. He is going to auction her house.

**Wealthy Man's Son:** That, if you like, can be stopped...

**Somu:** It cannot! They say that mother even tried weeping before him, but the sinner showed no compassion.

**Wealthy Man's Son:** He may show no respect for a poor person, but he will show respect for this note. Take it to him.

[He gives Somu the note.]

[The two go to the Seth's shop and give it to him. The Seth takes it and reads it.]

“Very well! We will stop the auction! You may go!” he says.

[As the two friends walk along the street...]

**Somu:** He has such a good heart. And yet...

**Balu:** You are asking why he abandoned the woman he loved!

**Somu:** Yes. I cannot understand it.

**Balu:** The Seth is the man who donates sugar to ants, is he not?

**Somu:** Yes.

**Balu:** Does the same Seth show that compassion to human beings?

**Somu:** No.

**Balu:** Why?

**Somu:** I do not understand.

**Balu:** You would not. He, who keeps the accounts, understands. Showing compassion to ants costs very little; showing it to human beings costs much more. He conducts a business whose merchandise is called compassion, buying fame at a low price. The wealthy man's son is the same. He did not have the courage to marry Ambal for love and bear great hardship and loss. Now he suffers a small monetary loss by clearing their debt. It is an ordinary expense he can bear. By making this small expenditure he acquires the title of a compassionate man! My friend, there are many such strange businesses.

**Somu:** Can this condition not be changed?

**Balu:** It cannot be changed by extolling the excellence and greatness of compassion. A path of preaching cannot turn everyone into a noble person. Many people today can live only through another person's compassion. That very condition must change. If the system that creates and sustains that condition is to change, Somu, speeches in the people's assembly about the greatness of the sacred site of Kashi must be reduced, and the merit of Moscow's creed must be explained more often. In place of saying, “Give aid, and you will be praised as a noble man,” the voice of reason must say: “Give people their rights! Do not ruin the world!”

[Somu becomes absorbed in deep thought. Balu takes leave of him and goes.]

*Dravida Nadu*, 24 August 1947.

## Translator's Notes

- `கருணாத்மா` (Karunatma) is an honorific meaning a compassionate or merciful soul. The irony is deliberate: Rathanlal earns the title through inexpensive ritual charity while dispossessing poor debtors.
- The source's “sixteen annas to a rupee” refers to the pre-decimal Indian currency system and is Somu's sarcastic accusation that Maragatham values money above all else.
- `காசி க்ஷேத்ரமகாத்மியம்` literally concerns the sacred greatness of Kashi. The final contrast with `மாஸ்கோ மணத்தின் மாண்பு` opposes religious sermonizing to a Moscow-associated socialist demand for material rights; the alliterative rhetoric has been retained in meaning rather than imitated artificially.
- Maragatham's colloquial phrase `கண்றாவிக் கலியாணம்` condemns the proposed marriage as appalling or ghastly; it is not a formal category of marriage.
- The wealthy young man is identified only as `சீமான் மகன்` (“the wealthy man's son”) in the source. No personal name has been supplied in translation.
