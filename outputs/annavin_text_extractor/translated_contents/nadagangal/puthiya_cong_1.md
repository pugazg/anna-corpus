## English Translation

Brother, I now invite you to witness a few scenes in the drama staged by those skilled in the art of bending each person to their will at election time by offering inducements and creating fear.

* * *

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Go away! Go! Have you come to argue on behalf of Congress? How much did you receive for it? Why should we vote for Congress? Because it dropped a stone upon our heads? Because it threw dirt into our livelihood? My vote will certainly not go to Congress. Nor will I tell a single fellow to vote for Congress, whether you seize my hand or fall at my feet in supplication.

**Mandalam:** I understand the reason for your anger very well, and I am not angry with you in the least. You are angry because Congress introduced prohibition and destroyed the occupation your family had carried on for two generations. But...

**Former liquor-shop owner:** But what? The usual sermon, is it not? That although a few lose money, many benefit; that it is a virtuous act and brings peace to poor families. Is that what you are going to say? My ears have soured from hearing it repeatedly.

**Mandalam:** Good heavens! Did I come to repeat that stale speech? There was once a single-minded insistence that prohibition had to be introduced. The Congress ministry felt it could not fail to implement even something the Mahatma had said. That is why the prohibition law was enacted.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Are you explaining the reason? Will money come pouring into my cashbox merely because you explain it?

**Mandalam:** Your anger boils over before hearing everything I came to say. Listen. When Congress first came to power, it could not immediately forget the Mahatma's words, so it passed the law. But the number of people within Congress who insist that prohibition must remain has now diminished. The view that prohibition has failed to produce the expected benefit is gaining strength.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** And so? You have unleashed the police in every direction to hunt people down. Is that what you mean?

**Mandalam:** That is not what I mean. Such haste and anger! Can you not listen patiently? An opinion is now forming that prohibition is useless and may be abolished.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** What! What! That prohibition is unnecessary?

**Mandalam:** Within the Congress party itself, the view that the prohibition law is unnecessary is growing. There are bright signs that it will gain enormous support before long.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** I cannot believe it. Congress seeks public support in the Mahatma's name. The Mahatma declared prohibition to be scripture. How could Congress oppose and abolish such a law?

**Mandalam:** You speak this way because you do not know the difference between the Congress of today and the Congress of that time. In today's Congress, those who formerly opposed Mahatma Gandhi are the more numerous. Their view is that the Mahatma ordered prohibition because he imagined everyone to be an ascetic like himself, but that it does not suit practical life.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Even so, the law remains in force.

**Mandalam:** It remains, but for how long? The call to abolish it is becoming stronger. Shops have already been opened in Maharashtra.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Around Bombay? Is Congress not in power there?

**Mandalam:** Congress governs there. The Congress party itself has said that prohibition must be relaxed. Congress government implements what the Congress party says. Is that not proper?

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Has the Congress there asked for the prohibition law around Bombay to be changed? Bravo! Bravo! Look at their courage. Here, Congressmen's thighs tremble. They preach that drink is one of the five great sins.

**Mandalam:** How long will they speak that way? Only as long as informed people like you remain outside Congress instead of joining it. People such as you must join the Congress party and work. You must explain the matter and boldly state that laws of this kind merely produce needless financial loss.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Will they allow me to speak? They will threaten me, saying prohibition was the Mahatma's programme and that no Congressman has the right to oppose it.

**Mandalam:** That is precisely what they cannot do. Democracy prevails. Under democratic principles, each person may speak without fear, mercy or favour about what he believes to be right.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Could I even argue that liquor shops must be reopened? Would they permit it?

**Mandalam:** Who has the right to deny permission? No one. You may ask: “Why do you shower condemnation upon me as soon as I say liquor shops should reopen for economic reasons and for workers' welfare, declaring that it is improper for Congress? Does not that very Congress govern Pondicherry, where liquor shops flourish? Is Gandhi's realm confined to Cuddalore and denied to Karaikal and Pondicherry?” What answer could they give? Shall I tell you the truth? If ten people like you speak this way, prohibition will disappear by itself.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Will they not mock me, saying a liquor seller demands that liquor shops reopen?

**Mandalam:** How could they? If you speak from outside Congress, condemnation, mockery and opposition will arise. But if you join Congress, can a single fellow wag his tongue? Would we not defend you by saying that you too are a Congressman?

**Former liquor-shop owner:** The thought grows sweeter the more I consider it.

**Mandalam:** That is why I say: support Congress in this election, work for it and secure its victory. Afterwards, you will see Congress run to answer your call.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Will the town not talk strangely about a former liquor-shop owner joining Congress?

**Mandalam:** A needless doubt. Plenty of people like you have already joined Congress.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** You must recommend me favourably to your leaders.

**Mandalam:** Watch my work and you will understand gradually. Set your mind at rest. Work to secure a Congress victory in this election, and I shall make you the Congress leader for this area.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Why would I want to be a leader?

**Mandalam:** Do you think we are incapable of it?

**Former liquor-shop owner:** No, no! Would I think that? Many people have told me about you. I shall act according to your advice. Is that satisfactory? My vote belongs to your Congress.

**Mandalam:** Why call it “your Congress”? Say “our Congress.”

**Former liquor-shop owner:** I have not even begun wearing khadi.

**Mandalam:** What of it? What is there in clothing? Everything must be in the heart, and it is.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** My boy, listening to you, I shall join Congress. We must certainly win, or the town will mock us.

**Mandalam:** What doubt can remain about victory? You are Congress now. Would you fail to demonstrate victory? There are three thousand votes in this area.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** At least a thousand people will obey my word, even by the lowest estimate.

**Mandalam:** And will the other two thousand defy you?

**Former liquor-shop owner:** I worry that if the opponent offers a quarter or half, they will grin and yield.

**Mandalam:** Would anyone enter your area and bargain at a quarter or half? Would the opponent dare? Does he not know that, if your pride is aroused, you will throw down even two or three per vote?

**Former liquor-shop owner:** If we consider pride, the expense may become excessive.

**Mandalam:** The opponent will have that fear too. Is he a greater man than you?

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Greater man or twig, he has only recently acquired a little money. Let the fellow come; we shall deal with him.

**Mandalam:** Then shall I leave? From now on, this Cart Stand area is not mine but yours.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Go attend to the other places, brother. Consider the Cart Stand votes already inside our ballot box.

**Mandalam:** What doubt is there? Please give me fifty.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Fifty rupees? Why, my boy?

**Mandalam:** Yes. I shall send you a set of khadi clothes.

**Former liquor-shop owner:** Yes, my boy. Send a flag as well.

**Mandalam:** One in silk for your mansion and ordinary cloth for the other places. Is that right?

**Former liquor-shop owner:** As though you did not know!

2 May 1965

## Translator's Notes

The title `புதிய காங்கிரஸ்காரர்` literally means “The New Congressman/Congressmen”; the plural English title reflects the source's satire of a recurring political type. `முன்னாள் கள்ளுக்கடைக்காரர்` is rendered “former liquor-shop owner,” referring to a hereditary vendor displaced by prohibition. `மண்டலம்` functions as the election organiser's name or sobriquet and is retained. The “quarter,” “half,” “two” and “three” in the vote-buying exchange are intentionally unitless colloquial rates in the source. All six local scans had been falsely recorded as containing no OCR text; their Tamil was restored by matching the scans to the corresponding Wikisource transcription before translation.
