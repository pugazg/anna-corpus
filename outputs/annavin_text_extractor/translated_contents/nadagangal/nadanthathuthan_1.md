## Image 1: 001-64f077f377.png

### Scenes That Escaped the Camera

[Two conversations at different times.

The first is a conversation between two men when the Congress came to power.

The second is between the same men after the government has been functioning for some time.]

### Coronation Day

“What is special in town today, my friend? Everything is in a commotion.”

“Today is the coronation! A new king is ascending the throne.”

“Very well! It is natural for the new king to rejoice at receiving the crown. But what makes you happy? What makes the town happy?”

“What a mad fellow you are! What do you know of the suffering the old king inflicted? Only now is that misery ending, with a new king arriving. The old suffering will be no more!”

“Oh! Say that, then. That is the important thing. Say that the hardship which tormented the town is ending. Whether or not the king changes, if kingship gives comfort instead of hardship, there is indeed reason to rejoice.”

“That is what I meant when I said a new king was coming. That is the reason for the people's happiness.”

“Very well, my friend! May your happiness remain, endure and grow. Was the old king such a great torment?”

“Why ask! His rule was nothing but misery. The manner of government was entirely as he pleased.”

“Explain it clearly. What kinds of hardship were there?”

## Image 2: 002-766874e41c.png

“What can I tell you, and what can I leave untold? Even rice was rationed. There was scarcity in everything. Poor teachers were not paid enough; if they asked, the king would rage. If a labourer said his wages were insufficient, the police baton came as the answer.”

“Good heavens! Was the old king so indifferent? Did the people in the town not protest?”

“A fine question! Protest brought imprisonment. If people tried to hold a meeting, a prohibitory order appeared. If they struck work, they were arrested.”

“Was that so? The poor must have suffered greatly. What a pity!”

“Yes, an indescribable misery. Profiteers too became scorpions stinging the poor. Some hoarded goods and bit the public like snakes.”

“It is painful even to hear of it. But you say that henceforth this misery will be gone. You say a new king is coming, hardship will end and happiness will be born. Very well, enjoy your happiness. I shall take my leave.”

The new government has now been in office for some time. The same two men meet again.

“Your face looks familiar! Oh, I remember now. Are you not the fellow who told me about the coronation celebration?”

“Yes, I am.”

“The new king...?”

“He is keeping well...!”

“Kings always keep well. I did not ask about that. I asked whether government has become pleasant since the new king came.”

“Ah! Government proceeds without any deficiency.”

“The practice of rationing rice has disappeared, has it not?”

“The ration is going to be reduced.”

## Image 3: 003-04fd660822.png

“What injustice is this! You said it was the old king who ordered rice to be rationed. Are they now reducing even the ration? You said there would be comfort when the new king came!”

“I did. They said all those things would happen.”

“Have all the profiteers been eradicated?”

“You ask whether they have been eradicated? They are living very happily!”

“Good heavens! Have they not been eliminated even now? Never mind. Have labourers' wages risen?”

“Wages have not risen! The shouting has increased.”

“Why?”

“Why? People believed the old misery would end when the new king came. It did not happen. Their hearts are wounded, and so they cry, ‘Sir! Is this just?’”

“When working people cried, ‘Is this just, sir?’ the old king would send the police, ordering them to disperse the crowd. Surely the new king does not do that?”

“He too sends the police.”

“But he would not order them to beat the worker. The new king would send the police to punish the employer who cheats the toiler!”

“Sir! Do not speak like this and turn my eyes into pools of tears. Like the old king, this new king sends the police precisely to suppress workers who protest.”

“Oh! Is that really true? How can I believe it? You said it was the old king who ruled through the police. Does this one do so too?”

“Do the police disperse meetings with baton charges, just as they did before?”

## Image 4: 004-982b48d1c7.png

“Baton charges, tear-gas shells, gunfire: everything happens.”

“Even under the new king? What injustice! Do they even arrest workers, as the former government did?”

“Indeed! What happened before keeps happening. Bus drivers in Madurai went on strike, and arrests took place there. A strike has continued at Spencer's in Madras for nearly a month; seventy-five workers have been arrested here too.”

“What a terrible time! Good heavens! Does this happen even under the new government?”

“The new government has arrested two women workers as well. To break the strike, it supplies Spencer's with replacement workers under police protection.”

“Has the new government sided with the employer during the strike?”

“Yes.”

“Who owns this Spencer company? Is he a relative of the new king?”

“No! It is a white man's company!”

“Does the new government give so much assistance to a white man's company? Why can it not compel that employer to act according to the workers' demands?”

“It arranged arbitration. The white employer refused to accept the arbitrator's decision.”

“If he refused, why could the new government not compel him? Does it lack authority?”

“Why would it lack authority? It even has the power to take the company from its owner and order the government itself to operate it.”

## Image 5: 005-80ef270cf8.png

“Even so, the new government arrests workers and collaborates with employers in arrangements to break strikes. What is this? You said on coronation day that the old government had done such things and thereby caused misery! Yet this government too...”

“Sir! Do not increase my anguish.”

“Never mind that. Have salaries risen?”

“Ah! Threefold.”

“Splendid! The hardship of poor government employees will now end.”

“Sir! Think whom you mean when you speak of a salary increase. It is neither for government employees nor for labourers.”

“For whom else?”

“For the ministers!”

“For the ministers? What a strange thing! Under the former government, wealthy people who loved pomp occupied office and wandered about crying, ‘Money, money!’ But in the new government, are there not people who show compassion towards the poor and humble...?”

“Yes! These very people will now receive up to 1,500 instead of 500. They passed a law increasing their salaries.”

“Why? What reason did they give for this increase?”

“What reason? Prices have risen. Expenses are high.”

“Why, that is the old government's refrain!”

“That is their refrain too. They sing the same old refrain with somewhat greater force and precision.”

“Very well. It is true that rising prices cause hardship. But that hardship most affects ordinary government servants, people in small occupations and labourers...”

## Image 6: 006-3acd7a8ae0.png

“What is to become of them?”

“The Premier has said that they will be considered later.”

“What is this? Was not ‘It will be considered later’ the phrase the old government habitually used for everything?”

“Yes! They too say exactly the same thing.”

“I do not like this at all. Never mind. These village officials receive extremely low salaries. Unable to bear their hardship, they protested before. Have their salaries risen?”

“No! The minister has said, ‘Be patient. We are considering it.’”

“This is no new language either. Is it not the old government's language?”

“Yes! The king is new, but the language remains the old language.”

“What a miserable time! On coronation day you said happiness would overflow under the new king! Never mind. What happened to the question of schoolteachers' salaries?”

“The minister said their salaries ought to be raised, that affection ought to be shown towards teachers, and that their suffering ought to be relieved.”

“That is something cheerful! Has he raised their salaries?”

“No!”

“Why?”

“There is no money! The Education Department receives money from the Excise Department; excise revenue may decline because of prohibition. The minister said this difficulty must be managed.”

## Image 7: 007-48c56cd614.png

“Damn it! Is that not what every official in the old government used to say? Does the new government say the same thing? Never mind. Has prohibition at least arrived?”

“The minister has said it is going to be introduced in four districts.”

“Why only four districts? Did we not formerly say that piecemeal prohibition would be ineffective and that prohibition must be introduced throughout the province?”

“Yes! But the minister said that if prohibition were imposed throughout the province all at once, government revenue would fall and the budget could not be balanced.”

“Go away! A fine minister and a fine government. Is that not exactly what the official in the former government said? If these people say the same thing, what use are they? If they repeat the old speech about everything, why did they come anew? What have they done after assuming power?”

“They are running the government!”

“Is that all? Is that the full extent of it? The existing misery has not gone; the old methods have not changed; workers' suffering has not diminished; convention continues unchecked. Yet that day you danced, saying happiness would overflow when the new government came!”

“The only result I found was the exhaustion caused by all that dancing.”

“Shame! I feel both angry and embarrassed. I shall leave and return after waiting a little longer. Then we shall see whether the new government works in a new way, removes the old misery, and gives the people a new and happy life.”

*Dravida Nadu, 9 June 1946.*
