## English Translation

## Scene 1

**Place:** The warriors' tent  
**Time:** Morning  
**Characters:** Warriors

[One warrior sings while the others relish the song. Another warrior runs in, listens, and then speaks.]

**Warrior:** Upon what do you swear? Upon what do you swear?

**Singing Warrior:** Why, upon my mother, upon my father, and upon Tamil.

**Warrior:** You swear upon Tamil Nadu? Truly?

**Singing Warrior:** Friend, why has doubt suddenly arisen in you?

**Warrior:** Friend, I swear upon my mother. We are warriors fighting to protect the honour of Tamil Nadu. But some regard that as mere empty talk.

**Singing Warrior:** Who are those fools?

**Warrior:** They say we are mere talkers, and our words are no different. They say we will do nothing.

**Singing Warrior:** Who said that? Did you stand like a tree when you heard it?

**Warrior:** No. I flew here like the wind to tell you of the swollen arrogance of those sinners who insult us.

**Singing Warrior:** What audacity! In whom was such conceit born? Where is that wooden-headed fellow? In Anga? In Kalinga? Where is he? Who is he?

**Warrior:** Those who reviled the Tamil, Tamil Nadu, and our king Cheran Senguttuvan are not in Anga. Those wretches are on the bank of the Ganges in Kalinga. Their names are Kanakan and Vijayan.

**Singing Warrior:** Aryan kings?

**Warrior:** Yes, those worthless fellows. A mob that lay subdued now roars. Those who stood with folded hands and covered mouths now crow. They speak contemptuously of our Tamils.

**Singing Warrior:** Ah! Have wastrels mocked warriors? Has a rabble of impostors insulted us who do not fear war? What times, what a spectacle! Has the end come for Kanakan and Vijayan, that they should possess such arrogance? Does a cat challenge a tiger to battle? Set out! Let us go to our sovereign, report those scoundrels, and march to war.

**Warrior:** We shall strike down those Aryan crown-bearers.

## Scene 2

**Place:** The Chera palace  
**Time:** Morning

[The Chera king and a Puranic storyteller converse alone. The storyteller narrates the Ramayana.]

**Storyteller:** In the forest, Sri Ramachandra wanders everywhere searching for Lady Sita and weeps. His younger brother Lakshmana says, "Brother, this is our time of misfortune. Should you lament so much for it?"...

**Chera King:** Brahmin, what did that Lakshmana say? Even knowing that someone had abducted Sita, did he tell his brother, "It is only an ill-starred time; what if she is gone?"

**Storyteller:** Yes, to soothe Rama's grief...

**Chera King:** Very well. Continue later; we shall hear it.

**Storyteller:** While wandering through the forest, Sri Ramachandra gained the friendship of Sugriva, leader of the monkey clan. Sugriva and his elder brother Vali were enemies. Rama promised to kill Vali and crown Sugriva.

**Chera King:** Who made that promise? Rama, bewildered after losing his wife and knowing no way to find her, promised to kill Vali? Brahmin, this is amusing. Many speak of Vali's heroic might and valour.

**Storyteller:** Yes, but was Sri Ramachandra an ordinary man? Was he not an incarnation of Sriman Narayana?

**Chera King:** It seems it was precisely the incarnation who faced the danger of losing his wife. Leave that amusement aside. Vali and Sugriva were enemies. Rama heard of it, and then?

**Storyteller:** He promised to kill Vali and crown Sugriva.

**Chera King:** Splendid! Your Rama appears to have been a great deceiver.

[The storyteller covers his ears.]

**Chera King:** Yes, Brahmin, even hearing of such treachery is improper. There was a quarrel between two brothers, and that monkey appealed to this Aryan Rama. Had Rama been just and righteous, he should have ended their hostility and reconciled them. Should he not have said, "Vali, come here. Should brothers born of one mother's womb oppose each other like this? Look at me. Beside me stands Lakshmana, my younger brother. My stepmother ordered only me to go to the forest for fourteen years, and I set out at once. Unable to bear separation from me, Lakshmana accepted exile too. Such should be the unity between brothers"? Instead, he entered the quarrel between them.

[Villavan enters while listening.]

**Villavan:** He hid himself, shot an arrow, and killed Vali.

**Chera King:** [Turning] While hidden?

**Villavan:** [To the storyteller] Say it, Brahmin. Did Rama not hide behind a tree and shoot the valiant Vali dead?

**Villavan:** When such awkward questions are asked, it becomes a story. Until questions arise, it is divine mythology. King, Aryan mythology consists of many such tales filled with deceit, betrayal, fraud and the like.

**Chera King:** Shame! Whatever else one may say, for a warrior and king to kill Vali from concealment is vile and disgraceful.

**Storyteller:** [To Villavan] Say that it was cowardice too, Villavan!

**Villavan:** You have spared me the trouble by saying it yourself. That cowardice was also joined with self-interest.

**Storyteller:** What self-interest? Did Rama seize Kishkindha? Did he not crown Sugriva?

**Villavan:** Crown him? For what? To launch the monkey army against the rakshasa army.

**Chera King:** True. Sugriva had no quarrel with Ravana, yet it was Sugriva's army that attacked Ravana's forces.

**Villavan:** Both sides suffered losses, while the Aryan army remained peacefully in Ayodhya.

[Two warriors run in and bow.]

**Villavan:** Why? What is it? Why such haste?

**Warrior:** Greetings, king! They are speaking contemptuously of Tamil Nadu.

**Villavan:** In what madhouse did you find those lifeless creatures? Who are the arrogant men so crazed that they speak meanly of Tamils?

**Warrior:** On the bank of the Ganges are two Aryan kings named Kanakan and Vijayan.

**Chera King:** Aryan kings! Are there kings even among Aryans?

**Villavan:** Besides being kings, those worthless fellows are swollen with pride. They have spoken basely of our Tamil heritage and mocked the Tamil people. Have you heard the conduct of these wicked minds? I have never seen a wild dog mock a lion, nor a blind man mock the one who guides him. Wastrels revile warriors.

**Chera King:** Ah! Have they grown so bold? Have the carcasses who came here struggling to find a place now become cobras raising their hoods? Warriors of Rome and Yavana praise Tamil valour; Kalinga, Gandhara, Vanga and Anga have praised the courage of the Three Crowned Kings. Shall a mob that came here to survive now speak contemptuously of us? Has Tamil Nadu fallen so low?

**Villavan:** O hero whose fame has spread in all eight directions!

**Chera King:** Enough. Stop. You stand here saying my fame has spread in all eight directions, while those Aryan kings there have spoken meanly of me, of you, and of Tamil Nadu that sustains us.

**Villavan:** Give the command, protector! I shall mingle the blood of those witless villains with the Ganges. Why these swelling shoulders? Why this sword hanging at my side? I shall bring their crowns and place them at your feet. Their countries shall become forests, their palaces mounds of earth, and their fields open wastes. I shall show you heaps of corpses.

**Chera King:** Villava, your heroic speech eases the wound in my heart a little. Yet my heart trembles when I think that a people without knowledge or ability mocked the manly Tamils. Where did such audacity arise? In what way has the North now risen? Why was this vanity born?

**Villavan:** The North has become an Aryan wilderness, king of kings. When war comes, king and warrior discuss military method: how to strike and rout the enemy, whether to arrange the army in a circle, attack the flank, place the archers first, or send the elephant corps. The priestly crowd intervenes and lies: "Perform this sacrifice in this fashion beginning tonight, and Indra will grant the thunderbolt. Feed so many thousands of Aryans for so many courses, and Lord Agni will show favour. Whoever opposes you, victory will be yours." The king looks at the minister; the minister looks at the treasurer; the priest states the expenses and takes the wealth. On the battlefield spears pierce warriors' eyes; in the hermitages varied foods pour into Brahmin stomachs. Thousands of soldiers roar in battle; in temples Brahmins occupy themselves with twilight worship, midnight service and other rites. The warrior's blood spills there. Here the Vedic crowd eats until its sides bulge, applies sandal paste to digest the meal, and the paste runs down. Limbs are severed amid tumult there; the royal treasury dwindles here through Brahmin arrangements. That is how Aryanism works there. It destroys kingdoms, guardian of the earth!

**Chera King:** A crowd that grazed goats by rivers and cattle on mountain slopes, slaughtered and ate deer and pigs, worshipped fire and water, drank soma and sura, and performed the Paundarika and other sacrifices: shall it disparage us Tamils, who shatter forts, reduce battlements to dust, defeat many armies, destroy many obstacles, and live to the sound of war poems calling upon all the earth to honour self-respect? Shall those shallow minds, stirred to desire at the sight of green abundance, mock Tamil warriors who spill red blood, establish pure Tamil valour, and shine like diamonds in golden forts? Ah, has that powerless mob grown so bold? Whose favour have those ritual mendicants gained? Who supports them? Indra? The Three? The waning moon? Vayu who plays with street dust? Varuna who sports even in drains? Trusting which imaginary god did they dare do this?

**Villavan:** Whoever stood before the Aryan kings and gave them such courage, king of kings, even if my body must fall upon the earth, I shall make those enemies kneel. This is certain. It is an oath. I swear upon Tamil Nadu. Give the command to wage war.

**Chera King:** Yes. War it shall be.

## Scene 4

**Place:** The military camp

[The army stands in formation. The king comes to inspect it, and the soldiers greet him enthusiastically.]

**Soldiers:** Long live the king of kings! Long live the Tamil realm! Long live Cheran Senguttuvan!

**King:** Long live the Tamil realm! Warriors, that is a true declaration. I have summoned you today to ask you to shed your blood so the Tamil realm may live and its fame spread. We have resolved to invade the Aryan land. Wars sometimes arise over land, woman, or gold. We march for neither land, woman, nor gold.

Tamil Nadu is the flower garden of the world. The moon-faced women of the martial Tamil clans are no fallen women who, beguiled by honeyed words, embrace a seducer without hesitation. They are heroic women. Gold lies at our feet. In our jewelled mansions, young women with honeyed coral lips live with their lovers, surpassing breeze and moon in delight. We fight for none of those three. We fight for honour.

A mob that survived by baring its teeth rules a wasteland beyond Tamil Nadu's border. It is no smiling flower garden or grove where classical Tamil flourishes. Two wretches named Kanakan and Vijayan are said to rule beside the Ganges, like jackals in a forest roamed by battling tigers. We neither prevented nor heeded their living there. We regarded their place as a weed at the edge of a jasmine garden.

Yet today, while we guardians of that border are not stone; while swords shine in your hands, courage in your thoughts, Tamil blood in your bodies, and self-respect in your lives; while I stand like a tall tree and rule, knowing and remembering the heroic victories of my forefathers; those conceited descendants of Manu have reviled the Tamils, Tamil Nadu, Tamil valour and self-respect.

This disgrace occurred during my reign. I am ashamed, warriors. Blessed to rule war tigers like you, I ought to offer you the victories of the Tamil people as a feast. That is the duty and repayment I owe for receiving the sceptre. Yet I, spoken of as foremost among the Three Crowned Kings, stand before you merely bringing news that Aryans have insulted Tamils. Why have I a sword? Why have I a crown? Why should I live upon Tamil soil?

Comrades, perhaps they think I am a useless tree. Are there no warriors in the Chera realm to sever the heads of these insolent men? Has the host of warriors become bent? Have we lost the art of war? Are strength of shoulder and strength of mind gone? Are we stains upon our ancestors' fame, termites consuming ancient maxims, an unmanly crowd, turtles living in fear, treacherous foxes? Are we people of that kind? Never! We are bulls. We are Tamils. We shall strike with the killing sword and remove cruelty. We shall rejoice as the enemy casts his weapons against the shield of our chests.

Even if you shed your blood and become corpses on the field, I have taken an advance upon that sacrifice: I have sworn upon your valour. I have sent the Aryan kings their final message. I have resolved to wash away with my blood the reproach that, in my time, my Tamils heard words of disgrace and remained idle. I call you to witness it.

Tamil Nadu asks for an offering: it asks for courage and sacrifice. Are there no warriors here? Men of valour, depart! Forget beautiful Tamil Nadu for a few days. Forget love, poetry and spectacle. Let the sky be your tent, the earth your mattress, and weapons your companions. That is what I summon you to. Let anyone who regards life as sweet as sugarcane withdraw. Those who value honour above it may come and kiss the sword. Tamil Nadu calls us to wear the wreath of victory. Tamil Nadu, which bore us, raised us, and made us proud, calls. The land of your fathers calls! Who agrees to come?

**Soldiers:** Everyone! Everyone!

## Scene 5

**Place:** The road

[The warriors march.]

**Warriors:** We shall strike, destroy chests, batter faces, tear out entrails, break waists, smash heads, wipe away insult, drink life, bar the way, settle the wrong. Now we march!

**Chant:** Strike the foe! Strike the enemy!

## Scene 6

**Place:** The battlefield  
**Characters:** A warrior and Villavan

**Warrior:** Commander, the enemy's courage has failed. Their battle line is breaking, and fear has appeared upon their warriors' faces. As we advance and strike, the enemy's battle line becomes a line of corpses. Kanakan, the enemy commander who arrogantly spoke meanly of the Tamils, flees in terror, with Vijayan following. I shall send our cavalry and seize him. Let his head roll upon the field. Let the remaining army's heart turn to cotton when it sees his corpse. Let those cowards cry, "Refuge! Refuge!" and fall at our king's sacred feet to beg. Give the order. I shall turn those helpless, bewildered men into corpses on the field and offer them as a feast to the vultures.

**Villavan:** No, heroic comrade. Let the terrified Vijayan and distraught Kanakan turn their backs and flee with their remaining army.

**Warrior:** Then they will reach their city and survive!

**Villavan:** Friend, let them flee to their city. We shall pursue them and enter it. Let the princesses of their royal house see the fleeing kings from the palace terrace and ask, "What spectacle is this? Is our victorious king such a coward?" Let the eyes and words of the bangle-wearing women unleash mockery. Let shame stab those kings in the ribs. While they sweat, tremble and lie in humiliation, we shall go and seize them. Let cries rise in the palace. Let the Aryan settlement learn that destruction follows battle with mighty Tamil warriors. Let those fugitives run.

## Scene 7

**Characters:** A Tamil warrior and an Aryan warrior

[The Tamil warrior catches the fleeing Aryan, drags him back, and strikes him on the back.]

**Tamil Warrior:** Hey, worthless Aryan! Where are your valour, magic, and sacrifices? Where is the weapon of fire?

[He strikes him again.]

**Aryan Warrior:** [Rubbing his back and crying out] Alas! That is the weapon of fire.

**Tamil Warrior:** What is?

**Aryan Warrior:** That blow you just gave my back. It burns like fire.

**Tamil Warrior:** Blockhead! Run away. Go tell ignorant people your blatant lies that you possess the weapons of fire and water.

[The Aryan weeps.]

Look how water overflows from your eyes. There is your weapon of water! A crowd without fitness, experience or training for war, yet never lacking in swagger.

## Scene 8

**Place:** The battlefield  
**Characters:** Kanakan, Vijayan, Villavan and warriors

[Tamil warriors surround Kanakan and Vijayan on all four sides. The two try to flee, fail, and submit. Villavan Kothai arrives.]

**Villavan:** Do not release them! Do not release those Brahmins!

[He comes near, looks Kanakan and Vijayan up and down, and speaks.]

Lions who fought so fiercely! Wretches who spat arrogance! Fools who dared play with fire, stand still. Raise your heads a little; let me look at your faces.

[He looks at them and smiles.]

Ah! Valour is streaming from you! Sparks of courage fly from those eyes! Have you no shame? How did you dare wage war against our king, majestic embodiment of heroic splendour? Trusting what did you enter this dangerous game? For your crime, I would tear your bodies limb from limb here and now and feast dogs and foxes upon them. You remain alive only because my Tamils desire to see you in this condition.

Let your eyes, which emit deceit, be blinded by the heroic flame from Tamil eyes. Come, see the mountain-like shoulders of our assembled Tamil warriors, fall at their feet and submit. Let the world learn the fate of one who spoke meanly of Tamil Nadu.

You who arrogantly abused the Tamils shall carry stones to Tamil Nadu. With the stone placed upon your heads, we shall build a temple for the heroic woman Kannagi. Warriors, comrades who guarded Tamil Nadu's honour, lift the stone onto the heads of these villains. Let this crowd, knowing nothing but fraud, carry it. Let them feel the weight of Tamil Nadu's fame. Depart!

## Scene 9

**Place:** The road  
**Characters:** Kanakan, Vijayan and warriors

[Kanakan and Vijayan approach carrying the stone, escorted by Tamil warriors with drawn swords.]

**Warrior:** Walk! Walk on, you treacherous foxes! You depraved fools ruined by your own mouths, keep walking!

[To someone approaching from the opposite direction]

New beasts of burden! Cheran Senguttuvan made these worthless cattle, who grazed along the Ganges, carry a stone to Tamil Nadu.

## Scene 10

**Place:** The Chera king's audience hall

[The warriors enter.]

**Warriors:** Victory to the king of kings! Victory!

**Villavan:** Greetings, leader of victorious warriors. I have arrested and brought Kanakan and Vijayan, who insulted the Tamils. Here are those wretches.

**Chera King:** Kanakan! Vijayan! Are these said to be enemies? Are they said to know how to wage fierce war? Hey, fools! Instead of passing your days beside the Ganges, why did you purchase trouble? Without knowing what you were doing, you committed a petty act. Is one who does not know that treading upon a tiger's tail means death by its teeth not a fool?

Aryans, your people are prepared to surrender anything to save their lives. When there is war, you perform smoking sacrifices and ask God for divine weapons. Why did men who do not know how to wield a sword seek this trouble? You asked Tamils for gold, and we gave it. You asked for a place to live, and we gave it. After receiving that life, did you insult the Tamils?

**Kanakan:** King, we bow at your feet.

**Chera King:** What is astonishing in that? What history does your lineage possess other than falling, generation after generation, at the feet of warriors? Hey, fools! Can you cite one proof of your valour, a single proof? You lavishly praise certain men as heroes, but does not the story always say that all their victories came through God's grace? Where is the proof of their valour?

Villavan, take these worthless men through every town so our people may see them, and then cast them into prison.

Tamil warriors, I express my gratitude to you. Tamil Nadu swells with pride at the sight of you. Villavan, your courage has given Tamil Nadu undying fame. Your victory has wiped away the stain cast upon the Tamil people. Here is the heroic sword that has belonged to the Chera line for generations. Let it remain in your hand henceforth.

[Villavan raises the sword and kisses it.]

**Villavan:** May this sword serve to protect Tamil Nadu's elevation. I shall make myself worthy to bear the victorious sword of this heroic lineage, king of kings.

[He bows and leaves.]

*Dravida Nadu*, 13 March 1949

## Translator's Notes

The title refers to the northern rulers Kanakan and Vijayan in the *Silappathikaram* tradition, whom Cheran Senguttuvan compels to carry a Himalayan stone for Kannagi's image. `கசடர்` is a contemptuous term rendered as “wretches.” `Villavan Kothai` is Senguttuvan's commander. `Three Crowned Kings` denotes the Chera, Chola and Pandya dynasties. `Yavana` is retained as the period term for western foreigners, often Greeks or Romans in classical Tamil usage. `Parani` is a Tamil victory-poem genre. `Paundarika` is the name of a Vedic sacrifice.

The recovered scan moves directly from Scene 2 to Scene 4; no Scene 3 heading is visible in the OCR source. This numbering has been preserved rather than silently repaired. The source contains several damaged OCR tokens, especially in the king's long martial speech; the translation follows the legible sentence-level meaning while leaving the source block unchanged pending scan-level Tamil correction.

The work's attacks on “Aryans,” Brahmins and religious traditions belong to the voice and political rhetoric of the original play. They are translated faithfully, not presented as editorial assertions.
