## Part 1

ஆழமான குழி! அறுபத்தேழாயிரம் அடி!
உள்ளத்தில் பட்டதை ஒளிக்காமல் சொல்கிறது கழகம்!
பலர் வாழச் சிலர் சாகலாம்!
கிளர்ச்சிகள் மூட்டிவிடப்படுவன அல்ல!
குழி பறிப்போன் குழியிலேயே வீழ்வான்!
தம்பி!
ஆழமான குழி; மிக மிக ஆழமான குழி - ஆயிரம், இரண்டாயிரம் அடி ஆழமல்ல, 67,000 அடி ஆழமான குழி தோண்டப்போகிறார்களாம், சொல்லிவிட்டார்கள்! எவ்வளவு கஷ்டம் பாவம், இவ்வளவு ஆழமான குழி தோண்ட என்று எண்ணும்போதே எனக்குப் பரிதாபமாக இருக்கிறது.
பரிதாபம் காட்டுவது இருக்கட்டும். எதைப்பெற இவ்வளவு ஆழமான குழி தோண்டுகிறார்கள்? விஞ்ஞான வளர்ச்சிக்குத் தேவைப்படும் ஏதாவது அரிய பொருள் அத்தனை அடி ஆழத்திலே புதையுண்டு கிடக்கிறது என்பதற்காகவா என்று கேட்கிறாயா! தம்பி! அதற்காக அல்ல. நமக்காக, கழகத்தைப் போட்டுப் புதைக்க!! 67,000 அடி ஆழக் குழி தோண்டிப் போட்டுப் புதைக்கப் போகிறோம், தி. மு. கழகத்தை 1967-ஆம் ஆண்டு என்று திருச்சியில் கூடிய காங்கிரஸ் மாநாட்டிலே பேசியிருக்கிறார்கள்.
என்ன செய்கிறார்கள் இந்த காங்கிரஸ்காரர்கள்? ஐந்தாண்டுத் திட்டம் என்கிறார்கள், சமுதாய நலத் திட்டம் என்கிறார்கள், வட்டார வளர்ச்சித் திட்டம் என்கிறார்கள், இப்படிச் சொல்லிக்கொண்டிருக்கிறார்கள் - நாட்டிலே ஏழை மக்கள் வாழ்விலே பாராட்டப்படத்தக்க விதமான முன்னேற்றம் எதையும் காணாததால் மனம் நொந்து நீ கேட்டிருக்கிறாய் பல முறை, என்னதான் செய்கிறார்கள் இந்தக் காங்கிரஸ்காரர்கள்? என்ன செய்யப்போகிறார்கள் இந்தக் காங்கிரஸ்காரர்கள் என்றெல்லாம். அவர்கள் கூறிவிட்டார்கள் திருச்சி மாநாட்டில் என்ன செய்ய விரும்புகிறார்கள் என்பதை; ஆழமான குழி தோண்டப்போவதாகச் சொல்லிவிட்டார்கள்; 67,000 அடி ஆழம்.
என்ன அண்ணா! நம்மைப் போட்டுப் புதைக்கக் குழி தோண்டப் போவதாக அவர்கள் பேசுகிறார்கள் அத்தனை ஆணவத்துடன்; அதைக் கேட்டு நீ பதறாமல் துடிக்காமல், மகிழ்ச்சியுடன் பேசுகிறாயே என்று கேட்கத் தோன்றும். நான் பதறாததற்கும் துடிக்காததற்கும் காரணம் இருக்கிறது.
67,000 அடி! கவனமிருக்கட்டும்! அத்தனை ஆழமான குழி! ஏன்? அவர்களுக்கு அவ்வளவு அச்சம், அத்தனை ஐயப்பாடு! சாதாரண ஆழமுள்ள குழி, ஆயிரம், இரண்டாயிரம் அடி ஆழமுள்ள குழி தோண்டினால் போதாது, அதிலே போட்டுப் புதைத்தால் பயனில்லை, மீண்டும் எழுந்து வந்துவிடக்கூடும், மிக அதிகமான ஆழம் தோண்ட வேண்டும் என்று தோன்றுகிறதே தம்பி! அவர்களுக்கு; அது எனக்கு நமது கழகம் எத்தனைப் பெரியது, எதிரிகளை எந்த அளவுக்கு அச்சப்படுத்தி வைத்திருக் கிறது என்பதை அறியச் செய்கிறது. அது எனக்கு ஒருவிதமான மகிழ்ச்சி உணர்வைக்கூடத் தருகிறது. தி. மு. கழகம் சாமான்ய மானதல்ல, இந்தியாவை பத்தொன்பது ஆண்டுகளாகக் கட்டி ஆண்டு வரும் காங்கிரஸ் கட்சியினர், இந்த கழகத்தைப் புதைத்துக் குழியில் போடவேண்டுமென்றால், ஆயிரம், இரண்டாயிரம் அடி ஆழமுள்ள குழி போதாது, 67,000 அடி ஆழமுள்ள குழி தேவைப்படுகிறது என்பதை எடுத்துக் காட்டுகிறது அவர்களின் பேச்சு! கழகத்தின் வளர்ச்சியின் அளவை நாடு உணர்ந்து கொள்ளவும் அந்தப் பேச்சு துணை செய்கிறது.
கழகமா? அது எங்கே இருக்கிறது என்று பேசிய காலம் போய், கழகமா? அதைப் பொதுமக்கள் சீந்துவார்களா என்று கூறிய காலம் போய்,
கழகமா? பத்துப் பேர் கூச்சலிடும் இடந்தானே என்று பரிகாசம் பேசிய காலம் போய்,
கழகமா! கொஞ்சம் துள்ளுகிறது, நாலு நாளில் தன்னாலே அடங்கிவிடும் என்று பேசிய காலம் போய்,
கழகமா? அது உட் குழப்பத்தாலே உருக்குலைந்து போய்விட்டது என்று பேசிய காலம் போய்,
கழகமா? அது உடைபட்டுப் போய்விட்டதே! இப்போது அதிலே என்ன இருக்கிறது!! என்று பேசிய காலம் போய்,
கழகமா? நாங்கள் மூச்சுவிட்டால் காற்றிலே பறக்காதா என்று ஏளனம் செய்த காலம் போய்,
கழகமா? எங்கள் கோபப் பார்வையாலேயே அதனைச் சுட்டுச் சாம்பலாக்கிவிட மாட்டோமா? என்று பேசிய காலம் போய்,
கழகமா? எங்களுக்கு அது ஒரு பொருட்டாகுமா! என்று அலட்சியம் காட்டிப் பேசிய காலம் போய்,
கழகமா? அதை 67,000 அடி ஆழமுள்ள குழி தோண்டி அல்லவா புதைக்கப் போகிறோம் என்று பேசும் காலம் வந்திருக்கிறதே!
இது எதைக் காட்டுகிறது? கழகத்தை அலட்சியப் படுத்தியவர்கள், அது தன்னாலே உருக்குலைந்து போகும், உடைபட்டுப் போகும் என்று எண்ணினவர்கள், கழகம் சாமான்யமல்ல, அதைப் போட்டுப் புதைப்பதானாலும், சாதாரணக் குழி போதாது, மிக மிக ஆழமான குழி வேண்டும் என்று கூறிடவேண்டிய அளவுக்குக் கழகம் வளர்ந்து, அவர்களைக் கலங்கிடச் செய்கிறதே, அது தம்பி! எனக்கு ஒருவிதமான மகிழ்ச்சியேகூடத் தருகிறது!
காலஞ் சென்ற சத்தியமூர்த்தி சொல்லிக்கொண்டிருந்தார், ஜஸ்டிஸ் கட்சியை 5,000 அடி ஆழத்தில் குழி தோண்டிப் புதைக்கப் போகிறேன் என்று.
இன்றைய காங்கிரஸ்காரர்கள் அவரை மிஞ்சி விட்டார்கள்; குழி தோண்டுவதில்! வாயால்! 67,000 அடி ஆழக் குழியாம்.
அவ்வளவு கடினமான வேலை செய்ய வேண்டும் என்றாகி விட்டது, கழகம் பெற்றுள்ள வளர்ச்சி.
ஒன்றை மறந்துவிடுகிறார்கள் - கோபம் கண்ணை மறைக்குமாமே, அதனால் என்று எண்ணுகிறேன் - 67,000 அடி ஆழக் குழிதோண்டிக் கழகத்தைப் புதைக்கத்தக்க வல்லமை உள்ளவர்களானால், இந்த வீரர்கள் கழகம் இந்த அளவு வளர்ந்ததே, என்ன செய்துகொண்டிருந்தார்கள்? ஏன் கழக வளர்ச்சியைத் தடுக்க முடியவில்லை?
கழகம் இவர்கள் கண்முன்பாகத்தானே வளர்ந்தது? வேறு எங்காவது ஓரிடத்திலே கட்டப்பட்டு இங்கு திடீரென ஒரு நாள் கொண்டுவந்து இறக்குமதி செய்யப்பட்டதா? இல்லையே! இவர்கள் இப்போது இருப்பதுபோலவே அறிவாற்றலுடன், வீராவேச உணர்ச்சியுடன் இங்கேதான் இருந்துகொண்டிருந் தார்கள், நாம் வளர்ந்தபோது. ஏன் தடுக்க முடியவில்லை கழக வளர்ச்சியை? வளர்ச்சியைத் தடுத்திட முடிந்திருந்தால் இன்று இத்தனை ஆழமான குழி தேவைப்பட்டிருக்குமா? பாவம்! 67,000 அடி ஆழமல்லவா தோண்டப்போவதாகச் சொல்லுகிறார்கள். ஏன் இத்தனைக் கஷ்டம்? தும்பை விட்டுவிட்டு வாலைப் பிடிப்பது என்பார்களே, அந்தப் போக்கா?
இப்போது இருப்பதுபோலவே அவர்களிடம் எல்லா வல்லமையும் இருந்தது! பிரசாரம், பண பலம், அதிகார பலம் எல்லாம்! இப்போது இருப்பதுபோலவே அப்போதும் இவர்களே ஆளவந்தார்கள்! இருந்தும்? கழக வளர்ச்சியைத் தடுத்திட முடிந்ததா? இல்லையே! கழகம் வளர்ந்து வளர்ந்து, இவர்களுக்கு மிரட்சி அதிகமாகி, ஆழமான குழி! மிக ஆழமான குழி! என்றல்லவா அலறுகிறார்கள்.

## Part 1

### A Deep Pit: Sixty-Seven Thousand Feet! - The Party Speaks Without Concealing What It Feels - A Few May Die So That Many May Live - Agitations Are Not Artificially Kindled - He Who Digs a Pit for Another Will Fall Into It

Younger brother!

A deep pit, an exceedingly deep pit: not one or two thousand feet, but a pit 67,000 feet deep. They have announced that they are going to dig it! Merely thinking of the hardship of digging so deep fills me with pity.

Leave pity aside. Do you ask what they seek by digging such a deep pit? Is some rare material needed for scientific development buried at that depth? No, younger brother. It is for us, to bury the party! At the Congress conference in Tiruchirappalli they said, "In 1967 we shall dig a pit 67,000 feet deep, throw the DMK into it, and bury it."

What are these Congressmen doing? They speak of a Five-Year Plan, a social-welfare programme, and a community-development programme. Seeing no praiseworthy improvement in the lives of the country's poor, you have often asked in anguish, "What are these Congressmen doing? What are they going to do?" At the Tiruchirappalli conference they announced what they wish to do: dig a deep pit, 67,000 feet deep.

You may feel like asking, "Anna, they arrogantly say they will dig a pit and bury us. How can you speak happily without panic or agitation?" I have a reason for neither panicking nor trembling.

Sixty-seven thousand feet! Take note, such a deep pit. Why? They have so much fear and doubt. A pit of ordinary depth, one or two thousand feet, will not suffice. If they bury us there, it will be useless because we may rise again. They feel they must dig extremely deep. That tells me, younger brother, how great our party is and how severely it has frightened its opponents. It even gives me a kind of joy. The DMK is no ordinary thing. The Congress party, which has ruled India for nineteen years, says that a pit one or two thousand feet deep is insufficient to bury it and that a pit 67,000 feet deep is required. Their speech helps the country understand the scale of the party's growth.

Gone are the days when they said, "The party? Where does it exist?"

Gone are the days when they said, "The party? Will the public even take notice of it?"

Gone are the days when they mocked, "The party? Is it not merely a place where ten people shout?"

Gone are the days when they said, "The party? It jumps about a little; in four days it will subside by itself."

Gone are the days when they said, "The party? Internal confusion has disfigured it."

Gone are the days when they said, "The party? It has broken apart. What remains in it now?"

Gone are the days when they jeered, "The party? Will it not fly away in the wind if we breathe upon it?"

Gone are the days when they said, "The party? Will we not burn it to ashes with an angry glance?"

Gone are the days when they dismissed it: "The party? Does it matter to us?"

The time has now come when they say, "The party? We shall dig a pit 67,000 feet deep and bury it!"

What does this show? Those who dismissed the party and believed it would disintegrate or break apart by itself have been forced to say that it is no ordinary party and that even burying it requires not an ordinary pit but one exceedingly deep. The party has grown enough to disturb them. That too gives me a kind of joy, younger brother.

The late Sathyamurthi used to say he would dig a pit 5,000 feet deep and bury the Justice Party.

Today's Congressmen have surpassed him in digging pits with their mouths. Theirs will apparently be 67,000 feet deep.

The party's growth has made such arduous labour necessary.

They forget one thing, perhaps because anger blinds the eyes. If these heroes possess enough strength to dig a pit 67,000 feet deep and bury the party, what were they doing while it grew to this extent? Why could they not prevent its growth?

Did the party not grow before their very eyes? Was it constructed somewhere else and suddenly imported and unloaded here one day? No. As now, they were here with all their intellect and heroic passion while we grew. Why could they not stop us? Had they prevented the growth, would so deep a pit be needed today? Poor men, they say they must dig 67,000 feet. Why such hardship? Is this not like letting go of the body and trying to catch the tail?

They then possessed every power they possess now: propaganda, money, and authority. They were the rulers then too. Yet could they stop the party's growth? No. It continued growing; their terror increased; and now they cry, "A deep pit! An exceedingly deep pit!"

They speak as though they had spent many years abroad on more important tasks, reforming America or bringing comfort to Russians distressed by revolution, and as though we had grown only by choosing a time when they were absent. They imagine they could have stopped us at once had they been here. But the party grew while all of them were here, wide awake and uttering boasts.

What were these mighty men, who now claim they will dig a pit 67,000 feet deep, doing?

Did they remain content, saying, "Let it grow; what can it do to us?" No. Even then they spoke in the same fashion:

"We shall destroy it.

"We shall dig a pit and bury it.

"We shall wipe out the whole brood.

"We shall eradicate it root and branch."

There were roars, threats, and intimidation.

Do you not know the sacred utterance made then by Kamaraj, today's president of the Indian National Congress? "We shall cut off the thumbs of party members!"

How many kinds of opposition and disorder did they foment?

What varieties of cases did they institute?

How many baton charges and terms of imprisonment?

Was there any shortage of conspiracies?

Were people not murdered from Thoothukudi Sami to Vannai Pandian?

Were only a few flagpoles broken and only a few flags burned?

Did they not fabricate stories and conduct blackouts skilfully?

Were the abusive words used against party leaders merely ordinary, rather than possessing a distinctive, piercing vulgarity?

Did they spare birth, upbringing, family, or lineage?

Was it only a little that they abused the party's leading figures as men lusting for land, demons lusting for women, and men seized by greed for gold?

These virtuous men, who today say, "We too display artists," once despised us as performers.

How many different opposition conferences and annihilation conferences did they conduct?

Thinking local goods insufficient, did they not import as many foreign goods as they wished and hawk them loudly?

Their non-violent speech overflowed: "A black flag? Break the arm of the man who displays it!"

What did they leave undone? They tried everything and could not stop the party's growth.

The party that stood in an open field entered the Legislative Assembly.

Even in the Assembly, it became the authorised Opposition.

The very mighty men unable to prevent this growth now claim that they will dig a pit 67,000 feet deep.

Apart from the confidence that they can obtain whatever money they ask because they have created a few thousand new rich men in the country, do they today possess the courage to say, "The people's hearts leap with happiness at the excellence of our rule, and therefore their overwhelming support belongs to us"?

Could opposition parties have grown so greatly if they had governed in a manner that gladdened the people?

Let us suppose they do dig the pit, younger brother, a deep pit. Will the public not ask:

"Good men who govern the country, why are you digging such a deep pit?"

"This? It is to throw the DMK into and bury it."

"If so deep a pit is required, the DMK must be a great party. Is that so? How then did it grow to this extent?"

"It somehow grew. Now we are going to throw it into this pit and bury it."

"Why? What harm did the DMK do? To whom did it do harm deserving burial in a pit?"

"You do not know. It is a very bad party, yes, a wicked party that will ruin the entire country."

"That is precisely the detail we ask for. What did the party do? Did it plunder the townspeople's property? Did it draw salaries of thousands and live in luxury? Did it fraternise with black-marketeers or lie close to profiteers? Did it indulge in corruption and extravagance? Tell us what it did."

What answer would the virtuous pit-diggers give in such a conversation?

They would say only, "Go away! You know nothing. If you speak any further, we shall throw you into this pit and bury you too."

But what would the public say among themselves?

"The growth of the DMK has terrified these Congressmen.

"It is the DMK that remains vigilant and opposes bad laws brought by Congress rule and condemns unjust taxes.

"The DMK condemns the corruption and extravagance writhing within Congress rule.

"The DMK boldly asks the Congress to show its accounts: how much tax is collected from the people and how many facilities are provided to them?

"Even after seeing so many remain silent, saying, 'What is it to us?' and 'Can we do anything?' the DMK works with the purity of saying what it believes right, whatever may happen to it.

"The DMK labours for language, artistic wealth, and industrial progress, and to ensure that such progress spreads through society rather than becoming trapped in capitalist hands.

"The DMK exposes the unwritten agreement between capitalists and Congress rule.

"It breaks open secrets, resists cruelty, and exposes the injustices of government."

That irritates the Congress: the irritation that the party is undermining its domination.

"Titles, offices, permits, licences, and abundant newspaper advertising are available. When we called, 'Come and receive them,' many ran and joined us. But these party people refuse to be seduced by blandishments or stray from their path. They remain in friendly contact with the public. Unless they are destroyed, our domination cannot remain or endure." That is their feeling.

Will the public not say that this is why the Congress rulers wish to bury the party and think a deep pit is required?

The public will naturally think: "This is unjust. We must protect and support the party that labours for us and the country."

The party asks, "Create a way for people to eat their fill." Will the Congress, which tells the poor, "Eat rats and survive," attempt to destroy it while we merely watch? Will the hard-hearted men who torment us set out to dig a pit and bury the party that argues for us, while we remain silent? How could the public fail to think in this fashion?

This is why every attempt made when Congress rulers spoke arrogantly as they do now has failed and the party has grown further: the goodwill and support of the public.

Younger brother, let them remain at their task of digging a deep pit.

We must devote ourselves entirely to the work of going among the people and explaining the true condition.

Irritated that the party undermines their domination, Congressmen will naturally become enraged. They will swing clubs, threaten to tear out eyes, and say they will dig a pit and bury us.

A thief coming at midnight to break through a wall naturally curses, "That accursed fellow is still awake!"

Do not stories describe a heroine waiting for her illicit lover, wringing her hands and saying, "That wretched man has still not gone out"?

Likewise, these people agonise: "They refuse to rest! They continue their opposition! They advance step by step! Their growth continues!" Naturally they think the party must be buried in a pit.

Their angry speech proves that our work proceeds with force.

Their threats and intimidation help us recognise not only that we labour without rest but that we possess the resolve and enthusiasm to labour still further.

Let them continue digging. We shall continue showing the people the good path.

They panic that their domination may be lost, and anger therefore surges in their speech.

We have undertaken the work of establishing good government for the people. If our task were useless, our method unclear, and our strength inferior, the rulers would never even think of us.

Today no other thought arises in them except thoughts about us.

Wherever they go and whatever they do, their minds dwell upon us.

Fear torments them: "What will they say if we do this? What will they do if we do that?"

That is why, even when wealthy men sit around them, lakhs are received as offerings, newspapers sing their praise, and they sit enthroned, their attention turns to the "astonishing" plan of digging a pit 67,000 feet deep.

Like their previous plans to destroy us, this plan too will prove useless.

Public anger against them has grown many times greater than it was when they made earlier plans to destroy us.

Previously people lived in the condition of saying, "At least there is no shortage of rice."

Today they hear the rulers say, "Why do you cry 'Food! Food!'? Put whatever you obtain into your stomach and fill it."

The people's anger, bitterness, and agitation are visible every day through the continuing processions, demonstrations, shop closures, and strikes.

Is any section of society free of bitterness and indignation?

People who once thought agitation was merely something factory workers did and did not suit them now conduct agitations through their own organisations.

We see Government employees rise in anger.

We see salaried bank employees revolt.

We see teachers trapped in unending sorrow conduct protest marches.

Who can remain satisfied under this rule? The rulers beat the drum of their tongues and declare, "The whole country is on our side!"

At every agitation, people roar, "No! No!"

Guns speak and corpses fall, yet people refuse even to run in fear.

They proclaim: "A hero has fallen, but courage will not fall. One man has died; the righteous struggle will continue so that others do not die!"

The police are insufficient and the army is summoned. Why? The upheaval refuses to subside.

Even the fear that they may have to die has greatly diminished among the people today, and continues to diminish.

"A few may die so that many may live" has become an accepted philosophy.

Conditions are developing in which one might say that philosophy has entered practice.

Younger brother, Congressmen make one claim: opposition parties deliberately incite these agitations.

For argument's sake, let us suppose opposition parties do incite them. Are people fools? Do they not know that baton charges will be used and guns fired to suppress an agitation? Knowing it, why do they participate?

There is upheaval in their hearts. Therefore the courage to agitate arises by itself.

No opposition party can summon people by saying, "Come to receive baton blows! Come to become prey to gunfire!" But those who do not understand that the agitations are the result of disordered government blame opposition parties.

A great thinker, one whom today's rulers themselves must praise without end, condemned this as foolishness:

"Ideas and economic conditions make revolutions. Foolish people in authority, blind to everything that does not fit in with their ideas, imagine that revolutions are caused by agitations. Agitators are people who are dis-contented with existing conditions and desire a change and work for it. But tens and hundreds of thousands of people do not move to action merely at the bidding of an agitator. Most people desire security above everything else, they do not want to risk losing what they have got. But when economic conditions are such that their day to day suffering grows and life becomes almost an intolerable burden, then even the weak are prepared to take the risk."

Who said this? Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru himself. It was not an impassioned remark made long ago in a moment of emotion. He wrote it in his book *Glimpses of World History*, intending the country to know, the world to understand, and coming generations to learn.

I am compelled to remind those who call themselves Nehru's heirs of it, younger brother. What can I do?

Unable to understand Nehru's ringing words, unable to recognise that agitations erupt rather than being artificially kindled, they fire upon people and imagine that society will submit when the people curl up and fall dead.

Did not Bhaktavatsalam, "the man who stood like a rock," say, "Yes, fifty people were shot dead!" as though no greater achievement had occurred in his lifetime? What does that position show?

It shows their determination to suppress agitation by shooting people down.

That is why, younger brother, a pit 67,000 feet deep is needed.

They said it was to bury the party. But the reason they think so deep a pit must be dug is that the party is not the only thing they wish to throw into it. There are many others:

- Gandhism;
- democracy;
- moral principle;
- promises made to the people; and
- the corpses of those killed while agitations were suppressed.

Perhaps it is because all these must fit that they feel such a deep pit is needed.

I remember reading somewhere that one who digs a pit for another will himself fall into the pit he dug.

Younger brother, let them continue digging. Even if you and I are to be pushed into it, until that final moment we shall continue telling the people what must be said. Let us see whether truth gains victory or not.

Your elder brother,

4 September 1966
