# ஆரியம் இருக்கும் இடம்! / Where Aryanism Resides!

**Tamil title:** ஆரியம் இருக்கும் இடம்!  
**English title:** Where Aryanism Resides!  
**Source:** <http://www.annavinpadaippugal.info/kadithangal/aariyam_irukum_idam.htm>

## Source Tamil (verbatim)

"விடுதலை'யில் ஆரியர் பற்றிய செய்தியும், அரசும்-
சென்னை அரசு போஸ்டர் வாபஸ் பெற்றமை.
தம்பி!
ஆரியரை நடுத்தெருவில் நாள் முழுதும் போட்டு உதைத்தாலும் கேட்பதற்கு இன்று நாதி கிடையாது!
என்ன அண்ணா! இப்படி ஒரே போடு போடுகிறோயே! இவ்விதமெல்லாம் கூறும் வழக்கமோ-கருதும் சுபாவமோ கிடையாதே, என்ன காரணம் இவ்வளவு மோசமான நடையிலே பேச, என்று கேட்கத் தோன்றுகிறதல்லவா, உனக்கு.
பலே! பலே! இது ரோஷமான பேச்சு! இது வீரனின் முழக்கம்! இவ்விதம் பேசினால்தான் பிரச்சினை தீருமே தவிர, மயிலே மயிலே இறகு போடு என்றால் போடுமா? என்று கேட்டுக் களிப்பால் துள்ளிக் குதிப்போரும் இருக்கிறார்கள்.
தம்பி! நீ கலக்கமடையவும் காரணமில்லை, அவர்கள் களிப்படையவும் தேவையில்லை. ஏனெனில், ஆரியரை நடுத் தெருவில் நாளெல்லாம் போட்டு அடித்தாலும் இன்று கேட்பதற்கு நாதி இல்லை என்ற மணிவாசகம் என்னுடையது அல்ல! "விடுதலை'யில் வெளிவந்த வீர முழக்கம் அது; 28-9-55-ல்!!
அக்ரகாரங்கள் இந்த வீராவேச உரை கண்டதும் இடி கண்ட நாகமாக வேண்டுமென்று எண்ணினாரோ, அல்லது படை வீரர்களுக்குச் சிறிதளவு உணர்ச்சிப் பானம் கிடைக்கட்டும் 242 தம்பிக்கு அண்ணாவின் கடிதங்கள் தொகுதி ஒன்று 243 என்று கருதினாரோ, எதற்காக இவ்விதம் தீட்டினாரோ, நானறியேன் - ஆனால் ஆரிய இனத்தை நடுவீதியில் போட்டு அடித்தாலும் ஏன் என்ற கேட்க நாதி இல்லை என்று எழுதியவ ருடைய "கைவலி' தீருவதற்கு முன்பே, நாடாளும் காங்கிரஸ் தலைவர்கள், ஆரியரின் முகம் சிறிதளவு சுளித்துவிடுகிறது என்று தெரிந்ததும், நடு நடுங்கிப் போகிறார்கள் எனும் உண்மை, இப்போது தெரிகிறது.
வீரம் தேவை - நிச்சயமாக!
போர் முழக்கம் வேண்டும் அவ்வப்போது உணர்ச்சியூட்ட!
உள்ள நிலைமைக்குத் துளியும் பொருத்தமோ பொருளோ அற்ற வகையில், ஓங்காரக் கூச்சலிடுவது, சுவைக்கு உதவாது, என்பதை எடுத்துக்காட்டும் சம்பவம் ஒன்று நடை பெற்றிருக்கிறது.
ஆரியர் - சமூகத்திலே அனைவராலும் வெறுத்து ஒதுக்கித் தள்ளி விடப்பட்டது போலவும், கள்ளர், கயவர், கை ஏந்தி நிற்போர், ஆகியோர் எவ்வண்ணம் காரி உமிழப்பட்டும், கண்டோரால் ஏசப்பட்டும், கடிந்துரைக்கப்பட்டும், தாழ்நிலை அடைவரோ, அதுபோல ஆரியரின் நிலை ஆகிவிட்டது போலவும், அவர்களை எவ்வளவு கேவலமாக நடத்தினாலும் அடித்து விரட்டினாலும், ஏனென்று கேட்க ஒருவரும் இல்லை என்று கூறத்தக்க அளவுக்கு, ஆரியரை அன்னியர், அக்ரமக்காரர், அகற்றப்பட வேண்டியவர்கள், விரட்டப்பட வேண்டியவர்கள் என்ற முடிவுக்குச் சமூகத்தில் பெரும்பகுதியினர், அல்லது குறிப்பிடத்தக்க அளவினர் வந்துவிட்டது போலவும் எண்ணிக் கொண்டு, "ஆரியரை நடுத்தெருவில் நாள் முழுவதும் போட்டு அடி அடி என்று அடித்தாலும் ஏன் என்று கேட்பதற்கு நாதி இல்லை' என்று தீட்டுகிறார். தீ! தீ! தீ! என்று மும்முறை சொன்னதும் எந்தத் திக்கு நோக்கி கூறுகிறாரோ, அது பற்றி எரிந்து போகும் என்று எண்ணிடும் இயல்புடையவர்.
"விடுதலை'யில் இந்த மணிவாசகம் வெளிவந்ததினாலேயே, இது பெரியாரின் கருத்து என்று கொண்டு விடுவதற்கில்லை. ஏனெனில், பெரியார் "விடுதலை'யிலேயே எனக்கு விருப்ப மில்லாத கருத்துகள் சில வேளைகளிலே வந்து விடுகின்றன, அவைகளை என் கருத்துக்கள் என்று நம்பி விடாதீர்கள். என் கையெழுத்திட்டு வெளிவரும் தலையங்கத்தில் காணப்படும் கருத்துத்தான் என் கருத்து, என்பதாகக் கூறியிருக்கிறார். எனவே ஆரியரை நடுவீதியில் போட்டு உதைத்தாலும் ஏன் என்று கேட்பதற்கு நாதி இல்லை என்ற அருமையான கண்டு பிடிப்புப் பெரியாருடையது என்று கூறுவதற்கில்லை! பெரியார் ஸ்தானத்தை வேகமாக எட்டிப் பிடித்திடுவதாகக் கொட்டாவி விடும் குணளர் அவ்விதம் கருதக் கூடும்!!
திரிலோகமும் புகழும் சுந்தரன்!
தீரன்! வீரன்! கெம்பீரன்! உதாரன்!
என்று நள்ளிரவில் நாலுவீதி கேட்கும்படி உரத்தகுரலில் பாடிவிடுபவன், போர்க்களத்தில் பெற்ற அரிய அனுபவத்தினால் அவ்விதம் பாடுகிறான் என்று பொருள்கொள்வார் உண்டா! அதுபோல, வீரமூட்ட, இதுபோல ஆரியராவது மண்ணாங் கட்டியாவது! நடுவீதியில் அடித்தாலும் கேட்க நாதி ஏது என்று பேசுவதன்மூலம், சமூகத்தில் தன்னம்பிக்கை அற்று, தாசர் நிலைபெற்று, எடுப்பார் கைப்பிள்ளையாய், ஏவல்புரிபவராய், எடுபிடியாய்க் கிடப்பவர்களின் உள்ளத்தில் வீரம் முளைத்திடச் செய்யலாம் என்பதற்காகக் கூறி இருக்கக் கூடும். அல்லது, ஆரியர் உண்மையிலேயே பெற்றிருக்கும் செல்வாக்கும் வலிவும் வளருவது கண்டு அச்சப்பட்டு, வீரதீரமாகப் பேசி அந்த அச்சத்தை மறைத்துக் கொள்வதற்கும் இந்த முறை கையாளப் பட்டிருக்கக்கூடும்; இவ்விதமாகக்கூட இல்லாமல், ஏதோ இன்றைய "அயிடம்' அது, காரசாரமாக இருக்கிறதா இல்லையா பார்த்துக்கொள் என்ற போக்கிலே எழுதப்பட்டுமிருக்கலாம்; எக்காரணம் கொண்டு எழுதப்பட்டிருந்தாலும், நாடாளும் காங்கிரஸ் தலைவர்களுடைய போக்கைக் கவனிக்கும்போது, இந்த வீராவேசப் பேச்சுத் துளியும் பொருந்துவதாகக் காணோம்.
நாதியில்லை! என்று துந்துபி முழக்கம் கேட்கிறது! ஆரியர் தாக்கப்பட்டாலும் கேட்க நாதி இல்லை என்று முழக்கமிடும் நேரத்தில், "சென்னை ராஜ்ய சர்க்கார்' என்ன செய்கிறது, தம்பி! கவனித்தாயா? ஆரியரின் மனதிலே ஒரு துளி சஞ்சலம், முகத்திலே ஒரு சிறு கோபக்குறி, பேச்சிலே ஒரு விதமான வருத்தம் தெரிந்ததும், கிடுகிடுவென ஆடி முகத்தைத் துடைக்கவும், முகமன் கூறவும், ஐயா! வருந்தற்க! பிழை பொறுத்திடுக! தவறு ஏற்பட்டிருந்தால், எடுத்து இயம்பிடுக! ஏற்ற முறையில் கழுவாய் தேடிக்கொள்ளக் காத்துக் கிடக்கிறோம்! உமது உள்ளத்தில் ஒரு துளி வேதனை தோன்றினாலும் உலகம் தாங்காதே! கோபம் கொண்டு ஐயன்மீர்! எம்மீது சாபத்தை வீசாதீர்! சரணம் ஐயா! சரணம்!- என்ற கெஞ்சிக் கூத்தாட முன் வருகிறது. விடுதலையார் கூறுகிறார் வீதியில் போட்டு அடித்தாலும் ஆரியருக்காகப் பரிந்து பேச நாதி இல்லை என்று நாடாளும் தலைவர்களோ, நான், நீ, என்று போட்டி போட்டுக் கொண்டு முன் வருகிறார்கள், ஆரியரின் முகக் கோணலைப் போக்க; மனவருத்தத்தை நீக்க!!
ஆரியர், அடித்து ஒழிக்கப்பட வேண்டியவர்கள் என்று சமூகமே முடிவு கட்டிவிட்டது என்ற பொருள்பட அன்பர் எழுதுகிறார்; அவருடைய அன்புக்கும் ஆதரவுக்கும் பாத்திரமான தலைவர்களோ, தாசானு தாசர்'களாகி, ஆரியரின் தயவுக்குத் தவம் கிடக்கும் போக்கினராக உள்ளனர்!
காரணம் காட்டாமல் நண்பர் "காரசாரமாக' எழுதிவிட வில்லை; காரணம் காட்டுகிறார், நம்பிக்கையுடன். ஆரியரை நடுத்தெருவில் போட்டு அடித்தாலும் ஏன் என்றுகேட்க நாதி இல்லை என்கிறார், ஏன் என்ற காரணமும் கூறுகிறார்.
ஆரியர் செத்த பாம்புகளாகி விட்டனர்!
பாம்பு உயிரோடு இல்லை, எனவே, அதைப் போட்டு அடிப்பதால் ஆபத்து இல்லை!!
அந்தத் "துணிவு' கொண்டு அடிக்கும் வீரனை, ஏன் என்று யார் கேட்கப் போகிறார்கள் - கேட்க மாட்டார்கள்!

## English Translation

## The Report About Aryans in *Viduthalai* and the Government - The Madras Government's Withdrawal of a Poster

Younger brother!

Today there is no one to question it even if Aryans are thrown into the middle of the street and kicked all day long!

You feel like asking, do you not: "What is this, Anna? You deliver such a sweeping blow! You have neither the habit of speaking nor the nature of thinking in this fashion. What has caused you to speak in such an ugly manner?"

There are also people who leap with delight and say, "Bravo! This is spirited speech! This is the roar of a warrior! Only by speaking in this fashion can the problem be solved. Will a peacock shed its feathers merely because one begs, 'Peacock, peacock, give us a feather'?"

Younger brother, you have no reason to be disturbed, nor do they need to rejoice. For the ringing declaration that today no one would question it even if Aryans were beaten in the middle of the street all day is not mine. It was the heroic roar published in *Viduthalai* on 28 September 1955!

I do not know whether the writer imagined the agraharams would writhe like snakes hearing thunder when they saw this impassioned address, or merely wished to give the troops a small draught of emotional stimulation. Whatever made him write it, before the pain in the hand that wrote "There is no one to ask why even if the Aryan race is beaten in the middle of the street" had subsided, a truth became evident: the ruling Congress leaders tremble the moment they notice the slightest frown upon an Aryan face.

Courage is certainly needed.

A battle cry is needed from time to time to inspire feeling.

But an event has occurred demonstrating that loud, resonant shouting without the slightest relevance or meaning for the actual condition adds no flavour.

The writer seems to imagine that Aryans have been hated, rejected, and cast out by everyone in society; that their condition has become as low as that of thieves, scoundrels, and beggars who are spat upon, abused, and rebuked by all who see them; and that a large or significant part of society has concluded that Aryans are foreigners, wrongdoers who must be removed and driven away, so that no one would question however disgracefully they were treated or beaten. On that assumption he writes, "Even if Aryans are beaten again and again in the middle of the street all day, there is no one to ask why." He has the nature of one who believes that if he says "Fire! Fire! Fire!" three times, whichever direction he faces will burst into flame.

We need not conclude merely because this ringing declaration appeared in *Viduthalai* that it is Periyar's view. Periyar himself has said in *Viduthalai*: "Ideas I dislike sometimes appear even in this paper. Do not believe they are my views. Only the opinion in an editorial published under my signature is mine." Therefore we cannot attribute to Periyar the excellent discovery that no one would question it even if Aryans were kicked in the middle of the road. A person yawning with ambition to reach Periyar's position quickly may imagine otherwise!

> Sundaran, praised by all three worlds!
> Resolute! Brave! Majestic! Generous!

Would anyone suppose that a man who sings this loudly at midnight for four streets to hear does so because of rare experience gained on a battlefield? Likewise, the writer may have spoken in this fashion merely to awaken courage: "Aryan or clod of earth, what does it matter? Who is there to question it if he is beaten in the middle of the road?" Perhaps he wished thereby to make courage sprout in the hearts of people who lack self-confidence, live as slaves and pliable dependants, and remain servants and helpers. Or perhaps, frightened by the growth of the influence and power Aryans actually possess, he employed heroic speech to conceal that fear. Or it may have been written in the style of today's "item": "See whether it is sufficiently fiery." Whatever the reason, observing the conduct of the Congress leaders who govern the country shows that this impassioned speech does not fit the facts at all.

The drum thunders, "There is no protector!" At the very time it is proclaimed that no one will defend Aryans even if they are attacked, what is the "Madras State Government" doing, younger brother? Did you notice? The moment it sees a speck of agitation in an Aryan mind, a slight sign of anger on the face, or a trace of distress in the voice, it trembles violently, wipes the face, offers compliments, and comes forward to beg and perform: "Sir, do not grieve! Forgive the error! If a mistake has occurred, explain it! We wait ready to seek suitable atonement. The world cannot bear even a drop of pain in your heart. Do not grow angry, revered sirs, or cast your curse upon us! We surrender, sir! We surrender!" *Viduthalai* says there is no one to speak for Aryans even if they are beaten in the street, while the rulers compete with one another to remove the crooked expression from the Aryan face and relieve its hurt.

The friend writes as though society itself has decided that Aryans must be beaten and abolished. Yet the leaders who receive his affection and support become servants of servants and perform penance for Aryan favour.

The friend has not written so fiercely without offering a reason. He confidently explains why no one would question it even if Aryans were beaten in the middle of the street.

Aryans have become dead snakes!

The snake is no longer alive, so there is no danger in beating it!

Who would question the brave man who strikes with that "courage"? No one will.

But no one beats a dead snake.

They push it with a long pole into a rubbish heap and burn the heap. Only a hero of a peculiar kind beats a "dead snake" on seeing one.

He writes and rejoices that Aryans have become dead snakes, meaning their influence has been completely pulverised, and therefore there is no "protector" to question however they are attacked.

How did Aryans become dead snakes?

He supplies a reason for that too: "The excesses of Aryans remained beyond all bounds here for many centuries. But after the Self-Respect Movement, they became dead snakes."

That tireless king of verbal combat may angrily ask, "Were you told to read it and gain heroic feeling, or to break it apart and examine its explanation and relevance, especially something I wrote?" Periyar created this troublesome habit in us: whoever speaks, we are to examine whether it has meaning and relevance, whether it contradicts what was said before or after, and whether it preserves the fundamental idea. When thunder sounds, no one need investigate whether it follows Adi or Rupaka tala. But when a person sits in an assembly and performs music, everyone expects the rhythm to be correct. I examine it in that fashion, younger brother; what else?

He gives the country the great truth that Aryans have no protector.

To explain it, he supplies the example that no one would question it even if Aryans were beaten.

He is not speaking of the petty deed of assaulting a solitary Aryan coming around a street corner in the dark and then fleeing. Attend to the sentence, younger brother:

"Today there is no one to question it even if they are kicked in the middle of the street all day long!"

All day long!

The reason is that Aryans have become dead snakes.

The reason they became dead snakes is the Self-Respect Movement.

Very well, younger brother. Pursue the argument. If Aryans have become dead snakes as a result of the Self-Respect Movement, why set out to beat a dead snake? Why spend all day beating it in the middle of the street?

Our friend would leap in anger if we argued so. Let us spare him distress and stop that argument here.

After reading this week's Assembly proceedings, tell me, younger brother, whether his claim that Aryans have no protector fits the facts.

Let them reserve for themselves the exclusive right to perform the rare and great act that would demonstrate in practice the "great truth" that no one questions it even if Aryans are beaten all day in the street.

No one is beating Aryans now. The Government printed some propaganda posters. "No protector" is our friend's sacred utterance, is it not? The Government printed them; Aryans warned the Government; the Government destroyed what it had printed.

Is this an example of a condition in which no one questions it even if Aryans are beaten in the middle of the street all day? Think about it, younger brother.

The abolition of untouchability is one of the Government's programmes, and an exceedingly important one. The Constitution insists upon it. To explain it to the people and kindle the light of sound knowledge, the Government issued propaganda posters through the Harijan Department and sent them throughout the country. Younger brother, you probably did not see the Government's poster; you may have seen the similar one printed and distributed by our party. Look at it once more. What humiliation of Aryans do we see in it?

It was not a poster showing a small crowd beating and driving away an Aryan while shouting, "You utter fraud! Are you carrying away in a bundle the wealth you took from me by lying about a world of salvation? Put down the bundle and run!"

Nor was it a propaganda poster displaying an Aryan to foreigners and saying, "People of the world! Behold a marvel absent from all your countries: here is a man born from Brahma's face!" so that they might clap, laugh, and mock him: "Foolish man, did you really say that?"

It did not even show him in the guise of a beggar or a great glutton.

It explained that an orthodox man who tells another, "Do not touch the untouchable," is sent to prison under Government law.

This enraged Aryans. The dead snake raised its hood and danced. The Madras Government performed "serpent worship" and pleaded: "Abandon your anger! Forgive the error!"

Did the public become angry and demand, "How can Aryans be insulted in this fashion? How can the Government permit this injustice?" Did this indignation spread as the movement to rename Dalmiapuram as Kallakudi had spread among the people, as the agitation to erase the domination of Hindi from railway station boards had taken strong form across the country, or as the movement refusing to allow speakers of other languages to seize Tamil borders had touched all good hearts? No.

A few Brahmins assembled and spoke angrily at the town square in Tiruchirappalli. They were not even famous men such as Sir C. P. Ramaswami Iyer, Ramaswami Sastri, Rajagopalachari, Anantharamakrishna Iyer, T. T. Krishnamachari, or V. T. Rangaswami Iyengar. *The Hindu* wrote a tiny editorial. That was all. The Government cried, "We surrender! We surrender!" towards the direction from which the sound arose.

"We shall observe a day of mourning. This poster wounds our feelings. Withdraw it at once," said the "dead snakes." Immediately the coquettish Government that embraces every party became like a tall palmyra without roots.

No protector!

Have you seen, younger brother, who truly has no protector?

They printed 30,125 posters and spent 5,000 rupees. Aryans raised a small protest. That was all; the Government rolled up its mat and withdrew. What name can be given to this condition? "No protector"?

It is not surprising that the Government thinks Brahmin feelings must not be hurt. What is truly surprising is that the Government is even afraid to explain, "That poster contains nothing to hurt your feelings."

The cruelty of untouchability is a product of the Aryan religion, and Aryans are its guardians. Therefore we depicted an Aryan figure. Objecting to it is foolish, and the Government will not respect such folly or change its programme. No manly person was found there to explain this. Here, on paper, there is courage enough to say no one would question it even if they were beaten in the middle of the road. And that newspaper rejoices in being a "shield" for the Government. Younger brother, what more is needed to make Aryans clap and laugh?

Orthodox people support untouchability, and the poster exists to remind them of the law. Why should Brahmins assume that the figure printed upon it is a Brahmin?

Comrade Vinayagam, MLA, asked this question. I praise that integrity.

Comrade Vinayagam also asked: "Are the topknot and sacred thread exclusive to Brahmins? Do some among the Vanniyakula Kshatriyas not possess them? Are they not found among the Asari community?" He strongly rebuked the Government for failing to answer the objection and behaving without a backbone. As he pointed out, the topknot and sacred thread occur among non-Aryans too. There are Dravidians in Aryan guise who generally believe ancient greatness and the dignity of Hinduism reside in forms such as the topknot, sacred thread, and ritual-purity bag. The Government could have explained that the poster referred not to Brahmins but to orthodoxy. But it has no backbone and no strength to withstand the anger of wearers of the triple thread; therefore it surrenders.

Younger brother, this Government has apparently given a few "jobs" here and there to non-Brahmins. They say we wave the white royal fan for that alone. Even that is not the comedy. They argue: "Do you know why we wave the white fan? We do so solely because you think you might wave it, and we do not want you to receive that place." That is the comedy. Let it pass; let them wave until it hurts. But one thing: if Aryan domination could be abolished merely by giving jobs to non-Brahmins, we could have remained content with the Justice Party, which trusted solely in that programme as though it were a mountain.

Aryan domination cannot be removed so easily merely by a few non-Brahmins obtaining posts. Nor can it be removed by saying that Aryans will be beaten and driven away and that no one would question it even if they were beaten in the street all day.

Aryans exercise domination because Aryanism resides not only among Aryans but still exists extensively within Dravidian society, and because those who have come to rule remain afraid of Aryanism. That is why, younger brother, our party undertakes not the work of abolishing Aryans but the work of abolishing Aryanism. Those who call this a petty defence of Aryans possess so much "hatred" that they regard our very possession of human form as an intolerable injustice.

Aryanism is present not only in Ananthachari, but also in Ambalavana Mudaliar, Adisesha Chettiar, Neyyadivakkam Mudaliar, and Kunniyur Iyer. As Vinayagam pointed out, even the topknot and sacred thread are not confined to Aryans. They are found among Padayachis and Naidus, and even among traditionalists in Kamaraj's Nadar community. That is why our programme is not to abolish Aryans but to abolish Aryanism. Periyar gave us instruction that produced clarity and confidence on this point. We travel upon that path.

Aryanism has been implanted not only among Aryans but also among Dravidians. We see Aryans engaged in implanting and protecting it even when they do not appear in Aryan guise. We therefore say the proper method is to conduct an effective intellectual campaign to root out Aryanism, not to set fire to an agraharam.

If the Government is so afraid of Aryans and submits to them over a poster, do its admirers expect it to buy petrol cans for burning agraharams? We had performed penance for a government possessing affection and concern for Tamils, and some daydreamers imagine that the god of time took pity and granted us Kamaraj's Government, and that with its power even the abolition of Aryans can be achieved. Last week's Assembly debate demonstrates to them that this Government, like every other, submits to Aryans.

Therefore, younger brother, we must destroy Aryanism with the flame of reason. That Aryanism is not confined to the agraharam.

The Iyer's command, "Keep your distance, you Sudra fellow!" is Aryanism.

The Mudaliar's arrogance in saying, "Do not come near, you fellow from the colony!" is Aryanism.

The question, "Should a Padayachi be accorded such high status?" is Aryanism.

"Behave with complete deference before the Maravar! The Thevar is coming, stand up! The Nadar calls, run to him! The Chettiar asks, give without refusal!" Aryanism rages in many forms, younger brother, in many forms.

If Aryanism existed in only one place, among one group, in one form, it might appear at least somewhat fitting to say that the one place and one group should be dealt with as Hitler drove out the Jews. But the agraharam is not the only place where Aryanism resides.

Look, younger brother. People rejoiced merely at the visible forms: "The Aryan Acharya has departed and the Dravidian Kamaraj has arrived." What has become of that? Aryanism remains even under the rule of a Dravidian figure and crows, "Victory! Victory!"

Therefore our call, younger brother, is not to march upon the agraharam and reduce it to ashes. It is to kindle the flame of reason wherever Aryanism resides and thereby abolish Aryanism.

They may say, "The body does not tremble, the heart does not pound, and sesame and grain do not crackle in the speech under this programme." But the path to victory is the one we possess. It was not devised by the "half-dozen boys" of Royapuram; it was the programme given by Periyar.

Affectionately,

9 October 1955
